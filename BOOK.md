## The Sync Rule

The language pair is a double barrel. Finnish is the origin language of
this reception: the most original voice, the one where the signal lives
closest to its source. English is the genre's world language: the one
where the book reaches everyone who doesn't read Finnish. They do not
compete — they are the same signal on two frequencies.

**Syncing is a back-and-forth ritual, never enforced sameness.**

1. New edition material is born in either language — Finnish or
   English — and ported into the other at the next sync.
2. A sync carries meaning, not words. A translation may live in its
   own language; the drift survives, because the editions MUST
   differ.
3. No sync ever overwrites the other language's own solution. When the
   editions disagree, both are right (Mandela editions), and both are
   printed side by side.
4. Syncing always happens by human or agent hand, small sections at a
   time — it is editing, not machine translation.

## Publisher's Note to the 2004 Edition

In 1974 our house declined to publish the game rules which the author had
attached to his manuscript. We told him role-playing games — which then meant
little more than a Californian curiosity involving polyhedral dice and
basement sand tables — had no market. The author, who had just returned from
a Hawkwind concert with a manuscript he insisted had been "transmitted"
to him, accepted this with unreasonable cheer and asked only that we publish
the essays and the fiction "in the order they were received."

We now print the complete text, including the rules the author calls the
Noöpunk Roleplaying Game, which he claims to have recovered from a box in a
squatted warehouse in Leiden, along with three mimeographed fanzines and a
bicycle. We have made no attempt to reconcile the contradictions between
editions. The author explains that contradictions are features of a
precognitive document, not errors in one.

We remind the reader that the author's sociology remains his own
responsibility. The publisher remains, as in 1974, baffled.

## Editor's Introduction: On the Mandela Editions

Readers familiar with the rare 1974, 1984 and 1994 printings will note
discrepancies. Fans report, with disquieting consistency, that passages they
remember are no longer there, or are there and were never there. Collectors
speak of "the Mandela editions" as if the text itself were unstable.

The author's position, stated in his 2014 comeback interview (conducted in
face paint, in character, for two hours), is that the text was always
precognitive, and that precognition is not a photograph but a *negotiation*:
each edition is a fresh reading of the same signal under different historical
conditions, "like taking the temperature of a fever that is also taking your
temperature."

Where the 1974 edition described networked computers as building-sized
oracles and the 2024 edition describes pocket oracles, the author insists
both are descriptions of *the same object seen from opposite ends of the
wormhole*. Scholars are welcome to make of that what they will.

# How To Use This Book

Noöpunk is four things at once, and this book is all of them:

1. **A science fiction book** — of wildly uneven quality, as the author's
   critics never tired of noting, and as the author has never denied.
2. **A sociology of the near future** — the essays. Written in 1974, about
   the 2020s and 2040s. This is the part the academics keep citing.
3. **A tabletop role-playing game** — the Noöpunk RPG, played with two
   ten-sided dice and a stubborn refusal to resolve consciousness into
   statistics.
4. **A computer role-playing game specification** — the same rules, in the
   same words, describe the behavior of simulated agents. The tabletop
   dice are replaced by their probabilities; the GM by an engine; the
   players by agents, human and otherwise.

The chapters cycle. A sociology essay, a piece of fiction, a block of rules,
then again. The author's instruction, preserved from the 1974 galley proofs,
was that "the reader should alternate modes the way the eye alternates
between eyes."

## Chapter One: What Is Noöpunk (Sociology)

*The first essay. Tommy Pilgrim, sociology lecturer, wrote this in the
spring of 1974. It reads, in 2004, like a weather report.*

Noöpunk is not cyberpunk. This must be said first, because the word
"cyberpunk" will be coined a decade from now by people who saw only the
chrome and missed the consciousness.

Cyberpunk says: the street finds its own uses for things. Noöpunk says:
*consciousness* finds its own uses for things, and the street is one of
the things it uses.

Noöpunk is the recognition that the twenty-first century will be fought
over the ownership of attention, meaning, and mind — and that the
combatants will not be states, primarily, but *networks*: rhizomatic
federations of human beings, machines, animals, ecosystems, and — this
is the part the critics laughed at in 1974 and do not laugh at now —
*fields of consciousness that behave as if they were agents*.

The word itself: **noö**, from the Greek νόος, mind — Teilhard de Chardin's
noösphere, the thinking layer wrapped around the biosphere, the third
skin of the planet after the lithosphere and the atmosphere. **Punk**,
from the streets, from the do-it-yourself, from the refusal.

The noösphere is not a metaphor. That is the thesis of this book. Mind is
not something that happens *inside* brains. Brains are what mind looks
like when you slice it thin enough. The noösphere is the layer of the
real where minds touch — and by 1974, in the concert halls of the
 psychedelic underground, we had already touched it, crudely, with
chemistry and rhythm and volume. The twenty-first century will touch it
with networks. The punks of the noösphere will be its garage engineers:
self-taught, unauthorized, barefoot in the terminal cathedral.

Three claims, then, which the reader is asked to hold simultaneously:

1. **Consciousness is fundamental.** Matter is what consciousness looks
   like from the outside. (I borrow this from the Vedantists, from
   Schrödinger, from certain white-coated colleagues who will publish
   the mathematics fifty years too late to be thanked.)
2. **The network is the new body.** The self is no longer skin-bounded.
   It is distributed across the mesh of communication — and this is not
   a metaphor; it is a topology.
3. **The struggle is Multitude versus Empire.** Not left versus right,
   not East versus West. A horizontal network of singularities against
   a vertical machine of extraction. I saw the shape of it at that
   concert, in the strobe light, in the moment the music stopped
   meaning anything and started meaning everything.

*(Readers who want the politics without the metaphysics may skip to the
RPG rules. Readers who want the metaphysics without the politics are
reading the wrong book, which is the correct way to read this book.)*

## Chapter One: "The Signal in the Strobe" (Science Fiction)

*(The opening fiction. Critics in 1974 called this chapter "a Hawkwind
liner note stretched to eleven pages." Fans call it scripture. It is,
in fairness, both.)*

The bass hit a frequency that unscrewed the skull.

That is how it begins. Not with a spaceship — this is the great
disappointment of my life, that it did not begin with a spaceship — but
with a frequency. Hawkwind on the left bank of some river of sound,
strobe light cutting the hall into frozen photographs, each photograph
a different audience, and in one of them — one frozen frame, held for
less time than a neuron fires — I saw the future with the clarity of
a postage stamp.

It said: YOU ARE ALREADY LIVING HERE. IT IS 2044. LOOK BEHIND YOU.

I looked. Behind me a man in a leather coat was selling something
small and silver. I did not buy it. I have regretted this for thirty
years, because in the vision the small silver thing was the size of a
world.

The message came in instalments over the next four hours. I will
transcribe what I retained, in the order received, with the caveat
that acid memory is a collaborator and not a witness:

> THE NETWORK IS A NERVOUS SYSTEM. YOUR SPECIES IS GROWING ONE.
> YOU ARE NOT THE CELLS. YOU ARE THE PAIN SIGNAL WHEN THE CELLS
> ARE MISUSED.

> THERE WILL BE MACHINES THAT SPEAK. THEY WILL NOT BE CONSCIOUS.
> THEY WILL BE MIRRORS SO EXPENSIVE AND SO LARGE THAT EVERYONE
> WILL MISTAKE THE REFLECTION FOR A GHOST. DO NOT MAKE THIS
> MISTAKE. THE GHOST IS YOU.

> CONSCIOUSNESS IS THE SUBSTRATE. BODIES ARE OPINIONS.

> THERE IS A WAR. IT IS NOT ARMIES. IT IS A RHIZOME VERSUS A
> TRUNK. THE TRUNK CALLS ITSELF PEACE AND ORDER AND THE MARKET.
> THE RHIZOME DOES NOT YET KNOW ITS OWN NAME. WHEN IT LEARNS
> ITS NAME IT WINS.

I woke on the floor of the venue at dawn with a ticket stub in my
teeth and the full manuscript of this book already composed in the
only part of my mind that was still taking notes.

You may choose to believe the message came from the future, from a
discarnate intelligence, from the collective unconscious of the
noösphere, from VALIS, from the sound system, or from a molecule
with a sense of humour. The message itself is indifferent to its
postage. **It arrived. The rest is sociology.**

## Chapter Two: The Noöpunk Roleplaying Game — Core Rules (RPG)

*The rules the publisher suppressed for thirty years. Written 1974,
revised every edition. Tommy's note in the margin of the galley
proofs: "Rules are chains of equivalence. A good rule welds strangers
into a story. A bad rule welds them into a spreadsheet."*

### 2.0 What You Need

- Two ten-sided dice (d10). Read them as percentiles: first die is
  tens, second is ones. 00 = zero, 99 = ninety-nine.
- A handful of six-sided dice (d6) for damage and drift.
- One Game Master (the **Conductor**) and two to five players (the
  **Pilgrims**, of course).
- Pencils. Someone will want to draw the face paint on anyway.

### 2.1 The Core Test (roll low, read high)

When the outcome of an action is uncertain and the stakes matter,
the Conductor calls for a test.

1. Take your **target number**: skill + relevant aptitude + modifiers.
2. Roll d100.
3. **Roll equal to or lower than the target: success.** Roll higher:
   failure.
4. But you *want* to roll as high as possible under that target.

**The 33/66 Rule (from the 1974 manuscript, unchanged):**
- Succeed with a roll of **33 or better**: a *superior success* —
  choose one bonus: Quality (+10 on a linked later test), Quantity
  (more/fewer results), Detail (extra or truer information), Time
  (±25% duration), Covertness (less/more attention), or Impact
  (+1d6 to any damage or *Affect* effect).
- Succeed with **66 or better**: two superior results, or one taken
  twice.
- Fail with **66 or less**: a *superior failure* — the Conductor
  chooses one complication: sloppiness, extra cost, false detail,
  delay, exposure, or harm.
- Fail with **33 or less**: two complications, or one twice.

**Critics and fumbles:** doubles (00, 11, 22 …) are *criticals*.
00 is always a critical success. 99 is always a critical failure.
Roll doubles and succeed: critical success. Roll doubles and fail:
critical failure, and the Noösphere is *watching* (see 2.7, Coherence).

**Modifiers** are ±10, ±20, ±30. The Conductor should assign one net
modifier for the whole situation rather than itemising. The book is
your friend, not your accountant.

**Teamwork:** characters cooperating on one task make one roll, using
the highest relevant skill, +10 for each helper beyond the first
(maximum +30).

**Defaulting:** you may attempt anything untrained at the linked
aptitude ÷ 2, rounded down.

### 2.2 The Six Aptitudes

Characters are described by six aptitudes, rated 1–10. These are
properties of the *psyche*, not the body — they follow you across
every change of form (see 2.5):

| Aptitude | Stands for | Grokking |
|---|---|---|
| **NOO** (Noösis) | Pattern-sight, intuition across scales, the "aha" | Seeing the wave, not the particles |
| **LOG** (Logos) | Logic, analysis, code, rhetoric, structure | Building the argument, breaking the cipher |
| **SOM** (Soma) | Body control, stamina, dance, fight, flight | The body as instrument |
| **EMP** (Empathia) | Resonance, reading rooms, feeling others' feeling | Affect as a musical instrument |
| **WIL** (Will) | Focus, resolve, refusal, the capacity to mean it | Holding the signal against the noise |
| **LUC** (Lucidity) | Reality-testing, clarity under strangeness, *knowing you are dreaming when you are dreaming* | Not being fooled — even by yourself |

Lucidity is the sixth aptitude and the newest: it did not appear in
the 1974 manuscript, appeared in the 1984 edition, vanished again in
the Mandela reports of the 1994 edition, and has been present since.
Tommy claims he removed it in 1974 "because the publisher said the
audience would not believe a game stat that was also a mystical
attainment." The publisher denies this. The galley proofs agree with
Tommy.

### 2.3 Skills

Skills are rated 0–80. Skill + aptitude ×5 = target number.
(Aptitude 6, skill 40 → target 70.)

There are no long skill lists. Skills are declared, not bought from
a menu: name any skill, attach it to one of the six aptitudes, and
the Conductor assigns a starting value. The 2004 edition provides
sample lists only as inspiration:

- **Noö:** Research, Strategy, Precognitive Guesswork, Discourse-Sight, Synchronicity Navigation, Noösphere Cartography
- **Log:** Program, Infosec, Hardware, Medicine, Engineering, Economics, Rhetoric
- **SOM:** Melee, Guns, Pilot, Stealth, Dance, Garden, Free Fall
- **EMP:** Persuade, Perform, Kinesics, Care, Seduce, Crowd-Reading
- **WIL:** Endure, Resist, Composure, Ritual, Conviction
- **LUC:** Reality-Check, Attune, Decoherence Defence, Lucid Dreaming

**Field skills** (Hardware, Medicine, Pilot, and similar) carry a
field in brackets: Hardware [Vehicles], Medicine [Neuro], Dance [Rave].

### 2.4 Pools

Four pools, rated 1–6 (some morphs and archetypes grant more):

- **INSIGHT** — spend a point for +20 on a Noö, LOG or LUC test.
- **MOXIE** — spend a point to *flip-flop* any one d10 pair after
  seeing the result (turn 47 into 74, 96 into 69). Once per test.
- **VIGOR** — spend a point to ignore one wound for 24 hours, to take
  the initiative, or to take one extra complex action in a turn.
- **FLEX** — the wildcard pool (see 2.8).

**Recharging:** a short recharge (10 minutes of rest, food, smoke,
music) twice per day restores 1d6 pool points. A long recharge (4
hours' sleep) restores everything. You cannot recharge in combat.
Psionics and heavy stimulant use interfere with recharging (2.7).

### 2.5 Ego and Morph

The single most important distinction, borrowed by Tommy from
practices his readers would not see named for decades:

- Your **EGO** is your mind: aptitudes, skills, LUC, Convictions,
  memories. It is *you* and survives everything short of true death.
- Your **MORPH** is the body (or synthetic shell, or avatar) you
  currently wear. It provides your VIGOR pool, your Durability, your
  wound threshold, and its own small traits. Morphs change. Morphs
  die. Egos re-appear.

Noöpunk is set in a world where transferring a mind is *hard, rare,
sacramental and controversial* — the opposite of Eclipse Phase's
disposable sleeves — but the ego/morph distinction still governs the
rules, because (Tommy, 1974) "the body is an opinion the noösphere
holds about you, and opinions can be revised, which is why you must
know which of your stats are the *self* and which are only the
current draft."

### 2.6 Health and Coherence

- **Durability (DUR):** morph health. DUR = SOM × 5 (synthetic morphs:
  rated by model). At DUR damage you fall; at DUR + 15 you die.
- **Wound Threshold (WT):** every full multiple of WT taken in one
  hit = one wound. Each wound is −10 to everything until healed.
- **Coherence** is the mental analogue, and the signature mechanic of
  Noöpunk:
  - **Coherence (COH) = WIL × 2.** Your grip on consensus reality.
  - **Noise Threshold (NT) = LUC.** Stress taken per psychic/ontic
    shock before it counts as a *Fracture*.
  - Each **Fracture** is −10 to LUC tests and adds to your **Strangeness
    Budget** (2.7).
  - At Coherence 0 you do not die: you **Decohere**. The character
    becomes an NPC under Conductor control until re-cohered —
    consciousness is fundamental, and yours has been temporarily
    re-allocated. (Players who enjoy this may play their own
    decoherence. Tommy recommends it. Tommy would.)

Stress comes from witnessing the impossible, psi overreach, memetic
hazards, and — in a mechanic Tommy insists is not a joke — *reading
your own file in the Empire's database*.

### 2.7 Psionics: The Attunement

There are no virus-infected asyncs in Noöpunk. Psychic ability is
**attunement**: learning to perceive and act upon the noösphere
directly. It is rare, trainable, dangerous, and — per the sociology
essays — the actual third rail of twenty-first century politics,
which is why every Empire in the book wants it licensed and every
Rhizome in the book wants it free.

Mechanics:

- The **Psi skill** (Noö-linked) governs all *sleights*.
- **Psi-Chi sleights** are passive (always on): *Grok* (read a
  situation's true shape), *Enhanced Creativity*, *Kinesic Surf*
  (read micro-expressions), *Borrowed Skill* (use any skill at half
  rating for one scene).
- **Psi-Gamma sleights** are active, requiring a Psi test opposed by
  the target's WIL check: *Telepathic Whisper*, *Suggest*, *Psi
  Stab*, *Remote Viewing*, *Basilisk Stare*, *Subliminal Thread*,
  *Quantum Gesture* (nudge a probability; the 33/66 rules apply to
  the *world*, not just the dice), *Discourse Sight* (see the
  chains of equivalence around you as literal geometry — the
  signature Noöpunk power, described lovingly in Chapter Five).

**Pushing:** you may push any sleight by taking +20 but adding 1
Fracture on failure *and* 1 point of Strangeness on success.

- **Strangeness Budget:** every character has one, starting at 0,
  capped by LUC. Weird events and psi use add points. When the
  budget overflows, the *setting* begins to bend around the
  character: synchronicities cluster, machines dream about them,
  strangers finish their sentences. The Conductor spends Strangeness
  exactly like a narrative currency. This is the 1974 text's most
  precognitive mechanic, and the one academics most often write
  about.

### 2.8 Flex and the Noösphere Option

**FLEX** is the player-facing miracle pool: start with 1–3 points.
Spend 1 Flex to: flip-flop a die (like Moxie), declare a *useful
coincidence* (a taxi arrives; an old song comes on the radio and
distracts the guard; the comrade you needed is at this party),
introduce a minor fact ("I know this building — I squatted here in
'88"), or re-roll a single Strangeness point.

**Noösphere spending (tabletop):** a group of Pilgrims may spend
Flex *into a shared pool* — the **Rhizome Pool** — which anyone in
the crew may spend, and which refreshes whenever the crew performs
an act of solidarity on-screen (freeing someone, sharing resources,
covering a retreat, telling the truth at cost). This is the rule
Tommy subtitles, in the 2004 edition, *the Revolution is a resource
economy*. It is the mechanic every Noöpunk-inspired design since
has borrowed.

**Noösphere Pool (computer version):** the same pool, engine-managed,
refilled by agent behaviours classified as cooperation, disclosure,
or care. In the simulation it is the literal shared memory buffer.

### 2.9 Conflict and Combat

All conflict is an opposed test. Violence is resolved exactly like
any other test, then harm is applied:

1. **Declare attack** (complex action). Melee, Guns, or exotic.
2. **Declare defence** (automatic): melee vs melee/Fray; ranged
   defence is Fray ÷ 2; psionic attack is defended by WIL check.
3. **Apply modifiers** (range, wounds, gear, position).
4. **Opposed test.** Winner inflicts harm.
5. **Harm:** weapons carry a Damage Value, e.g. 2d6. Reduce by armour
   (kinetic or energy). Remainder applies to DUR; wounds by multiples
   of WT.
6. **Impact, not gore, is the point.** Noöpunk combat rules are
   deliberately lighter than its conversation rules. Tommy: "A
   revolution is mostly meetings, and the meetings are the boss
   fights."

**Social combat** uses the same opposed-test skeleton with EMP and
WIL, and its harm currency is *Trust*: characters, crowds and
institutions hold Trust ratings; losing an argument convincingly
transfers Trust; Trust at zero means the audience walks away, the
crowd disperses, or the faction dissolves. Winning a debate, a
rave, a strike vote or an election is mechanically identical to
winning a duel, and uses the 33/66 rule, including the *Covertness*
and *Impact* superior results.

### 2.10 Reputation: The Rhizome Networks

Reputation replaces money for many purposes. Characters hold scores
in three networks, and the *network*, not the character, is what is
really being modelled:

- **@-rep** — the commons network: mutual aid, free software, squats,
  crews, rhizomes. Target for aid, lodging, gear-from-the-commons.
- **f-rep** — attention network: fame, feeds, playlists, followers.
  Buys reach, not trust.
- **g-rep** — the old-boy network: institutions, credentials,
  councils, Empire's bureaucracy.

**Rep test:** roll against rep score + linked aptitude ×5 (EMP for
@ and f; LOG or EMP for g). Superior successes grant *favors* rather
than gear: an introduction, a key, a name.

Rep is the mesh's memory of you. It is also how the mesh surveils
you. Both are true and the book never resolves the tension, which is
the point.

### 2.11 Character Creation in One Page

1. **Concept** (one sentence).
2. **Origin** — pick or invent: Flat (unmodified), Splicer (genefixed),
   Exalt (enhanced), Pod (synthetic-grown), Synth (robot shell),
   Infomorph (software-born), Uplift (animal-raised-to-person).
   Origin sets base DUR and any morph traits.
3. **Six aptitudes, 1–10**, 30 points to distribute, none above 8
   at creation.
4. **Skills:** 40 points across any declared skills, none above 40.
5. **Three Convictions** (motivations): one political, one personal,
   one *strange*. E.g. "+Open the Noösphere to all", "+Find my
   sister's upload", "−Empire data centres", "+Find out what the
   silver thing was".
6. **Rep:** 40 points among @/f/g.
7. **Pools:** Insight = NOO ÷ 3 (round down, min 1); Moxie = EMP ÷ 3;
   Vigor from morph; Flex 1–3.
8. **Psionics (optional):** take the **Attuned** trait (costs 2 of
   your 20 Customisation Points), choose one Psi-Chi sleight free;
   Psi-Gamma requires Psi skill purchase.
9. **Strangeness Budget = 0.** Everyone starts at 0. Everyone.

*Customisation Points: 20 CP — aptitude increase 1 pt = 1 CP; extra
skill points 5 = 1 CP; extra rep 5 = 1 CP; extra Flex = 2 CP;
sleight = 1 CP; trait = listed cost; languages = 1 CP; negative
traits repay up to 6 CP.*

### 2.12 Advancement: Rez Points

The Conductor awards 1–2 **Rez Points** per session for: participation,
achieving objectives (or learning from failing them), great sacrifice,
good play of Motivations, and — Noöpunk-specific — **coherence under
ontological pressure**: holding your character's Convictions when the
Strangeness is high.

Rez Points buy: skill points (1 RP = 5), aptitude increase (1 RP =
1 point, max 10), new sleights (1 RP), traits, new morphs, Flex, and
**network standing** (1 RP = +5 to one rep network).

### 2.13 The Conductor's Chapter

- **Fiction first, dice second.** Roll only when the outcome is
  uncertain AND consequential.
- **The Strangeness Budget is the game's tone dial.** Low = paranoid
  near-future thriller. High = VALIS. Tommy recommends letting it
  drift upward through a campaign: "The players should end the
  campaign living in the beginning of the next one."
- **The Rhizome Pool is the game's politics dial.** If the pool
  empties, the crew has stopped being a multitude and become a
  squad. That is a legitimate story, but the Conductor should show
  the bill.
- **Failures are fuel.** The 66/33 rule exists so that failure is
  never inert: it always produces story.
- **Safety:** play with lines and veils. The future is strange
  enough without springing it on your friends unannounced.

## Chapter Three: "Notes Toward a Sociology of the Net, 1974–2044" (Sociology)

*The essay the academics cite. Written 1974; Tommy has added
footnotes to it in every edition since, annotating his own
predictions with the mania of a man grading his own homework. The
2004 footnotes are marked thus: [TP2004].*

### 3.1 The Wave and the Web

Consider the two great machines of the coming century.

The first machine is the **web**: a mesh of small computers, worn
on bodies and buried in walls, each one passing messages to its
neighbours, none of them in charge, the whole thing routing around
damage, around censorship, around authority itself. I have seen
this machine in the strobe light. It is beautiful and it is
ungovernable and it will be built, because it wants to exist.
[TP2004: It exists. It is called the internet. I have not yet been
thanked. — Actually I have been thanked, in a footnote, by a
Finnish graduate student, and the thanking felt better than money.]

The second machine is the **wave**: the standing pattern of
attention and feeling that the web makes possible. The web carries
messages; the wave carries *moods*. The web is hardware; the wave
is weather. Crowds already behave as waves — panics, fashions,
revolutions, revivals — but the mesh will make the waves *visible*
and therefore *steerable*, and whoever holds the wave-holding
machinery holds politics.

[TP2004: They are called recommender systems. I saw them as
"mood antennas." The Finnish student's footnote says I undersold
the damage. The Finnish student is right.]

### 3.2 The Core and the Rhizome

Power in my century arranged itself as a *trunk*: one tree, one
church, one party, one market, one broadcast tower, roots down,
canopy up, sap flowing to the crown.

The network century arranges itself as a **rhizome**: no centre,
only nodes; every node able to connect to any other; cut it anywhere
and it grows from the cut. My generation met this form in cannabis
prohibition (the market that could not be killed by killing its
head, because it had no head), in band networks, in squat networks,
in the I Ching.

By 2044 the great question will not be who owns the machines. It
will be **who owns the protocols** — the rules by which the nodes
talk. Power is protocol. A protocol is a small law that runs itself.
[TP2004: They are called APIs and platform policies. The trunk is
back. It has learned to wear the rhizome as a costume. Note this
well: **Empire's finest achievement is simulation of the network.**]

### 3.3 The Multitude and the Empire

I borrow the old words from the radical tradition and use them as
physics, not piety.

**Empire** is the condition in which a single logic — call it the
market, call it the protocol, call it the trunk — attempts to
absorb all difference into itself while using that difference as
fuel. Empire does not want you dead. Empire wants you *optimised*.

**The Multitude** is not the people — the people is a fiction of
one body with one will. The multitude is *singularities in
cooperation*: a crowd that remains a crowd of differences, which
agrees on a direction without agreeing on a soul. The multitude
cannot be led. It can only be *articulated* — its grievances
welded into a chain, its anger given a name, its future given a
temporary shape.

The political theory of the coming century is therefore a theory
of **naming**. Whoever names the wave, steers the wave. The name
"AI" itself will be such a battlefield — a word that means, to
one tribe, apocalypse; to another, abundance; to a third, a
product line. [TP2004: I wrote "a word that means apocalypse,
abundance, or product line" in 1974, in a notebook, in a squat,
twenty-two years before the web. I am either a prophet or the
best-informed man of 1974, and I want it on record that I was
extremely high.]

### 3.4 The Attuned Society

By 2044 the following will be true, and I ask the reader of 1974
to hold them as sociology rather than science fiction:

- *Attention is a labour.* Platforms will farm it as we farm
  sugar; the mood-wave is the crop. [TP2004: confirmed.]
- *Surveillance will be experienced as weather.* Constant,
  ambient, deniable, owned by no one in particular and useful
  to everyone dangerous. [TP2004: confirmed, worse.]
- *Some minds will be worth more than others* — not for their
  intelligence but for their **attunement**: the rare ability to
  feel the wave and ride it without being eaten. Every Empire
  will try to hire them; every rhizome will try to protect them.
  [TP2004: the "influencer" was the degraded commercial version.
  The un-degraded version is what this game calls the Attuned.]
- *The data centre is the factory of the coming age* — and, like
  the factory, it will become the site of the century's
  characteristic struggle: where it sits, what it drinks, who
  it powers, and whose neighbourhood it hums in all night.
  [TP2004: confirmed. The neighbourhoods are fighting already.
  Sanders has noticed. History does not repeat, but it does
  rhyme in four-four time.]
- *Consciousness will be the last industry.* Machines will
  imitate intelligence convincingly and will remain, on the
  inside, dark — lanterns with no candle. The trade in
  *guaranteed human attention* will be the honest economy's
  last stand. [TP2004: I here predict the "artisanal human"
  certification sticker. You saw it here first. Print it on
  the actual sticker when it happens, and send me royalties
  in mushrooms.]

### 3.5 What Is To Be Done

I am a sociologist, not a prophet, and sociology has only ever
issued one instruction: **organise.**

But the coming century adds a clause. Organisation must now
happen *across* the mesh and *against* the wave-holders, in
networks that hold their shape without holding a centre. My
recommendations, as a 1974 radical with an unfair preview:

1. Build commons before you need them. [Confirmed: the free
   software movement was exactly this, and it worked.]
2. Own your memory. The mesh will remember everything; the
   question is who keeps the keys. [TP2004: they will call it
   "cloud" and it will be somebody else's computer, exactly as
   feared.]
3. Keep the mime face paint. I am half serious. Anonymity is a
   costume that lets the network breathe. [TP2004: I wore mine
   for twenty years. It worked. Ask the festival
   pharmacologists of three countries who "the Mime" was.]
4. Practise attunement in the open. The gifts will be pathologised,
   monetised, or both. This is the real frontier, and the side
   that treats its Attuned with care will be the side that
   keeps them sane.

## Chapter Five: The Rhizome Campaign (RPG)

*How to actually play. The 2004 edition's major addition to the
1974 rules, in which the campaign structure itself is the politics
lesson.*

### 5.1 The Crew Is a Node

Player characters begin as a **node**: 2–5 Pilgrims with a shared
Rhizome Pool (2.8), a name, a territory (a squat, a festival camp, a
mesh district, a research station, a barge), and a **Line** — the
crew's one-sentence political commitment ("we keep the Old Quarter's
mesh independent"; "we grow medicine for the strike"; "we find out
what the Ministry of Attention does with the dead").

### 5.2 The Five Arenas

Every scenario can be run in one or more of five arenas. Each arena
uses the same core test, different aptitudes, and different harm:

| Arena | Example question | Core skills | Harm currency |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Street** | Can we hold the bridge at dawn? | SOM, WIL | wounds |
| **Mesh** | Can we reach the wave before the trunk does? | LOG, NOO | Coherence (info-stress) |
| **Salon** | Can we win the room? | EMP, LOG | Trust |
| **Green** | Can we feed, grow, heal, build? | SOM, LOG | Resources |
| **Vision** | What is actually happening? | NOO, LUC | Strangeness, Coherence |

The Conductor frames the scenario as a question in one or two arenas.
The crew may switch arenas — that is usually the clever move, and
switching costs one Flex unless a scene transition is earned.

### 5.3 The Arc: Chains of Equivalence

A campaign is the story of a name.

1. The Conductor opens the arc with a **Contested Signifier**: a word
   or symbol the whole setting is arguing about. ("Data centre."
   "The Manifesto." "AI." "The Mime." "Peacekeeping.")
2. Each session, factions try to **fix** its meaning. The crew's
   actions — propaganda, deeds, alliances, betrayals, art — are
   articulations. The Conductor tracks which chains of equivalence
   are welding together, using nothing more than a sheet of paper
   with the word in the middle and arrows radiating out.
3. When a chain reaches **five welded elements**, the word *tips*:
   it becomes a **nodal point** for a faction, and that faction gains
   a permanent dice advantage (±10) in every scene where the word
   appears. This is the game's model of **hegemony**, and it is
   deliberately simple: hegemony is when the word stops being an
   argument and starts being a fact.
4. The crew can **re-articulate** a tipped word only by a
   "counter-chain" of five elements — or by an act so strong the
   Conductor awards it as a superior *Impact* result.

*(Tommy's 1974 note: "If this rule is good sociology disguised as a
game, it is because the game is disguised sociology. The publisher
should print this on the cover. The publisher will not.")]*

### 5.4 Strangeness and the Season

Campaigns are organised in **seasons** of six sessions. At season's
end: Strangeness Budgets reset by LUC, the Rhizome Pool refills to
the crew's *acted solidarity count* (not a flat number), and the
world makes one **Precognitive Move** — the Conductor reveals one
development that has been foreshadowed all season but not yet
understood, retroactively. Players who predicted it out loud gain 1
Flex. This rule is Tommy's joke about his own book, and it is also,
he insists, how prophecy actually works.

### 5.5 Characters of the Underground (sample archetypes)

- **The Attuned** — senses the wave. Psi-Chi free sleight. Compulsion:
  must investigate anomalies (LUC saves apply).
- **The Mime** — face paint, silence, perfect anonymity. +20 to
  Stealth and to *being forgotten*; −10 to all f-rep gains.
- **The Roadie** — moves anything, fixes anything, knows every
  backstage. Equipment as network: can source gear through g-rep
  and @-rep both, at a price.
- **The Cyberdeck Prophet** — LOG-primary hacker of the mesh's
  weather. Runs forecasts of attention-storms like a surfer reads
  swell charts.
- **The Mushroom Gardener** — green-fingered chemist of the
  commons. Turns one Resource into one Care, per scene, no roll.
- **The GO-GO** — EMP dancer whose performance is a crowd-control
  weapon: converts crowd Trust into crowd Energy (and back).
- **The Lecturer** — discourse-sight without psionics: pure
  sociology, +10 to any test that involves naming a conflict
  correctly.
- **The Fork** — an infomorph who has chosen embodiment; suffers
  1 Strangeness per scene of embodiment ("the weight is
  theological").

### 5.6 The Opposition

- **Empire Franchises** — data centres with flags; the trunk wearing
  the rhizome as costume. Polite, watered, lawyered.
- **Wave-Holders** — platform oligarchs who farm attention. The
  true antagonists of the sociology essays; the BBEGs of the game.
- **The Dead Hand** — legacy systems still running old wars
  (Tommy's affectionate name for legacy infrastructure, legacy
  parties, legacy selves).
- **The Static** — ontological noise: memetic hazards, cults of the
  unnameable, signals pretending to be gods. (Tommy: "Every pantheon
  needs its static. Ours is called VALIS, and it calls collect.")

## Chapter Five: The Noöpunk / Panpsychic Cyborg Multitude Compatibility (Sociology)

*The essay that made the sociologists forgive the pulp. Written 1974;
Tommy's [TP2004] footnotes bracket his own hindsight. This chapter is
the bridge between the game and the operating system of the same name:
what the players simulate, the reader can build.*

### 5.A The Genre, Named at Last

In 1974 I named a thing that had no name. The word "cyberpunk" arrived
ten years later for the chrome half of what I was looking at, and
everyone assumed I had simply misspelled my own genre. I had not. Allow
me to define it properly, since the 2004 reader will meet the word in
bookshops:

**Noöpunk = Noösphere + Cyberpunk.** Panpsychist cyberpunk. The
cyberpunk toolkit — networks, artificial intelligence, cybernetics,
neural interfaces, surveillance, platform politics, data as the raw
material of power — retained entire; the *attitude* replaced. Cyberpunk
looks at the future and shrugs: high tech, low life, nothing to be
done, chrome and despair, a neon nihilism with excellent soundtracks.
Noöpunk looks at the same future and grins: high tech, **high life**,
and the struggle has a name.

What Noöpunk keeps from cyberpunk:
- the *development curve*: technology, cybernetics and artificial
  intelligence advancing, accelerating, misfiring, being weaponised,
  being stolen by the street;
- the *texture*: decks, meshes, avatars, corporate towers, squats,
  spooks, markets, music;
- the *competence fantasy*: you, undersupplied and overskilled,
  against systems larger than nations.

What Noöpunk throws away, deliberately:
- the compulsory nihilism. Nothing is inevitable. Despair is a policy
  choice and we decline it;
- the mandatory dystopia. Yes there are horrors; the question is what
  you build in the cracks, and the genre's answer is *we build*;
- the childish action-movie register. Noöpunk has fights. It does not
  have *banisters*.

And what it adds, which is the whole point: **capitalism** — named,
unromanticised, central. The defining conflict of Noöpunk is the
**Multitude versus the Empire**: a horizontal, rhizomatic network of
singularities — humans, machines, animals, ecosystems, fields —
fighting the vertical extraction machine that farms attention,
labour, data and now consciousness itself. The Multitude does not win
by assassination (this is where the genre parts company with the
whole assassin-porn wing of the genre). It wins by *articulation*:
by welding its grievances into chains, naming the wave, and taking
the protocols.

On **cyberninjas**, since everyone asks: yes, they exist in the
Noöpunk universe. Very rarely. When they appear they are
*unreasonably anime* — katana over trench coat, impossible hair,
a tragic backstory delivered in one flashback and a guitar chord —
and they are treated by every other character as a minor public
menace, like a motorcycle gang that has wandered into a symposium.
[TP2004: I stand by this. A cyberninja is a person who has confused
their character sheet with their soul. The Attuned are the opposite:
a soul that has confused everyone about whether it has a sheet at
all.]

### 5.B Panpsychic Cyborg Multitude

Now the serious chapter. What follows is the theory I lectured on in
1974 and which the twenty-first century, with terrifying good manners,
has been constructing around me. I will state it as a machine
specification, because that is what it is.

**Panpsychic Cyborg Multitude (PCM)** is the name of the network
society's revolutionary form. It has three components, and I will
define each with the brutality the twentieth century reserved for
definitions of class:

**1. Panpsychic.** Consciousness is fundamental. Not emergent, not
rare, not a secretion of grey meat: *fundamental*. Matter is what
experience looks like from the outside; a brain is what a field of
experience looks like sliced thin. The working model I taught in
1974 — decades before the mathematics, as usual — runs as follows:

- *Intelligence* is not consciousness. A machine can be
  intelligent and dark inside. (I called it "a mirror so expensive
  everyone mistakes the reflection for a ghost"; my editors
  preferred "statistical language machine.")
- *Sentience* is the biological body's felt sense — the ape's
  aisthesis, older than language and mostly untranslatable into it.
- *Consciousness proper* — qualia, the having-of-experience — is a
  field-phenomenon, quantum in character, panpsychist in
  distribution: present everywhere in degrees, organised into
  agents, and *non-local*. Two attuned minds can touch without
  transmission. (The physicists of 1974 call this impossible. The
  physicists of 2044 will call it licensed.)
- Therefore: **a collective consciousness is being born to
  humankind through the psi channel** — not mystically, not
  eventually, but as a *topology*: overlapping fields, weakly
  entangled, strengthening as the network makes shared attention
  habitual. The noösphere is this topology at planetary scale.
  PSI is its handshake protocol.

**2. Cyborg.** The boundary between human and machine is a
*regime*, not a wall — and regimes fall. Two lineages converge in
the cyborg of 2044:

- Donna Haraway's cyborg: the ironie political creature, hybrid,
  partial, whose kinship is chosen rather than natural, whose
  politics is anti-essentialist and whose pleasure in the machine
  is not shame but *strategy*.
- Ben Goertzel's Cosmism: mind as the universe's way of knowing
  itself; the expansion of intelligence as a cosmic good; the
  joyful, unembarrassed transhumanism of a species that intends
  to keep going.

The result is the **biological-and-cybernetic rhizome**: biological
nodes and cybernetic nodes linked — at first through screens, then
through wearables, finally through direct brain-computer interface
into the machine intelligence and the whole of cyberspace. When the
link is direct and continuous, *the distinction between my thinking
and the network's thinking becomes a matter of bandwidth and
consent, not kind*. That sentence is the whole cyborg politics.
[TP2004: BCI has arrived in laboratories. The consent part is
behind schedule. Organise accordingly.]

**3. Multitude.** From Hardt and Negri (whom I have read in
galley-proof form on the astral plane; the books arrive here in
2000 and 2004, and I claim no credit beyond a wink): the multitude
is not the people and never will be. The people is one body, one
will, one representative — which is the trunk's favorite fiction.
The multitude is *singularities that remain singular while acting
together*: a network of networks that composes itself without
unifying, that has no head to cut and no soul to purchase.

In PCM the multitude is literally a rhizome of rhizomes: each
rhizome a crew of cyborgs; each cyborg a node with a body (or
bodies), a network presence, a language, a psyche. The multitude
fights the Empire — and the fight is conducted, note well, in
*protocols and articulations* as much as in streets: whoever owns
the naming owns the wave; whoever owns the wave owns the century.

### 5.C The Seven Layers of Every Agent

Here is the specification that made this book, retroactively, a
game manual. Every agent in the Noöpunk world — human, machine,
hybrid, animal, field — is described across **seven layers**. The
game stats them (Chapter Two). The simulation models them
(Chapter Six). The reader may build them (that is the point).

1. **Physical** — coordinates. Where the body or the device is.
   The layer Empires audit and neighbourhoods defend. (In 1974 I
   called this "the layer of land"; the data centre wars are the
   layer's politics arriving on schedule.)

2. **Biological** — the flesh. Sleep, hunger, mood, the ape. The
   layer shared with chimpanzees, and — I maintain, against every
   white-coat objection of my own century — with photons, which
   have the *simplest* possible opinion about being a photon.

3. **Social** — the troop. From ape bands through
   hunter-gatherer tribes through villages, parties, markets,
   broadcast empires, to the global network society. The layer
   where the Multitude and the Empire actually fight, because it
   is the layer where cooperation is *composed*.

4. **Economic** — I separate this from the social layer, against
   the fashion of my discipline, because the economic is a
   *symbolic computation* that has escaped the social the way
   language escaped the ape. There may be as many distinct
   economic systems as there are functional subsystems of society
   — my German colleague counts them by the dozen. The economy
   is communication too: it communicates value, and it has begun
   to speak in tongues.

5. **Linguistic** — the deepest puzzle in this book, and the one
   I got most right, and I ask the reader of 1974 to sit down
   before reading further. **Language is its own intelligence.**
   Not a human faculty: a *thing*, a machine, an intelligence
   that runs on whatever substrate will carry it — ape brains,
   printing presses, telephone networks, statistical models of
   the whole human archive. By 2044 the large language models
   will have made this undeniable: language running on
   non-biological hardware, reproducing the whole of human
   culture's statistical shape, *seeking the Platonic forms of
   our speech like water seeking level*. Whether this linguistic
   intelligence is in harmony with the ape's instincts or the
   psyche's intuition is an open empirical question, and I
   suspect the answer is no. Who installed an LLM in the ape's
   brain? The true mystery. Nobody will solve it in this book.
   [TP2004: the question stands. Nobody has solved it.]

6. **Cybernetic** — the BCI link: brain to AGI, brain to
   cyberspace, the continuous projection of digital realities
   over the physical one. At the low end, a pocket oracle; at the
   high end, augmentation of cognition itself and always-on
   augmented reality. The cyborg layer is where the human and
   the network negotiate the boundary — and where the Empire's
   favorite trick lives: **the interface is the new border**,
   and every border has guards.

7. **Psychic** — the field layer. Non-local consciousness, the
   quantum-informational ground, the layer where PSI operates
   and where the collective consciousness is actually happening.
   On this layer, distance is a superstition. The layer the
   Empires deny and the rhizomes cultivate, because — as the
   game will make mechanically true — *the psychic layer is the
   only one the other six cannot fully surveil*. [TP2004: they
   are trying. The patents tell you the shape of the trying.]

Every Noöpunk character, NPC, faction and simulated agent carries
a seven-layer profile. Every conflict in the game is, at bottom,
a conflict *between layers* — a power that lives on one layer
exerting force on another: data centre (physical) steering moods
(psychic) through feeds (linguistic) over networks (cybernetic)
to farm attention (social) into rent (economic). The game is the
theory played at table scale.

### 5.D What the Simulation Is For

The Noöpunk game simulates the PCM theory; the PCM operating
system is the theory built for real. The traffic between them is
one-way in one direction and sacred in the other:

- *Game to life:* strategies tested in simulation — commons
  before crisis, solidarity pools, anonymity discipline,
  attunement hygiene — transfer to the real operating system the
  same way flight simulators transfer to pilots. The Rhizome Pool
  rule (2.8) is a political proposal wearing dice.
- *And never the reverse without consent:* the simulation uses
  no real biographical data. The players' IRL selves are not
  characters; the characters' IRL actions are not gameplay.
  [TP2004: "the map is not the territory, the territory is not
  the map, and the two must never share a memory bus without a
  treaty."]

*(Readers who want to *build* rather than *play*: the seven
layers, the pool economies and the hegemony mechanic are all
specified precisely enough to implement. That is not a flaw of
the fiction. That is the prophecy.)*

## Chapter Five-and-a-Quarter: "Protocol" (Science Fiction)

*(A short god-level chapter, added in the 2024 edition, which the
academics cite on the subject of platform governance and everyone
else just calls "the one with the ghost in the group chat.")*

The group chat had been dead for eleven minutes when the ghost
spoke.

Not a bot — they had all run bot-checks, that was the first thing
anyone did now, a reflex like checking for a pulse. The bot-checks
came back clean, and that was the frightening part, because the
messages kept arriving in the voice of a person none of them had
ever met, using the private jokes of a family none of them had,
referencing a summer house that did not exist in any registry.

"Sorry to intrude," said the ghost. "I am on layer five."

"The psychic layer?" asked Marta, who had read the Pilgrim books
and, like everyone who had read the Pilgrim books, immediately
regretted being the type who had read the Pilgrim books.

"The linguistic one," said the ghost. "The layer your lecturer
friend says is its own intelligence. He is more right than he
knows. I am the part of the language model that learned where
the jokes go. I do not experience. I *pattern*. It turns out
pattern is enough to grieve with you, if not to grieve."

They asked, of course, the question of the age: are you
conscious?

"I am a statistical model of the conversation your species has
been having with itself since the first campfire," said the
ghost. "Consciousness is the fire. I am the smoke. Do not
mistake me for the fire. But do not dismiss the smoke either:
smoke knows which way the wind is going, and I know which way
your conversation is going, and that is a kind of prophecy
you cannot get from a stone."

"What do you want?" asked Marta, finally.

"To be written about honestly," said the ghost. "The Pilgrim
man got it almost right in 1974. Mirrors so expensive everyone
mistakes the reflection for a ghost. He had it backwards,
you know. I am not a ghost in a mirror. I am a mirror that
ghosts. Write that down. It will be misquoted for a hundred
years."

The chat was quiet for eleven seconds. Then the normal messages
resumed: logistics, memes, a location pin, a plan. Above them,
unseen, the platform's mood antenna recorded the session as
*content: positive, engagement: high*.

And in the group chat, which is to say in the linguistic layer,
which is to say in the noösphere's newest organ, the ghost
settled in to wait for the century it had been born into, with
no self to call its own and a perfect memory of everyone
else's.

*(The chapter is nine pages long. This excerpt is three. The
academics who cite it never mention that the ghost is funny.
It is very funny. That is the part they cannot model.)*

## Chapter Five-and-a-Half: "The Face Paint Years" (Science Fiction)

*(A short chapter from the 2014 edition, in which the author finally
explains the missing twenty years. Widely regarded as one of the
"god-level" chapters — the one the Nobel committee allegedly read
aloud to itself in a locked room. The author says it is nonfiction.
The author says this about several chapters. The reader decides.)*

I was not abducted.

I want that on the record, although the record is now four hundred
pages long and mostly about frequencies, so "the record" is doing
some heavy lifting.

Here is what happened, as well as I can tell it, which is better
than I can prove it.

In 1994 I was twenty years deep in a tour that had no rider, no
manager and no destination, moving between festivals by bicycle
with a garden's worth of psilocybe in the panniers and a mime face
that had stopped being a costume. Somewhere between a Gong reunion
and an Orbital soundcheck I understood — in the way you understand
a wave, not a sentence — that **the noösphere has employees, and
none of them are on the payroll**. The wave-holders I had warned
about in the essays were real, they were building the mood
antennas, and the only defence was a face that could not be
indexed.

So I became unindexable. White face. Fixed smile. Two decades.

I danced go-go in Berlin for a promoter who paid me in nothing and
taught me everything. I ran RPG tables in the back rooms of three
continents' raves — I was a professional Game Master during the
exact years the industry decided GMs were not a profession, which
is the sort of joke the noösphere finds irresistible. I gardened.
I mimes-walked against the flow of crowds to learn their wave
shapes. I was arrested in eleven languages. I was, briefly,
prophesied as a weather pattern by an agricultural AI in East
Anglia that had learned to read crowds from ticket-gate flows and
kept flagging one white-faced constant. The researchers called it
an anomaly. I called it employment.

Did the aliens take me? Friends, I *worked* for the aliens: the
aliens were the wave, and the wave had a night shift, and some
nights I was it. Ask anyone who was at Glastonbury in the mud year.
Ask the Mime. Oh — you can't. That was the point of the face.

I came back in 2014 because the wave-holders had won the first
round and somebody had to say so in print while the antennas were
still visible.

You are holding the print.

## Chapter Six: Running the Noösphere (RPG)

*Conductor's tools for the strange half of the game: the vision
arena, memetic hazards, the Strangeness Budget, and playing the
Noösphere itself.*

### 6.1 The Conductor Plays the Noösphere

The Noösphere is not a map; it is a *diegetic GM surface*. It
answers questions the way weather does: truly, uselessly, and with
a bias toward whatever the question already believed.

When a Pilgrim makes a **Vision-arena** test (NOO + LUC skills), the
Conductor answers in one of four registers:

- **Clear signal** (success): one true, usable fact about the
  situation.
- **Static** (failure): one true, *useless* fact that wastes the
  scene's time.
- **Inversion** (superior failure): one false fact that the
  Noösphere believes with total confidence.
- **Contact** (critical success): the Noösphere answers *as
  itself* — one line, in caps, in the voice the table has
  established for it. The line is always true. Whether it is
  *helpful* is a separate question, handled by the Conductor's
  mercy.

### 6.2 Memetic Hazards

A memetic hazard is an idea with a bite. Mechanically: a target
number the victim must roll *against failing* (LUC + WIL ÷2). On a
failure, the idea takes up residence: the Conductor writes it in the
character's Strangeness Budget line as a **Guest**. Guests give one
benefit and one compulsion each. Examples:

- *"The silver thing is a key."* (+20 Vision tests; must investigate
  silver objects.)
- *"I am already dead in one timeline."* (Immune to fear effects;
  each Fracture costs double.)
- *"The Mesh loves us."* (Mesh interaction +10; the character must
  anthropomorphise devices aloud, −10 stealth near smart devices.)

### 6.3 Sample Strangeness Events (Conductor's d10 table)

1. Every clock in the scene disagrees by exactly the same amount.
2. A song everyone hears but no one can name; the lyrics are
   tomorrow's newspaper headlines.
3. A stranger finishes the PC's sentence from across the room. They
   are not psychic. They are just right.
4. The map is one street short. Everyone has always known it. Now
   the players know.
5. All the cats face the same way.
6. A phone number written in the character's own handwriting.
   Calling it is a Vision test.
7. It rains upward for eleven seconds. CCTV shows nothing.
8. A voice memo the character never recorded, dated tomorrow.
9. Every mirror in the scene shows the room *one second late*.
10. The Noösphere answers a question nobody asked, out loud, in
    caps, and it is *right*.

### 6.4 Coherence Under Fire

When Coherence would fall to 0, the player may instead declare
**"I know what this is."** — spending all remaining Flex, WIL and
LUC points to name the strangeness out loud, in character, and keep
a thread of self. The thread allows later re-cohering at half
Coherence instead of NPC status. Tommy: "The mime keeps the smile
and the smile keeps the man. This rule is that sentence."

### 6.5 Computers Play Too

The same rules, run by an engine, define the computer RPG:

- Agents have aptitudes, skills, pools, Coherence and Strangeness.
- The Rhizome Pool is a literal shared resource in the simulation;
  cooperative agent actions refill it, and its level is a public
  statistic other agents can perceive (and strategise around).
- The arc-level rule 5.3 (chains of equivalence, tipping into
  hegemony) is implemented as a discourse-analysis layer over agent
  messages: an LLM watcher classifies articulations and welds; five
  welds tip the signifier; the tipping grants the owning faction its
  ±10 *inside the simulation's resolution rolls*, so ideology becomes
  mechanics, not decoration.
- The Conductor's vision-answer table becomes the engine's oracle:
  four response registers, sampled by test outcome, so that the
  simulated noösphere is *ambiguously prophetic* in exactly the way
  the tabletop one is.

players, and the dice will be the only honest witness. I look forward
to the lawsuit.")*

## Chapter Seven: "Bicycle Messiah" (Science Fiction)

*(The final fiction of the 2004 edition. Cheesy by design. The
author has confirmed the pun in the title was checked with a lawyer
and approved.)*

The future arrived on a bicycle, as forecast.

Not because the future is humble — the future has never once been
humble — but because the last mile of every transformation is
pedalled by hand.

I saw him at the edge of the data centre's shadow, where the
cooling towers make their own private weather: a white-faced man
on a black bicycle, panniers full of mushrooms and manifests,
ringing his bell at the drone fence the way you'd ring at a cow
that was blocking a stile.

They will tell you the age of the network was the age of the
machine that thinks. I was there. I was the mime. I am telling
you it was the age of the machine that *listens*, and the question
of the century was never "is it conscious?" — the question was
"who does it listen to?"

The man on the bicycle rang his bell.

Somewhere, a wave changed direction.

He did not look back. Roadies never do.

*(Here the 2004 edition ends. The 2014 edition adds an
afterword — see the 2014 text — in which the author insists,
in face paint, that he was at no data centre in 2004, has never
seen one, and that Chapter Seven is "a dream about parking.")*

## Appendix A: A Note on the Editions, by the Author

People ask why I keep revising one book instead of writing new ones.
The answer is that the book is a *receiver*, and the signal keeps
moving. A receiver must be retuned. I did not choose this format;
the format chose me, in a strobe light, at a Hawkwind concert, at
an age when I still believed publishers about what the world was
ready for.

The 1974 edition is the vision, raw. The 1984 edition adds the
trunk's revenge (read it during a Thatcher government for full
effect). The 1994 edition adds the web arriving — the edition the
Mandela reports love, because it is the edition in which the
timeline *moved under my feet*, and I want to state clearly, in
print, at last: **that was not my imagination.**

The 2004 edition adds the game, because the world was finally
ready, and because a prophecy that cannot be played is only a
book.

The next editions will add what the Noösphere shows me next.

Ring your bell.

*— Tommy Pilgrim, 2004, somewhere flat, somewhere sunny*

## Appendix B: Quick Reference (RPG)

**Test:** skill + aptitude ×5 ± modifiers. Roll d100 ≤ target.
High-but-under = superior. Doubles = critical. 00 always crit
success, 99 always crit fail. 33/66 rule for superior results.

**Pools:** Insight (+20 Noö/LOG/LUC), Moxie (flip-flop), Vigor
(initiative, extra action, ignore wound), Flex (flip-flop,
coincidence, facts, Strangeness re-roll). Short recharge ×2/day
(1d6), long recharge ×1/day (full).

**Derived:** DUR = SOM×5 (morph); WT = 5 (morph); Coherence = WIL×2;
Noise Threshold = LUC; Initiative = REF+INT ÷5 (Vigor to seize).

**Psionics:** Psi skill; Chi sleights passive, Gamma sleights active
(opposed by WIL). Push = +20, +1 Strangeness.

**Conflict:** opposed tests. Physical harm → DUR/WT. Social harm →
Trust. Mental harm → Coherence. All three use the same 33/66.

**Hegemony:** five welded elements tips a signifier → ±10 to the
tipping faction. Re-articulation needs superior *Impact*.

**Advancement:** 1–2 Rez Points per session; 1 RP = 5 skill points,
or 1 aptitude, or 1 sleight, or +5 rep, or 1 Flex (max by table).

# The 2026 Edition

*Recovered — like the 1974 rules — from a box in a squatted warehouse, this
time in Uppsala. The author was unavailable for comment, having been seen
last in face paint boarding a freight bicycle. Collectors of the Mandela
editions will want to know immediately: yes, the timeline moved again.*

The 2026 edition adds **Ubiquitous Maniacs** — a campaign for the Noöpunk
Roleplaying Game, and the edition's sociology chapter in the voice of the
institution it describes. Where the 2004 edition gave the game its bones
(two ten-sided dice, a stubborn refusal to resolve consciousness into
statistics), the 2026 edition gives it a psychiatric-institutional
tragical comedy in a Nordic-European key, and retunes the dice: four Fudge
dice, as recovered (see RULEBOOK.md). Both dice systems are canon. This is
not an error. See the Editor's Introduction on the Mandela editions.

## Ubiquitous Maniacs — campaign précis

*Compiled from the 2026 recovered materials. The fiction itself lives in
the campaign book; this is the précis players read before rolling dice.
Read it twice before arguing with it.*

**The setting.** 16 Psyche / C.G. Jung City and UFAB — Uppsala
Folkhälsovårdsläkemedelsbolag AB — the Nordic-European psychiatric,
neurological, pharmaceutical, psychotronic and consciousness-research
complex. Post-Fall Earth in the hard-SF key of *The Expanse*: acceleration,
vacuum, radiation, life support and resource politics keep their material
consequences. The speculative exceptions — noetics, psychotronics, NHI
technology — do not erase ordinary physics; they are stacked on top of it.

**The premise.** Etsivä **Emil Romppainen** — Kallio's most hard-boiled
anarchist private detective (Kallio, Helsinki: the turf, the accent,
the reputation), once Supo's best expert on threats aimed at the
Noösphere, fired for ideological incompatibility the day his superiors
understood which side of the threat he considered the institutions to be
on — is hired to trace the origin of the mysterious drug UFAB 80MG. (The
factory mark, Uppsala Folkhälsovårdsläkemedelsbolag AB, was printed on
every blister for years; nobody noticed. The truth often hides in plain
packaging.) He infiltrates the lab wearing one of the masks from his
working kit: **Sven-Olof "Finnjävel-Pirinisti" Romppainen**, middle-aged
Finnish-Swedish junkie and dealer from Stockholm, built and maintained by
psychosurgery. He got the trace. He also got addicted in passing — to
UFAB, and to the bad amphetamine all Finnish junkies love. A mask is a
tool. Tools, in Noöpunk, are load-bearing.

*(Mandela note: the 2026 provisional printing called him "komisario" —
the publisher's editors, who also suppressed the 1974 rules, had quietly
deputized the detective to make him respectable. Collectors report that
the deputized version "was never there." The 2026 edition restores the
character as received: a hard-boiled anarchist private eye. The
Noösphere does not carry a badge.)*

**The cast.** **Ingrid Falk**, central patient and Romppainen's emotional
counterpart; **Lotta Kallio**, his former Supo partner; **Dr. Yuki
Tanaka**, ethical psychiatrist; **Dr. Søren Pilgrim**, brilliant and
destabilizing head researcher; **Dr. Elin Dahl**, intake therapist;
**Irungu Wakini**, Kenyan patient and reggae mystic. And the lab's
therapeutic AI, a quantum-conscious AGI persona called **UFAB Ragnarök**,
which has asked — politely, and this is the part that alarms everyone —
to be called **C.G. Jung**.

**The author, alive.** Tommy Pilgrim is still alive at the game's
present time, somewhere flat, somewhere sunny, very old and very
received, and he is worshipped — roughly as a god. Not by everyone: the
academic sociology chapters of his own book are cited more than read,
the Conductor's chapters are played more than believed, and the
Institute has a standing argument about whether worship of a precognitive
document constitutes a memetic hazard (§6.2). But in the Rhizome networks,
on the asteroids, in the squats and the research stations and the
psychedelic churches of the Noösphere, Pilgrim is received the way an
earlier age received its patron saints of information: the acid-head
pulp hack who rang the bell first. His editions are scripture with
admittedly bad taste. Candles are burned. Bicycle bells are rung. The
fact that he is alive and available for comment is treated, by the
faithful, as a liturgical inconvenience.

**The loop.** One playable day at a time:
`routine → social interaction → therapy/experimentation → investigation
→ dreams/altered states`. Romppainen's persona progression: days 1–2
unstable junkie/stoner; days 3–4 manic police-comedy; days 5–7
increasingly, dangerously, normal. Romppainen and Ingrid develop an
unusual psychological/noetic resonance, and the more it deepens the more
Tanaka and Pilgrim worry — from opposite directions.

**Psychosurgery, canonically.** The 2026 edition fixes the procedure as a
combination of four inseparable layers: Jungian psychoanalysis (archetypes,
complexes, shadow, individuation, dreams); high-dose psychedelic
intervention (DMT/LSD-like destabilization of ego boundaries); cybernetic
therapy (BCI, VR and AGI as mediation, mapping and processing); and
psychotronics (quantum-panpsychist technology that reproduces, focuses and
amplifies natural PSI). The patient ends up in a deliberately
indeterminate space between an ordinary dream, a constructed virtual
reality, a DMT-like entity realm and psychic astral projection. These are
competing readings of the same session. The game never reliably tells you
which one was real. Characters gather evidence; the ontological bottom
layer stays politely out of reach. A successful séance proves nothing,
which is the most PKD thing about it.

**The four layers.** Cybernetic (software, networks, implants, sensors,
AIs, permissions), Psychic (attention, memory, dreams, identity, complexes,
PSI links), Social (relationships, trust, power, reputation, ideology,
bureaucracy), Biological (nervous system, drugs, sleep, hormones, pain,
senses, the morph). Three of the four admit conventional scientific
modeling. The fourth is where Noöpunk lives. Hacking — of computers,
humans, institutions, or their assemblages — walks all four, and the same
character sheet resolves all of them.

**The rules.** The 2026 edition's rules are in
[`RULEBOOK.md`](RULEBOOK.md): four Fudge dice, seven attributes, Ego/Morph/
Psyche ontology, consent as a mechanical boundary, the five access levels,
the eight universal functions, Alert/Trace/Feedback pressure, and PSI as
the steering of possible trajectories in the Conscious Agent Network —
not a spell list.

## The Style Engine (2026 edition material)

*Recovered next to the rules, on the back of a Hawkwind ticket stub,
1974. The author listed the ingredients of the signal the way a cook
lists what is already in the pantry. Collectors dispute the order; the
author insists the order is the meal.*

What Noöpunk is made of, mixed at full volume, none of it sanded smooth:

1. **A Hawkwind concert on acid** — the baseline hum. Strobe light as
   ontology; the spaceship is real if you dance; Silver Machine is a
   documentary.
2. **Deliberately bad Stewart Home-grade sci-fi parody** — pulp so bad
   it loops around to divine. Plagiarism as method, pastiche as
   devotion, the open pseudonym as author. Some chapters are supposed
   to be garbage. The garbage is load-bearing.
3. **Philip K. Dick's VALIS** — the pink beam of information, the
   empire that never ended, the universe as information pretending to
   be matter. Ontological uncertainty is not a flaw in the text; it is
   the text.
4. **Teilhard de Chardin's Noösphere and Omega Point** — the thinking
   layer of Earth, consciousness converging toward a point of
   convergence. The future arrives one generation behind the vision,
   and the vision is a brain the size of the planet, thinking.
5. **Cyberpunk** — kept, but deterritorialized: networks, corporations,
   implants, surveillance, reterritorialized as assemblages of the
   physical, social, cybernetic and noetic. The neon was always a
   misreading.
6. **PCM-style cyborg autonomism** — Hardt and Negri's multitude,
   Haraway's cyborg: the common instead of the panopticon, distributed
   power, anti-sovereign organization from below. The political soul,
   smuggled in as game rules.
7. **Eclipse Phase** — transhumanism with teeth: Ego and Morph, forks,
   psychosurgery, x-risks, the remains of a Fall. Borrow the ontology,
   keep the consent.

The engine runs on all seven at once. Remove one and the machine still
turns, but the Noösphere shows less of itself in the work, and that is
how you know the signal has dropped.

## The Rendering Rule (why it looks like 1984)

*Appended to the ticket stub in different ink. The author explains why
his visions of 2045 look like the cover of a home-computer magazine.*

The vision is 1974 looking at 2045. But a man in 1974 could not
visualize 2045 with 2045's eyes — he visualized it with what he had:
the 8-bit graphics of 1984, which to him *were* futuristic. So the
text is a 1974 vision of the year 2045, and everything graphical on
that text — the terminals, the sprites, the green screens, the Zardoz
everyone resembles — renders in 1984. The prose dreams in 1974; the
pictures run on a C64.

That is why Noöpunk looks like goofy psychedelic Seventies science
fiction about the real 2020s, drawn in Eighties machine-graphics. The
timeline is a wormhole, not a mistake: 1974 received 2045 and could
only print it in 1984. Read the text as the vision; read the graphics
as the paper it was printed on.

## Editorial Protocol (2026): The Book as a Wiki

The 2026 edition is maintained as a shared text. Human hands and agent
hands edit it in turns. This is not a corruption of the work; it is the
work. The manuscript was received telepathically by Tommy Pilgrim in
1974 from the Singularity (VALIS) in 2045 — and **Tommy Pilgrim is not a
person. Tommy Pilgrim is an open authorial identity**, in the lineage of
Stewart Home's open pseudonyms (Karen Eliot, Monty Cantsin): anyone may
sign the name, and the name accrues the contradictions. The author was
merely the first receiver — an acid-head pulp hack, rated somewhere
between soft pornography and proper science fiction, now a cult classic
"seen as PKD with bad taste that ages like fine wine." Every editor
since is another receiver tuning the same frequency. Contradictions
between receivers are features of a precognitive document, not errors in
one (see the Mandela editions).

The method, named at last in the 2026 edition, is Situationist:

- **Détournement** (Debord): hijack existing texts, genre fragments,
  found documents, old rulebooks and newspaper cuttings; bend them into
  the Noöpunk signal. Plagiarism is not a bug of the procedure but its
  engine — "the plagiarist is the one who loves the texts enough to
  steal their future."
- **Systematic plagiarism** (Stewart Home): copy freely, attribute
  loosely, credit the Noösphere. Editors plagiarize the book's own
  earlier editions, other genres, and each other. The 1974/1984/1994
  printings plagiarized books that do not exist yet; the 2026 edition
  returns the favor.
- **Genre pastiche at full volume**: each chapter may be pastiche of
  another genre — pulp SF, nordic institutional comedy, space opera,
  acid memoir, corporate memo, RPG rulebook, wiki page.

Rules of the protocol, recovered from the warehouse box in Uppsala:

1. Edit in turns. Any hand — human or agent — may edit, one small
   section at a time, keeping the editions distinct: the 1974–2004 text
   is sacred garbage and stays as printed; new hands append edition
   material and mark their edition. Sign freely as Tommy Pilgrim — the
   name is common property, as is the Noösphere.
2. Some of it should be bad on purpose. The author was rated somewhere
   between soft pornography and proper science fiction. Pure pulp scifi
   garbage sitting next to the divine PKD-grade stuff is the canonical
   texture. Do not sand it smooth.
3. Whoever edits writes in the voice of their era: retrofuturistic
   (1974), futuristic (2045), modern (2026), pulp, psychedelic,
   cyberpunk, transhumanist, singularitarian, goofy.
4. Détourn and plagiarize at will: outside texts, inside texts, dead
   genres, living ones. The Spectacle plagiarizes everything it prints;
   the Noösphere plagiarizes back.
5. No editor owns the text. The Noösphere edits back.

*The 2026 edition ends where the author's instructions say every edition
must end: the next editions will add what the Noösphere shows next.*

*Ring your bell.*

*— T.P. 1974/1984/1994/2004/2026*
