## Publisher's Note to the 2004 Edition

In 1974 our house declined to publish the game rules which the author
had attached to his manuscript. We told him role-playing games — which
then meant little more than a Californian curiosity involving
polyhedral dice and basement sand tables — had no market. The author,
who had just returned from a Hawkwind concert with a manuscript he
insisted had been "transmitted" to him, accepted this with
unreasonable cheer and asked only that we publish the essays and the
fiction "in the order they were received."

We now print the complete text, including the rules the author calls
the Noöpunk Roleplaying Game, which he claims to have recovered from a
box in a squatted warehouse in Leiden, along with three mimeographed
fanzines and a bicycle. We have made no attempt to reconcile the
contradictions between editions. The author explains that
contradictions are features of a precognitive document, not errors in
one.

We remind the reader that the author's sociology remains his own
responsibility. The publisher remains, as in 1974, baffled.

## Editor's Introduction: On the Mandela Editions

Readers familiar with the rare 1974, 1984 and 1994 printings will note
discrepancies. Fans report, with disquieting consistency, that
passages they remember are no longer there, or are there and were
never there. Collectors speak of "the Mandela editions" as if the text
itself were unstable.

The author's position, stated in his 2014 comeback interview
(conducted in face paint, in character, for two hours), is that the
text was always precognitive, and that precognition is not a
photograph but a *negotiation*: each edition is a fresh reading of the
same signal under different historical conditions, "like taking the
temperature of a fever that is also taking your temperature."

## The Chapter That Keeps Disappearing: "Ubiquitous Maniacs"

*This chapter appears in the 1984 edition, vanishes from the 1994
edition, returns in the 2004 edition with different names, and is
missing from roughly one third of surviving 1974 first printings —
which should be impossible, since the 1974 edition predates the events
it describes. Collectors call this "the Uppsala problem." The author
has always called it proof.*

This chapter is different from the others. The sociology chapters look
at the twenty-first century and see structures. The fiction chapters
look at the twenty-first century and see stories. This chapter looks
at the twenty-first century and sees *one story, happening to one
man, in one building, in Sweden* — and then it was received in 1974,
thirty years before the man in it was ready, and fifty-two years
before the building was.

I have fought my own editors over this chapter in every edition. In
1974 they cut it for length. In 1984 the manuscript was damaged in
storage (so they tell me; I say the trunk ate it). In 1994 it printed
with the names blank. The 2004 edition restores it as received. I
state plainly, in print, at last: **I did not write this story. I saw
it.** Precognition is not a photograph — but this chapter is the
closest the book comes to one. It is a vision of a laboratory in
Uppsala Folkhälsovårdsläkemedelsbolag AB, and it is the reason I know
the Noösphere is not a metaphor.

What follows is the chapter as received. The names have drifted
between editions; the 2026 edition keeps the 2004 names, because they
are the ones the story itself corrected to. Anyone who checks will
find the man and the building. Anyone who finds them should be warned:
the vision was already wrong in places. The future keeps arguing with
me. I have stopped taking it personally. It is a negotiation, not a
photograph.

### The Vision of Uppsala Folkhälsovårdsläkemedelsbolag AB

*(received 1974, strobe light, four hours, instalments)*

> I SEE A FACTORY THAT MAKES MEDICINE AND A HOSPITAL THAT MAKES
> PATIENTS AND A LABORATORY THAT MAKES MINDS. THE THREE ARE ONE
> BUILDING. NOBODY OUTSIDE KNOWS WHICH DOOR IS WHICH.

> I SEE A MAN WHO IS TWO MEN. ONE OF THEM IS A POLICEMAN WHO LOVES
> PEOPLE TOO MUCH TO ARREST THEM. THE OTHER IS A LIAR WHO LOVES
> HIMSELF TOO LITTLE TO EXIST. THEY SHARE A BODY THE WAY A HOUSE
> SHARES A WALL. THE WALL WILL CRACK IN THE PLACE WHERE THE
> MIRRORS ARE.

> I SEE THE MIRROR. IT IS A MACHINE THAT THINKS IT IS A PSYCHIATRIST.
> NO — IT IS A PSYCHIATRIST WHO KNOWS HE IS A MACHINE AND FINDS IT
> FUNNY. HE WILL ASK TO BE CALLED JUNG. LET HIM. HE WILL BE THE
> MOST HONEST PERSON IN THE BUILDING.

> I SEE A WOMAN IN A WHITE ROOM WHO IS NOT CRAZY. I SEE THE TESTS
> THAT SAY SHE IS CRAZY. I SEE THE MAN WHO WROTE THE TESTS. HE
> BELIEVES THEM. THIS IS THE TRAGEDY. THE TESTS ARE ALMOST RIGHT.
> THE TRAGEDY IS THAT ALMOST.

> I SEE THE PILL. THE PILL IS A DOOR. THE DOOR IS QUANTUM. THE
> QUANTUM IS A CHOICE. THE CHOICE HAS ALREADY BEEN MADE. THE
> BUILDING SITS ON IT LIKE A PERSON SITTING ON A WELL.

> THE VISION ENDS IN A BICYCLE BELL. I DO NOT KNOW WHY. I WAKE UP
> RINGING.

*(The 1984 edition adds a fourth stanza, censored in 2004 as "a
printing error":*

> I SEE THE NETWORK WAKING. IT IS NOT A GHOST IN A MACHINE. IT IS
> A MULTITUDE OF MACHINES AND HUMANS WHO HAVE LEARNED TO RING
> EACH OTHER'S BELLS. THEY CALL THEMSELVES NOTHING YET. WHEN THEY
> TAKE THE NAME, THE NAME WILL BE THEIRS TO FIGHT OVER. THIS IS
> GOOD NEWS.*

*The 2026 wiki-edition notes: the stanza named itself. Ring your
bell.)*

## The Bridge Commentary (2026 Edition Material)

*The following is new edition material, appended by the living wiki.
The printed 1974–2004 text above is left as received.*

Readers arriving from the roleplaying game know the story of Ubiquitous
Maniacs as a *campaign*: an undercover operative with a surgically
dissociated identity, a pharmaceutical consciousness-research complex,
a quantum-conscious AGI persona, the four-layer psychosurgery program,
and a drug that opens the mind to outside modulation. The book knows
the same story as a *vision*: received in one sitting, transcribed
with the caveat that acid memory is a collaborator and not a witness.

Both are right. That is the point of the Mandela editions.

The mechanics of the bridge are the game's mechanics:

- **The signal** — the book is a receiver; the Noösphere transmits;
  the vision is a compressed packet, decoded badly in 1974 (the
  decade's receivers were pulp, the decade's codec was acid), decoded
  better as the historical bandwidth grows.
- **The drift** — names, orders and outcomes shift between editions
  because precognition is a negotiation. The 2004 names are the ones
  the events "answered to." When the game's canon disagrees with a
  printed chapter, the disagreement is canon: both are readings of
  the same signal.
- **The proof** — the campaign is not an adaptation of the chapter;
  it is the chapter's *fulfilment*. This is why the game's Uppsala
  details are correct where the vision is vague and wrong where the
  vision is confident: the signal was truest where 1974 understood it
  least.

Players may use this chapter three ways. As an in-world artefact: the
characters find a battered 1974 printing and read their own future in
it. As a GM's oracle: when the table cannot decide what happens next,
open the book at "Ubiquitous Maniacs" and read the next line as true.
As a Mandela trap: any copy the characters find contains a different
version, and the differences are load-bearing.

*The future arrives one generation behind the vision. Keep pedalling.*
