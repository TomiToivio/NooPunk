## 1. The baseline compass: two axes, and tags that are not axes

**CANON (adopted from the issue).** The baseline is deliberately two-dimensional and explicitly
insufficient:

- **Economics:** left — redistribution, collective provision, state intervention ↔ right — markets,
  property, economic deregulation.
- **Social/cultural:** conservative — traditionalist ↔ liberal — pluralist and socially permissive.

**The one rule that keeps it honest: do not conflate economic liberalism with social liberalism.**
The four quadrants are all populated in Ukweli, and a party's economic position carries no implication
about its cultural one. This is also the axis pair the *setting* already relies on: the CPU is
economically left and (per its own self-understanding) socially pluralist; the UIP is economically
heterodox and culturally traditionalist; the ULP is economically hard-right and originally culturally
libertarian, drifting toward cultural reaction — which is precisely one of its internal contradictions
(§5).

**PROPOSAL — optional tags, recorded only when they carry information the compass loses:**
establishment–anti-establishment; authoritarian–libertarian; globalism–sovereigntism; secular–religious;
ecological priority; technocracy; postcolonial / national-liberation commitment.

**PROPOSAL — the discipline for the tags.** A tag is added when it *splits* a compass cell, not to
decorate it. If two actors share a cell and behave differently under the same shock, a tag is the
finding; if they share a cell and behave the same way, the tag is noise.

**Real-world formations as a comparative starting point (SOURCE, comparative only).** The issue asks for
recognisable 2026 formations to be compared while distinguishing geography and relative scale: far-right
nationalist/identity populism; centre-right / liberal-conservative / Christian-democratic currents;
centre-left / social democracy / democratic socialism and labour; red–green / green-left / progressive
formations; market-libertarian / techno-libertarian and accelerationist minorities; established
communist and postcolonial national-liberation traditions; religious/traditionalist movements and
nonparty networks.

**PROPOSAL — mark the scale difference.** The first three are broad formations with mass electorates;
the market-libertarian and accelerationist currents are **small ideological subcultures** that can
nevertheless hold outsized media, funding or network power. Ukweli's ULP is a small party in the second
sense (CANON: "low nationwide support... its reach disproportionately dependent on financing and
sympathetic media"). Conflating a subculture's *reach* with its *votes* is the specific error the
setting is built to make legible.

## 2. Why there are three crisis dimensions and not a third axis

**CANON (the issue's constraint).** "A clear 2D baseline compass + optional tags, and three independent
crisis-reaction dimensions (avoid collapsing into a misleading 3D political compass)."

**PROPOSAL — the reason, stated so the document cannot be read as a 3D compass.** A third axis makes
one claim: that a person has one position along it. The three paradigm-shift complexes are not like
that — the issue's own note is that a faction "can combine several, e.g. transhumanist but AI-safety
conscious, economically left but technologically accelerationist, or conservative but pro-military AI."
Three *independent* dimensions mean the spaces are **not collapsed into a product**: a party's answer on
Complex B tells you little about its answer on Complex C, and the interesting politics is exactly where
they diverge.

| Dimension | The question it answers | Not to be confused with |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A. Soft / archaic revival** | Does renewed cultural legitimacy of ancient belief get embraced, resisted, or appropriated? | conservative↔liberal. The issue is explicit that revival can be traditionalist, countercultural, ecological, emancipatory **or commercial**. |
| **B. Hard NHI/UAP** | What does the actor want done about verifiable nonhuman presence and hardware? | belief in NHI. An actor can accept the evidence and still want the border closed. |
| **C. Hard AI + human transformation** | Where does the actor stand on machine capability **and on changing human beings**? | a party. The issue names four *issue frames*, not parties. |

## 3. The three complexes

### A. Soft / archaic revival — renewed cultural legitimacy

**CANON (from the #325 brief, which governs the local form).** Ukweli's shamanic traditions, folk
religions and island mythologies already contain stories the fiction reads as NHI/PSI-related; Catholic
Christianity is significant in parts of rural Ukweli; and **most urban residents treat the whole
register as irrelevant, implausible, rural superstition or a New Age scam**, while belief and scepticism
cut across demographics.

**PROPOSAL — the playable reading.** Disclosure does not create new beliefs; it **re-legitimises,
reorganises and re-prices existing ones**, which makes the local traditions a resource and a
battleground rather than a backdrop. The UIP's selective appropriation of folk belief and Catholic
identity is **CANON-contested by practitioners and believers**, and that contest is the mechanic-free
version of the whole dimension.

**CANON — a guard rail on the labels.** "Archaic revival" is a **cultural interpretation, not a
universal anthropological law**; religious traditions differ, and **not all religions make identical
claims about PSI or NHI**. The same guard applies at home: the setting must not present contested
contemporary UAP/PSI claims as proven real-world science.

### B. Hard NHI/UAP — material presence

**PROPOSAL — the response map**, derived from the issue's own list and kept as *responses*, not
positions on an axis: disclosure and scientific investigation; diplomacy and recognition; military and
security preparedness; sovereignty and border control; panic, survivalism and existential-risk
doomerism; NHI worship and conversion; technology exploitation; markets and private alien enclaves;
anti-infiltration populism; rights of nonhuman persons.

**CANON — the evidence discipline.** Hard evidence must be distinguishable from rumour,
misinformation, staged events and faction propaganda. Ukweli's baseline is the strongest example: the
government **acknowledges unusual reports and denies possessing definitive knowledge of their causes**,
while privately knowing of recurrent events — so the *official register*, the *internal assessments* and
the *popular interpretation* are three different things, and the scenario already holds them apart.

### C. Hard AI + human transformation

**CANON (the four stances, adopted verbatim as issue frames).** Doomerism (catastrophic/existential
risk, moratorium, containment, prohibition); Accelerationism (fast development and adoption, possibly
deregulation or state-directed competition); Cyborgism (human–machine integration, agency, augmentation,
posthuman futures); Critical (distributional justice, labour, concentration of power, environmental
costs, democracy, surveillance, rights).

**SOURCE (relational, not a number).** Pew's April 2025 report compares U.S. public and AI-expert views
on artificial intelligence; the finding that matters for this document is the **existence of a
public/expert gap and cross-partisan regulatory concern**, which is exactly the political space the
Complex C dimension needs. **No figure from that report is quoted here**, because a figure would need to
be read in its own context and the issue forbids unsupported generalisation.

**CANON — the vocabulary's provenance.** "Relate terminology to LaclauGPT AI26 while preserving its own
research framework": the four stances are an **issue-frame vocabulary borrowed as inspiration**. No
LaclauGPT pipeline, corpus or classifier output is embedded in the game, and none is imported here.

### Borderline cases and crossings

**PROPOSAL — the crossing register.** The issue asks which parts are archaic revival, which are material
capability, and which are **both**. The useful cases are the ones that refuse the split: PSI-enhancing
technology; psychotronics; non-invasive BCI; NHI-mediated PSI; consciousness research; augmented
spiritual experience; machine-mediated revelation; commercial psychic services.

**PROPOSAL — the test.** Ask what would settle the question *in the fiction*. If an experiment could
settle it, it is a material claim wearing revival clothes; if the settlement is a matter of authority
rather than evidence, it is the reverse. That test is what keeps a case from being forced into a bucket.

## 4. PSI: from belief to institutions, rights and law

**CANON — the framing.** Not a "pro-PSI vs anti-PSI" spectrum, but: *what changes if demonstrable PSI
services exist within the fictional universe?* PSI effectiveness is **scenario-fictional**, with
different degrees of confidence and uncertainty across events.

**PROPOSAL — the institutional fault lines**, taken from the issue's own list and grouped by which
system the conflict lands in:

| Domain | The conflict it creates |
| --- | --- |
| Licensing and professional standards | Paid telepathy, precognition, healing, consultation; prohibited claims; consumer fraud and evidence standards |
| Consent and cognitive liberty | Mental privacy; nonconsensual influence; surveillance and policing |
| Use in the world | Medical vs religious vs entertainment vs intelligence uses; public access; inequality; workplace coercion |
| Courts | PSI evidence, forensic standards, due process, admissibility |
| Religion | Religious liberty and spiritual practice against commercial regulation and public safety |
| Enhancement | PSI implants interacting with AGI and BCI |
| Jurisdiction | Science, medicine, religion, markets, courts and security agencies claiming the same question |

**PROPOSAL — why the last row is the important one.** Every other row is a policy; the last row is the
setting's spine. These are the Luhmannian social systems the crossover's design names, and they each
have their own code — so a PSI scandal is simultaneously a licensing matter, a fraud matter, a
conscience matter and a national-security matter, and the political content is **which systems win**.
That is a *routing* question, and routing needs no numbers to be legible. Whether it becomes a
simulated mechanic belongs to the simulation spine and to the design gates, not to this document.

## 5. Cross-tab: the three canonical parties × the three complexes

**CANON — the baseline power asymmetry** (from the issue's own table): the CPU is the mainstream
incumbent with liberation legitimacy, electoral strength and institutions; the UIP is a mass-based
right-wing populist challenger with rural, cultural and identity mobilisation; the ULP has little
genuine electoral support but very large private funding and media/network reach from Sir Mars Eldrich.

**OPEN — the third party's name.** This issue's own table says **ULP — Ukweli Libertarian Party**. The
#325 brief and #326 say **LFP — Libertarian Freedom Party of Ukweli**, while
`strategy_game/data/ukweli_local.json` and `global_factions.json` use `ulp`. The #324 inventory flagged
this for the author (§4.2) and it is not settled here; this document uses **ULP**, following the issue
it is a deliverable of, and records the count rather than picking a winner.

**PROPOSAL — the reading of the table, in one line each.** The CPU's problem is that it must be the
*state* while its coalition wants incompatible speeds. The UIP's problem is that every one of its three
answers has a base that rejects the other two. The ULP's problem is that it is small, loud and
patronage-dependent, so its contradictions are survivable politically and fatal intellectually — which
is a satire of a real pattern, not a claim about any real party.

## 6. Signifiers, frontiers and the networks behind them

**CANON (the frame).** Frame Laclaudian empty/floating signifiers and antagonistic frontiers; the issue
names `Humanity`, `Freedom`, `Security`, `Progress`, `Tradition`, `Sovereignty`, `Disclosure`,
`Survival`, `Consciousness`. **PROPOSAL — the discipline:** those nine are candidates, not a closed
vocabulary. A signifier enters play when a **demand** is attached to it, and the interesting event is
when two actors attach opposed demands to the same word.

**PROPOSAL — the four signifiers that do the work here.** *Humanity* (UIP and Cyborgism fight over it),
*Freedom* (ULP's founding claim, contested by its own patronage structure), *Progress* (the CPU's
developmental record, and the word its critics turn against it), and *Sovereignty* (the only signifier
the CPU can never abandon, because it **is** the party's founding claim — which is what makes it the
most valuable word for anyone else to try to capture).

**CANON — the separation that must not collapse.** Track Castellsian media, funding and infrastructure
networks **separately from public consent**. The ULP is the setting's own worked example: it can hold
network power far exceeding its vote, and the two must be readable as different quantities. Six
quantities must stay distinct throughout: **opinion, membership, seats, office, institutional access,
and Laclauian hegemony** (the last recorded in the crossover's own global design).

**PROPOSAL — cross-divide cooperation and the wedges.** Coalitions that cross the old cleavages are the
point of the exercise, and each has a counter-wedge:

| Coalition that can form | What can split it |
| --- | --- |
| CPU social-development wing + egalitarian cyborgists + open-augmentation groups | Labour's demand for a slower, protected adoption |
| Scientific disclosure coalitions + civil-liberties groups + NHI-rights movements | Security's claim that disclosure must be controlled first |
| Traditional religious and syncretic revival movements (UIP base + nonparty congregations) | Contested appropriation: the practitioners the party claims to speak for |
| Commercial PSI guilds + consumer-protection regulators | The same licensing question, approached from opposite ends |
| Anti-infiltration populists + security institutions | The institutions' reluctance to be seen as a party's instrument |

## 7. Emergent organisations — a shortlist, not a roster

**CANON.** These are **not** immediately playable factions; the issue asks for "a small optional list of
emergent organizations, not a dozen immediately playable factions", and the MVP keeps **three** parties.

**PROPOSAL — the shortlist**, each with the condition that would make it appear:

| Organisation | Appears when… |
| --- | --- |
| Techno-accelerationist / corporate transhumanists | Augmentation becomes commercially viable in the enclave |
| Egalitarian cyborgist / open-augmentation groups | Adoption becomes a class marker instead of a curiosity |
| AGI/NHI doomers and survival networks | A global shock makes the risk legible to ordinary voters |
| Traditional religious and syncretic UFO-revival movement | Disclosure re-legitimises practice the UIP tried to own |
| Scientific disclosure / verification coalition with civil liberties | The official register and the internal assessments visibly diverge |
| NHI-contact diplomacy or alien-rights movement | A nonhuman person's legal status is actually at stake |
| Commercial PSI guilds; consumer-protection group; a security organisation | Licensing is attempted — the three answers to the same question |

**PROPOSAL — keep them tentative.** Each entry names a **condition**, not a schedule. A player should be
able to reach the end of a scenario without two of them existing, which is what "not foreordained" has
to mean in practice.

## 8. Three event vignettes

Each is written as the issue asks: the three parties' **distinct and defensible** choices, **one
nonparty actor**, divergent outcomes, and possible splinters — with the tonal requirement held:
*New Weird / Science Fantasy* ambiguity, *Social Science Fiction* institutional seriousness, *Science
Fiction Comedy* by default, and **no predetermined utopia, dystopia or apocalypse**.

### Vignette 1 — first verified NHI biologics/technology

A recovered body and a working device, both verifiable, both on the exclusion island the state has
always said was closed for biodiversity.

- **CPU:** verify in public, in order, under state custody — the official register becomes the asset, and
  the risk is that a slow process looks like a cover-up precisely because part of it is one.
- **UIP:** the island proves foreign interference; demand a border and a parliamentary inquiry. The
  wedge: its revival base reads the same discovery as ancestors returning.
- **ULP:** claim the recovery as proof that the state cannot be trusted with anything, and offer a
  private concession to study it. The wedge: its own libertarians ask who owns a body.
- **Nonparty actor:** the conservation authority, which now has to defend a genuine biodiversity regime
  against a state that wants the island for a different reason — the NoöPunk move is that it is right.
- **Outcomes:** a splinter if the CPU's disclosure wing forces publication early; a court question if
  the body's legal status is raised; an alliance if science and civil liberties publish together.

### Vignette 2 — AGI + BCI/cyborg mass adoption

A commercial augmentation clinic opens in the enclave; adoption spreads faster than any licensing
regime.

- **CPU:** a public programme on labour's terms — the developmentalist wing wants speed, the unions want
  protection, and the party owns both.
- **UIP:** an augmentation moratorium and a defence AI programme in the same platform. The contradiction
  is the story: the party needs the technology it campaigns against.
- **ULP:** celebrate it, deregulate it, and monetise it. The wedge: members who came for individual
  autonomy discover a party selling permanent contracts.
- **Nonparty actor:** a commercial PSI/augmentation guild, which is neither a party nor a firm and is
  trying to become a regulator.
- **Outcomes:** an egalitarian-cyborgist organisation forms if adoption becomes a class marker; a
  doomer network forms if one clinic's failure is vivid enough; a coalition forms if both happen.

### Vignette 3 — PSI commercial scandal under an information blackout

A paid-precognition firm sells a service that appears to work, and then Ukweli loses the ability to
verify anything abroad for a week.

- **CPU:** regulate under existing law and admit the evidence standard is inadequate. The risk is that
  admitting the gap reads as weakness.
- **UIP:** fraud and foreign manipulation; also a real constituency of believers who want the practice
  dignified rather than policed. Both are in its base.
- **ULP:** privatise the standard. The wedge: a party of maximal freedom proposing a private licensing
  cartel, and its own media empire having run the advertisements.
- **Nonparty actor:** a consumer-protection group that is the only party in the story with nothing to
  gain — which is why it becomes the most trusted voice for a turn.
- **Outcomes:** a court question if the firm is prosecuted and the standard becomes precedent; a
  splinter if the UIP cannot hold both its believers and its fraud line; a cross-divide coalition if
  the CPU's regulators publish alongside the consumer group.

**PROPOSAL — the vignettes' one shared rule.** None of the three can be won outright by any party, and
none ends the setting. Each one moves a signifier, a network or a vote — and the useful test of a
vignette is whether the three moves are *different kinds* of move, not different sizes of the same one.

## 9. A minimal proposed data schema (a document, not an implementation)

**PROPOSAL ONLY.** The issue asks for a "minimal proposed data schema suitable for a future
hand-authored Python JSON/YAML scenario (**no engine rewrite**)". This is a **shape**, written here so
the author can reject it cheaply. **No file is added and no engine code is touched by this document.**

```text
# proposed scenario fragment, not written to disk
formations:
  - id: cpu
    party_name: Communist Party of Ukweli      # display only; not canon-bearing
    compass: {economics: -2, social: 1}        # PROPOSAL: order is illustrative, not a scale
    tags: [establishment, national_liberation]
    crises:                                    # one entry per complex; absent = not yet answered
      archaic_revival: {stance: pragmatic_cultural, note: ...}
      hard_nhi:        {stance: controlled_disclosure, note: ...}
      hard_ai:         {stance: state_directed_adoption, note: ...}
    dissent: [{wing: social_development, diverges_on: hard_ai, note: ...}]
    signifier_demands: [{signifier: sovereignty, demand: ...}]
    networks: {media_reach: null, funding: null, institutional_access: ...}   # null = unknown
```

**PROPOSAL — four properties that make the shape safe.**

1. **`stance` is a string, never a score.** A number here would become the ideological slider the issue
   forbids, and it would migrate into any engine that read it.
2. **`compass` values are illustrative and explicitly not a scale.** If the author wants real positions,
   that is a sampling decision; the schema only reserves the shape.
3. **`networks` fields default to `null`, meaning unknown** — the same discipline as the crossover's
   `ProvenanceKind.UNKNOWN`: an unrecorded quantity must not read as zero, because "no media reach" and
   "media reach not measured" are different facts.
4. **`dissent` is part of the record, not a note about it.** The issue requires at least one plausible
   dissenting wing per party; a schema without that field would make the requirement droppable.
