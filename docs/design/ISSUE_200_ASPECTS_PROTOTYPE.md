# Aspects, narrative resource and consequences: a tested prototype (issue #200)

The second half of #200's kernel request. The [kernel prototype](ISSUE_200_KERNEL_PROTOTYPE.md)
answers *how a roll resolves*; this answers *what a tag, a cost and a consequence are allowed to do*
around that roll, under #200's own constraint:

> **"No tag-only character sheets:** keep STATs and Skills readable. Tags/Aspects affect fictional
> permissions, bounded modifiers or meaningful consequences; **avoid uncapped numerical exploits.**"

## Boundaries, enforced by tests rather than by comment

- `data/rules/core.json` and `src/rules/core.py` are **untouched** — still `STAT + Skill + 1d10`,
  and the tests assert the resolver still behaves exactly as it always has.
- `IS_CANONICAL = False`; no runtime imports this module, and a test fails if one starts to.
- **Every numeric value is DRAFT pending calibration**, centralised at the top of
  `src/rules/aspects_prototype.py`, and a test requires each to carry the flag. #200 is explicit
  that new numbers must be marked this way, and this file has seven of them.
- **The consequence vocabulary is the author's, not this prototype's.** `RULEBOOK.md` §51 defines
  `Scratched → Wounded → Critical → Down`; the tests extract §51 and assert this module against it,
  so the two cannot drift apart quietly.
- The dice maths is **exact** (rational, 81 equally likely 4dF outcomes) and is **cross-checked
  against the kernel prototype's independent computation**, so two prototypes agreeing is evidence
  rather than a coincidence.

## The two results that decide the design

**1. On 4dF, "bounded" cannot mean Fate's usual +2.** 63.0% of all outcomes fall within ±1, so a
+1 invocation moves the odds +19.8pp while a +2 moves them +32.1pp — turning "likely" into
"near-certain". The prototype adopts **+1**, and that choice is a measurement rather than taste.

**2. The cap is load-bearing, not tidiness.** Invocations are capped at one per roll. Without the
cap, **four aspects reach exactly 100% — auto-success with no roll that can fail.** That is
precisely the "uncapped numerical exploit" #200 names, and it costs a player four tags.

## What is deliberately not proposed

Names for the resource or for states beyond the authored ones, any new Skill, any change to the six
STATs, and any damage arithmetic. `RULEBOOK.md` §51 calls itself *"a fiction-first consequence
vocabulary, not a hidden damage engine"* and says harm does **not** advance by arithmetic — so a
Fate-style numeric stress track would contradict canon rather than extend it. The numeric track is
measured below **as the counterfactual it is**, for the author to accept or reject, and the module
returns `False` for "does the ladder advance automatically".

# Measured: the aspects / resource / consequence layer

Generated by `tools/issue200_aspects_report.py`. Dice probabilities are **exact**
(rational, 81 equally likely 4dF outcomes), so no figure depends on a seed. Every
parameter below is DRAFT pending calibration, per #200.

### 1. Why 'bounded' cannot mean Fate's +2
The 4dF roll itself, before anything is added:

| roll | -4 | -3 | -2 | -1 | 0 | +1 | +2 | +3 | +4 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| share | 1.23% | 4.94% | 12.35% | 19.75% | 23.46% | 19.75% | 12.35% | 4.94% | 1.23% |

**63.0% of all outcomes fall within +/-1.** That is the roll's
own typical spread, and it is the yardstick for what a 'bounded' modifier may be.

| invoked bonus | P(success) at step 0 | gain |
| --- | --- | --- |
| +0 | 61.7% | +0.0pp |
| +1 | 81.5% | +19.8pp |
| +2 | 93.8% | +32.1pp |
| +3 | 98.8% | +37.0pp |
| +4 | 100.0% | +38.3pp |

A single **+1** moves the odds +19.8pp -- a nudge, and the value the
prototype adopts (:data:`ASPECT_INVOKE_BONUS = 1`). A **+2** moves them
+32.1pp, turning 'likely' into 'near-certain'; **+3** reaches
98.8%. So #200's 'bounded modifiers' is narrower than Fate's
default, and +1 is where that line lands on THIS die.

### 2. The bound, and the exploit it prevents
Cap: at most **1** invocation per roll.

| aspects invoked | uncapped P(success) | capped P(success) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 81.5% | 81.5% |
| 2 | 93.8% | 81.5% |
| 3 | 98.8% | 81.5% |
| 4 | 100.0% | 81.5% |
| 6 | 100.0% | 81.5% |

**An uncapped stack of 4 aspects reaches exactly 100%** -- absolute
auto-success, with no die roll that can fail. That is the 'uncapped numerical exploit'
#200 names, and it is reachable with four tags. The cap is not tidiness; it is the
difference between a game and a formality.

For comparison, the kernel prototype's cap on situational modifiers is
+/-2 steps and its uncapped branch walks past the whole 4dF range.
A one-step aspect is deliberately smaller than that: aspects are frequent, so each
one has to be worth less.

### 3. The narrative resource: pool, rate and compel break-even
Starting pool **3**, costing **1** per
invocation, gaining **1** per accepted compel (all DRAFT).

| invocations per scene | scenes the pool sustains | compels needed per scene |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 3.0 | 1.0 |
| 2 | 1.5 | 2.0 |
| 3 | 1.0 | 3.0 |

At one invocation per scene the pool funds three scenes before it needs compels; at
three per scene it funds one. That is the whole pace lever, and it is why the compel
rate is not a footnote: **the resource only refills by accepting complications**, which
is #200's Narrativist requirement doing mechanical work rather than decorating it.

### 4. Permission use: free, deterministic, and worth no number
`UNSA forensic credentials` permits `examine the sealed evidence locker`:
- `permits_action('examine the sealed evidence locker')` -> **True**
- `permits_action('override the colony reactor')` -> **False**

Permission use rolls nothing and adds nothing. It establishes what is fictionally
possible, which is what #200 asks tags to do FIRST -- and it is the one aspect use that
cannot be gamed by stacking, because it grants no number to stack.

### 5. Consequences against the authored ladder
The authored vocabulary is `RULEBOOK.md` section 51: Scratched, Wounded, Critical, Down.
The tests assert this prototype against that text.

- after recording one state: worst = **Scratched**, taken out = **False**
- after the fiction establishes something worse: worst = **Critical**, taken out = **True**
- consequence penalty is bounded at 3 x 1 = **3 steps** maximum, even if a
  character somehow carries more: 3 steps
- harm advancing by arithmetic (which section 51 forbids): **False**

The states are recorded, never computed. Section 51 is explicit that a successful
attack does not automatically advance this and repeated harm does not automatically
step along it, so a numeric stress track would contradict canon rather than extend it.

The numeric counterfactual, measured only so the difference is visible. Note the
baseline: an UNADJUSTED 4dF roll already succeeds 61.7% at
step 0, because a tie goes to the roller -- so these paces are generous by construction:

| stress boxes | expected exchanges to fill |
| --- | --- |
| 2 | 3.2 |
| 3 | 4.9 |
| 4 | 6.5 |
| 6 | 9.7 |

A numeric track answers 'how many hits', which is precisely the question section 51
declines to make automatic. So this table is a design observation for the author, never
a proposed rule.

### 6. Relationships and scene stakes, and what stays open
Both are aspect-shaped in this prototype rather than separate subsystems:
- a **relationship** is a tag whose permission is social access and whose invocation is
  the same bounded step as any other aspect -- one number, one cap, no special case;
- **scene stakes** are a statement of what is at risk, which the compel path consumes:
  accepting the stake's complication is what refills the pool.

That keeps one economy rather than three. What is deliberately NOT proposed: names for
the resource or the states beyond the authored ones, any new Skill, any change to the six
STATs, and any damage arithmetic. Section 51's transition rules remain the author's.

### 7. Where this prototype stands
- Every parameter is DRAFT and centralised at the top of the module, so calibration is a
  one-place edit rather than a hunt.
- The dice math is exact, and `tests/test_issue200_aspects_prototype.py` cross-checks it
  against the kernel prototype's independently-computed figures.
- The consequence vocabulary is asserted against `RULEBOOK.md` section 51, so this file
  cannot drift from canon quietly.
- The canonical engine, `data/rules/core.json` and the six STATs are untouched, and the
  tests assert that too.

## Reproducing

`tools/issue200_aspects_report.py` regenerates every number above — a test fails if this document
quotes a figure the report no longer produces. `src/rules/aspects_prototype.py` holds the mechanics
with all seven DRAFT parameters in one block at the top, so calibration is a single edit.
`tests/test_issue200_aspects_prototype.py` pins the properties: the cap, the bound, the exact
rationals, the resource economy, the permission path, and the rulebook-pinned vocabulary.
