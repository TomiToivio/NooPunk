# Design canon — cyberpunk first, cosmic science fantasy underneath (#1007)

**Status: binding design decision.** This records what the player experiences and in what order. It does not
create mechanics; the rules are governed by
[`docs/rules/RULES_CONSTITUTION_1006.md`](../rules/RULES_CONSTITUTION_1006.md).

## The formula

> **NoöPunk = Cyberpunk + Law of One-inspired science fiction/fantasy.**

The *presentation* is cyberpunk first. The *depth* is cosmic. Those are not in conflict, but the order matters:
the player must be able to recognise the game as cyberpunk before anything suggests it is something else.

Law of One is a **fictional-world inspiration**, not in-universe doctrine, and not a claim about the real world.
In-world characters may believe it, argue about it, profit from it, or be wrong about it.

Tone: New Weird / social science fiction with understated comedy. 1970s psychedelic retrofuturist and Mœbius
aesthetics — **not** generic 1980s neon cyberpunk.

## The player

- A **young UNSA Academy graduate**, on their first field assignment in Helsinki.
- Inexperienced, and **skeptical in a specific way**: not a strawman doubter, but a person trained on
  procedures, who has been told a story about the world that is partially wrong.
- The PC's knowledge is partial and sometimes incorrect. The game must never assume the PC accepts an
  unbelievable proposition on trust — belief is something the player earns or refuses.

### Dr. Harri S. Romppainen — the trickster mentor

- Eccentric, quirky UNSA supervisor. A **trickster-magician**: vastly more knowledgeable than he lets on.
- He hints, jokes, deflects, stages puzzling learning experiences, and withholds classified or uncertain facts.
- He is **not a lore dispenser**. He must never lecture the cosmology into the player, and he must never remove
  agency by solving the case for them.
- His secrecy must be **credible**: he withholds because some things are classified, some are uncertain, some are
  dangerous to say, and some he genuinely does not know. "Because the plot needs it" is not a reason.
- He can be wrong.

## Revelation, in four layers

1. **Cyberpunk opening.** Rookie assignments, street crime, corporate misconduct, implants, cybersecurity,
   rogue AI, UNSA bureaucracy. Almost everything has a mundane explanation, and those explanations should be
   *true most of the time*.
2. **Anomalies.** Subtle PSI, inconsistent memories, suspicious NHI evidence, apparent hallucinations.
   Technological explanations remain plausible and should be offered.
3. **Disclosure and political consequence.** Progressively credible NHI contact, competing alien factions,
   powerful Noösphere phenomena, UNSA emergency powers expanding beyond their mandate.
4. **Ontological apocalypse.** Deeper consciousness and noetic cosmology; challenges to identity, reality,
   sovereignty and ethics.

Some genuine mysteries stay unresolved at every layer. **Not every campaign reaches layer 4** — the launch
build does not promise to.

## Spoiler architecture

Two bodies of knowledge, kept apart on purpose:

- **World truth (GM/agent-only):** what is actually going on.
- **PC-accessible knowledge:** what a rookie can know — initial briefings, rumours, clearance tiers, discoveries.

Rules:
- NPC dialogue and documentation must not accidentally spoil a later layer.
- A character may state a falsehood they believe; that is not a spoiler, it is a person.
- The same fact can be true, and still unknown to the PC. Separation is by *access*, not by *truth*.

## UNSA's origin: two existential threats

UNSA did not grow from one fear but two, and it was founded *before* either became undeniable:

1. **Rogue AI / malevolent ASI risk.** Before UNSA gains significant power, at least one **serious fictional
   rogue-AGI incident** has occurred.
2. **UFO/UAP disclosure and NHI risk.** A fictional **partial disclosure / verified anomaly** has occurred
   before UNSA gains significant authority; full NHI knowledge remains contested or classified.

Real-world inspiration: documented AI-agent control failures, and incremental governmental UAP investigation
and records release. **Do not confuse real UAP disclosure with established NHI confirmation, or a real AI
incident with a malevolent ASI.** The fiction is fiction; the fear is real.

UNSA evolves: civilian UN scientific coordination → emergency intelligence and security cooperation → a
*de facto* world government when the major crises hit. Distinguish **formal authority from actual power** — UNSA
remains partial, contested and dependent on member states, and many people outside it do not accept its
authority at all.

At campaign start the PC understands UNSA as a bureaucratic international agency. The secret history is not
front-loaded.

## The four launch modules

In the order they matter for the first playable MUD campaign:

1. **CWN core** — ordinary cyberpunk play, with SWN psionics integrated where needed. Unchanged foundation.
2. **Noösphere / ontological shock** (PbtA / *The Veil* inspired) — narrative moves, clocks and psychic/noetic
   experience as an **overlay**, never a replacement resolution.
3. **Investigation** (GUMSHOE inspired) — clue guarantees, investigative competencies, spend and interpretation,
   as an overlay on CWN.
4. **Ideology and factions** (LaclauGPT inspired) — actors, floating and empty signifiers, articulation,
   antagonism, reputation and faction relationships. A full faction simulation is later scope.

**Deferred:** the Law of One-derived rules themselves. They are built only when the unfolding narrative needs
them, from SWN/WWN/AWN wherever possible and NoöPunk additive mods where not. Nothing deferred may retroactively
change CWN.

## Working terminology (cyberpunkify the cosmology)

These are **working mappings**, not a mandated one-to-one ontology:

| Law of One term | NoöPunk working term |
|---|---|
| social memory complex | collective cognitive network |
| thought-form | noetic construct |
| density | higher-order consciousness state |
| channeling | nonlocal cognitive interface |
| wanderers | embedded NHI |

The mapping exists so that a cyberpunk world can talk about cosmic things without suddenly speaking a different
genre's language.

## What this means for implementation

- The first hours of play are **recognisably Cities Without Number**: a job, a street, a bureaucracy, a body.
- Every disclosure moment must be **playable**: evidence, witnesses, conflicting interpretations, consequences —
  not a cinematic and not an encyclopedia entry.
- Ordinary cyberpunk life does not stop being relevant after the cosmology arrives. Rent, work, corruption and
  inequality remain.
- Ambiguity is preserved where it is interesting; the game does not resolve questions the setting deliberately
  leaves open.
