# Step 1: three 2045 opening snapshots (issue #468)

**Status: proposal for the author's choice. Nothing here is canon until Tomi picks one.** This is §1's
own step-1 deliverable: 2-3 *short* alternative post-crisis openings with political equilibrium,
starting geographic scope, opening event, tensions and a core mystery - one recommended, none assumed.

**Everything below obeys the dated convention**: the scenario opens in **2045**; every background event
is a **`20XX`** entry in an **unordered** list; no chronology, causality or interval is fixed, and no
guess is made about when anything happened. **No Fall is completed** - a slow, geographically uneven one
remains possible. The earlier scenario canon is not erased. All three snapshots share this frame:

- **The worst immediate chaos has subsided.** Institutions survived, unevenly. Some territories are
  unstable, fractured, occupied or barely governed; others are functioning and mundane.
- **Four playable human factions** (#470): Libertarian Accelerationists, True Humans, Unity/Security/
  Stability, The Noösphere. **Orion, the Confederacy and the Swarm are three non-playable NHI strategic
  powers.** Unaligned humans, corporations, criminals, survivalists, trans/posthumans and the minor
  NHI categories (Biologics, Noetics, Constructs, Plasmoids) populate the world as *actors*, never as
  playable factions. Human **Biologic / Construct / Noetic** status is a state of being, not an
  allegiance - an uploaded agent is still a Unity officer or a Noösphere organiser.
- **The smallest playable map is one city and its region**, with global expansion later. The specific
  city is a canon decision and is deliberately left as a placeholder in all three.

Each snapshot therefore answers: what holds the peace, who is playing, and what the first turn is.

## Snapshot A - "The Quarantine Belt"

**Equilibrium: a managed emergency.** The crisis subsided because someone *held a line*: a cordon, an
inspection regime and a reconstruction authority that answer to the UN/UNSA machinery and, in practice,
to whichever member governments staff them. Order is real, provisional and visibly expensive - it runs on
renewed mandates, and everyone inside the belt knows it.

- **Scope:** one port city and its belt - the cordon, the port, the inland road, two or three districts
  that the cordon has made into different countries.
- **Opening event (turn 1):** the cordon's mandate comes up for renewal and the inspection finds
  something the cordon was supposed to prevent - reported, unverified, and immediately political.
- **Tensions:** the belt's economy depends on the emergency continuing; those inside it are protected and
  detained in the same gesture; the factions disagree about whether the cordon is keeping something out
  or something in.
- **Core mystery:** who is actually inside the belt - and whether containment is protecting the city or
  containing it.
- **Where the four start:** Unity runs the cordon and cannot afford to lose it. True Humans demand it be
  made absolute. Libertarians want it privatised and sold. The Noösphere wants it audited and shared.

## Snapshot B - "The Empty Seat"  (recommended)

**Equilibrium: a competition instead of a war.** The crisis subsided into a vacancy. The UN/UNSA
survived as contested machinery - it can still convene, staff and (sometimes) pay - but the authority it
claims is unexercised: the mandate that would let anyone settle the post-crisis order was not delivered,
and no faction can manufacture it alone. Competition is therefore rational and total war is irrational,
which is exactly the condition this game needs.

- **Scope:** one city whose institutions matter - a seat of the contested machinery, its port or
  spaceport, its districts, and the surrounding region that feeds it.
- **Opening event (turn 1):** the investiture that was supposed to end the vacancy fails - openly,
  procedurally, in public - and everyone who wanted that seat must now decide what to do with a city
  that no one is empowered to govern.
- **Tensions:** everyone can act and no one can legitimately decide; the factions' demands are all
  real and partly compatible, which is why coalitions form and why they can be broken; the three NHI
  powers read the vacancy as an opening, each in its own register.
- **Core mystery:** who holds the mandate - and whether the vacancy is an accident, a deadlock, or an
  outcome someone engineered.
- **Where the four start:** all four are credible claimants, which is the point - Unity has the
  institutions and the weakest mandate, True Humans has the street, Libertarians the money, the
  Noösphere the movement, and none of them can win alone.
- **Why I recommend it:** the issue asks what makes faction competition possible rather than immediate
  total war, and this snapshot *is* that answer - the vacant mandate is an empty signifier the whole
  political model already describes, so it needs no new canon, no war machine and no map larger than one
  city. It is turbulent, asymmetric and contested, and not apocalyptic; it leaves the three NHI powers
  as actors whose motives stay unresolved; and it makes the Laclau layer (#469) load-bearing from turn
  one rather than decorative. It is also the cheapest to prototype and to expand later.

## Snapshot C - "The Recovery Boom"

**Equilibrium: prosperity with a lien on it.** The crisis subsided because reconstruction *worked*. New
infrastructure, new contracts, a genuine upswing - financed, in the last instance, by the Confederacy,
whose help is generous, effective, paternalistic and impossible to read.

- **Scope:** a reconstruction city - a growing centre, a works site, a district for the people who
  arrived because of it, and a region being rebuilt around it.
- **Opening event (turn 1):** the first review. The Confederacy's delegation returns to look at what was
  built and to discuss what comes next - and every faction has to decide what it wants from a patron it
  cannot read.
- **Tensions:** the boom's winners and the sovereignty it costs; those who trust the benefactor and
  those who do not, who are not divided by faction; a dependency that would be cruel to refuse.
- **Core mystery:** what the Confederacy is actually doing here - the ambiguity the setting deliberately
  refuses to resolve, including the possibility that it is exactly what it appears to be.
- **Where the four start:** Libertarians want the contracts and no conditions. Unity wants the
  administration of the aid. The Noösphere wants it governed as a commons - and is accused of being the
  patron's creature. True Humans want it refused and the works dismantled.

## What all three deliberately leave open

Which of the `20XX` events happened first, what the three NHI powers truly want, how much of the Fall
completed and where, who is a hybrid and who is accused of being one, whether PSI is what it appears to
be, and which city this is. Every one of those stays a design choice or a play test - not a premise the
snapshots quietly settle.
