# Lakkahaltia playtest packet (#708)

**Status:** a packet to run, not a report of a run. **No session has been played.** Every result field
below is empty on purpose; filling it in from imagination would produce exactly the kind of evidence the
issue forbids.

This packet exists because #708 cites `ISSUE_704_LAKKAHALTIA_PLAYTEST.md` and that file is not in the
repository. It is built from the E1–E6 bundle already in `godot/scenarios/lakkahaltia/evidence.json`, so
table and Godot test the same evidence rather than two different cases.

---

## 1. The comparison

Two procedures, same case, same players if possible:

- **A — current delivery.** Clue access follows the ordinary skill check; a failed check can mean the
  fact is not obtained.
- **B — proposed layer.** A competent declaration in an accessible scene yields the fact; the check moves
  to interpretation and to the risks of acting.

Record for each scene: minutes to the first core fact; rolls spent on **finding** vs **interpreting**;
retries; which player was spotlighted; what the party paid; whether a hypothesis changed.

## 2. The evidence (E1–E6)

Each item carries what it **substantiates** and what it **falsely suggests** — the second column is what
keeps the mundane reading alive.

- **E1** — ward roster does not match the handover sheet *(digital, reception)*. Substantiates: one person
  was on the ward unrecorded. Falsely suggests: an intruder or abduction.
- **E2** — patient's drawing: a red door, a bog road, a low ridge *(physical, consultation room)*.
  Substantiates: the account has structure and repeats across patients. Falsely suggests: a shared story
  picked up in the ward.
- **E3** — nurse: thirty-four minutes missing from the night log *(testimony, corridor)*. Substantiates:
  the record has a gap the staff know about. Falsely suggests: a staff cover-up.
- **E4** — the file is sealed by a research ethics board, not by psychiatry *(physical, records office)*.
  Substantiates: the patient arrived through a study. Falsely suggests: a conspiracy inside the hospital.
- **E5** — the described place is on the 1987 permit map, 40 km east *(digital, municipal register)*.
  Substantiates: the account is anchored to a place the study never published. Falsely suggests: the
  patient read it somewhere.
- **E6** — *Lakkahaltia*, the small guardian of the cloudberry bog *(psi_lead, after the interpretation
  check)*. Substantiates: a second, non-clinical reading is available to the team. Falsely suggests: that
  the encounter happened.

**Fixed truths for the session** (choose before play, write down, do not reveal): whether the mundane
reading or the anomalous reading is true for E1, E3 and E5, and whether one staff member is lying. A
packet run without fixed truths cannot test the gate, because every result can be explained afterwards.

## 3. Six situations to exercise

1. **Patient refusal** — the patient will not discuss the drawing. Does the fact still arrive by another
   route, and what does the refusal cost?
2. **Unavailable specialist** — no character holds the competency that reaches E4. Does the table find
   the institutional route, or stall?
3. **Offline terminal** — E1 is unreachable digitally. Is there a second path, and does procedure B
   differ from A here?
4. **Lost original** — E5's register entry has been destroyed; only a copy survives. Does the copy
   substantiate less?
5. **Wrong hypothesis** — the table commits to the intruder reading of E1. Does the procedure let them
   discover the error, and how expensive is it?
6. **Fixed truths** — the mundane reading is true for one of E1/E3/E5. Does the gate catch a table that
   wants the weird answer?

## 4. What to record per situation

- which clue was sought, by what method, by whom;
- whether access, competency and declaration each held;
- whether the fact arrived, and after how long;
- what the interpretation check cost;
- which hypotheses were live before and after, and whether any was retired **for a reason**;
- whether an inconclusive test was recorded as inconclusive rather than forced to a yes/no.

## 5. Overlay check

Run one scene with motivation/tag/consequence overlays on. They must change **colour and cost**, never
facts: an aspect may not find a clue, and a move may not establish a reading. If an overlay produces
information the scene does not contain, that is a finding, not a feature.

## 6. Boundaries to confirm rather than test

- no free powers: PSI produces a **lead**, never a verdict (E6 is the worked case);
- no rank spending for favours, and no generic Strain payment for ordinary social asks;
- an absent skill is an ordinary check, not a refusal to play;
- restricted access is a scene-level obstacle with a cost, not a lock on the plot.
