# NoöPunk's three-layer world structure

> **Status:** author-specified setting and simulation structure.
>
> This document explains the three layers that make NoöPunk distinct as a world,
> RPG setting, and sociological simulation. It does not add game mechanics.
>
> The three layers overlap. They are not separate dimensions, genres, or game modes.

## 1. Material layer: Cyberpunk

The first layer is a relatively realistic cyberpunk future extrapolated from
present-day trends.

It includes:

- AI and automation
- corporate and state power
- surveillance
- platform and network society
- cybercrime and hacking
- biotechnology
- brain-computer interfaces
- cybernetics and augmentation
- inequality and technological exclusion
- infrastructure, compute, networks, and energy
- changing labour, ownership, and political economy

This side should remain recognisably descended from the world of the 2020s rather
than becoming generic far-future science fiction.

Tonally, NoöPunk leans **more toward Philip K. Dick than William Gibson**. Gibsonian
cyberspace and street-level cyberpunk remain important influences, but NoöPunk puts
extra weight on unstable reality, synthetic persons, identity, memory, paranoia,
institutions that define reality, and uncertainty about what kind of world people
actually inhabit.

The cyberpunk layer is the setting's **material transformation**.

## 2. Ontological layer: the Noösphere

The second layer occupies a role structurally similar to the "urban fantasy" layer
in some genre-mixing RPGs, but NoöPunk does **not** treat it as fantasy magic.

Instead it explores speculative paradigm shifts concerning mind, consciousness,
non-human intelligence, and reality itself.

Its central inspirations include:

- **Alexander Wendt's quantum-mind / panpsychist work**
- **UFO / UAP disclosure** as a social and ontological paradigm shift
- **psionics** as a phenomenon that becomes scientifically and technologically
  consequential inside the fictional setting
- **Jacques Vallée-style extra weirdness**, where NHI cannot be reduced neatly to
  extraterrestrial hardware and biology
- the emergence of a **Noösphere** connecting humans, AIs, networks, BCIs,
  collective intelligence, culture, and potentially non-local consciousness

A useful author shorthand is Wendt's two linked paradigm shifts: a
**consciousness-fundamental / quantum-panpsychist shift** and **UFO disclosure**.
In NoöPunk, both can intersect with psionics. Vallée adds the possibility that even
after disclosure the phenomenon remains stranger than a clean extraterrestrial
explanation.

These are **fictional and speculative premises**, not claims that the underlying
real-world hypotheses are established science.

The Noösphere layer is the setting's **ontological transformation**.

## 3. Social layer: politics, ideology, and sociology

The third layer asks what societies actually do when several paradigm shifts happen
at once.

Politics, ideology, institutions, class, social movements, corporations, states,
media, science, religion, security organisations, subcultures, and collective
identities are not background decoration. They are part of the game world itself.

The central question is not only:

> What new technology or phenomenon exists?

but also:

> Who defines what it means, who owns it, who benefits from it, who is excluded,
> who regulates it, who resists it, and what new political formations emerge around it?

NoöPunk therefore treats its world as a **sociological simulation of multiple
interacting paradigm shifts**.

This layer shares conceptual territory with the author's **LaclauGPT** and
**Panpsychic Cyborg Multitude (PCM)** projects:

- from LaclauGPT: ideology, discourse, articulation, hegemonic struggle, competing
  political projects, contested signifiers, and realignment around events
- from PCM: human-AI-cyborg assemblages, collective intelligence, distributed
  organisation, participant rights, identity, autonomy, and coordination

Those projects can inform NoöPunk's world modelling, but the game must remain its
own fictional system. Research concepts should become playable situations rather
than academic exposition.

The social layer is the setting's **struggle over the material and ontological
transformations**.

## The three together

A compact formulation is:

- **Cyberpunk = material transformation**
- **Noösphere = ontological transformation**
- **Politics / ideology / sociology = social struggle over both**

A campaign, mission, or scene may foreground one layer more than the others.
No scene is required to use all three.

The strongest NoöPunk situations often cross layers. A routine cyberpunk problem
can reveal a Noöspheric anomaly, which then produces political conflict over what
the anomaly means, who controls it, and what institutions are created around it.

That is the intended structure: not "cyberpunk plus psychic powers", but a cyberpunk
society undergoing several simultaneous technological, ontological, and social
paradigm shifts.
