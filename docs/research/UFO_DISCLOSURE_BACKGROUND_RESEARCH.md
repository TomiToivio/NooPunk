## 0. Method, and the labels that make the rest usable

Every claim in this dossier carries one of three labels. The labels are the point, not decoration:

| label | means |
| --- | --- |
| **(A) REAL RECORD** | What a primary or near-primary source states, cited by URL and date. Includes "a witness said X", which is a fact about the witness, not about the world. |
| **(B) NOÖPUNK READING** | The setting's fictional reinterpretation. Ours. Tomi's to approve, change or delete. |
| **(C) NOT SUPPORTED** | Inference the source does not carry. No real-world ET conclusion, no proven retrieval programme, no proven psi mechanism. |

Three further distinctions are used throughout, because collapsing them is how this subject produces
false certainty:

1. **attestation** — a named person said it, under oath or on the record;
2. **corroboration** — an independent source that could not simply have copied the first;
3. **proof** — inspectable material: hardware, imagery, documents, reproducible measurement.

A disclosure story built only on (1) is a story about institutions and belief, which is exactly what
NoöPunk needs, and is not evidence of nonhuman presence.

**What this document is not.** It is not a NoöPunk timeline, not a canon decision, and not an
endorsement. Where the setting needs a fact the record does not supply, the proposal document says so
in the open and marks it **(B)**.

The issue asks that Grusch, Barber, Davis and Elizondo be treated as canonical *for the fiction* while
their real-world status is preserved exactly. That means three columns: the claim, its provenance, and
what it would take to move it from attestation to proof.

Much of his account is explicitly **what others told him** | attestation + second-hand | A programme document, a facility, a material sample, or a named programme manager testifying with records |

| **Jake Barber** | Flew helicopter retrieval missions for a covert programme; recovered an egg-shaped object and an octahedral object; "the programme" exists | NewsNation interview, **18 January 2025**; subsequent podcast appearances; associated with the "Skywatcher" team | attestation (first-person), uncorroborated | Flight logs, contracts, crew corroboration, the objects themselves |
| **Eric Davis** | Four distinct non-human species are known to US programmes | May 2025 Capitol Hill briefing remarks, widely reported | attestation (second-hand summary of classified material) | Species-level biological evidence, or a programme document naming them |
| **AARO (DoD)** | No verifiable evidence that any UAP sighting is extraterrestrial; no authentic UAP NDA; no hidden reverse-engineering programme found in the historical record | *Historical Record Report, Vol. 1*, February 2024 ([aaro.mil PDF](https://www.aaro.mil/Portals/136/PDFs/AARO_Historical_Record_Report_Vol_1_2024.pdf)) | official finding | AARO's own reversal, or evidence AARO lacked access |

**The shape of the disagreement, stated fairly.** AARO's finding is a statement about *what the
historical record contains*, not about whether anything anomalous has ever flown. Grusch's and
Barber's claims are statements about programmes that, if real, are precisely what a historical-records
review would not contain. Both can be true. NoöPunk's fiction can therefore hold "the programme
existed" **(B)** while keeping the real-world record honest **(A)/(C)**.

**What the record does not support (C):** that any of these claims has been independently proven; that
a species count is exhaustive; that recovered materials have been examined by anyone outside the
alleged programme.

## 3. Government records and the shape of the disclosure channel

Rather than repeat the channel analysis in
[`PURSUE_UAP_DISCLOSURE_SOURCE_SHEET.md`](PURSUE_UAP_DISCLOSURE_SOURCE_SHEET.md), the points that matter
for this scenario:

* **The releases are real and partial.** [war.gov/ufo](https://www.war.gov/ufo/) and the AARO
  collections publish documents that were previously unavailable; the AARO Historical Record Report
  (Feb 2024) is itself a disclosure document — of a *conclusion*, which is a different thing from a
  release of material.
* **Congressional attention is episodic, not cumulative.** Hearings create record, not policy; the
  2023 hearing produced sworn testimony and no programme.
* **NARA holds the paper trail** of the older programmes — Project Blue Book, Project Sign — which is
  where a Finnish researcher would actually start, and where the 1947 Helsinki naval-attaché report
  surfaces (see §7).
* **The denial is part of the record.** AARO's "no evidence of extraterrestrial technology" belongs in
  the same dossier as the testimony, not in a footnote. In-world, a Europol analyst would notice that
  the denial is *narrower* than the public reads it.

**(B) The NoöPunk reading:** disclosure is a **managed process with several owners**, each releasing
what it can survive. That is the premise the proposal builds on, and it is a political claim, not a
factual one.

## 7. Finland: the documented layer

The Finnish material is thin, local and unusually well-dated, which makes it ideal for fiction that
wants to feel *researched*. Everything in this section is (A) unless labelled otherwise.

### 7.1 1933–34 — the ghost fliers

A Scandinavian wave of reports of large grey aircraft with powerful searchlights, flying at night in
weather no aircraft of the period could manage, over Sweden and Norway, with spill-over into Finnish
press and military attention. The wave coincides with the rearmament decade and with the first
serious Scandinavian air-defence anxiety.

**Record:** newspaper and military-attaché material; no physical trace.
**In-world use (B):** the first Finnish file that a 2045 analyst would find *already redacted by
someone else*.

### 7.2 1946 — the ghost rockets

The first reports are dated **26 February 1946, from Finnish observers**; roughly two thousand
sightings were logged between May and December 1946, concentrated over Sweden and Finland, with
reports of silent, rocket-shaped objects, sometimes transmedium
([Ghost rockets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghost_rockets); US intelligence review of the wave in the
war.gov UAP release).

**Record:** extensive press and attaché reporting; a Swedish military investigation; no recovered
object.
**Competing contemporary readings:** Soviet missile tests (the season of Peenemünde-evacuation
rumours), meteorites, mass suggestion, and — the reading NoöPunk keeps alive — something that was not
anyone's missile.

### 7.3 1947 — Helsinki, and the document that survives

A US Naval Attaché in Finland forwarded a translation of a Helsinki press report describing a
"rocket-like" phenomenon. Read in transcription on 2026-11-05
([Gross FOIA transcript](https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/htm/foia01.htm)), it is **Intelligence
Report Serial R-5-8-47**, issued by the Intelligence Division, Office of Chief of Naval Operations,
dated **10 February 1947**, from the Naval Attaché at Helsinki, source "Press and Official Liaison
Officer", **evaluation C3** — meaning, in the period's own grading, "fairly reliable source,
doubtful accuracy".

The transcription's substance, quoted closely:

> "Since 20 August 1946, no more 'ghost rockets' have been reported by the Finnish press." — after
> which "numerous observations had been made, mostly by inexperienced observers, of objects
> resembling rockets flashing across the sky."
>
> A further report on "the evening of 18 January at 21:30 … over Helsinki": an amateur observer,
> **M. Niamala**, "who was working at the Urma observatory", saw "a clear yellow light which was
> visible for almost 10 seconds, at three seconds intervals. It appeared and disappeared five or six
> times, after which the flame (light) changed to a white glow and finally a dark red color." The
> object "seemed to be on a curving course approaching from the north and at an altitude of about
> 2,000 meters", and "just before the rocket disappeared … an object resembling a parachute was seen
> to fall to earth", observed through a telescope.
>
> The attaché's own conclusion: "Numerous contacts have been checked but no additional information
> could be secured. The only information available is that which appeared in the newspapers."

**Two cautions.** The "Urma observatory" is almost certainly **Ursa**, the Finnish astronomical
association, and the name should be verified against the Finnish original before canon use. And the
document establishes only that *the report existed and was taken seriously enough to file* — not
that the object was anything in particular.

**In-world use (B):** the perfect NoöPunk artifact. Impeccable provenance, explicit confidence
grade, and an explicit admission that the only source is a newspaper. It lets a 2045 investigator
prove the archive was *curated* — the American copy exists, the observatory's logbook for that night
does not.

### 7.4 Later Finnish material

* **UFO organisations and archives.** Finnish UFO research has been organised, intermittently, since
  the 1960s–70s; the exact institutional history (names, splits, holdings) must be verified against
  Finnish-language sources before being written into fiction. Treat any specific claim about SUT or a
  named archive as **unverified** until checked.
* **Folklore and spiritism.** Finnish and Karelian folklore is rich in anomalous-person motifs
  (haltija, näkki, metsänpeitto — "forest cover", a state of being lost in a place that has become
  wrong). The setting may use *metsänpeitto* as the folk vocabulary for astral disorientation **(B)**,
  which is a translation, not a claim about the tradition.
* **Military and aviation records.** Finnish wartime and Cold-War air-defence reporting exists but is
  not public at the granularity fiction would like. Any specific intercept must be invented and marked.
* **Parapsychology and PSI subcultures.** Finnish research interest in parapsychology has existed in
  academic and popular registers since the early twentieth century; specific institutions and
  results need verification before citation.

### 7.5 The 1946 recovery question

The setting's Finnish "1946 recovered biologics/technology" is **(B)**. The record does **not** support
a Finnish recovery, and this dossier asserts none. What the record *does* support is a national
atmosphere in which such a recovery could plausibly have been kept quiet: a small country, a
militarised border, an intelligence service learning to keep secrets in two languages.
