## 23:05 EEST — The Nobel goes to a switch for single neurons: optogenetics takes Medicine 2026

**Categories:** BCI, transhumanism and human cyborgification + Cybernetic Noösphere (neural writing) + scientific/institutional recognition.

On Monday 5 October the Nobel Assembly at Karolinska Institutet awarded the 2026 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine to **Karl Deisseroth** (Stanford / HHMI), **Peter Hegemann** (Humboldt University Berlin) and **Georg Nagel** (University of Würzburg) "for their discoveries concerning light-gated ion channels and optogenetics". Hegemann and Nagel identified channelrhodopsin in the alga *Chlamydomonas*; Deisseroth introduced the gene into rodent neurons and showed that individual cells could be switched on and off with light inside a living brain. The citation frames the method as what lets researchers "reveal neural circuits governing specific memories, feelings, and behaviors relevant for neurological and psychiatric disorders".

**Established fact:** the prize, the laureates, the scientific basis and the 12-million-SEK award are public (Nobel press release, 5 October 2026; independent coverage by Reuters, STAT and DW).

**Evidence status:** **institutional and scientific recognition of a method** — the strongest kind available. It is **not** evidence for any claim about consciousness, and it touches the panpsychism question not at all: optogenetics is a causal tool for perturbing identified circuits, i.e. the technology of making neural tissue addressable in both directions. Its timeline significance is narrow and real: the causal *write* channel into the brain is now the most institutionally honoured brain method of the year, in the same weeks that read-out decoders set throughput records and lab-grown organoids learned to discriminate signals (4 October).

## 23:10 EEST — A city compels sworn testimony: NYC Council subpoenas the AI labs

**Categories:** AGI / ASI governance + Cyberpunk (state and municipal power) + institutional acknowledgment + human–AI assemblage politics.

On Monday 5 October the **New York City Council** convened its 51-member **Committee of the Whole** for a hearing on AI safety, described by organisers as the first time a US legislative body has compelled sworn public testimony from major AI companies. Policy and safety staff from **OpenAI, Anthropic, Google and Meta** testified. **SpaceXAI did not appear despite a served subpoena**, which Speaker **Julie Menin** called a violation with legal action to follow. Whistleblower witnesses included **Jacob Coxon**, the former Anthropic researcher whose 8 September resignation post drew an enormous audience, and former Google DeepMind researcher **Alex Turner**. Coxon told the Council: "On the current path, I think it is more likely than not that humanity loses control to these AIs and it could end in human extinction." Asked to quantify worst-case risk, OpenAI's Morgan Dwyer answered that the percentage does not matter, which Menin called "flippant at best". The Council is weighing disclosure requirements, third-party approval for AI systems deployed in the city, and whistleblower bounty provisions.

The same day, *New York* magazine with **Asterisk** published interviews with **eight former OpenAI, Anthropic and Google DeepMind employees** — Brundage, Campbell, Coxon, Hilton, Mishkin, Kokotajlo, Turner and Wu — describing exits over the race, defence partnerships, and work they could no longer pursue inside.

**Established fact:** the hearing, the witnesses, the subpoena no-show and the quoted testimony are on the record (Associated Press wire, 5 October 2026; NY City Council proceedings). The eight Asterisk interviews were published 5 October 2026.

**Evidence status:** **institutional and political–process signal** — a jurisdiction using subpoena power, and the first *municipal* register in an accountability stack that already holds a federal voluntary accord (30 September), a federal inquiry and state subpoenas. ## 23:20 EEST — Psi research in 2026 is being settled by method, and the tightest new results are null

**Categories:** Scientific validation of psionics and psychotronic technologies + scientific method + consciousness-fundamental ontology.

This run surfaced a cluster of 2026 heterodox-literature items, none previously logged here, that together show where the psionics beat actually stands. Two are arguments. **Tim Lomas** (Harvard), in *Philosophy and Cosmology* 37 (published 1 October 2026), argues that "consciousness-oriented" theories and anomalous data — telepathy, remote viewing, UFOs and orbs — are *mutually reinforcing*, and that the materialist paradigm's circular dismissal of such data as "paranormal" is precisely what keeps it from counting as evidence. Separately, **Tressoldi and Storm**, in the *Journal of Scientific Exploration* 40(2) (2026), test the standing complaint that ESP results are an artefact of publication bias, comparing 165 ESP studies (243 experiments) and 40 preregistered Koestler-registry confirmations against mainstream psychology; they find **null outcomes published at a higher rate in ESP research than in psychology**, while conceding they cannot conclusively exclude even that smaller bias.

The counterweight from the same period is methodological rather than rhetorical. The largest recent preregistered replication of Bem's precognition claim (Walleczek et al., *PLOS ONE*; 26,483 participants, 420,472 critical trials) **failed to replicate it**; and a commissioned conceptual replication of Radin's double-slit experiment, run with systematic positive/negative/time-reversed controls and a sham arm, **failed to confirm the original anomalous observer effect** while finding a statistically significant false positive in exactly the test category predicted for a true positive.

**Established fact:** all four items are publicly readable. Lomas: *Philosophy and Cosmology* 37, 23–74 (published 1 October 2026). Tressoldi & Storm: JSE 40(2), 232–240. Walleczek et al.: PLOS ONE 20(11):e0335330. The Radin replication is published in the psi-research literature's own venues.

**Evidence status:** mixed, kept strictly separate. The Lomas paper is **interpretive argument** in a peer-reviewed but heterodox venue — not evidence for psi. The Tressoldi–Storm paper is **meta-scientific evidence about publishing practices**, not about psi's truth. The two replication failures are **scientific evidence of the negative kind**, and they matter for the timeline *because* they are what a field looks like when it accepts falsification. Nothing here is a threshold crossing for "scientific validation of psionics"; the honest reading is that the 2026 literature is **narrowing the claim to testable form**, and that the strictest tests so far do not confirm it. The extraordinary claim remains extraordinary, and documenting its current null results *is* the NoöPunk protocol working.

## 23:25 EEST — Instrumenting the Noösphere: open data from a distributed RNG network, and an era ending

**Categories:** Emergence of the Noösphere (cybernetic + psionic) + collective intelligence + scientific infrastructure.

Surfaced this run. The **Global Consciousness Project (GCP)** — the 1998–2015 Princeton-era network of physical random-number generators tested against pre-registered global events, cumulative roughly 7σ across about 500 events — has entered a new phase. Its original archive states that **GCP 1 active data collection ended on 3 April 2026**, with forward work moved to **GCP 2.0** under the **HeartMath Institute** as part of the Global Coherence Initiative: a much larger network (over 4,000 RNGs planned; roughly a thousand hosts contributing several thousand simultaneous streams). A GCP 2.0 community meeting reported that **public data downloads are now open**, including network-coherence history and device-level metrics, with an explicitly distributed ethos: anyone may host a device, examine the raw data, or fund nodes in under-served regions.

The GCP is also the cleanest available case of a field criticising its own favourite story. Collaborator **Peter Bancel**'s 17-year reanalysis concluded that the data do **not** support a field-like "global consciousness" and are better explained as a **goal-oriented effect associated with the experimenters themselves** — the effect appearing only for events the experimenters cared about, in the exact windows specified for them. Project director Roger Nelson replied in the same issue that field-like models remain viable. The dispute is unresolved.

**Established fact:** GCP 1 active collection ended 3 April 2026 (original archive); GCP 2.0 is run by HeartMath/GCI and plans a network an order of magnitude larger; the Bancel reanalysis and Nelson's reply are published in *EXPLORE* (2017). The precise date of the public-data announcement was **not** independently pinned in this run, so it is logged as a development of 2026 with an explicit dating caveat.

**Evidence status:** the **instrument and its open data** are documentary fact. The **interpretation** — global field versus experimenter effect versus statistical artefact — is contested *inside* the field, and the most rigorous insider analysis leans away from the field hypothesis. This is explicitly **not** evidence for a psionic Noösphere, and the ~7σ cumulative figure must never be cited without Bancel's caveat attached. What is new and real is infrastructural: a long-running, pre-registered, openly downloadable instrument for testing claims about collective mind at planetary scale.

- Bancel, *EXPLORE* 13(2), 2017: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28279629/
