#### "Repentance in Blue" — Phoenix Alexander (*Lightspeed* Magazine, Issue 198, November 2026; 1,478 words; free to read online, published 12 November 2026)

**What it is.** A two-part story, "The Mother" and "The Son". A six-year-old, Ryan, reports "blue" light in his bedroom. His mother sleeps in the room to disprove it and wakes into a room filled with blue light, heat and "a bass, machinic purr"; the boy floats toward a figure with a three-fingered hand, and she is felled by a current running through them both. Her husband finds her screaming that they took their son. The child she wakes to she is certain is not her son: the dogs avoid him, he refuses red meat, fish and anything green, he flinches from her, and under hypnosis he speaks "words in no language I'd ever heard". The second half is his account: a life in which "trauma" and "abuse" were the explanations offered by everyone he knew, in which he stops visibly ageing around fifty, outlives everyone, wonders whether he was "rebuilt like the ship of Theseus", and on 8 June 2220 leaves cornflowers and a note on her grave — "Whatever I am, I remain always, Your son, Ryan x".

**Release state.** **Released** — free online 12 November 2026; audio edition narrated by Justine Eyre and Stefan Rudnicki.

**The specific affinity, in one disputable sentence.** Contact arrives as light that has *texture*, and the story never establishes whether the child who comes back is a replacement, the same boy altered, or a hypnotically produced certainty in his mother's mind — abduction staged as an unresolved substitution that the reader is made to hold without a verdict.

**What NoöPunk could take, and where it diverges.** Take the **two-witness structure**: the same night told once by the person who saw it and once by the being who lived it, with neither account usable to settle the other. That is the watch's own evidence discipline turned into narrative form, and it is more honest than any omniscient rendering of first contact. Take the **single residue**: the only physical trace of the event is that the returnee does not age, which lets a lifetime of ordinary biography carry the anomaly instead of a laboratory scene. Take the **undecidable ontology** as the point rather than a flaw: the mother's testimony, the psychiatric explanation and the returnee's own uncertainty all remain standing. Diverge: there is no institution, no disclosure, no sensor record and no cosmology; the anomaly stays inside one family across three centuries; psi is never named; and nothing in the text claims mind is part of physics. The abduction frame is borrowed (the author's spotlight cites the Betty and Barney Hill case), so the story's originality is in the refusal to adjudicate, not in the contact.

**Established fact:** venue, issue, word count, publication date, narrators, the two-part structure, the blue light and the three-fingered figure, the mother's certainty, the dogs and the food refusals, the hypnotist scene, the arrested ageing, the Theseus reference, the 2220 date and the closing note — all read in the published text. The author's stated inspiration is read in the same issue's author spotlight.

**Evidence status:** **primary text, read in full at 1,478 words.** Fiction is **zero evidence** about NHI, abduction, psi, identity continuity or the timeline. The story does not report an event; it stages the epistemics of one.

**Source:** https://www.lightspeedmagazine.com/fiction/repentance-in-blue/ ; issue contents: https://www.lightspeedmagazine.com/issues/lightspeed-magazine-issue-198-november-2026

#### *Proscribed Palimpsest* — edmond00 (roleplaying game for Windows and Linux; itch.io, $4.99, free three-run demo; early-access release, latest update 5 November 2026) — late catch

**What it is.** A procedurally simulated world — a spherical world to cross, inhabitants with schedules and personalities, bodies modelled "down to their organs and humors" — that the player never sees directly. You see it "through the mind of a unique character you shape, from their body to their childhood memories". The mind is the design: it is made of ***modi mentis***, "over hundreds of crafts, sciences, vices, and passions, each with its own voice", and **a local instruction-tuned language model speaks through the ones your character possesses**, so the world reaches you only as that character perceives it — "what they notice, how they judge it, what they consider doing, and what lies beyond them". The model ships inside the download, runs on your own machine, needs no internet, account or subscription, and can be swapped for any instruction-tuned GGUF model. The game is in active development, published in numbered "Volumes"; Volume 5 added the rule that beasts have no pineal gland, cannot learn its *modi mentis*, "do not think again or doubt", and pay full experience per level, and Volume 7 added an "Always Reject" option on the Memory tab so that a refused *modus mentis* "is never learned again".

**Release state.** **Released as an early-access version** and updated repeatedly; Volume 7 dated 5 November 2026. The storefront fields read did not display an original release date. A late catch: not previously logged here.

**The specific affinity, in one disputable sentence.** The character sheet is an assembly of faculties rather than a set of numbers, and the narration the player receives is generated only through the faculties that character actually has — identity as composition, with perception as a mechanic rather than a description.

**What NoöPunk could take, and where it diverges.** Take **the faculty-as-narrator rule**: prose is generated only through *modi mentis* the character possesses, so what a mind can say is bounded by what it is made of. That is a portable answer to a problem NoöPunk's own psionics design has not solved — how to make a point of view a rule rather than a writing style — and it is cheap to steal for any table: list the faculties, and let each speak only its own register. Take **the pineal gland as the organ of doubt**: a mind that can reconsider is an organ you either have or lack, beasts lack it, and second thoughts cost experience. That is a sharper model of metacognition than a willpower stat, and it makes reflection a body part rather than a mood. Take **irreversible refusal**: "Always Reject" on the Memory tab permanently bars a faculty, which is a written, non-negotiable boundary inside a character rather than a player preference. Diverge: it is a single-player simulation, not a table; there is no cosmology, no psi and no claim that mind is physics — the model is a rendering apparatus, and nothing in the design says the world is mental. The compute is local by construction, which is the lane's interest, but the game makes no political argument about it. The storefront's own description of model behaviour was not verified by playing.

**Established fact:** the simulated world and its organs-and-humours bodies, the *modi mentis* model of mind, the local model speaking through possessed faculties, the offline/no-account terms, the model-swap instruction, the early-access status, the Volume 5 pineal-gland and doubt rule, and the Volume 7 "Always Reject" behaviour — from the storefront page and the author's own development logs.

**Evidence status:** **storefront description and author devlogs (Volumes 5 and 7 read); no rules text examined and no play.** Zero evidence about psi, consciousness or the timeline.

**Sources:** https://edmond00.itch.io/proscribed ; https://edmond00.itch.io/proscribed/devlog/1723594/volume-5-doubt-considered-as-a-faculty ; https://edmond00.itch.io/proscribed/devlog/1754930/volume-7-cuir-bouilli-and-pattern-welding

#### *Animal Spiral* — Luis Othoniel Rosa, translated from Spanish by Katie Marya (novel; Charco Press, 448 pp., June 2026; reviewed by Niall Harrison in *Locus*, November) — late catch, review level

**What it is.** Middle-aged streamer twins in Bayamón, Puerto Rico become "the first human beings to successfully connect — sharing their consciousness across 34 translucent cables". The result is the **Animal**, an "intracerebral force" that grows to include "anthills of synaptically entwined bodies", a floating library-kitchen above the Mississippi and a transhuman compound in a future Cuba, and the novel runs across centuries from a revolution in San Juan to five post-human siblings with millions of bodies. The entire book is narrated by that collective, which calls itself "we someones".

**Release state.** **Translated and released** — Charco Press, June 2026 in the publisher's and US retail listings (an Australian listing dates the paperback 1 November 2026); reviewed in *Locus* in November 2026. A late catch at review level; the review's own publication date was not displayed on the page as read.

**The specific affinity, in one disputable sentence.** A collective consciousness is the narrator and the political subject at once — born from a cable splice, constituted by a revolution, and formally held open so that the individuals inside it are not dissolved into one voice.

**What NoöPunk could take, and where it diverges.** Take the **grammar**. The Animal says: "We don't have access to the other's thoughts, but we do learn to think as they think." That single sentence is the cleanest prose solution this watch has seen to the problem at the centre of the Panpsychic Cyborg Multitude's own rule — that an assemblage must not be collapsed into a sovereign voice — because it keeps a first person plural that admits it cannot read its own members. Take **authorship as assembly**: Rosa names thirteen further collaborators and describes the pandemic editing sessions as "general assemblies", which is the collective form the book describes being used to write it. Diverge: the cables are a fiction device, not a mechanism; the collective is post-colonial and Caribbean rather than a network protocol; nothing in the reviewed text claims psi, mind in physics, or that this has happened. A good novel, and the affinity is conceptual, not evidentiary.

**Established fact:** publisher, translator, length, price, the June 2026 date in US and publisher listings and the 1 November 2026 date in the Australian listing; the twins, the 34 cables, the Animal, the "we someones" self-description, the quoted line, the 2032/2042 chronology, the five post-human siblings at the end, and the thirteen-collaborator account and the "general assemblies" quote — all from the *Locus* review, with the quotations as the reviewer reports them.

**Evidence status:** **review-level; the novel itself was not read.** Zero evidence about consciousness, collectives or the timeline.

**Sources:** https://locusmag.com/review/animal-spiral-by-luis-othoniel-rosa-translated-by-katie-marya-review-by-niall-harrison ; https://www.newsouthbooks.com.au/books/animal-spiral
