## 23:05 EET — The corpus under the BCI boom is being taken from people who thought they were buying sleep coaching

**Publication date:** 4 November 2026, 13:40 ET; **discovered in this watch:** 5 November. **Timeline categories:** BCI, transhumanism and human cyborgification; Cyberpunk (data ownership, consent, platform power over the body, inequality of legibility); cybernetic Noösphere (a commercially assembled neural substrate, **not** a psionic one). **Not:** a thought-decoding breakthrough, a psi result, a mass-implant threshold or an NHI item.

**What is genuinely new.** *Science* published Jonathan Moens's year-long investigation, produced with the Pulitzer Center's AI Accountability Network, into what consumer EEG wearables do with the brain data they collect. The findings are concrete. Tens of millions of hours of EEG have already been collected by consumer neurotech firms, and multiple companies are using that corpus to train **"brain foundation models"** — general-purpose encoders of brain activity in the shape of large language models. Interaxon, maker of the Muse headband, has trained its model on roughly **50,000 hours** of user EEG; its privacy policy did not explicitly say user data could train AI brain models until *Science* asked, after which the company updated the policy and its chief marketing officer conceded that "users should be explicitly informed". Emotiv trains a "large brain model" partly on data from users of its higher-end devices and, after the same inquiries, moved to opt-in consent for de-identified training data. **Nine** consumer neurotech and AI-software companies have publicly acknowledged building a brain foundation model.

**The policy survey is the documentary core.** *Science* reviewed the privacy policies of **57** consumer-oriented companies that collect EEG data from around the world. **Four had no privacy policy at all**; **eleven** covered website visitors only. Of the 42 with a broader policy, about **64%** explicitly mention brain data; about **69%** allow corporate transfer of data if the company is acquired; about **88%** permit sharing with authorities such as police, government or courts on request; only about **14%** explicitly rule out selling brain data, with the rest silent. The commercial next step is already named: the Chinese company MATRIX plans **NeuraMATRIX**, a marketplace where "everyday users lease their sleep data and earn income". Institutional acknowledgment is present too — the UN Secretary-General's October report ([A/81/351](http://docs.un.org/en/A/81/351)) warns that neurotechnologies could give governments access to innermost thoughts, and UNESCO and the OECD have flagged consumer-grade brain-computer interfaces as a distinct concern.

**Why it matters for NoöPunk.** The BCI beat is usually watched for a device: an implant, a bandwidth record, a clinical first. This item shows the threshold work happening one layer earlier, in the **corpus and its licence**. A noösphere with a neural sensory layer needs a substrate; that substrate is presently being assembled as a by-product of wellness products, under consent given for a different purpose, in policies that a majority of companies do not even apply to brain data explicitly. The political question NoöPunk keeps asking about augmentation — who owns the body, who is legible to the state — arrives here in its most literal form: **88% of the surveyed policies permit disclosure to authorities on request, and the training already happened before the consent existed.** De-identification does not answer it, because stripped names are not stripped inference.

**Established fact:** the investigation, its methodology, its 57-company sample and the percentages above; the Interaxon and Emotiv statements and policy changes; the existence of nine self-declared brain-foundation-model efforts; the NeuraMATRIX plan as described by its chief marketing technology officer; the UN report. All of this is documented reporting and company statement, not independent technical audit.

**Evidence status:** **established investigative reporting plus institutional acknowledgment** — the strongest non-peer-reviewed class. It is **not** evidence that consumer EEG supports reliable thought decoding: the article itself records that most consumer devices carry one to six electrodes rather than 19–25, that most lack scientific underpinning, and that a 2018 review of more than 3,000 publications cited by consumer EEG companies found only 11 using gold-standard methods, ten of which found no benefit over control. Nothing here is evidence for psi, for a psionic noösphere, for machine consciousness, or for a crossed timeline threshold. The "brain foundation model" is a modelling ambition with a corpus, not a demonstrated general decoder.

**Strongest sources:** [*Science*, "The scramble for your brain data", 4 November 2026](https://www.science.org/content/article/wearable-device-makers-are-harvesting-brain-data-train-ai-without-telling-users); [UN Secretary-General's report A/81/351](http://docs.un.org/en/A/81/351).

**Key uncertainty / next test:** whether any jurisdiction classifies neural data as a sensitive category with purpose limitation rather than notice-and-consent; whether the nine acknowledged models can be retrained or withdrawn once the corpus is contested; whether "lease your sleep data" marketplaces become a supply relationship with a new commodity class; and whether the technical gap between one-to-six-electrode wearables and clinical-grade EEG is closed by hardware or simply asserted by marketing. A consent-purpose failure is not repaired downstream by decoder accuracy, and this watch will treat any future decoding claim that rests on this corpus as a claim about a specific licence, not only about a model.

### NoöPunk synthesis

The noösphere does not begin with a satellite or a psionic awakening. It begins with a headband bought for insomnia, a policy paragraph that never mentioned models, and a training run that cannot be undone — wearable → de-identified corpus → brain foundation model → transferable weights → a market that prices the sleeper's night. That chain is cyberpunk in the precise sense the genre watch reserves the word for: not chrome, but **who holds the model and who is owed a waking**. The psionic half of the NoöPunk noösphere stays untouched by any of this; what thickens is the cybernetic half, and it thickens as property.

## 23:05 EET — OpenAI answers a local democratic veto with a contract template for compute campuses

**Publication date:** 5 November 2026 (company announcement). **Timeline categories:** AI/AGI infrastructure and its political economy; Cyberpunk (who bears the cost of compute, local consent, corporate quasi-sovereignty); European/global comparison pending; cybernetic Noösphere (physical substrate governance). **Not:** an AGI capability event, a legal instrument, or a European development.

**What is genuinely new.** OpenAI published a **National Community Compact** and a **Compute Campus Policy Blueprint**, two documents that convert its earlier site-by-site concessions into a standing template. The headline commitment is that **every future compute campus OpenAI owns and operates will have its own local compact**, and that the company will "pay the full cost of the electricity we use and the infrastructure needed to serve us", providing financial assurances before major spending so that other customers are protected if a project is delayed, reduced or cancelled. The blueprint is a set of proposed policies for community voice, cost protection and broader distribution of economic gains. The announcement explicitly builds on the January Stargate community commitments, the Georgia and Ohio agreements, and the **Grid Protectors Program** published on 2 November.

**Why it matters for NoöPunk.** Yesterday's finding #6 recorded Ohio voters imposing a democratic veto on hyperscale AI infrastructure; today the same company answers that contest with a *contract form*. That is a real shift in the political grammar of the noösphere's material base: the question stops being "will the campus be built" and becomes "what is written in the compact, who signs it, who can enforce it, and what happens when the company that wrote it changes its mind". A private actor offering a governance template to localities is neither sovereignty nor a commons. It is the cyberpunk arrangement in its most contemporary shape — infrastructure delivered under negotiated terms by an entity whose obligations are self-authored — and NoöPunk's factions should be able to argue about it without either celebrating or dismissing it.

**Established fact:** the publication, the two documents, the commitments as stated by OpenAI, and the stated continuity with Stargate, Georgia, Ohio and the Grid Protectors Program.

**Evidence status:** **primary corporate self-publication** — a documented announcement of intent, not independently verified behaviour, and not a legal obligation. **Not** evidence that any community gained a veto, that costs are actually borne as stated, that the blueprint has legislative uptake, or that AI infrastructure development has become democratically accountable. A funded commitment is a fact about a promise.

**Strongest sources:** [OpenAI, "Setting a new standard for responsible AI infrastructure", 5 November 2026](https://openai.com/index/setting-new-standard-responsible-ai-infrastructure); [National Community Compact (PDF)](https://cdn.openai.com/pdf/community-compact.pdf); [Compute Campus Policy Blueprint (PDF)](https://cdn.openai.com/pdf/compute-campus-policy-blueprint.pdf).

**Key uncertainty / next test:** whether compacts are published in full, whether they create enforceable rights or arbitration, whether local governments can refuse or amend, whether financial assurances are escrowed and auditable, and whether other developers adopt the form or undercut it. Watch state legislatures and public utility commissions rather than the company's own page.

## 23:05 EET — A preprint argues that simulated multitudes lack the one thing that makes cooperation real: stakes

**Publication date:** submitted 3 November 2026; accepted (non-archival) at the SocialAgent Workshop, NeurIPS 2026; verified directly in this watch. **Timeline categories:** AGI/ASI research and its methods; cybernetic Noösphere (collective machine behaviour); the epistemology of social simulation. **Not:** a consciousness or psi item, and not a capability threshold.

**What is genuinely new.** Justin Downes, Robby Milletich and Newel Hirst's *Same Method, Different Task: What the Bitter Lesson Does Not License in Social Simulation* makes a narrow, checkable argument: in every case Sutton cites, the general method was scaled **against the same task** the domain engineering was solving, and social simulation substituted across tasks instead, replacing hand-built agents with "an artifact optimized for predicting text". Their sharper claim is that "prediction is a solitary act: the predictor bears no consequences from the interaction it models", so that cooperation, defection and norm enforcement — "phenomena of interested parties" — do not exist for a disinterested observer at any scale. They show that excess cooperation predating alignment training, collective agreement without individual fidelity, and population homogeneity all follow from this account, then report a **540-agent pilot with pre-specified hypotheses**: consequences reshape strategies in **two of three model families but never produce human-like heterogeneity**.

**Why it matters for NoöPunk.** Both NoöPunk's social simulation and LaclauGPT's agent work treat LLM populations as models of a multitude. This paper names the artefact: a stake-free population that "self-organises" is reporting a property of text prediction, and a population that gains strategy under a scoreboard without gaining plurality is precisely the "people" that the multitude is defined against. Homogeneity should therefore be read as an artefact marker rather than as consensus — a methodological rule NoöPunk can state in its own simulation design without adopting any of the paper's ontology.

**Established fact:** the preprint, its abstract, its venue acceptance, its 540-agent pilot and the two-of-three result as reported by the authors.

**Evidence status:** **scientific preprint, workshop-accepted, not peer-reviewed** in the archival sense, and a bounded pilot rather than a general result. It is **not** evidence about human societies, about machine consciousness, or about any timeline beat. Its value to this watch is methodological.

**Strongest source:** [arXiv:2611.05717](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05717).

**Cross-project note:** PCM's [5 November report](https://github.com/TomiToivio/Panpsychic_Cyborg_Multitude/blob/main/docs/reports/2026-11-05.md) independently curated this preprint and gave it the same reading. ### *There Is No Antimemetics Division* — qntm (novel; expanded edition; UK and rest-of-world paperback released 1 October 2026)

**What it is.** The expanded print novel built from qntm's SCP serial, in which an organisation tries to contain entities that are erased from memory as a condition of being perceived. Its own publisher's line states the premise without ornament: humanity is under assault by "antimemes — ideas that attack memory, identity, and the fabric of reality itself". The book's governing rule is that a record of the threat cannot survive contact with a mind; the Division works through mnestic drugs, written protocols and people who cannot be told what they are for.

**Release state.** Not a new work. The expanded hardcover was published in North America on 11 November 2025 (ISBN 9780593983751); the **UK and rest-of-world paperback was released on 1 October 2026** (Penguin), and a signed, numbered limited edition from Conversation Tree Press is announced for late 2026 or early 2027. This entry is a **new-edition catch**, not a 5 November release, and it is reported here because the lane had not previously logged the book at all.

**The specific affinity, in one disputable sentence.** It is paradigm-break fiction in which **the world's rules break at the level of the record**, not of the protagonist's life: an entity whose defining property is that knowledge of it cannot persist forces an institution to treat its own memory as the contested terrain — which is the NoöPunk noösphere's failure mode stated as horror rather than as cosmology.

**What NoöPunk could take, and where it diverges.** Take the *institutional-memory hazard*: a mechanic, or simply a table rule, in which a piece of knowledge must be re-derived each session or it is unavailable, and in which the cost of preserving it is paid in the bodies of the people who carry it. That is portable to the crossover game's Psyche layer without any psi, and it is a better model of ontological shock than most fiction manages, because the shock is procedural and repeatable rather than revelatory. Diverge: the enemy is entity-shaped; there is no claim that mind is part of the physics, no noösphere, no psi. It is an epistemology thriller, and its politics are bureaucratic rather than emancipatory.

**Established fact:** author, imprint, ISBN, the November 2025 hardcover date, the 1 October 2026 UK/rest-of-world paperback, the announced limited edition, and the publisher's premise text quoted above.

**Evidence status:** primary — publisher and author pages. This watch has **not read the novel**; the affinity is argued from the published premise and the book's well-documented serial structure, and it is fiction, therefore zero evidence about the world.

**Sources:** https://qntm.org/consumer ; https://qntm.org/antimemetics ; https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/783041/there-is-no-antimemetics-division-by-qntm ; https://conversationtreepress.com/pages/antimemetics-division-qntm

### *Reapers* — Anthony Ryan (novel; Blackstone, released 3 November 2026)

**What it is.** A post-Glitch novel: neural implants that were "supposed to be the savior of humanity" malfunctioned five years ago, killing many of their hosts and leaving others as drifters or killers. The protagonist is a convict turned **Contracted Collection Agent** with a quota — a number of implants he must harvest "by any means" to buy his freedom — working a city abandoned to the Glitched, where the Glitched begin behaving more human and strange symbols appear on walls.

**Release state.** Released 3 November 2026 in the publisher's listing for the month; a trade release from Blackstone, not an indie or crowdfunded title.

**The specific affinity, in one disputable sentence.** The unit of value is the **implant inside someone else's body**, and the protagonist's liberty is denominated in harvested hardware, so the novel's economy is explicitly a political economy of augmentation — who owns the device, who is owed the body it sits in, and who becomes unlegible once the device fails.

**What NoöPunk could take, and where it diverges.** Take the *quota*: a debt denominated in other people's cyberware is a cleaner engine for a cyberpunk scenario than a cash price, because every increment of progress requires a body and makes the player complicit rather than heroic. Diverge, on the available evidence: the Glitched are described in zombie-horror terms, the "more human" turn reads as a mystery hook rather than an ontological claim, and nothing in the publisher's description makes mind part of the physics. It is a body-horror survival premise that happens to sit on the correct political economy; whether the book does anything with that is unknown to this watch.

**Established fact:** author, publisher, release date and the plot and quota details as given in the publisher's own description.

**Evidence status:** **publisher copy only** — the weakest basis this lane accepts, and weaker than the review-level screening used for *Resonance* on 4 November. **No review has been read and the text has not been read.** Funded or released is a fact about publication, not about quality, and no claim is made that the book delivers the affinity it advertises.

**Source:** https://reactormag.com/new-science-fiction-books-november-2026/ (publisher-supplied description, Blackstone, 3 November 2026).
