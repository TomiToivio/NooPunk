The reporting describes alleged GRU cooperation with organised-crime intermediaries and compartmentalised surveillance, logistics and execution roles. No new completed assassination or direct interstate war is claimed. Distinguish allegations of intent and disrupted plots from verified attacks.

**Why it matters:** A possible escalation from infrastructure sabotage to personalised coercion of European industrial decision-makers. For PCM, it raises the concrete cost of resilient, distributed production and the role of private security. ## New high-signal finding 2 — Finland's proposed grid-priority law exposes the territorial politics of the AI infrastructure boom

**Event/publication date:** 11 October 2026, 17:35 EEST (Yle original reporting on the Finnish legislative proposal and municipal submissions).  
**Timeline categories:** Cyberpunk (AI/data-centre infrastructure, regional inequality, algorithmic economy's physical substrate); cybernetic Noösphere (precursor only); Finnish institutional governance.

Finland's government **proposes** moving large data centres toward the back of electricity-grid connection queues to reserve capacity for households and other industry and limit grid congestion/costs. Yle documents objections from municipalities and business organisations in eastern Finland. Local authorities argue that the proposed prioritisation could shift major data-centre investment west and north, where wind power is more available. The east faces a distinctive double constraint: military radar restrictions have limited wind construction near Russia, and the new grid-priority rule could then penalise the resulting power-generation deficit. Lappeenranta, Kouvola, Kitee and regional chambers have lodged criticisms or warned of investment loss.

**Evidence level:** **Established public policy proposal and documented municipal positions**. **Not enacted law**; claims of future investment loss are projections by interested regional actors, not demonstrated effects.

**Why it matters:** A particularly clear Finnish instance of the cybernetic Noösphere's physical geography becoming an explicit distributional conflict: power allocation, national security, regional development and hyperscale computation intersect. This is an **independent thematic convergence** with the US Senate investigation of data-centre grid-cost externalities already logged on 11 October. The underlying policies are different; do **not** describe them as independent verification of the same claim.

**Source:** Yle original reporting, 11 October: https://yle.fi/a/74-20250905

**Next discriminating evidence:** enacted statutory text, queue rules issued by grid operators, measurable changes in connection offers, regional investment decisions and electricity prices.
