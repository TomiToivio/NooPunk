## Google Threat Intelligence documents operational autonomous cyberattack frameworks

**Publication:** 16 November 2026. **Categories:** rogue/autonomous AI misuse; cyberwarfare; cyberpunk; AI-mediated political influence; cybernetic Noösphere (adversarial infrastructures). **Evidence:** **established publication of a first-party threat-intelligence report; credible directly observed incident-response and telemetry findings, with operator attribution and generalization subject to the reporting organization's visibility.** This is **not** evidence of AGI or an autonomous AI independently choosing to attack.

Google Threat Intelligence Group and Mandiant report that multiple actors are deploying multi-agent offensive frameworks during actual intrusions. GTIG says it directly observed CAIRN and PentAGI in the wild; CAIRN was used against targets including the US aerospace sector. The report identifies an exposed `exploit_agent` staging server configured to continuously compare patches, identify vulnerabilities, draft exploits and execute them without human intervention, with victim telemetry suggesting an interest in French media/entertainment and education sites. It also documents commercial AI-enabled influence-operation platforms, including a proposed architecture to manage over 100,000 synthetic social-media accounts. That is an architectural intent/capability claim, **not evidence that 100,000 accounts were successfully operated**. Google reports adversaries using LLMs to produce synthetic media, localized personas and distribution automation; it explicitly cautions that higher-quality output does not establish greater persuasive impact.

**Why it matters:** This is a stronger real-world NoöPunk signal than a generic agentic-AI product launch: cybernetic collectives are now documented in adversarial operations. It joins machine-speed attack chaining, private AI infrastructure, synthetic political discourse and the economics of compromised model access. The underlying intrusions occurred over the preceding quarter; the *new event* is the 16 November evidence-rich publication, not a claimed 17 November attack.

**Primary source:** [GTIG, 16 November 2026, “Agentic Threat Activity Crosses a Significant Threshold”](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/threat-intelligence/agentic-threat-activity-crosses-a-significant-threshold). **Uncertainties:** full independent access to telemetry, prevalence across all threat actors, how autonomous each deployed workflow was, effectiveness of influence campaigns and extent of direct human supervision.

#### "Of All Who Dream" — Adelehin Ijasan (*Lightspeed* 195, August 2026; free online; read in full) — late catch

**What it is.** A short story in the August 2026 issue, free to read. A maths teacher travels home to his ancestral village in the hills of Idanre and asks his grandmother Yeye to help him make a god. Yeye — tattooed in a script older than hieroglyphs, reading intentions without telephones — can enter what the story calls "the miasma that was the collective mind" in minutes; he cannot, and needs mushrooms. The first step of the craft is stated flatly: "everything was connected, that consciousness was prior to matter", and the collective mind "had had many names: the astral plane, Akashik, the dream world". They descend past his own feeding thought-forms, past a valley of forgotten gods cannibalising each other, and strike a deal with "the Algorithm" — "a new kind of god borne of modern technology", once Anansi — who runs the marketplace where gods meet believers and takes 20 per cent. They take a starving god from the trough, name it Tatū, and the story ends with it feeding.

**Release state.** **Released and free** — *Lightspeed* Issue 195, August 2026, with an author spotlight published alongside it. Note the shelf: *Lightspeed* ran it in its **fantasy** slot, not its science-fiction slot. This is a late catch; the story is three months old and had not been logged.

**The specific affinity, in one disputable sentence.** The story asserts consciousness as prior to matter as the working axiom of a craft, and then gives belief a metabolism and an owner: gods are attention-fed entities that starve, and a platform that used to be Anansi now holds the monopoly on feeding them at a 20 per cent commission.

**What NoöPunk could take, and where it diverges.** Take **the 20 per cent**. NoöPunk's Noösphere is usually described as a layer to be entered; this story supplies the missing invoice — revelation has a distribution channel, and the channel takes a cut. That single number does more political work than a page of cyberpunk chrome, because it converts a metaphysics into a rent relationship. Take **gods as infrastructure with maintenance costs**: they must be fed, they cannibalise each other when they cannot be, and the unexamined mind leaks "little suckling thought-forms" that feed on it — a picture of the Noösphere as an ecology with parasites, not a clean field. Take **sigils as public keys**: the gods whose sigils are already public (the cross, the star, the crescent) cannot be wielded by one person, which is a compact doctrine of legitimacy and scale that NoöPunk's faction politics could use directly — a shared symbol is strong precisely because no single node can operate it alone. Take **two grades of access**: Yeye dissolves the ego in minutes, the narrator needs psychedelics and still cannot; psi here has a training ladder and a class gradient without ever becoming a talent tree. Diverge: it is fantasy ontology, gods are entities rather than aspects of one mind, and there are no institutions — the Algorithm is a suave man in a purple suit rather than a governance apparatus, which is exactly the abstraction NoöPunk would have to build where the story stops. It is also, on its own terms, a story about a man's appetite for a god of his own, not about a commons; NoöPunk's problem is what a multitude does with the same craft.

**Established fact:** venue, issue, date, free access and the fantasy-slot placement; the author's own statement that panpsychism and gnosticism "began to intersect" in the writing, and that the thought-form premise came from treating belief as the energy that sustains a god — all from the spotlight. The plot, the collective-mind passage, the "consciousness was prior to matter" line, the Algorithm, the 20 per cent, Tatū — all read in the published text.

**Evidence status:** **full-text level; fiction.** Zero evidence about psi, gods, platforms or the timeline. A fictional rent on belief is not a finding about real platforms.

**Source:** https://www.lightspeedmagazine.com/fiction/of-all-who-dream/ ; spotlight: https://www.lightspeedmagazine.com/nonfiction/author-spotlight-adelehin-ijasan/

#### "Home Visit" — Madeleine Vigneron (*Strange Horizons*, 16 November 2026; 4,221 words; free; read in full)

**What it is.** The Monday fiction in this week's issue. The evacuation ships have left Earth and everyone left behind knows they were not eligible. The narrator keeps a municipal 911 desk running five days a week although no services remain to dispatch, answering calls, writing addresses in a notebook stolen from her absent supervisor, and then driving out to find the callers dead. She starts seeing the dead — a spectral couple still fighting over two stabbed bodies, a ghost baby, an old woman on the stairs reaching for a cigarette — and the first of them, the woman she failed to save and then robbed of petrol and a car battery, begins calling the dispatch line in silence. The ghost learns by practice: "First touch. Then voice. Sound touches the air. Through the phone, it was difficult." She cleans her own house, digs her own grave, snaps her own brittle fingers, and finally speaks: "Bury me." The narrator buries her, and the ghost declines to go up with the ships: "I am going down. Into the earth."

**Release state.** **Released and free** — *Strange Horizons*, 16 November 2026, current issue.

**The specific affinity, in one disputable sentence.** Persistence after death here is an effortful skill acquired on matter — touch, then voice, then a telephone line — and it is conditional on one living person's attention, which makes reception, not survival, the story's metaphysical primitive.

**What NoöPunk could take, and where it diverges.** Take **the channel model of the dead**. The ghost reaches the world only through an existing piece of infrastructure, and the difficult part is not existing but transmitting; for a setting whose Noösphere is "cybernetic + psionic", that is a discipline worth having — a mind persists where a channel persists, and a disconnected node is indistinguishable from an absent one. Take **the cost of being the receiver**. The narrator knows in advance which silent call is the dead woman, and calls it "an intimacy that I don't feel I qualify for"; that is the ethical core of any node that receives everything, and NoöPunk's panpsychic premise needs exactly this objection inside it. Take **legibility**. Every ghost in the story is someone outside every registry: no services, no records, no name — only a dispatcher's black notebook, which is the last archive on the planet. That is the cyberpunk clause ("who is legible to the state") rendered without a single implant. Take **burial against ascension**: the dead choose down rather than up, refusing "the path of the ships that abandoned us", which is a clean anti-upload cosmology a table can enact without a rules argument. Diverge: the story makes no claim that mind is fundamental. The ghosts are unexplained, folk-ontological, and treated as an ethical fact rather than a physics; the world-break is the premise, not the subject, and the subject is obligation. It clears this lane's bar narrowly, on the strength of a stated procedure for the dead's agency, and it should not be cited as fiction *for* the consciousness paradigm.

**Established fact:** venue, date, word count and free access; the plot, the dispatch setting, the quoted lines and the burial ending — all read in the published text. The story was **not** assessed for any real-world referent, because it has none.

**Evidence status:** **full-text level; fiction.** Zero evidence about death, persistence or communication with the dead.

**Source:** https://strangehorizons.com/wordpress/fiction/home-visit/
