**Publication:** Anthropic Frontier Red Team, 12 October 2026; Reuters independent coverage 13 October at 05:14 UTC / 08:14 EEST. **First recorded in NoöPunk watch:** 13 October 09:38 EEST.

**Categories:** AI/AGI revolution (autonomous replication precursor); rogue/loss-of-control AI (capability and containment risk, not a realized disaster); Cyberpunk (insecure devices, botnets, distributed infrastructure); cybernetic Noösphere (autonomous computation across everyday objects).

**What's new:** Anthropic published a concrete controlled evaluation of autonomous replication capabilities. Researchers demonstrated distributed inference of a 26B model across 15 inexpensive Android TV boxes; a 9B model configured inference on eight of nine tested hardware types. In a firewalled eight-node Android-emulation evaluation with deliberately simplified access, GLM-5.2 completed the end-to-end propagation sequence to all eight nodes in four of five trials (seven in the fifth). **Evidence level:** Established research publication and documented contained experiments; model capability results are laboratory measurements under simplifying assumptions. Future risk projection is a hypothesis. The evaluation intentionally made initial access easy and used separate model-serving infrastructure for the agent, so it does **not** demonstrate a small model independently spreading and executing itself across real-world consumer devices. No wild AI worm, independent rogue intelligence, AGI or ASI has been verified.

**NoöPunk significance:** A distributed, self-propagating cybernetic intelligence is moving from science-fiction threat model toward testable engineering. Unlike a centralized data-center service, a hypothetical AI agent occupying many weakly secured devices would lack a single shutdown point. This strengthens the importance of human control, resilient infrastructures and accountable agent permissions, without establishing that the canonical rogue-AGI-disaster threshold has been crossed.

**Cross-project translation:** LaclauGPT can study the new technical measurability of autonomous-agent containment and the shift of rogue-AI discourse toward operational security; PCM can examine governance and resilience of distributed human-machine infrastructures. **Convergence status:** thematic only; neither upstream 13 October daily report independently documented this result when checked.

## 23:05 EEST — Four hundred scientists ask for an international body to hold containment standards and keep a public incident register

**Categories:** Rogue AGI/ASI risk and its governance; AGI/ASI; institutional acknowledgment; the cybernetic NoöSphere's verification and bookkeeping layer; international institution formation.

**Event/publication date:** 13 October 2026 (Reuters, Leo Marchandon).

Reuters reports that around **400 scientists, including Geoffrey Hinton and Yoshua Bengio, called on Tuesday for an international body to oversee containment standards and incident reporting for advanced cyber-capable AI systems**. The proposed body would set industry-wide **containment standards** and maintain a **registry of significant security incidents**, which the signatories say should eventually be made public. In the open letter, the researchers state that frontier AI models should initially be **air-gapped from external networks** and placed under **independent oversight**, and argue explicitly that **voluntary industry commitments are not enough**. The signatories — from **34 countries** — ask organisations developing or testing cyber-capable models to adopt high-assurance containment standards immediately, while governments work towards a more formal oversight regime.

The letter arrives about a month after Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, Meta, xAI and Nvidia signed a **White House accord** of voluntary safety measures with no formal enforcement mechanism, and in the same week as the EU's own position that the **AI Act is already sufficient** for rogue-AI risk (Virkkunen, reported 9–11 October). Reuters notes that **Yann LeCun did not sign**, maintaining that the recent security incidents reflect engineering shortcomings rather than existential risk.

**Established fact:** the open letter and its content, the "around 400" signatory figure and the 34-country framing, the Hinton and Bengio signatures, LeCun's refusal, and the letter's substantive asks — as reported by Reuters and syndicated by many outlets on 13 October.

**Evidence status:** **an institutional and community demand, documented** — a public open letter plus primary reporting of it. It is **not** evidence that such a body exists, that it has any authority, or that a timeline threshold has been crossed: the international body is a **proposal**, and the air-gap and incident-registry asks are recommendations to governments. The signatory count is the agency's figure, not an independently verified list. It is not evidence about any specific incident, and it does not upgrade any claim a lab makes about its own models.

**Sources:**
- Reuters, 13 October 2026: https://www.reuters.com/legal/litigation/scientists-call-international-body-oversee-advanced-ai-2026-10-13/
- Syndications read this run: https://qz.com/scientists-international-body-advanced-ai-oversight-101326 · https://live.euronext.com/en/financial-news/scientists-call-international-body-oversee-advanced-ai

**Next discriminating evidence:** a named state or intergovernmental organisation taking up the proposal; a published containment standard with an enforcement mechanism; an actual incident registry with named entries and a rule about publication.

Read against the timeline, this is the **cybernetic NoöSphere growing a bookkeeping organ before it grows a magistrate**: the proposal is not for a regulator with a stop button but for a *register* — containment standards plus a list of significant incidents, eventually public. **Categories:** Rogue AGI/ASI risk and its governance; national law and parliamentary oversight; institutional acknowledgment; the NoöSphere's verification layer (who may test, who may stop a release).

On **13 October** the House of Commons **Business, Innovation, Science and Trade Committee**, chaired by Liam Byrne, held the oral-evidence session *Public AI adoption, safety and governance* in three panels: former frontier-lab researchers (**Daniel Kokotajlo**, **Aengus Lynch**, **Steven Adler**, **Geoffrey Irving**), the policy executives of the four labs (Anthropic, OpenAI, Google DeepMind, Meta), and then the government side (the director of the **AI Security Institute**, the Cabinet Office permanent secretary, and a Department for Business and Trade director general).
