## 03:09 EEST — AI existential-risk concern becomes mass public opinion

**Categories:** AGI / ASI governance + rogue-AI risk + Cyberpunk + cultural/political legitimation.

A newly surfaced Quinnipiac University national poll reports that **73% of U.S. adults are very or somewhat concerned that future AI systems could threaten human survival**. The same poll reports **91%** support for AI regulation, **86%** support mandatory safety standards even if they slow innovation, **81%** prioritize safety over innovation, **74%** report little or no trust in AI-company executives, and **72%** oppose AI data centres in their own communities. At the same time, **69%** say it is important for the United States to keep pace with China in AI development.

**What is genuinely new:** this is not another expert warning, lab forecast, or online x-risk subculture signal. Existential-risk language has become measurable mass public opinion, while the same respondents simultaneously express geopolitical-competition pressure. That makes the acceleration/safety contradiction itself politically salient.

**Evidence status:** established polling/public-opinion signal. It measures reported attitudes, not whether AI actually poses an extinction risk, whether AGI/ASI exists, or whether a rogue-AI catastrophe is imminent. Poll results are sensitive to wording, sampling, and contemporary news context.
