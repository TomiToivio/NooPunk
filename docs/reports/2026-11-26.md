#### *Warped Beyond Recognition* — Quadra / PARADISO (Mothership 1E module and psionics supplement; 32 pp. A5; third printing; print and PDF $28) — late catch, played-review level

**What it is.** A research vessel, the RSV Fidanza, has gone silent after experiments on six teenage test subjects, and the player characters go aboard while the Tannhäuser corporation wants its investment back. Each subject acquired powers through unprotected exposure to hyperspace, and several have been altered past the point of a human shape — machinery, other bodies, severed limbs and the ship itself have become extensions of them. The lead scientist was fitted with a morality inhibitor so that she would be ruthless enough to finish the work. Around this, Quadra supplies an open-ended psionics package that player characters can take up by undergoing the same process, plus a full soundtrack and a mixer app keyed to the ship's decks.

**Release state.** Kickstarted 5–22 February 2023 and raised $23,086, which the designer's own page reports as 1,154% of the funding goal; that page now reads "in its third printing", and print plus PDF are in stock at Tuesday Knight Games at $28. The 2026 development is not the rules but the selection: the module was announced as a *Mothership Select* title during Mothership Month 2026 (Ansible Uplink, 18 November 2026), placing it in the publisher's curated third-party line. The 5 November review is this pass's strongest evidence because its author ran the module at the table.

**The specific affinity, in one disputable sentence.** The module's claim is that psi is not a power a character has but a condition a body is put into — the six psychics got their abilities from unprotected hyperspace exposure and cannot be separated from the damage — so its psionics are priced in the same currency as its horror, and the body that holds them is a corporate asset with a recovery value.

**What NoöPunk could take, and where it diverges.** Four things, three of them mechanical.

- **Effect scale as a negotiation, not a table lookup.** Psionic use costs Stress, and "the Warden and player negotiat[e] the scale of an effect". That is a psi resolution NoöPunk does not have: magnitude is agreed at the table and paid for immediately, so a scene can scale from a whisper to a hull breach without a spell list. For a setting whose psi is meant to reorganise a scene rather than add a bonus, this is the cheapest available chassis.
- **Powers that look like where you were and what you wear.** The rules develop abilities "from environments and personalities", and the review notes the advice that patches and trinkets can inform them. That is an embodied, materialist account of psychic style: a faction's psi should look like its infrastructure. NoöPunk currently keys psi to ontology and institution; this keys it to kit, which is more playable.
- **Antagonists on the players' own stress track.** Each test subject has a six-step Sanity and Behaviour table, an initial rating and one driving goal — safety, purpose, autonomy — and confrontation worsens their condition while reassurance or assistance improves it. The review calls them "puzzle NPCs". NoöPunk's NHI adversaries are described from outside and have no such dial; this is the same fix *Black Ice Burning* suggested on 21 November, arrived at independently and with numbers attached.
- **The ownership clause stated as a balance sheet.** The corporation's motive is literal: the investment must be recovered. With the morality inhibitor, the module makes the political economy of augmentation an entry in the accounts rather than a theme.

Divergence: the psi is trauma-induced and exogenous — hyperspace, not a feature of the physics — and there is no Noösphere, no Earth and no disclosure question. The genre frame is horror, which makes containment the correct institutional response to a psychic; NoöPunk's contact layer is built to make that response the villain's. The module is also four years old, and what is new about it in 2026 is a printing and a publisher's taste, not a design.

**Established fact:** designer, editor (Fiona Maeve Geist), page count, format and price; the 2023 campaign dates and total; the third printing; the Mothership Select announcement of 18 November 2026; the Fidanza, the six test subjects and their hyperspace exposure; the Tannhäuser recovery motive and the morality inhibitor; the psionics package's Stress cost and negotiated scale; the six-step Sanity and Behaviour tables and the three named goals — the last four as reported by a reviewer who ran the module, not read in the text by this watch.

**Evidence status:** played-review level; game rules and fiction. Zero evidence about psi, hyperspace, corporate neuroscience or morality inhibitors. A rule that makes psychic power cost Stress is a design decision, not a finding about minds.

#### *Slow Elephants of Milan* — Ángel Bonomini, tr. Jordan Landsman (Transit Books, 11 August 2026; 152 pp., $18.95; twelve fictions; first English translation of the 1978 collection) — late catch, review level

**What it is.** A traveller in postwar Italy watches two elephants cross Milan in the fog and cannot stop writing about them, though he shows the reader none of what he writes; a couple on a road trip take an unplanned detour to Laar, a city of canals and palaces that may be no city at all and does not appear on the map; two men named Willoughby and Satié talk on a bridge about a blind boy and each finds the other gone. Bonomini was a contemporary of Borges and Bioy Casares, admired by them and mostly forgotten after his death; this is the second of his collections to reach English in Landsman's translation, after *The Novices of Lerna*.

**Release state.** Released 11 August 2026 in trade paperback by Transit Books, 152 pp., $18.95, ISBN 9798893380484; the twelve stories were first published in Spanish in 1978 and appear in English for the first time. Reviewed at length in Strange Horizons by Dawn Macdonald on 23 November 2026, and earlier in *Asymptote*'s August translation round-up. Not previously covered in this lane; the lateness belongs to the translation, not the review.

**The specific affinity, in one disputable sentence.** The collection's claim is that a paradigm break is not an event but a permanent condition of perception — nothing in these stories violates physics, and yet the world becomes less believable the more closely it is approached, so the only honest instrument is fog.

**What NoöPunk could take, and where it diverges.** Four things.

- **The anomaly no institution investigates.** Elephants in Milan are not a disclosure; nobody convenes a committee or files a report, and the story spends its energy on the narrator's merging with the world rather than on establishing a fact. That is a scene rule NoöPunk keeps needing: an anomalous event can be spent on changing a character's ontology instead of adding a faction to the map.
- **"Real magicalism" as a register for the pre-Fall timeline.** The review's phrase for Bonomini — nothing happens outside the laws of physics, and the ordinariness of existence is shown in its strangeness — names exactly the tonal problem of a 20XX Earth where the Noösphere is arriving but has not been announced. The setting's strangeness currently arrives with institutions attached; this is how it looks before anyone has named it.
- **Devotional vocabulary for a consciousness-fundamental claim.** The narrator's merging ends in a sentence NoöPunk's secular QIP register has no equivalent for: "everything, anything, anything that happens is nothing more than a veiled metaphor for God." The review's defence — that an experience of oceanic merging is reported by about half the population — is not evidence, but it is a licence: the setting's Contact religions will need this register, and it is better borrowed from a 1978 Argentine than invented badly.
- **A structure for an encounter book.** Twelve self-contained fictions joined by one recurring image rather than by a plot. A NoöPunk sourcebook of Noöspheric encounters could hold together the same way: an image that returns, no continuity of cast, no campaign spine.

Divergence: there is no psi, no Noösphere and no cyberpunk; the metaphysics is theological rather than physical, the merging is mystical rather than reproducible, and the politics is absent — nobody owns the elephants and no state registers the narrator. Its "God" is not a hypothesis about information, and the collection should not be cited as fiction for panpsychism. Its usefulness is formal and tonal, not evidential.

**Established fact:** translator, publisher, publication date, page count, price and ISBN; the 1978 Spanish original and the first English appearance; the twelve stories and the recurring elephants; the Laar episode and the hotel; "Conversation on the Bridge" and the two names; "The Journey" as the third-to-last entry; the quoted lines on writing, on God, and on the elephants' "specific and unacceptable barbarity"; the *Asymptote* and Strange Horizons reviews — all from the publisher's page and the review. **Not** established: the prose at length, or the collection's own handling of its ending; the book was not read.

**Evidence status:** review level; fiction. Nothing here documents oceanic merging, divine immanence or a perceptual paradigm break. A story in which reality becomes implausible under close inspection establishes only that implausibility can be written well.

#### Screened and excluded

- *Loss Protocol* — Paul McAuley (Gollancz; UK paperback 12 November 2026). Near-future England, a wildlife ranger, and a cabal of "deep dreamers" reviving a cult belief that the world can be healed by collective dreams fuelled by psilocybin. The affinity holds only if the novel affirms the cult's cosmology, and the one long review read (Niall Harrison, *Locus*) treats that material as part of the novel's "eerie but somewhat ramshackle conspiracism" and as its dream register — a reading of method, not a premise. Held until a text or a second review establishes that mind is part of its physics.
- *Galaxy Black III: Star Travel and the Secrets of the Sub-ether* — Dreaming Gynoid (DCC RPG toolkit). It carries the lane's exact cosmology: the Noosphere as "the psychic plane of information" exerting pressure on the Sub-ether, persistent Idea Gods of the Inner Core, and a rule that nothing is ever forgotten. It is also back catalogue rather than a 2026 release — its storefront gives no date, and a bundle-submission feed places it in the designer's older catalogue — so it is held for a future printing rather than spent as news.
- *XENOSOMNIA* — Maddie Elder (one-page, two-player GMless game; a sentient psychic planet against the administrator sent to strip it). Psychedelic, planetary, and the border between two consciousnesses breaks down as a mechanic; it is a 2025 jam game with no state change.
- *Lightbreakers* — Aja Gabel (Riverhead 2025; UK paperback 5 November 2026). Consciousness and quantum physics are the husband's "unprovable faith", and the Janus machine folds memory rather than mind; a grief-and-time novel, not a panpsychic one.
- *Line of Descent* — Adam Oyebanji (DAW, November 2026). A generation-ship crime novel with a cult listening for a silent Earth; no psi and no noöspheric cosmology.
- "The Tongue That Makes the Mountain Sing" — *Lightspeed* (from the Dartmouth Speculative Fiction Project anthology, MIT Press 2027). Compelled truth-telling as colonial magic: a curse on a tongue, not mind in the physics.
- "Ticket to a New Body" — Malcolm MacDougall (*Baffling* 25, October 2026). A fairy-ticket body change; body politics without psi.

Held without state change: *Not Yet Gods* (Kaya Press) still has no established release — retail listings give 3 November 2026, 26 November 2026, 29 December 2026 and 26 January 2027, while the publisher's own page still says July 2025 — and *City of Espers*, *ULTRADELVE*, *Project Psyche*, *SOPHONT* and *A Most Brilliant Spark* remain where they were put.
