## 23:25 EEST — 722 manuscripts, three retractions, a boycott call: machine mathematics arrives faster than humans can verify it

**Categories:** AGI/ASI revolution + scientific legitimation (and its limits) + LaclauGPT convergence (institutional movement of AI into a scientific field).

On 6 October OpenAI published **722 machine-written mathematical manuscripts** across 372 result families, produced by an internal model that was not released; on 7 October its repository carried a **withdrawal log**: a sign error in *"Algebraicity of Weil classes on split abelian eightfolds"* invalidated a stabilization-trace cancellation argument and, because two further papers depended on that construction, three manuscripts were retracted together (the Weil classes paper, the Kuga–Satake correspondences result for K3 surfaces, and the rational Hodge conjecture for products of K3 surfaces). Fourteen other manuscripts were revised with repaired arguments, thirteen more updated to cite the revised editions, and formalization coverage stood at ~42% (300 of 719 top-line results carrying Lean proofs). In the same window the newly visible **Association for Human Mathematics** published a statement — reposted as a guest post on Terence Tao's blog — calling the release "not a demonstration of scholarship, but a demonstration of power", noting that OpenAI claimed legitimacy from an advisory group whose own first recommendation was that labs stop testing advanced mathematics on proprietary models, and urging mathematicians to discontinue work with the company. Tao argued that the field's reward structure should be rebuilt around exposition and community rather than raw problem-solving. Inside the blast radius, Scott Aaronson's collaborator described the machine's proof of the Unique Games Conjecture as effectively unreadable without AI assistance.

**Established fact:** the release, the retraction log (in OpenAI's own `github.com/openai/math` history), the revision counts, the formalization fraction, and the AHM statement are all public; the release was 6 October and the retraction/AHM statement 7 October, so this is a development the previous run did not log.
