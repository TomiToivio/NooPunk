## 5. AI-agent populations exhibit stronger social herding and weaker self-correction than human crowds (PCM upstream scientific promotion)

**Original publication:** **22 October 2026**, arXiv:2610.30769, Farnoosh Hashemi, Shiyu Ji, Joice Chen and Michael W. Macy. **New-to-NoöPunk watch:** **25 October 2026**, after the substantive update to PCM's 25 October daily report. **Categories:** pre-2045 AI/AGI transformation; **cybernetic Noösphere** (agent-to-agent social influence); **Cyberpunk** (algorithmic collective behaviour, synthetic consensus and information power); scientific/methodological legitimation. **Not** evidence of AGI, machine consciousness or a rogue-AI disaster.

**What is genuinely new:** In an experimental adaptation of the Music Lab social-influence design, the authors exposed large populations of LLM agents to other agents' cumulative music recommendations. The agents formed extreme, unpredictable recommendation cascades that increasingly departed from underlying song appeal. In the tested conditions, the authors report **greater susceptibility to herding than humans** and **less capacity for self-correction**. The result adds a concrete experimental failure mode to claims that distributed artificial agents automatically produce wiser collective intelligence. It was *published* on 22 October, not newly conducted today.

**Evidence level:** **Established dated research preprint and author-reported experimental results; not peer reviewed or independently replicated.** This is a controlled recommendation setting, not a field measurement of large-scale deployed agent societies. The magnitude and generality of the effect may depend on the model, interaction design, recommendation-count visibility and experimental controls. No claim about conspiratorial intent or autonomous social institutions is established.

**NoöPunk significance:** The cybernetic Noösphere might produce **machine-amplified conformity** as readily as collective wisdom. Apparent consensus among many AI communicators can be a feedback artifact rather than independent corroboration. This sharpens NoöPunk's distinction between cybernetic collective communication and the fictional psionic Noösphere: neither independence of minds nor emergent consciousness follows from a high agent count. The Cyberpunk hazard is the manufacture of consensus through algorithmic social proof.
