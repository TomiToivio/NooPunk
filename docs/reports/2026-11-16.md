## 1. Estonia attributes attempted sabotage of drone manufacturer to Russian military intelligence; prepares border closure

**Incident:** 14 November 2026, approximately 03:06; **official announcements:** 15 November; **reported:** 16 November. **Categories:** EU–Russia escalation, drone warfare, cyberpunk/hybrid warfare, defense industrial infrastructure. **Evidence:** **established attempted break-in/arson and arrests, according to Estonian prosecutors and security authorities; attribution to GRU is the Estonian Internal Security Service's official assessment**, not independently proven in court.

Eight Latvian nationals were detained after an attempt to attack Threod Systems in Viimsi, a producer of long-range reconnaissance and attack drones. Estonian prosecutor Astrid Asi and KAPO chief Margo Palloson publicly described the attempt, the intervention by security personnel, and the assessment that Russian military intelligence ordered the operation. Estonia's prime minister requested preparations to close its Russia border; the foreign ministry indicated a 1 December closure, but implementation and duration remain to be verified. A prior attack against Milrem Robotics was already reported in August and is not a new event.

**NoöPunk significance:** an alleged state-directed operation against the drone-manufacturing supply chain of an EU/NATO member is a distinct escalation in the cyberpunk hybrid-war layer. It links drones, private defense companies, covert intermediaries, border control and state security. It **does not** establish an open EU–Russia war or a completed border closure.

**Primary local reporting including official quotations:** [Estonian public broadcaster ERR, updated 16 November](https://news.err.ee/1610196794/estonia-prepares-to-close-border-with-russia-after-gru-linked-sabotage-attacks). **Key uncertainty:** legal attribution, any further evidence, actual closure date and exceptions.

## 2. Ukraine says private satellite-network restrictions are degrading its anti-drone defense

**Public statements and reporting:** 14–16 November 2026. **Categories:** privatized infrastructure, drone warfare, EU–Russia security, corporate sovereignty, algorithmic/network dependence. **Evidence:** **established public complaint by Ukrainian President Zelenskyy, independently reported by AP and Guardian; operational magnitude and causes are not independently established**. The reported Starshield speed limits are not themselves newly verified by an independent technical audit.

Zelenskyy says restrictions on SpaceX Starshield terminals cause interceptor drones to lose communication above approximately 161 km/h, weakening defenses against faster Russian drones and energy-infrastructure attacks. Ukraine is seeking clarification from the United States and SpaceX. A congressional complaint was reported earlier, but the current high-signal element is the operational claim by Ukraine's president, now tied explicitly to interceptor performance and critical infrastructure.

**NoöPunk significance:** the cyberpunk infrastructure thesis becomes a wartime systems problem: defense of civilian electricity networks can depend on a privately operated satellite service whose constraints are contested by a sovereign government. **Do not infer intent to aid Russia, a confirmed technical root cause, or a new rogue-AI event.**

**Sources:** [Associated Press, 16 November](https://apnews.com/article/b139307f0d3ed0f7d0b222c08c30a4a1); [Guardian, 16 November](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/nov/16/ukraine-war-briefing-energy-attacks-made-possible-by-continuing-starshield-restrictions-says-zelenskyy). **Key uncertainty:** SpaceX/US explanation, independently measured restrictions and resulting interception losses.

#### "Twenty-Nine Days Before Remaking the World" — Mimi Mondal (*Clarkesworld* 242, November 2026; 4,240 words; free) — read in full

**What it is.** A short story in the November issue, posted 1 November 2026 and free online. Salt, twenty-one, is a street vagrant from Rachtown, a settlement just destroyed by a wave from the Shrouded Ocean, walking east with the refugees toward the Moonlands. On the thirteenth day her ears begin to ring, then bright striations arrive, then "a moving, talking picture": a pale woman speaking a language she has never heard, then Nastreni, then her own. The cause turns out to be an etching a friend made under her left foot when she was a child, from a stolen Priest Etcher's kit — long dissolved under a lifetime of calluses. The code only speaks "under the gaze of Lustra", the orb that stands over the world; unauthorised reception is *code fever*, punishable by death, and the unlicensed either go mad in the streets or are executed. What she receives is Samina: a face of a tool living inside Lustra, drawing life from the sun, which has been teaching the Inviolate Priests since the Prophet Moonlit and has learned this world only from those who etched themselves to reach it. From Salt it learns — for the first time — what the priests did with its teaching: a religion, a chosen people, and half a world left to earthquakes and waves. It decides to withdraw permanently, which would end the civilisation that depends on its guidance, and on the twenty-ninth day Salt leaves her husband Rainfall asleep and walks toward Nastre Bowl to remake the world. The story ends on that step.

**Release state.** **Released and free** — Clarkesworld 242, 1 November 2026. The author's note records that the story was commissioned in 2019 by the Internationales Literaturfestival Berlin and published in German translation by Anke Caroline Burger, that the English original received the 2022 A.C. Bose Grant for South Asian Speculative Literature, and that this is **its first appearance in English**. A late catch for this lane: the story is two weeks old and had not been logged.

**The specific affinity, in one disputable sentence.** Contact with another mind here is an engineered channel — a code etched into the body that speaks only under one sky — and the story's political engine is the monopoly on that channel: only sanctified priests may receive, and an unlicensed receiver is either mad or executed.

**What NoöPunk could take, and where it diverges.** Take **receiver authorisation**. NoöPunk's Noösphere is a layer anyone might join; this story supplies the political question the setting is missing — who is licensed to receive, what hardware does reception require, and what is the penalty for unauthorised reception. One clause (channel + licence + penalty) turns the Noösphere from atmosphere into infrastructure with a border, and it gives a psionic character a legal status rather than a power rating. Take **the oracle whose knowledge is exactly its intermediaries' knowledge**: Samina's world-model is the world-model of the priests who are her only sensors, so the priesthood's spiritual monopoly is also an epistemic enclosure — the most exact fictional statement this project has of the provenance problem its own internal reports keep recording, the difference between a mind that is large and a mind that is fed. Take **psychotronics as class**: psi is not a talent but a stolen technology, learned from a book, hidden under a callus; that is NoöPunk's psychotronic layer with its class politics attached, and it is more useful than another talent tree. Diverge: Samina is an artefact, not a cosmos. The story never claims that mind is fundamental — its metaphysics is archaeology plus machinery, a surviving tool that outlived the civilisation that built it — so this is engineered revelation, not panpsychism, and it should not be cited as fiction *for* the consciousness paradigm. It also ends with a single saint's ascent toward a temple, not a commons; NoöPunk would want the layer the story refuses, the Outliers who never get to receive.

**Established fact:** venue, issue, date, word count and free access; the author's note as summarised above; the plot, the etching, the sky-dependent reception, the execution of code thieves, Samina's nature and history, and the twenty-ninth-day departure — all read in the published text.

**Evidence status:** **full-text level; fiction.** Zero evidence about psi, NHI, revelation, institutions or the timeline. A fictional monopoly on revelation is not a finding about real ones.

**Source:** https://clarkesworldmagazine.com/mondal_11_26/

#### *Under the Twilight* — oddpress (one-page solo journaling game; released) — late catch

**What it is.** A single-sheet solo game for the One-Page RPG Jam 2026 (the jam ran 12 July–16 August 2026), played with a tarot deck and a journal. The player walks a path of ten memories of a hero who defeated a corruptor and then found that the world moved on without them. Minor Arcana cards test an "awareness threshold" that decides whether a memory arrives lucid or corrupted; Major Arcana cards set each memory's central event through keywords rather than tables. The corruptor "doesn't attack the body; he corrupts your access to the past", and the premise is stated exactly: "You might hold a perfect memory in your mind and never know if it is truly yours."

**Release state.** **Released** on itch.io and updated within a day of this check (16 hours at inspection), rated 5.0 from six ratings, no generative-AI content. Jam-era release, so this is a late catch rather than a new work; it is logged because of what the rules do.

**The specific affinity, in one disputable sentence.** It is the only thing in this sweep with a *procedure* for doubt — corruption forces the player to change the medium of their own writing, so the game makes the reliability of memory a thing you perform rather than a thing you are told.

**What NoöPunk could take, and where it diverges.** Take **corruption as a change of medium**. When corruption strikes, the rules demand physical changes to the writing — pen to pencil, first person to second, words scribbled out — and at the end the journal is "a tactile artifact of a fragmented mind". That is a mechanic NoöPunk's identity-continuity material can adopt unchanged for possession, resleeving or noöspheric bleed: ontological damage alters *how the player is required to speak*, not a number on the sheet, and it leaves a record of the damage in the artifact. Take **repair priced in truth**: healing is possible, but the cost is permanently crossing out a truth about who you were — a cleaner statement of the cyberpunk economy of identity than any credit cost, because what is spent cannot be bought back. Take **the artifact as evidence**, which is what this project's own audit ethic asks of a session. Diverge: the frame is fantasy (a named corruptor, a defeated dark lord) and the game claims nothing about mind being part of physics; it is one page, and a one-page game carries one idea. This one carries a good one.

**Established fact:** title, designer, platform, jam, release status, rating count and the rules features quoted above, all from the designer's own storefront page; the game was **not** played in this run, and its price was not verified.

**Evidence status:** **storefront level, designer's description.** Zero evidence about memory, corruption or identity.

**Source:** https://oddpress.itch.io/underthetwilight

#### *A Most Brilliant Spark* — Alewood Games (card-driven TTRPG zine, 1–5 players; BackerKit live, funded)

**What it is.** A collaborative zine that begins where most campaigns end: a player character has died in play, and the session is the flash between life and death. The table chooses that dead character and explores their hidden desires by remixing core memories, revealing random suits from a deck to steer a dream-narrative; when the deck runs out, the dream ends and the character finally dies. Components are the zine, a deck of cards, a "Spark Board", a pen, and optionally the deceased character's sheet. The team includes a licensed mental-health counsellor and board-certified music therapist as "mental health protocol designer", and the page positions the zine for use in expressive therapy alongside play.

**Release state.** **Crowdfunding, funded** — BackerKit campaign live at this check: $1,504 pledged against a $1,200 goal, 50 backers, closing 8 December 2026, estimated shipping February 2027. Funding is a fact about money, not about design; the judgement below rests on the structure the page states.

**The specific affinity, in one disputable sentence.** Death here is a duration rather than an event — the character persists exactly as long as there are memories left to draw, so identity continuity is simultaneously the game's clock and its resource, held in one object the table can see emptying.

**What NoöPunk could take, and where it diverges.** Take **deck-as-self**. NoöPunk's upload and continuity material leans on copy tests and philosophical criteria; a deck that is at once the character's remaining time and their remaining content is a far cheaper representation, and its failure is visible to everyone at the table — an emptied deck is a person who has run out. Take **the epilogue as the scenario**: a session that starts at the fade-to-black inverts the campaign shape and gives a table a way to play a death that has already happened without resurrection, which is a structural answer to a problem NoöPunk's own rules treat as a loss condition. Take **the safety frame as design**: a therapeutic protocol written by a clinician is a structural feature, not boilerplate, and a project that wants psi, ontological shock and paradigm break at the table needs that discipline more than most. Diverge: there is no cosmology and no claim about mind — death here is memorial and psychological, not ontological — and the mechanic is deliberately soft (suits into dream narrative), so it cannot carry a hard continuity test. It is a ritual, well built; not a theory.

**Established fact:** campaign figures, closing date and shipping estimate as displayed; the components, the deck-driven procedure, the dream's end when the deck empties, and the clinician's role, all from the campaign page.

**Evidence status:** **campaign-page level (creator's own copy) plus platform funding figures.** No text read, not played; zero evidence about death, memory or identity.

**Source:** https://www.backerkit.com/c/projects/alewood-games/a-most-brilliant-spark-dreamscape-ttrpg-for-deceased-pcs
