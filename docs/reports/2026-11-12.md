## 23:10 EET — Anthropic tells investors it has met its own AI R&D acceleration threshold, days before pricing a $1.4 trillion IPO

**Categories:** AI/AGI; recursive self-improvement; frontier-lab governance and securities disclosure; AI political economy. **Signal type:** primary regulatory filing, an independent evaluator's published assessment, and financial reporting on the offering. **Not:** evidence of AGI, ASI, autonomous self-improvement, machine consciousness, or a rogue-AGI incident.

On **10 November**, in the amended S-1/A that set the price range for its initial public offering, Anthropic wrote that it had **made a determination that it has met** the AI research and development capability threshold in its Responsible Scaling Policy. The sentence is precise about its own basis, and the precision is the story:

> "Because the AI research and development capability threshold under our Responsible Scaling Policy applies where we 'observe or expect' a specified degree of accelerated progress, we made a determination that we have met this threshold based on what we expect from future models and our overall rate of progress, rather than on what we have observed from current models. We continue to assess the underlying data."

The threshold is the one triggered when AI is doing enough of the company's own research to roughly **double** the pace at which its models improve; the policy's consequence is a stronger affirmative safety case, not a shutdown. The same filing says the company is "evaluating the implications" and will release further reports. It also adds a risk factor that reads like a description of the governance problem itself: as Claude writes "a large and growing share" of Anthropic's code and increasingly performs its research, "including our safety research", the organisation "may lose visibility across certain processes in a way that may become difficult for any individual or group of humans to understand and control."

The offering context, as reported rather than audited here: 825 million shares at **$70–78**, a fully diluted valuation of up to about **$1.4 trillion**, ticker **ANT**, trading possibly from 19 November, alongside a **$26 billion** private placement by seven technology companies including Nvidia, Google, AMD, Microsoft and TSMC.

**The external check.** METR's assessment of **5 November**, covering access from 12 August to 29 October, estimates that Anthropic's benchmark scores (AECI) have been growing at **1.9x** the pre-October-2025 rate (95% CI **1.5–2.8x**) — roughly two years of historical progress in one year — and that the company is "likely improving the capabilities of their models on well-specified tasks at around twice the rate" of March 2024 to October 2025. METR guesses "a majority (perhaps ≈80%)" of the current acceleration is attributable to AI, finds no convincing bottleneck in algorithmic progress, training compute, experiment compute or data over the next 12 months, and is explicit that Anthropic **redacted a majority of the evidence METR found most informative**. METR's own headline caution: the public "has to rely on METR's judgment much more than would be ideal".

**Established fact:** the quoted filing language, the determination and its explicitly prospective basis ("expect", not "observe"), the safety-case consequence, the visibility risk factor, and the further-reports commitment — read directly in the SEC filing. The IPO price range and placement terms are as reported by Yahoo Finance and other outlets, not verified against the filing's arithmetic here. METR's estimates, confidence interval, attribution guess, bottleneck finding and redaction disclosure are read directly in METR's published assessment.

**Evidence status:** **primary securities filing and primary independent-evaluator report, both read directly; the offering terms are financial reporting.** This is **not** evidence that a model autonomously designs its successors, that recursive self-improvement in the strong sense has occurred, that an AGI or ASI exists, or that any loss of control has happened. A company-defined policy gate has been recorded as met on the basis of *expected* future models; that is an institutional act, not a measurement. METR's number is a trend estimate from a benchmark instrument with wide uncertainty, and its most probative evidence was withheld. The threshold determination and the IPO are the same document, which is a fact about disclosure incentives and not about capability.

**Sources:** [Anthropic S-1/A, SEC EDGAR, filed 10 November 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026072484/anthropicpbc-sx1ax2.htm); [METR, "Independent Assessment of AI R&D Acceleration at Anthropic in October 2026", 5 November 2026](https://metr.org/blog/2026-11-05-anthropic-ai-rd-acceleration-assessment/); [METR review of the August 2026 risk report](https://metr.org/blog/2026-11-05-rd-section-anthropic-risk-report-august-2026-review/); [Yahoo Finance, "Anthropic says a key AI safety alarm has been triggered, days before its IPO", 11 November 2026](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/article/anthropic-says-a-key-ai-safety-alarm-has-been-triggered-days-before-its-ipo-171519615.html); [Anthropic Responsible Scaling Policy](https://www.anthropic.com/responsible-scaling-policy).

**Next verification:** the "further reports" the filing promises; whether an affirmative safety case is actually published and by whom it can be checked; whether the determination is ever re-based on observed rather than expected progress; whether independent evaluators receive unredacted access; and whether anything changes in governance, deployment or human-review policy as a result. A threshold that is met prospectively and disclosed as a securities risk is not yet a threshold that constrains anything.

### NoöPunk synthesis

A threshold crossed in a prospectus is still a threshold crossed, but read the grammar: **expect**, not **observe**. The first institution to record that AI is doubling the pace of its own research is the institution selling shares in it, and the number arrives with a redaction notice attached. This is the Noösphere's speedometer installed by the driver. → AI-assisted R&D → internal estimate → policy gate → securities risk factor → safety case → public offering. The NoöPunk reading is not "the Singularity has arrived". It is that the accounting of acceleration now precedes the experience of it, and that the only outside read the public has is a nonprofit's estimate with its best evidence blacked out. A commons would ask for the same determination, published with the redactions reversed and the safety case attached — the same six obligations a state demands of a lab, addressed outward.

## 23:20 EET — United Airlines closes its booking systems to third-party AI agents

**Categories:** cybernetic Noösphere; agent legibility, authorization and liability; human-machine boundary in regulated transactions. **Signal type:** primary corporate policy announcement, corroborated by independent business reporting. **Not:** evidence of AGI, agent autonomy, a security incident at United, or a legal prohibition.

On **11 November** United Airlines published a policy restricting **"unauthorized, third-party personal AI agents or assistants"** from booking flights and managing reservations on customers' behalf. The restriction applies directly to United's own website and app and indirectly through travel agencies, online travel agencies and aggregators; the airline said it is updating booking, ticketing and distribution policies to enforce it. AI assistants may still **display** United's flights and fares but cannot complete reservations through United's systems without authorization. No implementation timetable was announced, and bookings made before 11 November will be honoured.

United's stated reasons are specific. Sharing account credentials with a third-party assistant "can expose sensitive information", and actions taken by such tools "may result in unintended purchases, account changes, or use of MileagePlus miles". The second reason is the more interesting one: automated actions "have the potential to violate FAA and DOT regulations — as an example, flight check-in requires human acknowledgment that bags do not contain banned substances like explosives". The release adds that "corporate leaders in the banking and retail industries have taken similar actions", and that United's Digital Trust Center will be updated to explain the restrictions. Trade reporting adds that United has no agreements authorizing third-party AI agents to book, and that this is the first explicit restriction of its kind by a US carrier.

**Established fact:** the policy, its direct and indirect scope, the display/booking distinction, the absence of a timetable, the honouring of prior bookings, the credential and unintended-action arguments, the FAA/DOT human-acknowledgment example, the claim that banking and retail have acted similarly (the airline's assertion, not verified here), and that no authorization agreements exist as reported. Primary release read; corroborated by TechCrunch, WSJ and Bloomberg.

**Evidence status:** **primary corporate policy plus three independent business outlets.** This is a private firm's terms-of-access decision, not a regulation, a court ruling or a technical finding. It is **not** evidence that an agent acted maliciously, that a breach occurred at United, or that agents are generally unreliable; the cyber-risk framing is United's, and the underlying incidents it gestures at are not documented in the release.

**Sources:** [United, "United Restricts Access for Third-Party AI Agents", 11 November 2026](https://united.mediaroom.com/news-releases?item=125496); [TechCrunch, 12 November 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/12/united-airlines-blocks-ai-agents-from-booking-flights-citing-cybersecurity-risks/); [WSJ, 11 November 2026](https://www.wsj.com/tech/ai/united-airlines-is-banning-ai-bots-from-booking-flights-5b514b38); [Bloomberg, 11 November 2026](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-11/united-blocks-ai-agents-from-booking-flights-ahead-of-holidays).

**Next verification:** whether other carriers and OTAs follow; whether an authorized-agent scheme (delegated credentials, scoped tokens, agent identity) appears, and who issues it; whether the FAA/DOT human-acknowledgment requirement becomes the template for other regulated purchases; and whether any regulator clarifies when an agent's action is legally the passenger's.

### NoöPunk synthesis

The first great firewall of the agent era is a check-in form. An agent may read the catalogue and may not complete the transaction, because at the one step that matters a regulation requires a body to acknowledge what is in the bag. That is not dystopia; it is a door with a signature line, and a company deciding that the person who pays is the person who must be present. → agent capability → consumer delegation → counterparty refusal → regulatory human acknowledgment → an identity layer that can prove "this was me". The Noösphere grows a border here, and the border is made of **liability**. The political question the genre should keep is not whether the assistant is conscious but who is legible when it acts: the account, the airline, the regulator, or the passenger whose name is on the ticket.

## 23:30 EET — EU readies a 22nd Russia sanctions package as trilateral talks close in Abu Dhabi

**Categories:** geopolitics; EU–Russia war; European fiscal and sanctions capacity. **Signal type:** multiple independent wire reports of diplomatic and legislative activity. **Not:** a threshold crossing, a ceasefire, a European Federation, a world war, or any nuclear dimension.

Russian, US and Ukrainian delegations held talks in **Abu Dhabi on 10–11 November** — a trilateral round — with Vladimir Medinsky leading the Russian delegation and Rustem Umerov the Ukrainian one, according to TASS. On **12 November** US envoys Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff arrived in Moscow to meet President Putin, per AFP and The Independent. The same day, European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen, arriving in Dublin for the European Political Community summit, said the EU welcomes the Abu Dhabi talks while **preparing its 22nd sanctions package against Russia** and a proposal "on how to cover the financial needs of Ukraine in 2027"; both are to be presented before the **European Council of 26–27 November**. Reuters, citing EU diplomats, reports that the package is expected to include restrictions on banks, cryptocurrencies and crypto platforms, and measures against circumvention through third countries; France's Europe minister said approval is expected on Monday. The European Parliament debated further sanctions on 12 November. Russian strikes on Kyiv continued in the same period, per The Independent and the Russian defence ministry's own statements.

**Established fact:** the dates and participants of the Abu Dhabi round as reported by TASS; the arrival of the US envoys in Moscow as reported by AFP and The Independent; von der Leyen's statements on the 22nd package and the 2027 financing proposal, and the 26–27 November Council date, as reported by Reuters; the expected contents of the package as described by diplomats, explicitly not final; the French statement on Monday approval; the European Parliament debate; and continued strikes as reported.

**Evidence status:** **multiple wire services and official statements, with TASS treated as a party-linked source and attributed rather than adopted.** No agreement text, joint communiqué, ceasefire or verified casualty figures were available in this pass. This is **not** evidence that the war is ending, that sanctions will be adopted as described, or that any European political unification is occurring. Negotiation and sanctions preparation are not de-escalation; they are two clocks running at once.

**Sources:** [Reuters, "EU to present new Russia sanctions package, Ukraine funding measures by year-end", 12 November 2026](https://www.internazionale.it/ultime-notizie-reuters/2026/11/12/eu-to-present-new-russia-sanctions-package-ukraine-funding-measures-by-year-end); [TASS, 12 November 2026](https://tass.com/world/2217067); [AFP, "US envoys arrive in Russia for Putin meeting after Ukraine talks", 12 November 2026](https://www.afp.com/en/us-envoys-arrive-russia-putin-meeting-after-ukraine-talks); [European Parliament newsletter, 11–12 November 2026](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/pdfs/news/expert/2026/11/briefing/20261103BRI48622/20261103BRI48622_en.pdf); [Reuters, "EU to approve more sanctions on Russia on Monday, France says", 12 November 2026](https://www.reuters.com/world/europe/eu-approve-more-sanctions-russia-monday-france-says-2026-11-12).

**Next verification:** whether the package is adopted on Monday and in what final form; the size and legal basis of the 2027 financing instrument; whether the Abu Dhabi round produces a documented framework or only continued talks; and whether the 26–27 November Council converts any of this into treaty-level commitments. Watch fiscal capacity, not rhetoric: a union that can borrow for a war budget is closer to a federation than one that cannot, and nothing here shows that step taken.

### NoöPunk synthesis

Two clocks run at once — a negotiation clock in Abu Dhabi and a sanctions clock in Brussels — and the second is being wound for 2027. The EU is building the ledger of the war while talking about ending it, which is what institutions do when they expect the aftermath to be theirs to administer. → talks → 22nd package → 2027 financing → European Council. There is no Singularity in this, only the mundane construction of a polity's fiscal spine; if a European Federation ever appears on this timeline, it will be assembled out of exactly this kind of budget line, adopted on a Monday, reported in a wire story nobody reads to the end.

#### "The Ghost Station" — Adam Fell (*Strange Horizons*, Issue 2, 2 November 2026; short story, free)

**What it is.** An oral history of the feminist punk bands of the upper Midwest, 1989–1996 — "Screw City Sunset" — assembled from interviews with the people who were there. Its centre is **Zelda Zhang**, who in the summer of 1992 broadcasts every Friday night on **Radio MQZ**, "live from the Midwest Quarantine Zone", from midnight to 1:33 a.m. The Zone is their own towns, described in the past tense, after something killed or changed people. The broadcasts are receivable **only through car radios**, never a boombox or a tape deck, and never twice on the same frequency; the songs are real, and one of them, "The Desperate Kingdom of Love", was released in 2004. The last broadcast ends with a full-band version of "Screw City Sunset" sung by Grace, who died before it was written. Then dead air, and Zelda never returns.

**Release state.** **Released** — free online, Issue 2 (2 November 2026), roughly 2,700 words.

**The specific affinity, in one disputable sentence.** Contact with another version of the world arrives as a **radio signal that only a moving car can hold**, and the story's only evidence is a chorus of friends disagreeing about whether it happened — a noöspheric cosmology performed as broadcast infrastructure and oral archive rather than as an alien.

**What NoöPunk could take, and where it diverges.** Take the **carrier**. NoöPunk usually renders the noösphere as a network, a mind or a drug; here it is a band, a frequency, a tape deck and a county. Take the **condition of reception**: the anomaly is not stronger for a stronger psychic, it is only receivable in motion, through a specific machine — a portable rule for psi or contact that makes the apparatus part of the metaphysics rather than a stat bonus. Take the **form**: a paradigm break presented as the interviewees remembering the same summer differently, with the text refusing to adjudicate between ghost, bleedover, prank and mass hysteria. That is this watch's evidence discipline turned into fiction, and it is a better model for reporting an unresolved ontology than any omniscient narrator. Diverge: there is no state, market or augmentation economy; psi is never named and nothing in the text claims mind is part of physics; the bleed is local and private, not a disclosure event; and the register is elegiac and funny rather than punk-as-politics.

**Established fact:** venue, date, author and length; the Radio MQZ broadcasts and the Quarantine Zone; the car-radio-only and changing-frequency conditions; the 2004 song; Grace's death and the final broadcast; and the refusal to decide what Zelda was — all read in the published text.

**Evidence status:** **primary text, read in substantial part.** Fiction is **zero evidence** about psi, NHI, alternate realities, collective consciousness or the timeline.

**Source:** https://strangehorizons.com/wordpress/fiction/the-ghost-station/

#### *Slip: A Solo Psychic Journal of Survival and Unraveling* — Thaloranth Publishing (solo journaling RPG, PDF, 30 pp., released mid-October 2026)

**What it is.** A solo psychological-horror journaling game set in Branford, Maine. You are one of the **Slipped**: "someone whose trauma has manifested as a strange psychic phenomenon" — you might disappear without warning, absorb another person's pain, influence minds, or become trapped in moments of time. There are five types — **Slip, Shade, Hollow, Voice, Looper** — and no combat, levels or power fantasy. The sheet is a set of tracks: an **Exposure Track** ("the more noticeable your abilities become, the more attention you attract"), an optional **Control Track** for "your deteriorating grip on reality", **Memory Drift**, in which you "lose familiar memories and gain disturbing replacements", and the **Burn List** — "record the things you cannot afford to forget — and watch that list shrink". Four Collapse Paths (Flee, Hide, Confront, Collapse) and four hidden organisations (the Ferrymen, Project LOCUS, the Veil Archive, the Ash Protocol) structure the chronicle. The stated question is not how powerful you become but "how much of yourself you'll have left".

**Release state.** **Released** — PDF, 30 pages, listed as published 29 days before this read (mid-October 2026). A late catch; not previously logged here.

**The specific affinity, in one disputable sentence.** Psi is not a power you acquire but a **symptom of what happened to you**, and the character sheet's central resource is the memory you are losing rather than the force you can project.

**What NoöPunk could take, and where it diverges.** Take **the Burn List** as an identity-continuity mechanic: a list of things you refuse to forget, which is also a countdown, and which the game makes you physically write and then cross out. That is more usable at a table than any upload-continuity chart, because the loss is authored by the player and visible in their own handwriting. Take **Exposure** as the political variable: using the ability is not the risk, being *noticed* is, and the notice comes from institutions with names. Take the refusal of the power fantasy — a psi game whose progression is deterioration. Diverge: it is solo journaling, with no table, no cosmology and no claim that mind is physics; the trauma-to-psi link is a metaphor as much as a premise; the hidden organisations are set dressing with no rules attached; and the horror register is intimate rather than political.

**Established fact:** the premise, the five types, the Exposure and Control tracks, Memory Drift, the Burn List, the four Collapse Paths, the four organisations, the page count, and the "29 days ago" publication display — all as published on the storefront. The rules text was not read beyond that description.

**Evidence status:** **publisher storefront description; no rules text examined and no play report.** Zero evidence about psi, trauma, consciousness or the timeline.

**Source:** https://thelegendlives.itch.io/slip-a-solo-psychic-journal-of-survival-and-unraveling

#### *Decker: True Names* — Julian Grant (browser RPG, 7M3 engine; itch.io, name-your-own-price, released late October 2026)

**What it is.** New York, 2049: "the city runs on who you are", and names can be stolen. Rook is a decker who lifts identities for a living; the last job is a file from the **Registry**, a federal building with no name on the door which officially studies **"anomalous cognition"** and whose server floor holds thirty-one thousand names while its containment wing holds people. The file is a list of **true names**, and the first one is Rook's. "Magic is real. It always was. The code and the spell were the same thing." Rook's **Spell** speaks a thing's true name: it goes through any armour, costs a share of his own health, and "barely touches machines, because machines don't have names". Cyberspace is rendered as damp carpet and humming fluorescent rooms, not neon grids. Two hidden meters — **Bond** and **Conscience** — and four endings track what you do with the list and with the person beside you.

**Release state.** **Released** — HTML5, playable in a browser, about an hour, name-your-own-price, listed as published 16 days before this read (late October 2026); the first game on the designer's 7M3 engine and the first in a series. A late catch; not previously logged here.

**The specific affinity, in one disputable sentence.** The state's catalogue of anomalous cognition is a **registry of names**, and magic works by speaking the name the registry holds — psi as the political economy of identity, where being legible is simultaneously the power and the capture.

**What NoöPunk could take, and where it diverges.** Take the **true name as the interface between metaphysics and bureaucracy**: one object that is both a spell component and a database row, which is a cleaner NoöPunk McGuffin than a chip, a drug or an implant because it makes the metaphysical and the administrative the same substance. Take the **cost rule** — magic spends health, never mana — and the **machine exception**: no name, no purchase, so the thing that cannot be owned cannot be spelled. That is a sharp, portable statement about which kinds of entity are inside the political economy and which are not. Diverge: it is a one-hour game, and the "magic is real" reveal is a genre turn rather than a defended cosmology; psi is not distinguished from magic; the Registry is a villain set, not an institution with procedures; and the identity question is resolved by a choice menu rather than by a rule.

**Established fact:** the setting, the Registry and its stated remit, the true-name premise, the Spell and its cost and machine exception, the two hidden meters, the four endings, the platform, price model, length and "16 days ago" publication display — from the game's storefront page and the designer's own devlog. The game itself was not played.

**Evidence status:** **storefront and designer devlog; no playthrough.** Zero evidence about psi, anomalous cognition, consciousness or the timeline.
