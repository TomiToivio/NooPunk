## 5. EU Court Advocate General issues first major Gemini / press-publisher copyright opinion

**Event and publication:** **3 November 2026**, Court of Justice of the European Union press release No 155/26 and Advocate General Maciej Szpunar's Opinion in **Case C-250/25, Like Company v Google Ireland**. **Timeline categories:** Cyberpunk (AI platform power, information intermediaries, media economics, privatized knowledge infrastructure); AI governance; European institutional capacity; social-science / institutional legitimation of AI-mediated communication.

**What is genuinely new:** An Advocate General of the EU's highest court has now delivered a concrete legal interpretation addressing generative chatbot answers that retrieve and reproduce press publications. The opinion distinguishes factual reporting (not copyright-protected as such) from recognizable reuse of a publisher's expression, especially where a chatbot circumvents access controls or its answer substitutes for the original article. It identifies potential provider liability where the relevant use is inherent in the business model or adequate safeguards are absent. On the training questions, the Advocate General recommends inadmissibility because use of the claimant's material for Gemini training was not evidenced; comments on the text-and-data-mining exception are therefore not a binding resolution of AI training legality.

**Why NoöPunk cares:** The EU is beginning to articulate how AI answer engines redistribute power and revenue between information producers and platform intermediaries. This is an unusually concrete instance of *algorithmic communication becoming a legal-institutional object*: control over news access, attribution, extraction and the economic basis of the public sphere. For the LaclauGPT sensorium it is an institutional signal about AI-mediated discourse; for PCM it raises the political economy of common knowledge infrastructures. **Neither upstream report independently covered this event**, so these are NoöPunk interpretations, **not** a cross-project convergence claim. No AI personhood or consciousness claim is implicated.

**Evidence status:** **Established fact:** official publication of a judicial officer's legal opinion on 3 November. **Disciplinary/institutional-legitimation signal:** legal framing of generative AI as an information intermediary potentially liable for answer-time reproduction. **Not established:** a binding EU Court judgment, proven infringement by Google, mandatory payments, or a general prohibition on training. The Court's judges will deliberate and rule later; the referring Hungarian court will decide the underlying dispute.

**Primary source:** [Court of Justice of the European Union, press release No 155/26, 3 November 2026](https://curia.europa.eu/site/upload/docs/application/pdf/2026-11/cp260155en.pdf). **Independent corroboration:** [Reuters, 3 November 2026](https://www.reuters.com/world/googles-use-press-by-ai-chatbot-may-infringe-copyright-rules-eu-court-adviser-2026-11-03/).
