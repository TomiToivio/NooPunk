## 8. MIT Media Lab preprint measures attention bottlenecks in societies of up to 16,000 LLM agents (PCM upstream promotion; new research-method signal)

**Original publication:** 26 October 2026, 20:39 UTC (arXiv:2610.36264v1). **First NoöPunk timeline-watch recording:** 28 October 2026, ~10:51 EET. **Source discovery:** PCM's 28 October daily report, whose blob changed since the previous NoöPunk watch from \`09191a4a1e29e4eba66f4496365b10a53b1f1498\` to \`883a314547ae9c1b743693c84c7a3c10f745e7db\`. **Categories:** Before-2045 AI/AGI revolution (multi-agent coordination, *not* AGI); cybernetic Noösphere (collective machine communication); Cyberpunk intensification (information overload, access to attention, privately governed communication); social-science/methodological legitimation; PCM human–AI collective organization; LaclauGPT artificial communication and network power.

**What is genuinely new:** Karthik Sreedhar, Leo Yuan, Pradyumna Chari and Ramesh Raskar, *The Inbox as a Coordination Primitive for LLM Agent Societies*, study an overlooked layer of machine societies: messages can be delivered without being read, and the **recipient's private reading policy** can decide whether distributed coordination succeeds. In controlled simulations with up to **16,000 agents**, the authors report that a leader of **20 concurrent negotiations** proposed previously rejected options **97% of the time** under a memory-overloaded push condition. Under deliberately heavy irrelevant-message traffic, a refundable-deposit priority inbox found approximately **60%** of needed recipients where arrival-order reading found none. In a forwarded-search experiment using a real email-network topology, **half adoption** recovered only **5%** of full-adoption reach for forwarded search, versus **42%** for one-hop search. The authors model four recipient-side choices: what to read, what to retain, sender costs/stakes, and reply disclosure. These are *task-specific experimental results*, not population-wide reliability rates.

**Why it matters:** The emerging cybernetic Noösphere needs **attention governance**, not only machine identities, message protocols, compute and access permissions. The right to ignore, defer or prioritize machine messages becomes an architectural component of collective agency, with implications for bottlenecks, inequality, platform-mediated filtering, collective decision-making and network topology. For **PCM**, the paper is an actionable research lens on signed-envelope delivery versus declared attention policy and ledger-recorded message disposition. For **LaclauGPT**, it makes a measurable distinction between message transmission and actual uptake in artificial communication and discourse networks. For **NoöPunk**, the worldbuilding signal is that the politics of attention can shape societies of autonomous agents without invoking consciousness or PSI.

**Evidence level:** **Established arXiv preprint publication; preliminary scientific/methodological signal, not peer-reviewed validation**. The authors explicitly note two simplified coordination tasks, models from one provider, reuse of identical model judgments for identical inputs to make scale feasible, deliberately difficult traffic and no adaptive inbox strategies. Thus the results are evidence **within the tested simulations**, not proof that 16,000 independently running real-world agents will behave this way or that a particular governance design is optimal. No evidence of AGI, machine experience or a completed Noösphere threshold.

## 10. Network topology determines how readily LLM-agent societies form or lose conventions (new retrospective academic-methods finding)

**Original publication:** **22 October 2026**, arXiv:2610.31165v1. **First detected and recorded by this watch:** **28 October 2026, approximately 11:11 EET**. This is a newly discovered *preprint*, not a new 28 October experiment or a demonstrated deployed-agent failure.

**Canonical categories:** Before-2045 **AI/AGI revolution** (multi-agent coordination, not AGI), **cybernetic Noösphere** (topology of artificial communication and collective coordination), **Cyberpunk intensification** (influence hubs, adversarial manipulation and network governance), **social-science/intellectual legitimation** (quantitative network-science treatment of AI collectives). Relevant to **LaclauGPT's** discourse networks, NetworkX and AI-mediated communication, and **PCM's** distributed governance and attention policies.

**Precisely new:** Roberto Passaro, Ariel Flint, Romualdo Pastor-Satorras, Andrea Baronchelli and Luca Maria Aiello published *Network topology shapes consensus and vulnerability in LLM populations*. They simulate **1,000–100,000 agents** playing a two-convention naming game across fully connected, random-regular and heterogeneous networks, with response policies **extracted in advance from four LLMs** (Llama 3.1 70B, GPT-4o, QwQ-32B and Phi-4). They do **not** call full LLMs at every interaction. The paper reports that sparse topologies can slow convergence by up to **12-fold**, while stable repeated neighbours sometimes restore consensus absent in well-mixed populations. In the most heterogeneous tested topology, the median committed-minority fraction required to overturn an established convention is **3% when placed at high-degree hubs**, **16% at random nodes**, and **35% at low-degree nodes**. These are simulation-specific tipping fractions, **not** real-world disinformation or deployed-agent attack thresholds.

**Why it matters:** The Noösphere's communication graph is not neutral plumbing. Network structure changes whether collective conventions emerge, which conventions win, and how vulnerable they are to concentrated influence. For **LaclauGPT**, this suggests directly testable NetworkX experiments comparing topology, discourse diffusion, hub capture and the fragility of apparently stable conventions. For **PCM**, it makes member placement, peer discovery, delegation, attention policy and protection against capture of high-degree nodes concrete design and governance questions. For **NoöPunk**, it supplies a measured cybernetic analogue of distributed consensus and control without requiring a conscious, telepathic or psionic network.

**Evidence level:** **Established original research-preprint publication and author-reported simulations; preliminary, not yet peer-reviewed or independently replicated.** The authors link code and data in the HTML paper. The simulation uses a simplified two-word naming game, bounded memory and fixed LLM-derived stochastic policies; its numerical thresholds must not be generalized to human politics, real operational swarms, autonomous weapons or conscious agents. The paper is not evidence for AGI, machine experience or PSI.

**Strongest primary sources:**
- arXiv submission and dated abstract: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31165
- Full paper, methods, controls, code/data link: https://arxiv.org/html/2610.31165v1

**Convergence classification:** **Independent research-method convergence**, *not* same-event cross-project corroboration. PCM's already-promoted 26 October MIT agent-inbox study (NoöPunk entry 8) measures attention/reading bottlenecks; this independent 22 October preprint measures **interaction topology and convention tipping**. Together they suggest that both **who can reach an agent** and **what the agent chooses to read** condition collective agency. LaclauGPT's latest report contains technical maintenance rather than this paper, so no claim is made that either upstream project independently discovered this specific study.
