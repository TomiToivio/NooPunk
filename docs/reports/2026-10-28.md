In the most heterogeneous tested topology, the median committed-minority fraction required to overturn an established convention is **3% when placed at high-degree hubs**, **16% at random nodes**, and **35% at low-degree nodes**. **Why it matters:** The Noösphere's communication graph is not neutral plumbing. Network structure changes whether collective conventions emerge, which conventions win, and how vulnerable they are to concentrated influence. For **LaclauGPT**, this suggests directly testable NetworkX experiments comparing topology, discourse diffusion, hub capture and the fragility of apparently stable conventions. For **PCM**, it makes member placement, peer discovery, delegation, attention policy and protection against capture of high-degree nodes concrete design and governance questions. For **NoöPunk**, it supplies a measured cybernetic analogue of distributed consensus and control without requiring a conscious, telepathic or psionic network.

**Evidence level:** **Established original research-preprint publication and author-reported simulations; preliminary, not yet peer-reviewed or independently replicated.** The authors link code and data in the HTML paper. The simulation uses a simplified two-word naming game, bounded memory and fixed LLM-derived stochastic policies; its numerical thresholds must not be generalized to human politics, real operational swarms, autonomous weapons or conscious agents.
