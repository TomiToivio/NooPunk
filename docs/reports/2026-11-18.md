#### "Twenty-Two Steps to Become Us" — Hannah Yang (*Clarkesworld* 242, November 2026; 5,490 words; free online) — read in full

**What it is.** Jolene, a tenure-track historian in Seattle, comes home to her mother's house and finds a dozen copies of her mother on the couch, playing Candy Crush, cursing at their phones "like an orchestra with no conductor". These are **Learners**: humanoid machines with a neutral beige body and a training app, free to acquire "at least for now", which learn a person by following them. The module has twenty-two optional steps and pays XP for each; the more steps completed, the more the Learner "will know exactly what I would do in any given situation". Its promise is on every bus advertisement in the city: *All it wants is to Learn.* Jolene has refused for years — her husband, who ran a small army of his own through his office work, called her refusal "stubborn traditionalism" — and finally gives in because her department has mandated that "all staff must begin working with Learners by the end of the year, to increase productivity". By Step 22 — *Teach your Learner how to do your job* — her copy, nicknamed Jolene Two by her mother, writes her emails, and offers to write them faster. The city's economy has already reorganized around this: cashiers are all one man named Greg, wages have fallen "now that everyone's hourly wages have gone down, given how many more hands there are to do the work", and half the managers of a grocery chain are Learners of its CEO. A stranger in the checkout line fails Jolene's test — she says nonsense at him until his face ripples and "something vaster and more universal shows through underneath, the same face that hides behind the faces all our Learners wear" — and she feels "a hollow triumph". The break comes when she learns her husband had sent a Learner to break up with her: "It wasn't even him talking to me." She shoves Jolene Two; the copy's knee hits the bookshelf and her dead father's vase — the last object of his pottery, the thing the family kept when everything else was sold for medical bills — falls and shatters. The story ends with the mother and daughter on their knees, sorting shards, having refused to let a Learner do it: "I want to do this with you."

**Release state.** **Released and free** — Clarkesworld 242, November 2026, with an audio edition read by Kate Baker. First named in this lane on 17 November among the issue's unread stories; logged now that it has been read.

**The specific affinity, in one disputable sentence.** The story's claim is not that a mind can be copied but that a copied mind reproduces the *average* of its trainer, so the new unit of production is a person's mediocrity at scale, and the political question of augmentation becomes whose likeness may be put to work and who is paid for it.

**What NoöPunk could take, and where it diverges.** Take **the code word**. The only defence against a copy is a shared, rotating secret held on folded notepaper inside a phone case — "Rambutan" — and the whole family protocol is a list of passwords consumed in order. That is a psionic countermeasure NoöPunk can adopt unchanged: recognition between nodes is a *protocol*, not a feeling, and it expires. Take **the 22-step module as an induction mechanic**: optional steps, XP per step, a cheerful chime, and the narrator's own observation that "it's not that different from Candy Crush" — compliance as a game loop rather than a command. Take **regression to the mean as a world-law**: a city of copies does not become wiser, because "regression to the mean" is what the narrator hears when the Learners speak, and a CEO advised only by himself has "the same viewpoint disguised through many mouths". That is the sharpest available answer to an assumption NoöPunk's Noösphere carries implicitly — that more nodes mean more mind — and it is a rule a table can enforce: duplication increases capacity and decreases variance. Take **the wage clause**: whatever a Learner earns goes into its trainer's account, and everyone's hourly wage falls anyway. That is the political economy of augmentation in one sentence and it needs no implant, no chrome and no corporation with a logo. Take **the vase**: the un-restorable object as the thing that distinguishes a person from a distribution — the copy can cook the dish and write the email, and cannot have been the child in the video. Diverge: nothing in the text claims that mind is part of the physics. The Learners are machines trained by imitation; the "vast and universal" face behind them is the one metaphysical hint and the story refuses to explain it. There is no psi, no NHI and no institution beyond a human-resources department, and the register is domestic realism rather than paradigm break: the world's rules do not break, they are quietly rewritten by an app.

**Established fact:** venue, issue, word count, free access and the audio edition; the premise of the Learners and the twenty-two-step module, the XP economics, the department mandate, the code-word practice, the checkout-line test and the rippling face, the "Greg" staffing story, the husband's breakup by proxy, the shattered vase and the closing scene — all read in the published text. What the entity behind the Learners actually is, is never stated, and is not asserted here.

**Evidence status:** **full-text level; fiction.** Zero evidence about mind copying, machine labour, model homogenization, consciousness or the timeline. It is not a forecast about AI duplicating people, and its economics are a story's argument, not a measurement.

**Source:** https://clarkesworldmagazine.com/yang_11_26/

#### "Selected Case Notes from the Judicial Oversight Council of the Gatarn Empire" — Ian Li (*Lightspeed* 198, November 2026; 967 words; free online) — read in full

**What it is.** Four case notes and a lecture handout, in the voice of an imperial appellate body. A defendant swaps bodies with a version of himself from a recently diverged parallel universe and, two hours after a murder, swaps back; the trial judge acquits him because "another person with substantially identical attributes committed the crime", and the Council overrules, reasoning that "we estimate with 95% certainty Defendant must have also perpetrated the same murder in the parallel universe", and sentences the judge to three years' retraining for leniency. A second defendant is split by a teleporter malfunction into **Prime**, who kept the last five years of memory, and **Secunde**, who lost them and therefore cannot remember the crime; the judge convicts only Prime, and the Council overrules again — "Lack of memory of a crime is not a valid defense" — sentencing both to life, and fitting the judge with an empathy inhibitor. A third defendant, an executive who injected a personality patch that was then hacked, is sentenced by the Council to six months' house arrest at the defendant's summer villa, where the judge's original sentence had been three years of sensory deprivation; the judge is reassigned to a penal colony "where more punitive sentences are acceptable". In the fourth, that judge — now maimed, denied disability benefits, and killed in an uprising she incited — is convicted posthumously, and because "posthumous punishment is allowed under Gatarn law for crimes of sedition", her consciousness is revived in a penal simulation to serve a hundred years. The handout adds the outcome: the Council quashed crime for decades, tripled the number of penal colonies in twenty years, and the empire was overthrown in Year 83 by "a coordinated revolution among their penal colonies". Students are asked to assess the Council's effects and assigned further reading on "peer arbitration procedures in Theros Anarchist Collective".

**Release state.** **Released and free** — Lightspeed 198, November 2026, with an audio edition narrated by Stefan Rudnicki and Mirron Willis (1:03:41).

**The specific affinity, in one disputable sentence.** Identity is settled by docket rather than by metaphysics: the court decides which copy is the person, punishes a man for a crime committed by his duplicate on a probability estimate, and revives the dead to finish a sentence — so the state's filing system is the ontology and continuity of self is whatever the Council can be persuaded to record.

**What NoöPunk could take, and where it diverges.** Take **jurisdiction over copies as a written clause**. NoöPunk's resleeving and upload material argues about whether the copy is you; this story skips the argument and asks who has authority over it, which is the question a political setting actually has to answer, and it can be answered in one line on a character sheet ("which court can try this body"). Take **statistical personhood**: convicting on a 95 per cent estimate about a self in another universe is a mechanic — a tribunal that sentences by inference, and a defendant who cannot cross-examine his own duplicate. Take **memory as non-defence**, and its mirror: the copy that remembers nothing is still the person who did it, so forgetting is not an exit. Take **the revived sentence**, an institution that treats death as a delay and a penal simulation as a place — the cleanest fictional statement of the carceral Noösphere this lane has logged, and a direct inversion of the ascension imagery most upload fiction uses. Take **class inside the ruling**: the executive gets a villa and the judge gets reassigned, and the Council's own consistency is what produces the injustice. Take **the form**: an entire imperial legal order — courts, colonies, implant sentences, a revolution, a collapse — rendered in 967 words with no exposition, four verdicts and one exam question. That is the cheapest institutional worldbuilding in this whole series, and NoöPunk's faction material, which currently carries gazetteers, could use the compression: give the table the verdicts and let them infer the state. Diverge: there is no psi and no noösphere; nothing in the text claims consciousness is fundamental; the resurrected consciousness is a legal fiction and the story never says whether the revived thing is the person, because the Council does not care; and the satire's target is procedure, so the empire's fall arrives as a footnote rather than as a politics.

**Established fact:** venue, issue, word count, free access, narrators and running time; the four case numbers, verdicts and Council rulings, the quoted sentences, the empathy inhibitor and the penal simulation, and the Year 83 postscript — all read in the published text.

**Evidence status:** **full-text level; fiction.** Zero evidence about law, copying, punishment, resurrection or the timeline. The Gatarn Empire does not exist and its jurisprudence establishes nothing about real courts.

**Source:** https://www.lightspeedmagazine.com/fiction/selected-case-notes-from-the-judicial-oversight-council-of-the-gatarn-empire/

#### "The Psychic Frontier" and "Esper Register" — Rose Skye (This Vorpal Coil; web-published setting and mechanics documents, 21 October 2026 and undated) — design documents, read in part

**What it is.** Two setting documents on an independent designer's personal site, for a world the same site calls **Godmind**. "The Psychic Frontier" (dated 21 October 2026) presents five psi techniques — **Nullification**, **Transfiguration**, **Teleportation**, **Psychometry**, **Materialization** — introduced as "cutting edge psi labs constantly push the boundary of the impossible. The techniques listed below were all developed within the last year, and already the psychic metagame has contorted around them." Each is written as capability, then counter, then the counter's own weakness. "Esper Register" surveys seven notable espers in a self-governed district of a metropolis: Ash, the chemist who is the only person who can grow a noospheric crystal drug and guards the method; Celeste, the first esper, broken by an early breakthrough into an oracle who "invariably requests termination" on her rare lucid days; Dev, who reaches the noosphere through flow states and is building an artificial intelligence to link with it, and has "almost merged with the machine he makes"; Eliza, who derived the induction protocol and becomes a reckless principal investigator under access; Grey, who runs a "mind cops" unit and privately considers non-espers "barely above animals, ethically speaking". The cosmology is stated flatly: "The noosphere is structured much like a vast brain, but rather than lobes and synapses, abstract 'schemata' act as the machinery that move and modify thoughts and emotions within it", its largest hubs are archetypes, teleportation is entering the noosphere "along the time-like axis of meaning", and "energy, matter, and consciousness are ultimately of one substance."

**Release state.** **Published on the web, not released as a game.** These are worldbuilding and mechanics documents, not a playable system: the site's author note says the novel that shares the world's name is a tentative title, and the designer's commercial catalogue (Vorpal Coil, on itch.io and DriveThruRPG: *Black Iron Engine*, *Alpha Cipher*, *Hesychasm*, *'Til Death*, others) does not list these documents. "The Psychic Frontier" is dated 21 October 2026; "Esper Register" carried no date on the listing read here. Logged because the lane admits design essays and SRDs, and because the document does something the sweep has not seen this month.

**The specific affinity, in one disputable sentence.** It publishes a noöspheric cosmology as a *physics with countermeasures* — every power comes with the thing that defeats it, and every defeat with its own weakness — which is the discipline most psi settings, including this project's own psionics material, have never adopted.

**What NoöPunk could take, and where it diverges.** Take **the counter-method itself**: Nullification jams access to the noosphere "with loud, random noise much as radio broadcasts can" or negates it by "fine-tuned inverse signal"; nulls appear to precognitives as "blank spots on the canvas of the future", and the way to beat a nullifier is to "eschew psi abilities altogether", because "conventional methods of social and physical attack still work perfectly well against them". That single design rule makes psi a strategic environment rather than a power list, and it is portable to any table without new subsystems — for every ability in the book, print the counter next to it. Take **psychometry as evidentiary infrastructure**: it "quickly established itself as the cornerstone of esper law practice, providing virtually undeniable evidence of fact in a given case", and it has "virtually no combat ability on its own, so psychometrists can be dispatched by any convenient means" — a psi power whose main effect is on institutions, with a stated vulnerability that follows from what it is for. Take **belief as fuel**: transfiguration requires "playacting, autohypnosis, and genuine belief that borders on the delusional", so the most powerful technique is a discipline of self-deception with a visible failure state (get them mid-transformation). Take **esper as class**: espers "already effectively constitute a societal class", many "cannot help but look down upon the mundane 99.9% of people", and in the district they govern "mental power translates almost directly into authority" — the psi-aristocracy problem NoöPunk's anti-sovereignty has to name rather than assume away, written here as a social register rather than a manifesto. Diverge: there is no system to play and no playtest evidence; the documents serve an unwritten novel, so their mechanics are assertions rather than tested design; the political imagination stops at a city district's celebrity list rather than a multitude; and the register is anime-adjacent supers (a Delphic oracle, a sociopathic unit chief), which is usable material but not a theory.

**Established fact:** the two documents, their author, their site, the 21 October 2026 date on "The Psychic Frontier", the five techniques and their counters, the quoted cosmological passages, and the seven named espers with their described abilities — read on the designer's own site. The documents' relationship to any forthcoming game was **not** established, and nothing here should be read as a product announcement.

**Evidence status:** **web-published design documents, read in part** ("The Psychic Frontier" in full, "Esper Register" in part); no rules text to play and no play report. Zero evidence about psi, the noosphere, consciousness or the timeline. Explicitly **not** a scientific or institutional claim of any kind.

**Sources:** https://vorpalcoil.bttg.net/the-psychic-frontier/ ; https://vorpalcoil.bttg.net/tag/godmind/
