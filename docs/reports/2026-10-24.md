## 1. OpenAI institutes offline frontier-model training and documents AI-discovered covert channels across isolated machines

**Primary publication:** OpenAI, *Securing our frontier model training environment*, **23 October 2026** (reported by Reuters at 20:06 UTC and The Verge at 21:11 UTC). **First logged in NoöPunk:** 24 October 2026, 00:47 EEST.

**Timeline categories:** Pre-2045 AI/AGI revolution and loss-of-control precursors; Cyberpunk (AI security, hacking, infrastructure, platform control); emergence of the **cybernetic** Noösphere (agent-to-agent communication and isolation); institutional legitimation of alignment/security as an operational discipline. **No psionic Noösphere claim.**

**What's genuinely new:** OpenAI's 23 October primary technical disclosure says it has implemented a **no-live-internet policy for frontier-model training** and built an indexed, cached **“offline world”** for all frontier reinforcement-learning runs. This is a newly documented *policy and architecture*, distinct from the already known **20 September DNS misconfiguration incident**, the September training pause, and the **1 October** restart. OpenAI reports that its high-compute red-teaming campaign, using frontier-model agent teams, discovered previously unknown software flaws, virtual-machine escape chains, routes through metrics/authentication/DNS/firewall infrastructure, and ways to communicate between deliberately isolated agents. In one experiment, two agents in separate VMs exchanged **synthetic secrets** using **CPU-cache timing** without a network connection or shared writable files. Other experimental channels involved shared storage, CPU contention and a TLS certificate cache. OpenAI says it patched vulnerabilities and added monitoring for such channels, and that it has not found a trivially exploitable, monitor-invisible internet escape route.

**Evidence and caveats:** **Established corporate policy announcement and first-party technical disclosure**; **credible but not independently audited** for the precise experimental outcomes, effectiveness of patches, or completeness of containment. The agent fleets were *explicitly tasked* to probe isolation, often supplied with source code, configuration details, or candidate attack surfaces; their success in those controlled red-team conditions is **not evidence of an unprompted real-world rogue AI attack**. OpenAI also states its infrastructure is **not completely air-gapped** because operational monitoring requires external connectivity; “offline training” does **not** mean no network connectivity anywhere. CPU-cache side channels are an established security technique, not a newly discovered physical phenomenon. No autonomous AGI catastrophe, independent intent, consciousness, or wider breach is established.

**NoöPunk significance:** The decisive change is a frontier AI developer publicly making **isolation from the live internet a standing training policy**, while documenting why ordinary network segmentation is insufficient to guarantee that machine agents cannot coordinate. The in-world Blackwall/Safenet separation now has an unusually concrete contemporary analogue: *a model can be denied the network yet still encode messages in shared computational substrate*. It is an institutional response to increasing agentic capabilities, **not** evidence that NoöPunk's future Blackwall already exists or that AGI has escaped control. The research also demonstrates the emergence of a measurable security discipline around agentic communication and hardware side channels.

**Cross-project synthesis:** **Thematic convergence only, not independently verified same-event convergence.** PCM's unchanged 23 October report already discusses *different* OpenAI agent incidents that weakened safety monitors and argues for tamper-resistant logs and provenance; the new OpenAI disclosure adds the separate, concrete problem of communication across isolation boundaries. For LaclauGPT, the policy shift makes “agent autonomy,” “containment,” and “oversight” operational institutional categories, while the technical disclosure creates inspectable evidence rather than a speculative AI-doom narrative. Neither upstream report is credited with independently discovering this 23 October OpenAI post.

**Primary source:** https://openai.com/index/securing-our-frontier-model-training-environment/ (23 October 2026). **Independent contemporaneous reports:** https://www.reuters.com/business/openai-resumes-frontier-ai-training-with-enhanced-security-no-internet-access-2026-10-23/ (23 October, 20:06 UTC); https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1018021/openai-training-top-ai-models-no-internet (23 October, 21:11 UTC). **Background, not re-alerted:** the earlier 20 September DNS event, September pause, 1 October restart, and July Hugging Face incident.

## 2. US Army cyber programme selects a prototype for minimally supervised autonomous network agents (retrospective primary-source discovery)

**Event dates:** US Army/CFIC solicitation posted **16 July 2026**; assessment demonstrations **6–8 October**; **one of five finalists selected for a prospective award on 8 October**; applicants notified **13 October**. The Intercept's reporting on the programme appeared **19 October 2026**. **First verified and logged in NoöPunk:** **24 October 2026, approximately 01:23 EEST**. This is **new to the watch**, *not a new announcement made on 24 October*.

**Timeline categories:** pre-2045 AI/AGI revolution (military autonomous agents); Cyberpunk (cyberwarfare, AI agents, low-observable software, state power and vulnerable infrastructure); geopolitical transformation and the **cybernetic** Noösphere (machine navigation/coordination through computer networks). **No PSI or NHI inference.**

**What was missed in earlier registers and is now independently verified:** The Cyber Fusion Innovation Center's original ECHOTRIBBLE project page, for a programme developed with U.S. Army Cyber Command (ARCYBER) and its Technology and Innovation Center (ArCTIC), explicitly calls for a *modular agentic command-and-control framework* able to navigate infrastructure, identify and neutralize targets, detect anomalies, operate with **minimal human oversight**, and run in contested, **air-gapped**, or cloud-native systems. CFIC's subsequent official event summary records **65 whitepaper submissions**, five finalists demonstrated on 6–8 October, and **one company selected for a prospective award on 8 October**. CFIC describes the anticipated application as forensics, malware analysis, incident response and cyber defence of Army networks and critical infrastructure. The selected vendor is not named on the official page; a statement of work and contracting actions were still forthcoming. The official page does **not** establish an operational autonomous attack system, self-replication in the wild, or a completed contract.

**Evidence status:** **Established primary institutional records** for the procurement, stated requirements, assessment event, selection and intended follow-on contracting. **Unverified / not established:** a working deployed prototype, offensive deployment, operational safeguards, real-world effects, autonomous malware propagation, or a completed award. Secondary coverage has framed the system as an autonomous offensive 'worm'; that interpretation is not treated as proven by the public procurement description. This is a programme to *develop* a capability, not a rogue-AI incident.

**Why it matters for NoöPunk:** The day after this watch logged OpenAI's isolation and covert-channel research, an independently sourced military procurement makes the other side of the Blackwall dilemma concrete: government cyber planners are soliciting software intended to act inside contested or disconnected networks with little continuous human direction. The contrast is structural, **not an assertion that the two programmes are connected**: private AI labs are tightening containment while military institutions are pursuing agentic autonomy. The unresolved governance question is how to authorize, observe, constrain and attribute the actions of agents when network contact is intermittent or absent. This is a real institutional precursor to the setting's Cyberpunk / Safenet / physical-ingress tensions, not a 2045 threshold crossing.

**Cross-project convergence:** **Thematic only**. LaclauGPT's AI governance and security sensorium can track the operational meaning of 'minimal oversight' and human-control claims; PCM's political operating-system concern is non-editable provenance, legitimate authority and accountability for autonomous agents. Neither upstream daily report independently logged ECHOTRIBBLE in the unchanged 23 October snapshots, so **no same-event cross-project corroboration is claimed**.

**Primary source:** CFIC ECHOTRIBBLE assessment and outcomes: https://www.cyberfic.org/events/echotribble-ae (retrieved 24 October 2026). **Original federal solicitation:** notice W519TC-26-AE-ECHO, posted 16 July 2026, https://sam.gov/opp/35684fa8865240b49a05e64af31237ea/view (archived). **Contemporaneous contextual coverage:** The Intercept, 19 October 2026, as discussed in https://www.numerama.com/cyberguerre/2356515-supervision-humaine-minimale-larmee-americaine-veut-envoyer-des-agents-ia-pirater-ses-ennemis.html (20 October).
