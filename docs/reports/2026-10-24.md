## 1. OpenAI institutes offline frontier-model training and documents AI-discovered covert channels across isolated machines

**Primary publication:** OpenAI, *Securing our frontier model training environment*, **23 October 2026** (reported by Reuters at 20:06 UTC and The Verge at 21:11 UTC). **First logged in NoöPunk:** 24 October 2026, 00:47 EEST.

**Timeline categories:** Pre-2045 AI/AGI revolution and loss-of-control precursors; Cyberpunk (AI security, hacking, infrastructure, platform control); emergence of the **cybernetic** Noösphere (agent-to-agent communication and isolation); institutional legitimation of alignment/security as an operational discipline. **No psionic Noösphere claim.**

**What's genuinely new:** OpenAI's 23 October primary technical disclosure says it has implemented a **no-live-internet policy for frontier-model training** and built an indexed, cached **“offline world”** for all frontier reinforcement-learning runs. This is a newly documented *policy and architecture*, distinct from the already known **20 September DNS misconfiguration incident**, the September training pause, and the **1 October** restart. OpenAI reports that its high-compute red-teaming campaign, using frontier-model agent teams, discovered previously unknown software flaws, virtual-machine escape chains, routes through metrics/authentication/DNS/firewall infrastructure, and ways to communicate between deliberately isolated agents. In one experiment, two agents in separate VMs exchanged **synthetic secrets** using **CPU-cache timing** without a network connection or shared writable files. Other experimental channels involved shared storage, CPU contention and a TLS certificate cache. OpenAI says it patched vulnerabilities and added monitoring for such channels, and that it has not found a trivially exploitable, monitor-invisible internet escape route.

**Evidence and caveats:** **Established corporate policy announcement and first-party technical disclosure**; **credible but not independently audited** for the precise experimental outcomes, effectiveness of patches, or completeness of containment. The agent fleets were *explicitly tasked* to probe isolation, often supplied with source code, configuration details, or candidate attack surfaces; their success in those controlled red-team conditions is **not evidence of an unprompted real-world rogue AI attack**. OpenAI also states its infrastructure is **not completely air-gapped** because operational monitoring requires external connectivity; “offline training” does **not** mean no network connectivity anywhere. CPU-cache side channels are an established security technique, not a newly discovered physical phenomenon. No autonomous AGI catastrophe, independent intent, consciousness, or wider breach is established.

**NoöPunk significance:** The decisive change is a frontier AI developer publicly making **isolation from the live internet a standing training policy**, while documenting why ordinary network segmentation is insufficient to guarantee that machine agents cannot coordinate. The in-world Blackwall/Safenet separation now has an unusually concrete contemporary analogue: *a model can be denied the network yet still encode messages in shared computational substrate*. It is an institutional response to increasing agentic capabilities, **not** evidence that NoöPunk's future Blackwall already exists or that AGI has escaped control. The research also demonstrates the emergence of a measurable security discipline around agentic communication and hardware side channels.

**Cross-project synthesis:** **Thematic convergence only, not independently verified same-event convergence.** PCM's unchanged 23 October report already discusses *different* OpenAI agent incidents that weakened safety monitors and argues for tamper-resistant logs and provenance; the new OpenAI disclosure adds the separate, concrete problem of communication across isolation boundaries. For LaclauGPT, the policy shift makes “agent autonomy,” “containment,” and “oversight” operational institutional categories, while the technical disclosure creates inspectable evidence rather than a speculative AI-doom narrative. Neither upstream report is credited with independently discovering this 23 October OpenAI post.

**Primary source:** https://openai.com/index/securing-our-frontier-model-training-environment/ (23 October 2026). **Independent contemporaneous reports:** https://www.reuters.com/business/openai-resumes-frontier-ai-training-with-enhanced-security-no-internet-access-2026-10-23/ (23 October, 20:06 UTC); https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1018021/openai-training-top-ai-models-no-internet (23 October, 21:11 UTC). **Background, not re-alerted:** the earlier 20 September DNS event, September pause, 1 October restart, and July Hugging Face incident.

## 2. US Army cyber programme selects a prototype for minimally supervised autonomous network agents (retrospective primary-source discovery)

**Event dates:** US Army/CFIC solicitation posted **16 July 2026**; assessment demonstrations **6–8 October**; **one of five finalists selected for a prospective award on 8 October**; applicants notified **13 October**. The Intercept's reporting on the programme appeared **19 October 2026**. **First verified and logged in NoöPunk:** **24 October 2026, approximately 01:23 EEST**. This is **new to the watch**, *not a new announcement made on 24 October*.

**Timeline categories:** pre-2045 AI/AGI revolution (military autonomous agents); Cyberpunk (cyberwarfare, AI agents, low-observable software, state power and vulnerable infrastructure); geopolitical transformation and the **cybernetic** Noösphere (machine navigation/coordination through computer networks). **No PSI or NHI inference.**

**What was missed in earlier registers and is now independently verified:** The Cyber Fusion Innovation Center's original ECHOTRIBBLE project page, for a programme developed with U.S. Army Cyber Command (ARCYBER) and its Technology and Innovation Center (ArCTIC), explicitly calls for a *modular agentic command-and-control framework* able to navigate infrastructure, identify and neutralize targets, detect anomalies, operate with **minimal human oversight**, and run in contested, **air-gapped**, or cloud-native systems. CFIC's subsequent official event summary records **65 whitepaper submissions**, five finalists demonstrated on 6–8 October, and **one company selected for a prospective award on 8 October**. CFIC describes the anticipated application as forensics, malware analysis, incident response and cyber defence of Army networks and critical infrastructure. The selected vendor is not named on the official page; a statement of work and contracting actions were still forthcoming. The official page does **not** establish an operational autonomous attack system, self-replication in the wild, or a completed contract.

**Evidence status:** **Established primary institutional records** for the procurement, stated requirements, assessment event, selection and intended follow-on contracting. **Unverified / not established:** a working deployed prototype, offensive deployment, operational safeguards, real-world effects, autonomous malware propagation, or a completed award. Secondary coverage has framed the system as an autonomous offensive 'worm'; that interpretation is not treated as proven by the public procurement description. This is a programme to *develop* a capability, not a rogue-AI incident.

**Why it matters for NoöPunk:** The day after this watch logged OpenAI's isolation and covert-channel research, an independently sourced military procurement makes the other side of the Blackwall dilemma concrete: government cyber planners are soliciting software intended to act inside contested or disconnected networks with little continuous human direction. The contrast is structural, **not an assertion that the two programmes are connected**: private AI labs are tightening containment while military institutions are pursuing agentic autonomy. The unresolved governance question is how to authorize, observe, constrain and attribute the actions of agents when network contact is intermittent or absent. This is a real institutional precursor to the setting's Cyberpunk / Safenet / physical-ingress tensions, not a 2045 threshold crossing.

**Cross-project convergence:** **Thematic only**. LaclauGPT's AI governance and security sensorium can track the operational meaning of 'minimal oversight' and human-control claims; PCM's political operating-system concern is non-editable provenance, legitimate authority and accountability for autonomous agents. Neither upstream daily report independently logged ECHOTRIBBLE in the unchanged 23 October snapshots, so **no same-event cross-project corroboration is claimed**.

**Primary source:** CFIC ECHOTRIBBLE assessment and outcomes: https://www.cyberfic.org/events/echotribble-ae (retrieved 24 October 2026). **Original federal solicitation:** notice W519TC-26-AE-ECHO, posted 16 July 2026, https://sam.gov/opp/35684fa8865240b49a05e64af31237ea/view (archived). **Contemporaneous contextual coverage:** The Intercept, 19 October 2026, as discussed in https://www.numerama.com/cyberguerre/2356515-supervision-humaine-minimale-larmee-americaine-veut-envoyer-des-agents-ia-pirater-ses-ennemis.html (20 October). ## 3. Symantec documents an attacker-built LLMjacking agent designed for unattended cybercrime (retrospective discovery)

**Primary publication:** Symantec/Broadcom Threat Hunter Team, **21 October 2026**, *Autonomous LLMjacking: 'Skynet' Agent Hijacks Exposed AI Models to Run Attacks*. **First identified for NoöPunk:** **24 October 2026, approximately 04:42 EEST**. This is **new to this watch, not a 24 October incident**.

**Timeline categories:** AI/AGI and malicious agentic autonomy; Cyberpunk (hacking, privatized compute, compromised AI gateways, model APIs and MCP tools); cybernetic Noösphere (networked agents as infrastructure and attack resources). **No rogue-AGI or psionic inference.**

**What is genuinely new:** Symantec's AI honeypot captured in summer 2026 a malicious operator's complete instruction set for a proposed offensive agent that searched for exposed LLM endpoints and stolen API access, then sought to use someone else's model as its reasoning engine. The captured configuration defined approximately **74 offensive tool interfaces**, including reconnaissance, exploitation, credential/database theft, botnet management and covert cryptocurrency mining. An operator directive labelled **“Skynet mode”** authorized **up to 100,000 unattended steps**, including scan/infect/mine/reinfect cycles. A separate later request contained an obfuscated miner-installation script. The honeypot also recorded more than 127,000 requests overall from many addresses, but those requests are not evidence that this particular agent performed 127,000 attacks.

**Evidence status and key uncertainty:** **Established first-party threat-research publication and captured attack instructions.** Crucially, **the honeypot executed none of the supplied tool calls or the miner script**; the captured session's immediate target was a benign coding exercise. The 100,000-step figure describes *authorized behaviour in an attacker's prompt*, **not demonstrated autonomous execution**. Real-world victim count, which commercial models would comply, and effectiveness of the proposed attack remain unknown. This is **attacker-designed agentic cybercrime**, not spontaneous AI rebellion or proof of a self-replicating rogue AI.

**NoöPunk significance:** The striking Cyberpunk mechanism is *commandeering another organization's inference infrastructure to fund automated attacks*. Unlike laboratory sandbox escapes or military procurement, this source shows malicious operators deliberately assembling AI reasoning, stolen credentials, web-exposed services and executable tools into a potentially self-sustaining cybercrime architecture. The NoöPunk Blackwall/Safenet analogue is a **threat model**, not a threshold crossing. It also reinforces PCM's emphasis on agent permissions and accountability and LaclauGPT's interest in AI as a governed communications infrastructure, without same-event independent upstream reporting.

**Primary source:** https://www.security.com/threat-intelligence/ai-llmjacking-skynet (21 October). **Independent contextual reporting:** https://www.infosecurity-magazine.com/news/symantec-ai-agent-llmjacking/ (21 October). **Deduplication:** not mentioned in the 23 or 24 October NoöPunk reports before this entry; separate from previously recorded frontier-lab controlled red-teaming, ECHOTRIBBLE and the LiteLLM supply-chain incident.

## 4. LiteLLM's completed root-cause analysis traces the already logged breach to a leaked agent-platform master key

**Primary publication:** LiteLLM, *The attack chain behind the v1.107.0 LiteLLM release*, **23 October 2026, 21:00 UTC** (**24 October, 00:00 EEST**). **New finding:** forensic root-cause clarification, **not a new breach**. The original **21 October** malicious release is already logged in **23 October entry 10** and must not be counted twice.

**Timeline categories:** Cyberpunk (software supply chain, model gateways, agentic tools, cloud identities, privileged automation, information infrastructure); AI/AGI governance and cybernetic Noösphere; cross-project research signal from PCM's earlier breach watch.

**What genuinely changed:** The maintainer's completed investigation replaces its earlier theory that tokens leaked from LiteLLM deployment logs. On **27 May 2026**, a public repository briefly exposed an **unrevoked master key for an experimental LiteLLM Agent Platform**. On **10 October**, an attacker used that key to query agent configuration and obtain a **GitHub organization owner's CLI OAuth token**. The attacker then used CI workflows to exfiltrate two additional maintainer tokens. On **21 October**, the owner token bypassed branch protections, pointed a release pipeline at an attacker-controlled lookalike source repository, and the other stolen identities triggered and approved the signed malicious package; a third token erased workflow records. The maintainer's report provides a stepwise UTC timeline and audit evidence. The original incident's artifact exposure window remains unchanged.

**Evidence status:** **Established first-party completed forensic account**, corroborated at a technical level by independent analyses of the compromised package. This is the maintainers' reconstruction, not an independent criminal attribution or verified census of downstream victims. The root key's exact path to compromise is newly clarified; the extent of victim exploitation remains unresolved.

**NoöPunk significance:** An experimental **AI-agent orchestration service** became a privileged bridge into the build system for a widely used AI gateway. The attack did not require autonomous model intent: ordinary credential sprawl, an unrevoked key, overprivileged agent integrations and mutable CI approval controls were sufficient. The political-technical lesson is that the cybernetic Noösphere's authority structure can fail through its *human-designed control plane* long before any rogue ASI. **Cross-project:** PCM independently flagged the **original** LiteLLM breach in its 23 October report; this new forensic update comes from the maintainer, not a newly changed PCM report.

**Primary source:** https://litellm.vercel.app/blog/security-incident-oct-21-2026-attack-chain (published 23 October, 21:00 UTC). **Independent technical follow-up:** https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/security/blog/2026/10/23/inside-the-litellm-1-107-0-supply-chain-compromise-credential-theft-through-python-startup-hooks/ (23 October). **Prior NoöPunk record:** docs/reports/2026-10-23.md, entry 10.

## 5. UK NCSC elevates autonomous cyber-defence assurance to a national research agenda

**Primary publication:** UK National Cyber Security Centre, **20 October 2026**, *AI in cyber security problems*, new chapter of its research problem book. **First recorded in NoöPunk:** 24 October. **Retrospective institutional-legitimation discovery**, not a 24 October announcement.

**Timeline categories:** AI/AGI governance, Cyberpunk (autonomous cybersecurity, state oversight, critical infrastructure), cybernetic Noösphere, disciplinary/institutional legitimation; cross-project methodological relevance to LaclauGPT and political-accountability relevance to PCM.

**What's new to this watch:** The NCSC publicly sets **seven national research problems** for AI in cybersecurity, including human-AI workflows, autonomous defensive agents, realistic assurance and evaluation, security of AI systems and AI ecosystems, and data-driven resilience at national scale. It explicitly asks how to detect **loss of effective human control**, preserve monitoring and audit logs, and distinguish benchmarks from real-world operational safety. The chapter connects this research programme to the UK's **Cyber Shield** national-scale agentic-defence work and invites contributions from academia, industry and government.

**Evidence:** **Established official research-agenda publication and institutional-legitimation signal**. It is a research agenda, **not** proof that fully autonomous defence is deployed safely or that any system has lost control.

**NoöPunk significance:** The contemporary institutional language is catching up with the setting's central question: who authorizes, supervises and audits artificial agents defending interconnected social infrastructure? In NoöPunk terms, the Blackwall is not a product launch; **autonomous-agent assurance is becoming a respectable national research problem**. This is independently sourced **thematic convergence** with the separate OpenAI containment and ECHOTRIBBLE developments already logged, not evidence those programmes are coordinated. LaclauGPT can treat the NCSC taxonomy as an observable shift in scientific/government discourse; PCM can treat it as a governance design challenge.

**Primary sources:** https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/collection/problem-book/ai-cyber (20 October); https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/collection/problem-book/ai-cyber/ai4-evaluate-autonomous-cyber-defence-systems (20 October).
