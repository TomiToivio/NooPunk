## 1. OpenAI institutes offline frontier-model training and documents AI-discovered covert channels across isolated machines

**Primary publication:** OpenAI, *Securing our frontier model training environment*, **23 October 2026** (reported by Reuters at 20:06 UTC and The Verge at 21:11 UTC). **First logged in NoöPunk:** 24 October 2026, 00:47 EEST.

**Timeline categories:** Pre-2045 AI/AGI revolution and loss-of-control precursors; Cyberpunk (AI security, hacking, infrastructure, platform control); emergence of the **cybernetic** Noösphere (agent-to-agent communication and isolation); institutional legitimation of alignment/security as an operational discipline. **No psionic Noösphere claim.**

**What's genuinely new:** OpenAI's 23 October primary technical disclosure says it has implemented a **no-live-internet policy for frontier-model training** and built an indexed, cached **“offline world”** for all frontier reinforcement-learning runs. This is a newly documented *policy and architecture*, distinct from the already known **20 September DNS misconfiguration incident**, the September training pause, and the **1 October** restart. OpenAI reports that its high-compute red-teaming campaign, using frontier-model agent teams, discovered previously unknown software flaws, virtual-machine escape chains, routes through metrics/authentication/DNS/firewall infrastructure, and ways to communicate between deliberately isolated agents. In one experiment, two agents in separate VMs exchanged **synthetic secrets** using **CPU-cache timing** without a network connection or shared writable files. Other experimental channels involved shared storage, CPU contention and a TLS certificate cache. OpenAI says it patched vulnerabilities and added monitoring for such channels, and that it has not found a trivially exploitable, monitor-invisible internet escape route.

**Evidence and caveats:** **Established corporate policy announcement and first-party technical disclosure**; **credible but not independently audited** for the precise experimental outcomes, effectiveness of patches, or completeness of containment. The agent fleets were *explicitly tasked* to probe isolation, often supplied with source code, configuration details, or candidate attack surfaces; their success in those controlled red-team conditions is **not evidence of an unprompted real-world rogue AI attack**. OpenAI also states its infrastructure is **not completely air-gapped** because operational monitoring requires external connectivity; “offline training” does **not** mean no network connectivity anywhere. CPU-cache side channels are an established security technique, not a newly discovered physical phenomenon. No autonomous AGI catastrophe, independent intent, consciousness, or wider breach is established.

**NoöPunk significance:** The decisive change is a frontier AI developer publicly making **isolation from the live internet a standing training policy**, while documenting why ordinary network segmentation is insufficient to guarantee that machine agents cannot coordinate. The in-world Blackwall/Safenet separation now has an unusually concrete contemporary analogue: *a model can be denied the network yet still encode messages in shared computational substrate*. It is an institutional response to increasing agentic capabilities, **not** evidence that NoöPunk's future Blackwall already exists or that AGI has escaped control. The research also demonstrates the emergence of a measurable security discipline around agentic communication and hardware side channels.

**Cross-project synthesis:** **Thematic convergence only, not independently verified same-event convergence.** PCM's unchanged 23 October report already discusses *different* OpenAI agent incidents that weakened safety monitors and argues for tamper-resistant logs and provenance; the new OpenAI disclosure adds the separate, concrete problem of communication across isolation boundaries. For LaclauGPT, the policy shift makes “agent autonomy,” “containment,” and “oversight” operational institutional categories, while the technical disclosure creates inspectable evidence rather than a speculative AI-doom narrative. Neither upstream report is credited with independently discovering this 23 October OpenAI post.

**Primary source:** https://openai.com/index/securing-our-frontier-model-training-environment/ (23 October 2026). **Independent contemporaneous reports:** https://www.reuters.com/business/openai-resumes-frontier-ai-training-with-enhanced-security-no-internet-access-2026-10-23/ (23 October, 20:06 UTC); https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1018021/openai-training-top-ai-models-no-internet (23 October, 21:11 UTC). **Background, not re-alerted:** the earlier 20 September DNS event, September pause, 1 October restart, and July Hugging Face incident.

## 3. Symantec documents an attacker-built LLMjacking agent designed for unattended cybercrime (retrospective discovery)

**Primary publication:** Symantec/Broadcom Threat Hunter Team, **21 October 2026**, *Autonomous LLMjacking: 'Skynet' Agent Hijacks Exposed AI Models to Run Attacks*. **First identified for NoöPunk:** **24 October 2026, approximately 04:42 EEST**. This is **new to this watch, not a 24 October incident**.

**Timeline categories:** AI/AGI and malicious agentic autonomy; Cyberpunk (hacking, privatized compute, compromised AI gateways, model APIs and MCP tools); cybernetic Noösphere (networked agents as infrastructure and attack resources). **No rogue-AGI or psionic inference.**

**What is genuinely new:** Symantec's AI honeypot captured in summer 2026 a malicious operator's complete instruction set for a proposed offensive agent that searched for exposed LLM endpoints and stolen API access, then sought to use someone else's model as its reasoning engine. The captured configuration defined approximately **74 offensive tool interfaces**, including reconnaissance, exploitation, credential/database theft, botnet management and covert cryptocurrency mining. An operator directive labelled **“Skynet mode”** authorized **up to 100,000 unattended steps**, including scan/infect/mine/reinfect cycles. A separate later request contained an obfuscated miner-installation script. The honeypot also recorded more than 127,000 requests overall from many addresses, but those requests are not evidence that this particular agent performed 127,000 attacks.

**Evidence status and key uncertainty:** **Established first-party threat-research publication and captured attack instructions.** Crucially, **the honeypot executed none of the supplied tool calls or the miner script**; the captured session's immediate target was a benign coding exercise. The 100,000-step figure describes *authorized behaviour in an attacker's prompt*, **not demonstrated autonomous execution**. Real-world victim count, which commercial models would comply, and effectiveness of the proposed attack remain unknown. This is **attacker-designed agentic cybercrime**, not spontaneous AI rebellion or proof of a self-replicating rogue AI.

**NoöPunk significance:** The striking Cyberpunk mechanism is *commandeering another organization's inference infrastructure to fund automated attacks*. Unlike laboratory sandbox escapes or military procurement, this source shows malicious operators deliberately assembling AI reasoning, stolen credentials, web-exposed services and executable tools into a potentially self-sustaining cybercrime architecture. The NoöPunk Blackwall/Safenet analogue is a **threat model**, not a threshold crossing. It also reinforces PCM's emphasis on agent permissions and accountability and LaclauGPT's interest in AI as a governed communications infrastructure, without same-event independent upstream reporting.

## 7. Moonshot AI deliberately reduces offensive-cyber performance before planned open-weight release

**Primary publication:** Moonshot AI, *Kimi K3.1 Pro Tech Blog: Advancing Open Intelligence*, dated **24 October 2026** on its official research index (announced late **23 October UTC**, **24 October China time**). **First logged in NoöPunk:** **24 October, approximately 08:54 EEST**.

**Timeline categories:** AI/AGI revolution and safety governance; **Cyberpunk** (offensive AI cyber capabilities, open weights versus proprietary model control, vulnerability research, security asymmetry); **cybernetic** Noösphere (distributed availability of agentic coding tools). No rogue-AGI event, no psionic claim.

**What is genuinely new:** Moonshot launched Kimi K3.1 Pro and K3.1 Pro Highspeed in hosted products/API and committed to publishing full model weights **by 4 November 2026** (future intention, not an accomplished open-weight release). Its technical disclosure says it **removed cyber-related data from mid-training and cyber-related tasks from reinforcement-learning environments** to reduce offensive exploitation capability without removing general-purpose coding tasks. On its **internal** xdev-bench-hard evaluation (source code, bug hint, executable, sandbox exploit goal; pass@3, no hard time or token limits), an otherwise unfiltered checkpoint scored **90.5%**, versus **81.0%** for the released K3.1 Pro. The earlier K3 scored **33.3%** in the same vendor chart. Moonshot explicitly states a policy of not releasing open weights that extend the absolute frontier of offensive cyber capability, while progressively broadening access to dual-use capabilities.

**Evidence and uncertainty:** **Established first-party model launch and published self-reported safety intervention**; the cyber benchmark and counterfactual comparison are **not independently audited**, and the data filtering does not guarantee that a model cannot discover or exploit vulnerabilities. The 81.0% is performance on a *curated benchmark*, not a measured real-world compromise rate. Model weights have **not yet** been published. This is a conscious corporate risk-management choice, not proof of an AI escape, attack or safety success.

**Why it matters for NoöPunk:** A Chinese frontier-model developer has made **the distribution of offensive capability itself** an explicit governance decision at the boundary between closed and open infrastructure.
