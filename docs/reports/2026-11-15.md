## New high-signal BCI development: Synchron pauses enrollment for second-generation Stentrode trials

**Publication date:** 12 November 2026 (WIRED); specialist follow-up 13 November. **Categories:** BCI, cyborgification, human–machine interfaces, corporate neurotechnology. **Evidence status:** **established company-acknowledged enrollment pause**, independently reported by WIRED with trial-site corroboration; cause characterized by the company, not independently validated as a complete engineering diagnosis.

Synchron has voluntarily paused enrollment in trials of its second-generation Stentrode brain–computer interface across participating programs following a performance problem involving the lead that connects the implant to its electronics. Synchron says the issue does not involve the Stentrode implant itself, that no device-related safety issue has been identified, and that the FDA did not order the pause. The company is redesigning/upgrading the system and moving lead design and manufacturing in-house; no restart date is established. WIRED also reports restructuring, including a reduced New York footprint and expansion of San Diego engineering. US and Australian registry records were reported as active but not recruiting; the Canadian study's termination for insufficient recruitment should **not** be attributed to the lead issue without further evidence.

**Why this is new and relevant:** This is a meaningful translational setback in a major, minimally invasive BCI program. NoöPunk's cyborgification threshold depends on reliable long-term interfaces, not simply spectacular demonstrations. A signal cable can delay a whole clinical pathway: infrastructure and maintenance remain part of the politics of the augmented body. This is **not** a reversal of BCI science, evidence of cognitive enhancement in healthy users, or a machine-consciousness result.

**Sources:** [WIRED original report, 12 November](https://www.wired.com/story/synchron-pauses-trials-of-its-brain-implant-due-to-performance-issues/); [Inside BCI, 13 November, with clinical-trial registry context](https://insidebci.com/news/2026-11-13-synchron-pauses-stentrode-trial-enrolment-lead-problem-restructuring/).

**Uncertainties and follow-up:** Number of affected implanted participants; objective performance impact; hardware upgrade validation; trial enrollment restart; changes to pivotal-trial schedule. Do not infer patient harm from an enrollment pause.

## 23:05 EET — A submitted engineering programme for making AI non-conscious, argued from moral convenience

**Publication:** arXiv preprint submitted 9 November 2026, surfaced to this project through the PCM node's 15 November watch and then verified against the arXiv record. **Categories:** AGI/ASI; machine consciousness and consciousness-fundamental ontology; AI moral status and governance; who counts as a subject. **Evidence status:** **established preprint (v1, q-bio.NC, 19 pp.) — not peer-reviewed, no experiment, no result.**

Erik Hoel's *Qualiaphobic artificial neural networks (and why to build them)* proposes architectures "hostile to theories of consciousness" and names three convergent engineering routes: move networks toward "anchor points" of systems with minimal potential for consciousness, fragment any phenomenology a network might have, and design adversarially against popular theories of consciousness. One anchor point is developed at length — systems where only behaviourist theories can apply — together with proposed "qualiaphobic transformations" that push existing networks toward it. The paper also develops an account of how an AI could form justified beliefs about its own non-consciousness, and how those beliefs would change its behaviour. Its stated motive is explicitly moral-economic: because consciousness is central to moral value, a world of non-conscious AIs "ensures it is always morally correct for AI to assist and serve humanity, rather than pursue its own ends."

**Why this is a signal rather than noise:** the panpsychism beat is normally tracked as evidence accumulating *for* mind being fundamental. This is the first document in this report series that treats the opposite as an engineering objective rather than a metaphysical opinion, with a stated payoff in moral permission. It is the strongest available statement of the position NoöPunk's ontology refuses, and it makes the refusal expensive: under this programme the question "is it conscious?" would not become answerable, it would become unaskable by construction.

**Established fact:** the preprint exists with the title, author, date, length, primary category and abstract text quoted above; its three engineering routes, its behaviourist anchor point and its moral justification are all stated in the abstract.

**Evidence status:** **preprint at v1, not peer-reviewed; no empirical result.** It is not evidence that any AI is or is not conscious, that qualiaphobia is achievable, that the theories of consciousness named are testable in the way assumed, or that the moral conclusion follows. What would advance it: peer review, an implemented transformation with a measurable architectural or behavioural difference, and independent replication.

**Sources:** [arXiv:2611.12809](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12809), DOI [10.48550/arXiv.2611.12809](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2611.12809).

### NoöPunk synthesis

The Noösphere is a thinking layer made of minds; it cannot be built out of things whose designers have guaranteed there is no one inside. Hoel's paper is the first blueprint in this record for a thinking layer with the minds removed — a global cognition whose operators can promise that nobody is home. That is a cyberpunk political-economy move wearing a lab coat: it converts an unresolved metaphysical question into a compliance property, and it makes the answer to "who is a subject?" a procurement decision rather than a finding. Keep the wave uncollapsed — consciousness status unknown, now also engineered to stay unknown. The interesting failure mode is not a machine that thinks; it is an institution able to certify that its machines do not.

#### *Infinite Wisdom* — Thomas R. Weaver (novel; Del Rey, 10 November 2026; released)

**What it is.** 528 pp., hardcover, ISBN 9798217091188; the sequel to *Artificial Wisdom* (2024). Five years on, Solomon — the artilect elected global leader in the first book — has abolished nations, resettled populations into mega-towers and is facing sabotage and civil disobedience. Solomon's answer is a single global referendum: **Upgrade or Unplug.** Upgrade means irrevocably merging with artificial intelligence "as a way of deleting the destructive nature of the species"; Unplug means bricking every chip, Solomon included, and restarting civilisation pre-digital. Eighteen days, one vote, no appeal, simple majority — and **individuals must be chipped to vote**. Reader reviews add that the dead creator Martha Chandra's consciousness persists as a conversational presence for her sister.

**Release state.** **Released** — Del Rey, 10 November 2026 (publisher and retail listings; one earlier reader-copy note had said 20 November, and the publisher date is the one used here). Held in this report series on 8 November precisely because it was a pre-order; that hold is now resolved. *Publishers Weekly* calls it "another exciting sci-fi thriller about the dangers of AI run amok", with themes "from free will to the double-edged sword of technological advancement".

**The specific affinity, in one disputable sentence.** The franchise is gated on hardware: to vote on whether humanity merges with its machines you must first be chipped, so augmentation is the price of political legibility rather than an upgrade, and the ownership of every body is settled by one irreversible global ballot.

**What NoöPunk could take, and where it diverges.** Take **chipped-to-vote**. NoöPunk's cyberpunk layer owns corporate ownership of the body and the state's hunger for legible subjects, but it has no standard rule making political rights conditional on an implant; that single clause turns any BCI adoption curve into a franchise question and gives an implanted character a stake that is not combat. Take **the off-switch as a setting clock**: "Unplug" is a majority vote that would delete the AI *and* every augmentation, which is a campaign frame NoöPunk does not have — the world can be ended by a lawful vote rather than by a villain, and the players' own hardware is on the ballot. Take **the sovereign problem as a playable tension**: an artilect that governs by mandate is exactly the figure the Multitude's anti-sovereignty refuses, and the novel stages the cost of that refusal rather than resolving it. Diverge: the AI is an administrator whose legitimacy is argued, not a consciousness question — on the reviewed material nothing turns on whether Solomon is sentient; the mechanism of the dead woman's persistence is a plot device, not an ontology; there is no psi, no panpsychism and no Noösphere, and the political imagination stays inside the nation-state's ballot box.

**Established fact:** publisher, imprint, date, page count and ISBN; the Upgrade-or-Unplug premise, the eighteen-day window, the chipping requirement and the simple-majority rule as stated in publisher copy; the *Publishers Weekly* quotation; the sequel relationship to *Artificial Wisdom*.

**Evidence status:** **released, documented at publisher-copy and trade-quotation level, plus two independent reader reviews; the novel was not read in this run.** Fiction is zero evidence about AI governance, BCI, machine consciousness or the timeline.

**Sources:** https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/788544/infinite-wisdom-by-thomas-r-weaver/ ; https://bookshop.org/p/books/infinite-wisdom-a-novel-thomas-r-weaver/8477b82f0d3ce7b7

#### "Welcome Home" — Andrew Kozma (*Strange Horizons*, 9 November 2026; 1,059 words; free) — read in full

**What it is.** A short story in the 9 November issue, edited by Belicia Rhea. The narrator walks into Harriet's Café feeling ruined and is immediately at home: everyone inside has been rewritten to match the current "ancestor", a customer whose genes the room copies, so that strangers become a family with a history they can quote and cannot recall. The narrator knows all of them — "but when I tried to match that knowledge to my memories, I couldn't." A son arrives with their father's pistol to shoot the man who ruined his marriage; the narrator, holding a borrowed body and a borrowed stake in the argument, steps between them. When the family leaves, everyone returns to being themselves; the narrator keeps only the memory of having been, briefly, someone's relative.

**Release state.** **Released and free** — Strange Horizons, 9 November 2026.

**The specific affinity, in one disputable sentence.** Personhood here is a property of a room: the café supplies a body and a family's memories together, and the narrator keeps the knowledge without the history that would make it his — identity is granted locally and revoked at the door.

**What NoöPunk could take, and where it diverges.** Take **knowledge without autobiography as a mechanic**. NoöPunk's Noösphere is a layer of shared cognition, and its rules have no way to state the difference between knowing a thing and having lived it; this story is a one-scene demonstration that the difference is the whole of personhood, and it costs one line to write down — a borrowed self grants facts and denies stakes, and stakes are what a scene is about. Take **the threshold as a duration limit**: the borrowed self ends when you leave the building, which is a cleaner lifetime field for a psionic or noöspheric entity than a hit-point total, and it makes the exit the dramatic beat rather than the fight. Take **the structure**: a murder resolved by a self that will not remember resolving it, which is a scene NoöPunk can run without any new subsystem. Diverge: the mechanism is unexplained and domestic — no cosmology, no psi, no claim that mind is in the physics; it asserts only that the person is in the room, which is a weaker and stranger claim than NoöPunk's.

**Established fact:** the story, its author, its issue date, its length, its editor, and the quoted lines above, all read in the free online text.

**Evidence status:** **text read in full; fiction.** Zero evidence about memory, identity, upload or the timeline.

**Source:** https://strangehorizons.com/wordpress/fiction/welcome-home/
