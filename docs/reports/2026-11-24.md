## Human–LLM neural communication: ChatBCI-Assist

**Date:** Rutgers announcement 20 November 2026; newly surfaced through PCM on 24 November. **Categories:** BCI, cyborgification, cybernetic Noösphere, human–AI communication. **Evidence:** university research announcement describing a published IEEE study, not independent replication. A noninvasive EEG P300 interface lets users select letters or locally generated LLM phrases. Researchers report nearly twice the exact-copy speed and nearly three times the meaning-based communication speed versus a dictionary-based predictor. Tests were with healthy participants; ALS clinical testing is planned. This detects deliberate selection, not unrestricted thoughts. The local model avoids requiring cloud transmission. NoöPunk significance: the human authorizes meaning while a machine supplies linguistic form; semantic fidelity, correction, refusal and authorship become measurable governance questions. Not PSI or evidence of shared phenomenal consciousness.

#### *Ghost-Eye* — Amitav Ghosh (novel; John Murray, April 2026; Farrar, Straus and Giroux, 16 June 2026; 336 pp., $29) — late catch, judged on three reviews and one long critical essay

**What it is.** Calcutta, September 1969. Three-year-old Varsha Gupta, born into a strict vegetarian Marwari family, suddenly demands fish — and then demands it in Bengali, insisting she is a fisherman's daughter who was killed by a snakebite in an earlier life. The family's psychologist, Shoma Bose, who works on "cases of the reincarnation type", takes the case and establishes it forensically through the one thing the child could not have learned: an encyclopaedic, bodily knowledge of fish and of the Sundarbans' flora and fauna. Decades later her nephew Dinu, sorting her papers in Brooklyn during the pandemic, finds the case file and goes looking for Varsha, by then a woman whose knowledge is the last thing standing between a mangrove forest and a coal plant. The title belongs to Tipu, an environmentalist with heterochromia — the "ghost-eye" of South Asian folklore, thought to mark clairvoyance.

**Release state.** **Released.** John Murray (UK) in April 2026, Farrar, Straus and Giroux (US) on 16 June 2026, 336 pp., ISBN 9780374298395. Reviewed in *Kirkus* (posted 23 March 2026), the *Washington Independent Review of Books* (Sally Shivnan, 28 July 2026), *Locus* (Gary K. Wolfe) and at length in *Outlook India* (Akshat Upadhyay, 20 May 2026). Not previously covered in this lane.

**The specific affinity, in one disputable sentence.** *Outlook*'s reading is that the novel argues a continuum of consciousness distributed across species, matter and ecosystems rather than confined to the human mind, and that it locates the obstacle to that continuum not in evidence but in who is authorised to count a claim as knowledge — so the paradigm shift arrives as an epistemic-political event, not a discovery.

**What NoöPunk could take, and where it diverges.** Four things.

- **The keeper of the file.** Shoma Bose is an institution of one: a clinician who documents the anomalous for forty years with no agency, no budget and no clearance. NoöPunk's psi infrastructure currently consists of secret programmes and contact cults; a records-keeper whose archive is the plot engine is the third and cheapest option, and it makes the setting's memory a person rather than a database.
- **Admissibility as the mechanic.** The novel's political claim is that a phenomenon's reality is settled by the tribunal that agrees to hear it — the same claim the *Outlook* essay makes about Jagadish Chandra Bose's plant experiments, inadmissible in Europe precisely because they resonated with traditions European science had already classified as primitive. As a table rule: an anomalous event has a *recorded* status, not a true one, and status changes with whoever signs the report.
- **Continuity without upload.** Two timelines are joined by a memory the mind cannot have had, and the body — not the brain — is where it is stored. That is a fourth model of identity continuity for a resleeving setting, alongside copy, transfer and reconstruction.
- **A cost structure the setting lacks.** The knowledge that saves the forest is a dead fisherman's knowledge of fish. The competence the book prizes is not psi but expertise that arrived by the wrong route — which is the least romantic and most usable version of a panpsychic premise.

Divergence: the world's rules do not break for the reader. Two of the three trade reviews call the book magical realism — *Kirkus* "magical realism with all-too-real climate change", the *Washington Independent* likewise — so the anomalous is continuous with the everyday rather than a rupture in the physics, and the novel's target is Cartesian dualism and colonial epistemology, not the hard problem. There is no cyberpunk, no Noösphere and no contact; and a novel that asserts a continuum of consciousness is not a test of one. The philosophical layer is also, on *Outlook*'s account, argued rather than dramatised, and the ending resolves by deus ex machina — a structural weakness rather than an evidential one.

**Established fact:** author, both publishers, both publication months, page count, price, ISBN; Varsha's demand and claimed previous life; Shoma Bose's specialism and her forensic method; Dinu's discovery of the file and his search; the Sundarbans and the coal plant; Tipu's heterochromia and the folkloric reading of the "ghost-eye"; the JC Bose reference; the *Kirkus* and *Washington Independent* "magical realism" characterisations and the *Outlook* characterisation of the argument — as reviews, not as text read end to end. **Not** established: the prose, how the ending's deus ex machina resolves, or whether the panpsychist thesis belongs to the novel's narrator, its characters or its author.

**Evidence status:** review level; fiction. Nothing here documents reincarnation, clairvoyance, plant sentience or a scientific paradigm shift. The one datum worth keeping is that three professional reviews of the same book disagree about what kind of claim it is making — a thesis or a mode — which is exactly the dispute NoöPunk's contact layer is built to stage.

#### *Not Yet Gods* — Djuna, tr. Jihyun Park and Gord Sellar (linked-story collection / mosaic novel; Kaya Press; release state unresolved) — judged on publisher and translator copy, not on the text

**What it is.** The Korean original, *Ajigeun sin i aniya* (2013), is a mosaic novel of interlinked stories. After a nuclear explosion set off in North Korea, an ordinary South Korean high-school classroom becomes ground zero for the discovery of a new energy source: children who conduct and amplify the telepathic and telekinetic powers of those around them. The race is then on to hunt them down and harness them as human "batteries" for "everything from electricity to space travel to facelifts". The publisher's own summary states the consequence plainly: the primary energy source running the world is housed in teenagers' brains and bodies, "subject to hormonal shifts and eddies, family loyalties, and interpersonal conflicts". The stories cycle through detective procedural, fantasy horror and speculative SF to interrogate power, disability and illusion.

**Release state — unresolved, and that is part of the record.** SFT's 2026 roll gives **11 August 2026**; *The Blossom Review* gives 24 September; *Philly Chapbook Review* lists 13 October; ARTBOOK/D.A.P. still lists it "Forthcoming 12/29/2026 — this title is not yet published in the U.S."; Kaya's own product page still reads "Forthcoming July 2025". SFT's May 2026 review of Djuna's *Everything Good Dies Here* noted that *Not Yet Gods* "has experienced a few unexplained delays". No review of the book itself was found in this sweep, and no excerpt was read. Translator-announced first edition of 2013 original; 224 pp.; ISBN 9781935717119; afterword by Djuna and Gord Sellar.

**The specific affinity, in one disputable sentence.** Psi here is not a secret discipline but an energy infrastructure: the telepath is a grid asset, so the political question the book poses is who owns the battery and what a society does when the battery is a moody adolescent.

**What NoöPunk could take, and where it diverges.** Two things, both structural rather than cosmological.

- **The battery clause.** One sentence converts psionics into a labour and ownership question — no patents, no implants, just a body the grid cannot do without and cannot fully predict. It is the cleanest version of the augmentation political economy this lane has found applied to psi rather than to chrome: legibility to the state is replaced by dependence of the state on the body.
- **Mosaic form as a transition model.** A society-wide psionic shift narrated through genre changes, each story the same event seen from a different institution, is a formal answer to a problem NoöPunk has: a threshold crossing is not one scene, and a linked anthology can show four institutions misreading the same children.

Divergence: the psi is radiation-induced mutation — a physical accident, not a feature of the physics — so there is no panpsychic ground, and the book's questions are power, disability and illusion rather than ontology. The state here is a predator and a purchaser, not a metaphysics. And the judgement is provisional in the strongest sense: this is blurb copy, not a text, and the repeated delay means the book may reach readers materially different from its description.

**Established fact:** the 2013 Korean original and its title; translators, publisher, page count, ISBN, afterword; the classroom, the nuclear explosion in North Korea, the conducting children, the "batteries", the range of uses, the emotional-instability clause, the mosaic structure and genre cycling; the conflicting release dates across five listings; the translator's and reviewer's recorded note of unexplained delays. **Not** established: whether the book is on sale anywhere today, the mechanics of the psi, the quality of the translation, or any review judgement.

**Evidence status:** publisher and translator level; fiction; release state genuinely uncertain. Zero evidence about psi, telepathy, energy, or any real psionic economy. A fictional battery is a design argument about where to put the cost, which is the only thing this lane takes from it.
