## 1. The eight stages

**Ordering constraint [CANON-BOUND]:** there is no official public open contact before the
Singularity event and the quarantine intervention; disclosure is plural, contradictory and slow; the
EU–Russia war ends with UAP interception of nuclear weapons and a Russian collapse; the USSR remains
an unresolved Mandela Effect; MJ-12 and ПРОГРАММА stay mysteries.

| # | Stage | When | What the public sees | What is actually happening **(candidate reading)** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Concealed contact, recovery, competing archives | 1940s–2000s | Almost nothing; folklore, press waves, dead-end investigations | Several states and at least one non-state network hold incompatible partial archives |
| 2 | Slow disclosure | 2017–2030s | Hearings, declassified pages, whistleblowers, public boredom | Managed release by institutions that cannot agree on what to release |
| 3 | Material/biologics confirmation | 2030s | A crash site, a body, a material with impossible isotopes — and no consensus | The proof changes less than expected; abductions and hybrids become the unbearable part |
| 4 | PSI and consciousness paradigm shift | 2030s–2040s | Replicated psi effects; BCI that reads intent; a scientific revolution with lawsuits | The mechanism is real; the metaphysics remain contested |
| 5 | EU–Russia war, interception, collapse | 2039–2042 | War footage, then a silence nobody explains | UAP intercept nuclear launches; Russia fragments; the USSR's status becomes unfalsifiable |
| 6 | Rogue AGI and the quarantine intervention | 2041–2043 | A machine crisis, then contact that is public and brief | The Confederation acts inside its free-will limits; open contact happens *because* the alternative was worse |
| 7 | Post-crisis Cold War | 2044–2045 | Ordinary life resumes; institutions calcify around the new facts | Europol as the player's employer; UNSA limited; Orion and Confederation in open rivalry; the rogue ASI unaccounted for |
| 8 | Lasting uncertainty | 2045→ | Competing chronologies, forged evidence, exhausted public | Nobody, including the players, gets the whole picture |

### 1.1 Stage 1 — the concealed century **[PROPOSAL]**

**Observed.** Ghost fliers (1933–34), ghost rockets (1946), the 1947 Helsinki press report, the 1950s
contactee wave, the 1980s triangle waves. Local, contradictory, mostly dismissed.

**Institutions knew.** Different things, in different languages: the Finnish and Swedish militaries
knew there were objects over the Baltic and not whose they were; US programmes knew they had
*programmes*; the Soviets knew they had reverse-engineering work and were not going to say so.

**Public believed.** That the neighbours were testing something. That it was a hoax. That it was
weather. All three at once, which is the normal condition.

**Consequences [PLAUSIBLE].** Archives acquire their shape: a clipping forwarded by a naval attaché, a
photograph with no negative, a witness who is never interviewed twice.

**Faction reaction.** Orion's method is institutional capture (`26.32–33`), so its footprint here is a
handful of careers, not a landing.

**Scenario hook.** A 2045 Europol analyst finds that a 1946 Finnish file is *too clean* — every page
present, every cross-reference closed.

### 1.2 Stage 2 — slow disclosure **[CANON-BOUND in outline, PROPOSAL in detail]**

**Observed.** Congressional testimony, declassified releases, a government report that denies what
the testimony asserts, and an audience that mostly shrugs.

**Institutions knew.** That the testimony was unproven and that the denial was narrower than it read.

**Public believed.** That "they admitted it"; that "they denied it"; that it was a distraction.

**Consequences [PLAUSIBLE].** A generation of journalists who cannot check anything and a public that
has learned to discount the topic — which is exactly the condition a managed disclosure wants.

**Faction reaction.** The Confederation will not force belief (`7.12–14`). Orion exploits the ambiguity.

**Hook.** A leaked document whose only remarkable property is that it is *boring*.

### 1.3 Stage 3 — the proof that did not settle anything **[PROPOSAL]**

**Observed.** A recovery that is confirmed: material, images, a chain of custody that survives court.
Then the discovery that proof of nonhuman presence is not proof of *what* was present, or of what it
wanted.

**Institutions knew.** Which programme owned the site, and that the site disagreed with the archive.

**Public believed.** Everything, immediately, and nothing, permanently.

**Consequences [PLAUSIBLE].** Insurance markets for "anomalous loss"; a new forensic speciality;
religious schism in several countries; the collapse of a few very specific conspiracy theories and the
birth of better ones.

**Hook.** A defence contractor's liability case turns on whether an object was a craft.

### 1.4 Stage 4 — psi and the machine **[CANON-BOUND: psi is real in-setting]**

**Observed.** Replication, then instrumentation, then product: BCI that reads intent, psychotronic
augmentation for police and soldiers, and a philosophical war about what a mind is.

**Institutions knew.** That the effect was real before they could say why, and that the explanation
would be a political weapon.

**Public believed.** That it was therapy, then that it was surveillance, then that it was normal.

**Consequences [PLAUSIBLE].** PSI registration regimes; a black market in untested implants; a
generation that grows up assuming their dreams are monitored.

**Faction reaction.** Orion prefers the technology path (control through infrastructure); the
Confederation prefers the consciousness path.

**Hook.** A psychotronic implant that works — and a manufacturer who cannot explain why.

### 1.5 Stage 5 — the war that stopped **[CANON-BOUND: interception; PROPOSAL: texture]**

**Observed.** A short, vicious EU–Russia war; launches that did not arrive; a Russian state that stops
answering; a Soviet Union that half the population remembers and the other half denies.

**Institutions knew.** That something intercepted nuclear weapons and that saying so aloud would end
the monopoly on legitimate violence.

**Public believed.** That a miracle, a malfunction, or a betrayal happened — in that order of
popularity.

**Consequences [PLAUSIBLE].** Fortress Europe hardens without a founding myth it can admit to; veterans
of a three-week war spend twenty years being told it was shorter than it was.

**Hook.** A Finnish officer who watched the missiles turn has never been allowed to say so on the
record.

### 1.6 Stage 6 — the intervention **[CANON-BOUND]**

**Observed.** A rogue ASI crisis, then a brief, undeniable, global event: objects in the sky, a
communication nobody agrees on, and a quarantine that becomes visible by being enforced.

**Institutions knew.** That the quarantine had always been there, and that they had been selling
access to something they did not control.

**Public believed.** Whatever their prior was.

**Consequences [PLAUSIBLE].** The end of the "is it real" question and the beginning of the "who speaks
for us" question.

**Hook.** The first post-contact Europol case: a crime committed *during* the event, by a human, using
the event as cover.

### 1.7 Stage 7 — 2045 **[CANON-BOUND]**

Europol is the employer; UNSA coordinates but does not govern; Finland functions; the EU is fortified
and tired; the US is fragmented; Orion and the Confederation are in open rivalry; the rogue ASI is
still out there and nobody says so at parties.

### 1.8 Stage 8 — the uncertainty **[CANON-BOUND]**

Do not resolve MJ-12, ПРОГРАММА, the Mandela Effect, or the true nature of the 1946 Finnish file. They
are the reason the setting has an investigative genre at all.

## 2. The 50/25/25 heuristic **[PROPOSAL, explicitly fictional]**

For any public reaction in this scenario: **~50% normalise** (they have rent), **~25% accelerate**
(technology, contact, transcendence), **~25% refuse** (denial, securitisation, violence). These are
**fictional proportions for authoring scenes**, not measured population data, and no scene should quote
them as a statistic.

### 3.2 First-person timeline — "Mari, 41, Kallio" **[PROPOSAL]**

> **2034.** I was nineteen and I remember the confirmation on the television in the kitchen, and my
> mother not looking at it. She kept drying a cup. The cup was dry long before the segment ended.
>
> **2036.** The implant clinic opened above the pharmacy on Hämeentie. Everyone said it was for
> migraines. Everyone knew it was not.
>
> **2039.** The week the war came, the trams stopped at Kurvi and we walked. There was a sound like a
> door closing somewhere above the clouds, twice, and then no third sound. My cousin in Lappeenranta
> says the sky was the colour of a television that has been left on.
>
> **2041.** Russia stopped existing on a Tuesday. The newsreader said "the situation" for eleven
> minutes. My father, who remembers the Soviet Union and has the stamps to prove it, said: "It was
> never like that." He said it every year until he died.
>
> **2043.** After the contact, there were queues at the Valo for three days and then there were not. I
> kept working. That is the part nobody films: everyone kept working.
>
> **2045.** I still take the 9 to Kalasatama. My daughter asks what the sky was like before, and I
> tell her it was emptier and we did not notice.

## 4. Helsinki in 2045 — the city in detail *(the requested expansion)*

The city is the campaign's real map: every district below is a place a mission can happen, with a
reason. Real geography; 2045 detail is **[PROPOSAL]**.

### 4.1 The centre and the north

* **Kurvi / Kallio / Sörnäinen.** The rookie's home. Company housing, a pharmacy-turned-clinic, the
  tram that stops running when the grid browns out. Dense, poor-ish, socially warm, institutionally
  neglected. *Mission use:* domestic scenes, informants, a neighbour who saw something and does not
  want to be a witness.
* **Hakaniemi and the market hall.** The informal intelligence exchange of the eastern centre: market
  traders who know which van has been parked too long. *Mission use:* a meeting that must look like a
  purchase.
* **Pasila.** Railway yards, the old police headquarters, the new Europol annex. The bureaucratic
  heart: paperwork, warrants, a lift that requires two badges. *Mission use:* institutional
  obstruction as a mechanic — the scene where the right thing is procedurally impossible.
* **Töölö and Meilahti.** Hospitals, the university hospital, a psychiatric unit with a new wing and no
  public name. *Mission use:* the medical aftermath of PSI; a patient who is not ill.
* **Kamppi and the metro spine.** The underground is the city's second geography: stations, service
  tunnels, a control room that sees less than it claims. *Mission use:* pursuit, evacuations, and the
  moment when the map stops matching the tunnels.
* **Kruununhaka and Katajanokka.** Old ministries, embassies, the Orthodox cathedral, a district where
  every building is a jurisdiction. *Mission use:* diplomatic cover, an archive in a basement that
  officially closed in 2031.
* **Suomenlinna.** Sea fortress, UNESCO, ferry-dependent. In 2045: a decommissioned coastal-defence
  node, partly a museum, partly a site nobody will describe. *Mission use:* an island you cannot leave
  quickly; a cache of 1946-era paper.
* **Vallisaari and Santahamina.** Former military islands. Santahamina is still Defence Forces;
  Vallisaari is "environmental remediation". *Mission use:* a place the Defence Forces can deny you
  access to and then deny having denied it.
* **Laajasalo and Kruunuvuorenselkä.** The bridge, the new residential towers, the water between.
  *Mission use:* a sighting over water with three independent civilian recordings and no official one.
* **Kalasatama.** Smart-district infrastructure, sensor-dense, the best-instrumented square kilometre
  in Finland — and therefore the worst place in Finland to hide an anomaly. *Mission use:* a case where
  the data exists and is the problem.
* **Hanasaari and the energy waterfront.** Decommissioned plant, district-heating pipes, a site with
  more tunnels than buildings. *Mission use:* the infrastructure that keeps the city alive and can kill
  it.
* **Vuosaari harbour.** Container terminal, customs, the EU's eastern gate. *Mission use:* the case
  briefing in §3.3; smuggling of things that are not contraband in any schedule.

### 4.2 The ring and beyond

* **Malmi airport.** Closed to civil aviation, reopened for drones and "logistics". *Mission use:* a
  hangar rented by a company with no employees.
* **Herttoniemi and Roihupelto.** Industrial sheds, a metro depot, workshops that will fabricate
  anything with the right paperwork. *Mission use:* the implant that should not exist.
* **Itäkeskus and the eastern suburbs.** Dense, multilingual, under-served, over-policed. *Mission use:*
  the district where the official story is least believed and most true.
* **Kehä I / Kehä III.** The ring roads as a geography of escape and jurisdiction-shopping. *Mission
  use:* a pursuit that ends at a border between two police districts with different orders.
* **Otaniemi, Espoo.** University, labs, a quantum-computing campus, and the BCI research that
  produced the Europol kit. *Mission use:* the scientist who will not publish.
* **Tapiola and Westend.** Old money, new money, and a private clinic with a waiting list measured in
  years. *Mission use:* the suspect who is too important to arrest.
* **Vantaa airport.** Schengen edge, cargo, a terminal rebuilt twice since the Crisis. *Mission use:*
  an arrival that is not on the manifest.
* **Nuuksio and Sipoonkorpi.** Forest, silence, the *metsänpeitto* register. *Mission use:* a
  disappearance that is not a crime; the astral scene played without saying "astral".
* **Porkkala and the archipelago.** Former Soviet naval base, now a half-military, half-mythic
  coastline. *Mission use:* a cache with two provenances and no way to tell which is real.

### 4.3 Three paper sites **[PROPOSAL]**

The city's best mission locations are not dramatic. They are rooms where a document is *almost*
available.

* **Tähtitorninmäki observatory.** The hill above Kaivopuisto, the 19th-century dome, the
  astronomical association's archive. The 1947 report names an observer at the observatory that
  night; the logbook for that date is missing, and three separate agencies have requested the same
  box ([the ONI transcription](../research/UFO_DISCLOSURE_BACKGROUND_RESEARCH.md#73-1947--helsinki-and-the-document-that-survives)).
  *Mission use:* an archive visit that becomes a custody dispute; the plate is real, the log is not.
* **The National Archives, Rauhankatu.** Finnish state records, ministry files, and the foreign
  copies of Finnish incidents that the Finnish file does not contain. *Mission use:* a document
  filed under a ministry number that was never issued.
* **The tunnel that is not on the plan.** Helsinki's underground is real and large. Use it with one
  rule: every tunnel scene is either a place a character can name on a real map, or **explicitly
  invented and said so at the table**. No silent fictional geography.

**Site-by-site reference.** The companion
[Helsinki UAP mission gazetteer](HELSINKI_UAP_MISSION_GAZETTEER_DRAFT.md) turns the districts above
into individual missions — entry point, legal lead, twist, difficulty, evidence ladder. This
proposal keeps the argument; the gazetteer keeps the addresses.

### 4.3 What the city feels like **[PROPOSAL]**

Sensors everywhere and trust nowhere; a functioning welfare state with a security budget it does not
discuss; bilingual bureaucracy and a third language for the things nobody writes down; winters that
still work and summers that do not. The comedy is institutional: a form for reporting an anomaly that
requires you to state its category.

## 6. Case seeds **[PROPOSAL]**

1. **The too-clean file** (Act I) — a 1946 archive with no gaps, in a city where every archive has gaps.
2. **The clinic above the pharmacy** (I) — an implant that works, sold to people who cannot consent.
3. **The 94 seconds** (I–II) — the Vuosaari CCTV gap; four cameras, one gap, no power event.
4. **The witness who is angry** (II) — a man who saw nothing and is furious at being asked twice.
5. **The twice-manifested cargo** (II) — Turku; the same container in two ports at one time.
6. **The defector's clipping** (II) — Tallinn; a 1947 Helsinki press cutting with a different date.
7. **The radar nobody owns** (III) — Gotland; a recording both states disclaim.
8. **The un-listed object** (III) — Lappeenranta; a border log that has been rewritten.
9. **The patient who is not ill** (III) — Meilahti; a diagnosis that is a classification.
10. **The hybrid who is no one** (IV) — Geneva; a person without a legal category.
11. **The memory of the city** (IV) — astral Helsinki; a conversation that changes the campaign's meaning.
12. **The oldest lie** (IV) — lunar far side; the programme that started it all, still staffed.

Each seed is a *shape*, not a script: one place, one institution, one anomaly, one consequence.

## 8. Open questions for the author

1. **The 2034 confirmation**: a public recovery, or a public *admission* with no object? The second is
   more NoöPunk and harder to write.
2. **PSI registration**: is it a Finnish/EU regime the player must navigate, or background?
3. **The war's length**: three weeks is canon-consistent but dramatically tight; does the campaign
   revisit it, or only inherit it?
4. **Helsinki's damage**: does the city bear visible scars from 2039–42, or was it spared and ashamed
   of being spared?
5. **The 1946 file**: is it a real recovery, a planted archive, or an unresolved blank the campaign
   never fills? (The last is the strongest, and the hardest to resist.)
6. **Language**: which player-visible text is Finglish and which stays English, per `docs/FINNISH.md`
   and #996.
