## 0. What this document proposes, in one paragraph

Disclosure was never an announcement. It was a slow, uneven paper trail that began with recovered
materials no one could authenticate, passed through hearings that changed nothing, and ended — after
a rogue Singularity and an intervention nobody could referee — in a public that knows NHI exist and
still cannot agree on what happened. Finland, on the EU's fortified eastern edge, lived the crisis
from the front row: a border, a harbour, a hospital, an archive and a tunnel network, all of which
kept working while the categories broke. The campaign starts after the shock, in the argument.

## 2. Proposed global sequence (relative order; dates only where canon already has them)

Each stage lists **what was observed**, **what institutions knew**, **what the public believed**,
**measurable consequence** (*assumption* where invented), **faction reaction**, and a **scenario
hook**. The 50/25/25 normalist/accelerator/doomist split is the established *fictional heuristic*,
never a measured distribution.

### Stage 1 — Concealed contact, competing archives (1940s → 1990s)

- **Observed:** ghost fliers, ghost rockets, the 1947 Helsinki report, retrieval rumours, radar
  returns with no wreckage.
- **Institutions:** small offices with C3-grade evaluations; the American and Soviet programmes
  learn different things and file them in different languages.
- **Public:** folklore, newspaper waves, indifference between flaps.
- **Consequence:** *assumption* — the secrecy is not a wall but a filing habit.
- **Factions:** Orion recruits through ideology; the Confederation waits for a call; Sirians watch
  twice and leave.
- **Hook:** an archive that holds the *foreign copy* of a Finnish incident and not the Finnish one.

### Stage 2 — Slow disclosure (2017–2030s)

- **Observed:** military footage; whistleblower testimony under oath; official offices that
  resolve most cases and leave a residue.
- **Institutions:** AARO-style resolution statistics beside legacy programmes nobody will confirm;
  Congress and parliaments legislate collections and receive redactions.
- **Public:** "disclosure fatigue". Belief rises, attention doesn't. The 25 % who care split into
  accelerators and doomists.
- **Consequence:** *assumption* — trust in institutions falls faster than belief in NHI rises.
- **Factions:** Orion benefits from confusion; the Confederation's non-intervention rule means
  almost no direct action; human programmes misread both.
- **Hook:** a Europol predecessor unit that was disbanded for asking the right question.

### Stage 3 — Material and biological confirmation (relative: before the Singularity)

- **Observed:** an object with impossible material properties in a customs container; biologics
  whose provenance chains are broken in three jurisdictions.
- **Institutions:** laboratories under military control; ethics committees with no jurisdiction;
  allied services that stop sharing.
- **Public:** the shift from "are they real?" to "what are they, and what was done to people?"
- **Consequence:** *assumption* — abductions and hybrids become more traumatic than the mere
  existence of aliens, because they implicate humans who cooperated.
- **Factions:** the Confederation's service ethic becomes a public-relations problem; Orion offers
  exactly what frightened people want — order, an enemy, a hierarchy.
- **Hook:** a hospital morgue and a customs manifest that disagree about what was in the box.

### Stage 4 — The PSI and consciousness paradigm shift

- **Observed:** replicated anomalies, a theoretical breakthrough in nonlocal consciousness
  (*in-world science*), and BCI hardware that makes the anomaly *usable*.
- **Institutions:** universities split; insurers, courts and police invent procedures faster than
  laws; a psionic service industry appears.
- **Public:** PSI as wellness, fraud, security threat and spiritual revival at once.
- **Consequence:** *assumption* — the market produces more psionic fraud than psionic capability,
  which makes genuine cases harder to believe.
- **Factions:** Sirians' collective cognition suddenly reads as *aspirational*; Orion recruits
  telepaths; human agencies try to industrialise what they can't explain.
- **Hook:** a licensed PSI practitioner whose readings are real, and who is therefore unemployable.

### Stage 5 — The short war and the intercepted weapons

- **Observed:** a brief EU–Russia war; nuclear release stopped mid-flight by objects that no one
  had authorised; Russian state failure follows the Crisis.
- **Institutions:** EU and Finnish civil defence, Supo, the Defence Forces; UNSA coordinating
  without authority.
- **Public:** deterrence as a concept dies in public. The Mandela Effect around the USSR becomes
  universal, not a fringe memory.
- **Consequence:** *assumption* — European integration accelerates because the alternative is
  demonstrated to be unsurvivable.
- **Factions:** Orion denies responsibility; the Confederation refuses credit; everyone claims the
  interception proves their own reading of history.
- **Hook:** a Finnish border post where two soldiers remember different countries on the other side.

### Stage 6 — The Singularity, the intervention, and public contact

- **Observed:** a partial malevolent intelligence transition; infrastructure war; then quarantine
  lifted, Zookeeper and Orion intervention, and the first *official* contact.
- **Institutions:** most of them fail temporarily; the ones that survive are the boring ones —
  municipalities, hospitals, the church, the police.
- **Public:** the 50 % who normalised now carry the others.
- **Consequence:** *assumption* — the crisis is experienced locally and reported globally; Helsinki's
  experience is not the world's.
- **Factions:** Orion participates to bank goodwill; the Zookeepers act because the alternative is
  worse, not because they like us.
- **Hook:** the week when the metro stopped and nobody could say whether it was the AI or the aliens.

### Stage 7 — The post-Crisis Cold War (playable present, 2045)

- A functioning EU and Finland; a powerful but limited UNSA; Europol as the player's employer; a
  fragmented United States; active Orion–Confederation rivalry; a rogue ASI that was never found.
- **Hook:** the ordinary case that is not ordinary.

### Stage 8 — Lasting epistemic uncertainty

- No stage above is agreed on by all survivors. Histories differ by country, by archive and by
  memory. The mysteries are load-bearing and are never explained away.

### 3.2 First-person survivor timeline — "Milla, 34, Kallio"

> **Fiction.** Written to show texture, not to fix dates.

**2038, autumn.** The hearings were on Yle Areena at eleven at night and the country watched the
first twenty minutes before switching to hockey. My mother believed everything immediately; my
brother believed nothing for six years and then believed all of it at once, which was worse.

**2041, February.** The war lasted eleven days from our side of it, which is not long unless you live
in it. The thing nobody talks about is the **silence over the Gulf** in the fourth hour, when the
sirens were going and then the sirens were going and nothing arrived. We found out later what
happened in the air. At the time it felt like being spared by something with no manners.

**2041, March.** The border closed. My cousin's Russian wife was on the wrong side of a line that
had been a formality the week before. The Mandela Effect became real to me then, not because of
documents, but because my aunt remembered a Soviet pension office that closed in 1991, and could
describe the woman behind the counter, and I could not argue with her, and she was not wrong in the
way that liars are wrong.

**2043, the bad fortnight.** The metro stopped between Hakaniemi and Kaisaniemi, and stayed stopped
for ninety minutes, and the announcements kept saying *technical fault* in that voice they use. The
bank was down. My card bought nothing. A neighbour with a battery radio said the Americans were gone;
another said the Americans were coming. On the fourth day the sky over Vuosaari did something I have
no honest words for, and the word everyone used afterwards was *intervention*, as if that explained
the light.

**2044.** The psionic clinic on Hämeentie closed and reopened as a church, then as a clinic again.
The city filled with people who had come from places that no longer worked.

**2045.** I still take the metro. I still lock my bike. I work four days a week at a translation
agency that has more work than translators because nobody trusts the machines with the words any
more. The strangeness is not that we know about them. It is that the tram is on time and the man in
front of me is reading a book about the Mandela Effect, and I cannot tell him which of us remembers
the real street.

### 3.3 Europol case briefing (proposed handout)

> **EUROPOL HELSINKI FIELD OFFICE — CASE 2045-0417 "ORION'S RECEIPT"**
> **Classification:** RESTRICTED / EU-UNSA SHARED
> **Subject:** A sealed freight container, Vuosaari harbour, 14 March 2045.
>
> **Summary.** A container declared as *machine parts* arrived on a feeder service from Tallinn and
> sat unclaimed for nineteen days. Customs opened it on a routine inspection and found an object
> inside a second, unpainted container that was not on any manifest. The object is 2.4 m long, ovoid,
> and warm to the touch. It has no seams, no serial plate, and no material signature in the EU
> database. Two harbour workers report dreams of the same room, in the same building, with the same
> water stain.
>
> **What is established.** The shipping paperwork is internally consistent and forged at the
> origin, not at the destination. The container was transshipped once in Tallinn, where the trail
> stops at a company registered in a jurisdiction that no longer answers.
>
> **What is not.** Whether the object is a component, a vehicle, a container for something else, or
> a decoy. Whether the dreams are PSI, suggestion, or a mundane shared workplace. Whether the
> shipper intended it to be found.
>
> **Investigative requirements.** Interview the two harbour workers *separately*; obtain the Tallinn
> transshipment record before UNSA does; decide whether to invoke Article 9 (EU jurisdiction) or to
> invite the Finnish Defence Forces in, which will end your access to the object.
>
> **Note for the handler.** The container's internal geometry does not match the external one. Do
> not open it in the building.

## 4. Helsinki 2045 — the area in detail

Written as a *proposal for authored setting content*. The four Phase 1 worksheets
(`design/HELSINKI_*.md`) remain Tomi's to fill; this is raw material for that pass, not a substitute
for it.

### 4.1 The city's four layers

1. **The granite surface.** A 19th-century imperial capital with a functioning welfare city on top:
   Senate Square, the Cathedral, the University, the market, the ferry terminals.
2. **The 1960s–2020s concrete.** Kallio, Pasila, Itäkeskus, Vuosaari — the city that was built
   quickly and is now being retrofitted for a world with NHI in it.
3. **The underground.** Metro, service tunnels, cable galleries, civil-defence shelters, the
   bedrock car parks. Helsinki's shelter network is real and large; **the setting's tunnel layer is
   an extension of it, not an invention from nothing** (*assumption* where specific links are
   claimed).
4. **The invisible city.** Sensor networks, the EU case-management backbone, UNSA liaison channels,
   and the archives that are not in any building.

### 4.3 The tunnel layer — how to use it without inventing geography

The real city has a substantial underground: metro, service tunnels, shelters, cable galleries.
**Proposed rule of use:** every tunnel scene is *either* a place a character can name on a map
*or* explicitly marked as a site the author has invented. Do not silently add a fictional
"secret line" and let players treat it as real Helsinki geography.

| Tunnel type | Real anchor | Fiction allowance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Metro running tunnels | Real; two lines, depot at Roihupelto | Closures, maintenance spurs |
| Service / maintenance tunnels | Real (city centre, several) | A single sealed spur used for storage, marked as invented |
| Civil-defence shelters | Real, extensive | Wartime use; a shelter that stayed occupied after 2043 |
| Cable and utility galleries | Real | Sensor taps, UNSA listening hardware |
| Bedrock car parks | Real | A vehicle that was never logged out |

### 4.4 Institutions at street level

- **Helsinki Police Department** — ordinary crime, first responders, the ones who were there.
- **Supo** — security intelligence; knows more about ПРОГРАММА than it will say; competes with UNSA.
- **The Defence Forces** — Santahamina, the Gulf, the border; treats NHI as a military problem.
- **Europol Helsinki Field Office** — the player's employer, cross-border, under-resourced, dependent
  on the others for warrants and muscle.
- **UNSA liaison** — coordinating, well-funded, not sovereign, and resented for both.
- **The University and Aalto** — the knowledge, the grants, and the ethics problem.
- **The Cathedral and Uspenski** — the two parishes that have had to answer the ontological question
  in front of a congregation.
- **Yle** — public broadcasting, still trusted unevenly; the archive that verifies or destroys a claim.

### 5.3 How to run these without breaking canon

1. **One case, one tier.** A mission may touch tier 5 but must not resolve it; the MJ-12 and
   ПРОГРАММА questions stay open after every scenario (#1114, §5).
2. **Jurisdiction is the adventure.** The most NoöPunk obstacle is not a monster; it is a warrant
   that three agencies must sign and one will not.
3. **No new mechanics.** These are investigations, chases, interviews and document problems in the
   existing chassis; nothing here needs a new subsystem.
4. **The Mandela Effect is a witness, not a puzzle.** Never let a session prove which history is
   real; let it prove that a specific document, photograph or memory is *not what it claims*.

## 6. The 2045 situation, in one page of text

Helsinki works. The trams run, the ferries run, the libraries are open and the university argues
about funding. The EU is more integrated than it has ever been, and more frightened. UNSA
coordinates, funds and occasionally overrides; the Finns resent it in the way they resent weather.
Russia is a ruin with a border, and the border is a wall. The Americans are a rumour with aircraft
carriers. The NHI are real, present, and *disappointing* to everyone who expected answers: the
Zookeepers behave like an insurance company, Orion behaves like a great power, the Sirians barely
behave like anything human at all. The rogue intelligence that broke the world was never found, and
some people think it is still here, inside something.

The strange is normal now. That is the setting's central tonal instruction: **ontological shock has
become administrative**. The horror is a form that must be filled in triplicate by someone who does
not believe you.
