# Campaign 01, Act I case — *The Tuesday Complaint*

> **Status: reviewable tabletop manuscript — not canon, not implemented.** This is the first
> complete case written under the #1120 pivot. It extends the “Tuesday Complaint” proposal in
> [`CAMPAIGN_01_EUROPOL_ROOKIE.md`](CAMPAIGN_01_EUROPOL_ROOKIE.md) and uses the CWN baseline in
> [`WITHOUT_NUMBER_BASELINE.md`](../../rulebook/WITHOUT_NUMBER_BASELINE.md).
>
> **Spoiler warning:** §2, §6 and §8 are GM material. Give players only §1, §4, §5 and the
> handouts in §10.
>
> **Scope:** scenario-local names, records and crimes. It does not redefine Helsinki, Europol,
> the NHI Crisis, PSI, the timeline or any rules subsystem. The GM ledger is
> [`CAMPAIGN_01_LEDGER.md`](CAMPAIGN_01_LEDGER.md).

## 1. At a glance

**Logline.** A Lithuanian clerk asks Europol why her dead brother's employment record is still
being updated. The answer is not a ghost, an AI or an alien: it is a small business selling
continuity to families, employers and one compromised Europol officer.

**Player characters.** The default campaign unit: a Europol rookie (the PC), Agent Laura Wendt,
and Dr Harri S. Romppainen as the office's forensic anchor. A second player can take Laura; the
GM runs Romppainen and the witnesses.

**Length.** One session, 3–4 hours. Six scenes; two are optional depending on approach.

**Content.** Grief, pension fraud, identity theft, a knife in a stairwell, institutional
self-protection. No on-page sexual violence, no harm to children, no graphic torture. The
stairwell fight can be avoided or fled.

**Rules used.** CWN only: 2d6 + attribute modifier + skill rank against difficulty; CWN combat,
saves and damage. No PSI, no hacking program catalogue, no AWN, no new subsystem.

**The one weird thing.** A wrong-number call in which the clerk hears her brother's voice. It is
never explained. It may be grief, a crossed line, a recording, or something else. Do not resolve
it in this case.

## 2. What actually happened (GM only)

Marius Vaitkevičiūtė died in 2043 in a warehouse accident in Kaunas. His employer, NordBaltic
Freight, should have closed his payroll record and stopped his occupational pension. Instead,
payroll manager **Jukka Salmi** kept Marius “employed” and routed the pension and a phantom salary
through **NordBaltic Continuity**, a side business that sells one service: keeping a dead person
employable so a family keeps income, a lease or a pension.

The service is illegal, but it is not supernatural. Jukka has fourteen deceased “principals” on
the books and three living clients who buy legacy cover identities. One of those clients is
**Eerik Kallio**, a Europol liaison analyst, who used a “legacy” identity for a deceased informant
whose cover had to survive him. Eerik is not the mastermind; he is a compromised officer who wants
the complaint closed before Internal Affairs sees his name.

Rūta's complaint reached Europol because the employer is registered in Helsinki and the pension
crosses an EU border. The local angle is Kurvi; the financial trail is Vallila; the institutional
problem is the Europol office itself.

**What this case is not:** Marius is not alive; the records are not haunted; Jukka is not an AI;
Eerik is not an NHI; the wrong-number call is not a clue the players can solve.

## 4. Opening — Tuesday, 08:40, Europol Helsinki Field Office

Read or paraphrase:

> The office smells of wet wool and printer toner. Pia Koskinen has put a complaint file on the
> rookie's desk with a sticky note: **“Cross-border. Lithuanian. Pension still active. Check the
> footer.”** Romppainen is already in the doorway with coffee. “It's a data-integrity complaint,”
> he says. “Which means it is either nothing, or it is someone's whole life.”

**What the PC can do without rolling:** read the file. It contains:

- Rūta's complaint: her brother Marius died in 2043; his employment record was updated in 2045;
  his pension is still paid to their mother in Kaunas.
- A death certificate (Kaunas, 2043) and a payroll extract (Helsinki, 2045) that both name Marius.
- A footer on the payroll extract: **NordBaltic Continuity — “we keep your people on the books.”**

**Core clue C1 (no check):** the death certificate and payroll record cannot both be true. The
case is a records/fraud investigation, not a missing-person case.

**Pressure:** Eerik Kallio passes the desk and says the complaint “sounds like a consular matter.”
He is not the PC's boss; he is a liaison. He can make the PC's life difficult, not order an arrest.

## 5. Scene sequence

### Scene 1 — The complaint (office; 20 minutes)

**Purpose:** establish the grounded procedure and the institutional stakes.

- **Read the file:** no check; C1.
- **Check the footer:** `Administer` (INT) at routine 8, or `Notice` (WIS) at routine 8. Success
  reveals the Continuity registration number; failure costs a shift and a call to the Lithuanian
  liaison, but C1 remains.
- **Ask Pia about the footer:** `Connect` (CHA) at routine 8. Success: she admits she has seen
  three similar footers this year; failure: she clams up and the PC must use the registration
  number instead.
- **Ask Eerik about jurisdiction:** `Administer` or `Talk` at routine 8. Success: he confirms
  Europol can support a Finnish fraud case but cannot search or arrest. Failure: he offers to
  “handle the liaison” and becomes a blocking figure earlier.

**Scene exit:** the registration number points to a Kurvi address. The PC can travel by tram
(no check; ordinary Helsinki) or request a car (Administer, routine 8; failure means a delay).

### Scene 2 — Kurvi freight office (investigation/social; 45 minutes)

NordBaltic Freight occupies the second floor above a shuttered kiosk. Rūta works at a terminal;
Jukka's office is at the back. Mikko is not here during the day.

**Core clue C2 (no check, with access):** Rūta's paper folder contains a **Continuity agreement**
naming Marius as a “principal,” Jukka as “administrator,” and a clause: “The principal's family
will not inquire into the source of continuity payments.” Rūta kept it because she did not trust
the digital copy.

**Talking to Rūta:** `Talk` (CHA) at routine 8.
- Success: she explains the pension, the wrong-number call, and her fear that reporting will get
  her fired or deported. She gives the folder.
- Failure: she withdraws and asks the PC to leave; the folder is still on the desk and can be
  obtained through a lawful records request (Administer, demanding 10) or a later `Connect`
  approach. **C2 is not lost.**
- Coercion or threats: she calls her union representative; the case slows, and the PC gains a
  Reputation consequence.

**Talking to Jukka:** `Talk` or `Administer` at demanding 10. He is polite, precise and
unhelpful. Success: he admits “continuity” is a service but calls it “bereavement administration,”
and lets slip that “the liaison” is a client. Failure: he says the records are confidential and
asks for a warrant.

**Noticing the office:** `Notice` (WIS) at routine 8. Success: a 2038 newspaper clipping on the
wall shows Jukka with a much younger Marius at a company event; the caption is ordinary. Failure:
nothing; it is colour, not a clue.

**Scene exit:** the payroll extract names a Vallila tram depot account. Jukka's office has a
paper ledger; getting it requires a warrant, consent, or a later night visit.

### Scene 3 — Vallila tram depot (investigation/stealth; 45 minutes)

The night dispatcher's office is a glass box over the tram yard. Mikko Rautio is on shift. The
payroll server is a locked cabinet, not a networked terminal.

**Core clue C3 (with access):** the access logs show payroll edits made with a **Europol liaison
token** (Eerik's), not a NordBaltic account. The logs are on a local terminal; obtaining them
requires one of:

- a lawful request through Finnish police (`Administer`, routine 8; takes a day; no risk);
- an authorized Europol forensic request (`Program` or `Administer`, demanding 10; success gets a
  copy; failure means the data is preserved but the chain of custody is contested);
- a night entry (`Sneak` or `Fix`, demanding 10; failure triggers Mikko).

**Talking to Mikko:** `Talk` (CHA) at demanding 10, or `Connect` with a local contact at routine 8.
Success: he admits Jukka asked him to “deliver a message” to Rūta; he did not hurt anyone. Failure:
he becomes hostile and the stairwell confrontation moves earlier.

**Surveillance:** `Notice` (WIS) at demanding 10. Success: the PC spots Mikko photographing them;
failure: Mikko follows them and the confrontation happens at the office instead. Either way, the
case continues.

**Scene exit:** the Europol token belongs to Eerik. This is the case's institutional turn.

### Scene 4 — The liaison (social/bureaucratic; 30 minutes)

Eerik Kallio is in a small meeting room with a view of the courtyard. He is not a cartoon
corrupt cop. He is tired, precise and frightened.

**Core clue C4 (with evidence):** if shown the access logs or the Continuity agreement naming
“the liaison,” Eerik admits he bought a legacy identity for a dead informant whose family would
otherwise have lost their residence status. He did not know Marius was a real deceased employee
rather than another “principal.” He wants the case closed; he can offer the PC a favour, a
transfer, or a quiet internal resolution.

**Checks:**
- `Talk` (CHA) at demanding 10: success, he gives a written statement; failure, he lawyers up and
  the case goes to Internal Affairs with the PC's notes.
- `Administer` (INT) at routine 8 with the ledger: success, the contradiction between his account
  and the records is documented; failure, he stalls until Romppainen intervenes.
- `Lead` (CHA) at demanding 10: success, he agrees to cooperate with Finnish police; failure, he
  resigns the next day and the office closes ranks.

**No check:** Romppainen, if present, reads the room and says: “You can protect the source or the
record. You cannot protect both and call it procedure.”

**Scene exit:** the PC must choose whether to refer Eerik to Internal Affairs, use him as a
witness, or bury his role to protect the informant's family.

### Scene 5 — The stairwell (confrontation; 20 minutes, optional)

Mikko waits in the stairwell of the Europol building or the freight office, depending on earlier
choices. He has a knife and a phone; he wants the PC to stop. He is not a professional killer.

**Options:**
- **Talk him down:** `Talk` (CHA) at demanding 10, with +1 if the PC mentions Jukka's blame and
  Mikko's own risk. Success: he gives a statement and leaves. Failure: he panics and attacks.
- **Retreat:** `Exert` (DEX) at routine 8 to get out; success, no fight; failure, he corners the PC.
- **Fight:** CWN combat. Mikko has a knife (CWN knife statistics), no armour, morale 7. He flees
  at half HP or when the PC's backup arrives. The PC can be injured; the campaign continues.
- **Call for backup:** `Connect` or `Administer` at routine 8; success, Laura arrives in 1d4
  rounds; failure, the call is delayed.

**Failure state:** the PC is injured and Mikko escapes. The case continues; Mikko can be found
later through Jukka's ledger. Do not kill the PC in a stairwell; use CWN mortal injury only if
the table has explicitly accepted that risk.

### Scene 6 — Resolution (30 minutes)

The PC has enough for a Finnish fraud/identity case and an internal Europol conduct case. The
resolution is a choice, not a boss fight.

**Option A — Refer everything.** Finnish police arrest Jukka; Rūta's pension is frozen pending
review; Eerik is referred to Internal Affairs. The office is embarrassed; the PC gains a
reputation for thoroughness and loses a mentor. Rūta may lose the pension temporarily, but the
record is corrected.

**Option B — Protect the source.** The PC gives Finnish police Jukka and Mikko but redacts Eerik's
name. Eerik's informant's family keeps their status; Eerik owes the PC a debt; Internal Affairs
eventually notices the gap. Rūta gets a partial correction but not the full truth.

**Option C — Internal first.** The PC reports Eerik first. Europol's internal process takes the
case; Jukka has time to destroy the paper ledger; the criminal case weakens. Eerik may cooperate
or resign. The PC learns how the office protects itself.

**Option D — Bury it.** The PC closes the complaint as a consular matter. Rūta keeps the pension;
Jukka continues; the wrong-number call remains unexplained. This is a legitimate, costly choice,
not a “bad ending” the GM punishes with a lecture.

**Consequences:** record one on the PC's sheet: `Europol: thorough`, `Europol: compromised`,
`Kurvi: trusted`, or `Kurvi: closed door`. These are narrative tags, not a new alignment meter.

## 7. CWN adjudication quick reference

- Task: **2d6 + attribute modifier + skill rank** vs difficulty. Routine 8, demanding 10, hard 12.
- Untrained ordinary skill: −1. Untrained attack: −2.
- Attacks: 1d20 + attack bonus + attribute modifier + combat skill vs AC.
- Saves: 1d20 vs target; natural 1 fails, natural 20 succeeds.
- Damage, Shock, Trauma, System Strain, healing: CWN as written.
- No PSI, no hacking program, no AWN stress track. The depot terminal is an authorized records
  terminal, not a cyberspace dungeon.
- The GM should announce intent, access and stakes before a roll. A failed roll changes cost,
  time, risk or optional detail; it does not delete the case.

## 8. GM guidance

**Tone.** Begin with bureaucracy and grief, not neon. The funniest line should come from an
institution behaving plausibly: a form that cannot be filed, a jurisdiction that does not exist,
a liaison who says “consular matter” while avoiding eye contact. The knife is not funny; the
paperwork after it is.

**Pacing.** If the players are engaged in the office, let them stay. If they stall, Pia's sticky
note or Romppainen's “check the footer” moves them. Do not use Eerik as a deus ex machina; he is a
pressure source.

**The 50/25/25 in this case.** Rūta is a continuity defender who wants her life back; Jukka is an
accelerationist who treats grief as a market; Pia is a worried doomer who suspects every record is
an infiltration; Eerik is an accelerationist who made one compromise and cannot stop. None is a
representative of a bloc.

**Do not resolve:** the wrong-number call; whether Eerik's informant is really dead; whether Jukka
believes his own marketing. These are future-case material.
