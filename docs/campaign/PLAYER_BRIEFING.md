# Player briefing — what a new Europol field agent knows on day one

> **Author draft — template only, no prose yet.** This is the one page a player reads before the first
> scene, so that the opening investigation and Tony's memories can be played **without a universe
> encyclopedia** (the acceptance criterion of [#1216](https://github.com/TomiToivio/NooPunk/issues/1216)).
> **Tomi writes this document.** Agents must not fill it in, and must not turn it into a lore summary:
> if it does not change what a player would do in the first hour, it does not belong here.

## How to use this file

> **Prompt — not prose.** Keep it to the length of a briefing sheet somebody hands you on your first
> morning: what you are, where you are, what you may do, what it costs, and what nobody agrees on. Write
> in the second person if you like. Everything here must be something an ordinary officer could know or
> believe, including the things that are wrong.
## 1. What you are

> **Prompt.** Newly graduated Europol field agent, first posting, Ratakatu 12. What does the badge mean
> to the person wearing it, and what does it not mean?

**[AUTHOR TO WRITE]**

## 2. Where you are

> **Prompt.** Helsinki after the Crisis, in one paragraph a local would recognise. Which parts of the
> city are ordinary, which are guarded, and which are simply gone?

**[AUTHOR TO WRITE]**

## 3. What you may do, and what you may not

> **Prompt.** Public network research, records searches, a warrant, an emergency. State plainly that
> arrest and search on Finnish soil belong to the Finnish police, and that a signature with your name
> on it is the thing that will be read aloud later.

**[AUTHOR TO WRITE]**

## 4. What you carry

> **Prompt.** Uniform, radio, phone, terminal, forensic scanner, body camera — and whatever else you
> want the player to feel the weight of. One line each; no equipment tables here.

**[AUTHOR TO WRITE]**

## 5. What everyone knows

> **Prompt.** The public history: the acceleration, the Crisis, the Disclosure, the reconstruction. Only
> what a citizen would repeat at a bus stop.

**[AUTHOR TO WRITE]**

## 6. What everyone argues about

> **Prompt.** The political weather. The network is slow on purpose. The system keeps you safe and it
> watches you. Give both sides a fair sentence, and let a player disagree with either.

**[AUTHOR TO WRITE]**

## 7. What nobody agrees on

> **Prompt.** Rumours, contested incidents, things a colleague believes and another denies. These are
> the hooks a case can pull on later; they must read as uncertainty, not as hints.

**[AUTHOR TO WRITE]**

## 8. Who you answer to

> **Prompt.** Your supervisor, the office, the ministry, the auditors, the court. One line each, and say
> which of them can end your career.

**[AUTHOR TO WRITE]**

## 9. What this briefing must not contain

> **Prompt.** The truth about the phenomena, the answer to the nightmare, a timeline of everything, a
> glossary of factions, or any rule the player will not meet in the first session. List what you cut,
> so the next editor does not put it back.

**[AUTHOR TO WRITE]**
