## 2. The new propositions, with their status

Nothing below is written into canon by this document; the column says what it is and who decides.

| # | proposition from #1123 | status | what it may touch |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Species ≠ faction.** Greys, Reptilians, Pleiadians, Sirians and humans belong to different political factions or none | **[PROPOSAL]** — consistent with `12_BEINGS.md`'s per-entity faction fields, but not yet stated as a rule | every scene with a nonhuman; **no** change to existing faction entries |
| 2 | **Greys as a galactic service proletariat** — employed across civilisations, not a monolithic faction | **[OPEN]** — a social/class hypothesis the author wants explored | class politics in Helsinki scenes; it must not become "all Greys are servants" |
| 3 | **Pleiadians and Greys may both be future human descendants** (Masters) | **[PROPOSAL]** — extends the canon extratempestrial mechanism to the two iconic humanoids | the Nordics' appearance and the abduction lore; **not** a claim about real-world abduction accounts |
| 4 | **Pleiadian origins are unresolved to the Pleiadians**: Atlantean breakaway vs human colonists from the future | **[PROPOSAL]** | how a Pleiadian NPC answers the question; two traditions, no answer |
| 5 | **Finno-Ugric linguistic and Finnish/Swedish genetic hints** as clues, not proof | **[OPEN — handle with care]** | one line of a linguist's aside at most; a real ethnic group is **not** an alien species |
| 6 | **Flying-jellyfish UAP as a genuinely disturbing biologic or construct**, nature unresolved | **[PROPOSAL]** | a Hyvinkää or coastal encounter; horror, not comedy |
| 7 | **President's Pleiadian starseed claim** during Disclosure | **[OPEN — author only]** | her speech and the public reaction; the setting must not confirm or refute it |
| 8 | **Tridactyls as guardians' engineered vehicles** (Law of One passage) | **[OPEN — speculative only]** | a footnote in a research document, never an early reveal |
| 9 | **Four-step escalation** — ETH → IDH → extratempestrial → cryptotempestrial, each *verified in-universe* in turn | **[PROPOSAL]** — the campaign's disclosure ladder | the roadmap's later cases; **nothing** in Act I |
| 10 | **AWN Mentalist as the closer PSI reference than SWN Psychic** | **[OPEN — see §5]** | which published system the bounded PC list is modelled on |

**Read §2 as a menu, not a canonisation.** Items 1, 6 and 9 are cheap to adopt; 3, 4 and 8 change how the setting
answers its own biggest questions, and 5 and 7 are the two that can go badly wrong if handled carelessly.

### 2.1 The two that need a warning label

* **Item 5 (Finno-Ugric / genetic hints).** The setting may hint that the Nordics share language or ancestry with
  Finns. It must never let a real ethnic group stand in for an alien species, and it must offer at least two
  explanations (contact, descent, convergent pattern, manipulation). One aside from a linguist who is not sure is
  worth more than a chapter of genetics.
* **Item 7 (the starseed claim).** Keep the institutional delivery serious and the claim unverified. If the
  president is lying, deluded, telling the truth or speaking metaphorically, the setting does not say — and the
  scene is better for it. The Kekkonen agreement and Finland's responsibility for the Sirian refugees stand
  regardless.

## 3. The escalation ladder belongs to another page

The four rungs — extraterrestrial → interdimensional → extratempestrial → cryptotempestrial, each verified in-world
before the next is available — are recorded properly in
[`ONTOLOGY_ESCALATION_1123.md`](ONTOLOGY_ESCALATION_1123.md), which owns that subject. This page only needs the rule
that touches the memoir:

* **the ladder is the world's structure, not the character's.** A teenager in 2041 hears rung 1 on television and
  rung 2 as a corridor rumour; rungs 3 and 4 are things she learns as an officer, and only if the campaign goes
  there. The memoir stops at rung 1, as television and confusion.

## 4. The memoir boundary: what she could plausibly know, phase by phase

This is the scaffold the author writes *from*. It is a list of **channels and limits**, not prose, and not a
timeline of events.

| phase | the channel | what it can deliver | what it must not deliver |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Acceleration (childhood)** | school, family, a BCI in the classroom, adverts, the news her parents leave on | that the world is changing fast and unevenly; that adults are excited and frightened in the same sentence | any understanding of what AGI is, or that a race is being lost |
| **Information collapse** | **Finnish television**, emergency bulletins, radio, a phone that stops working, neighbours | isolation, contradictory instructions, the end of the internet as a thing you use | a view of events outside Finland; a reliable account even of events inside it |
| **Loviisa** | the broadcast, then the silence where a region used to be, then the rumours | that something happened at a nuclear plant and that the state is managing it | whether it was sabotage, accident or war; who did it |
| **Hyvinkää** | evacuation notices, a school closing, a relative who does not come back, later the exclusion zone | fear, absence, the specific smell of a place you cannot go | what the machines were doing, or what ended them |
| **The president's speech** | one broadcast, watched with other people | that contact is real and older than she thought; that her country has been keeping a secret | the Kekkonen agreement's terms; the Sirian refugee arrangement's origins; whether the president means what she says |
| **The academy** | instructors, case files, other cadets, the institution's own version | a professional vocabulary and a set of official truths that contradict each other | the true structure of European jurisdiction, which nobody in the building understands either |

**The Hyvinkää veteran branch.** If the author chooses it, the character was **there** — deployed, young, and
unprepared. That changes the memoir's centre of gravity: the Crisis is no longer something she watched but
something she was inside, and the academy becomes a way of making it mean something. It does **not** grant her
knowledge of the cause, and it should not predetermine the trauma's content: what a soldier saw at a machine
factory is a thing the campaign can return to later, in a scene she is not ready for.

**Two rules for whoever edits this:**
1. The memoir may be *wrong*. Distorted recollection, rumour and propaganda are the point.
2. Nothing in it may be an exposition dump. If a paragraph explains the setting, cut it and keep the detail that made
   the character notice.

## 5. The PSI decision record

### 5.1 Precognition — no active power, anywhere

`#1123` repeats a rule the campaign already has: **precognition is never a selectable, trainable, player-triggered,
repeatable or mechanically reliable power.** It appears as authored dreams, déjà vu, a line remembered before it is
spoken, and it never becomes a tactical advantage or a shortcut around an investigation.

**A conflict that needs the author's eye.** [`rulebook/6_PSYCHIC.md`](../../rulebook/6_PSYCHIC.md) §6.7 lists
`Precognition` as a **PSY skill** in the legacy PSI system, while §6.4.2 already treats precognitive dreams as
**GM-resolved**. Three layers now disagree:

| layer | what it says | compatible with #1123? |
|---|---|---|
| `rulebook/6_PSYCHIC.md` §6.7 (legacy tabletop system) | `Precognition` is a selectable PSY skill | **no** — this is the entry that conflicts |
| `rulebook/6_PSYCHIC.md` §6.4.2 | precognitive **dreams**, resolved by the GM | yes |
| #1178 + campaign §8.2 + #1123 | no active precognition for the PC; scripted scenes only | yes |

**Recommendation (author decision):** keep §6.4.2, and mark the §6.7 `Precognition` entry as **GM-resolved only** —
the skill exists as a competence the GM may call on for an authored dream, never as a button the player presses.
That is a one-line edit to a live rulebook chapter, so it is yours, not an agent's.

### 5.2 AWN Mentalist vs SWN Psychic

The two instructions are not actually in collision if read as **reference versus carrier**:

* **AWN Mentalist is the closer conceptual model** (#1123): subtler, investigative, scene-scaled powers rather than
  artillery with a mind.
* **The SWN-compatible engine remains the mechanical carrier** (#1178): PC-selectable disciplines are **Telepathy and
  Telekinesis**, with Effort as the only pool, and the psychotronic implant as the starting kit.

**And the repository already implements this split**, in
[`src/rules/psi_access.py`](../../src/rules/psi_access.py): `PC_DISCIPLINES` is `("Telekinesis", "Telepathy")`, while
`PC_EDGES` is `("Mutant", "Mentalist", "Spellcaster", "Summoner")`. Mentalist is therefore a **selectable Edge with
its own published mechanics**, not a SWN discipline — which is exactly the "restraint without a second accounting
system" reading. The open question is narrower than it first looked: whether the *Mentalist's own* Effort-like
accounting is allowed to run beside the SWN Effort pool, or must be converted. That one is the author's.

### 5.3 The bounded PC list, and the specialists

* **Natural PC PSI and implants** come from an explicitly bounded list — the #1178 pair, plus device-granted
  abilities whose rules live in the equipment, not in the character.
* **Major PSI is scripted**; **specialist psykers** are mission assets — assigned, limited, and not transferable to
  the PC. Their availability is a faction and mission decision, not a character option.
* **CWN stays the immutable core.** None of this changes a roll, a save or a skill.

## 6. What this document is not

* **Not the memoir.** No prose in Tomi's voice, no first-person fragments, no "sample memory" that an implementer
  could mistake for canon.
* **Not a new species chapter.** `11_ONTOLOGY.md` and `12_BEINGS.md` remain the authority; §2 records proposals to be
  folded into them *by the author*.
* **Not a rules change.** §5 records decisions and one recommendation for a one-line rulebook edit.
* **Not a resolution of the origins.** The Pleiadians do not know where they came from, the Greys may be a labour
  class, the Reptilians may be terrestrial, and the Tridactyls may have been here the whole time.

## 7. Open decisions

1. **The veteran branch** — Hyvinkää soldier, civilian witness, or television-only childhood?
2. **The president's starseed claim** — in the memoir at all, and at what distance (a speech she half-understands)?
3. **The jellyfish** — a Hyvinkää encounter, a coastal rumour, or a later case?
4. **The §6.7 precognition edit** — mark it GM-resolved only?
5. **AWN Mentalist** — conceptual model (recommended reading) or replacement carrier?
6. **The Finnic hint** — one linguist's aside, or nothing at all?
7. **What the school taught her** — the one official sentence about the Crisis that she still repeats, and later
   discovers is wrong?

Number 7 is the one I would write first. It is the smallest thing on this list and it does more work than any of the
others: a sentence a child learns, that an adult finds out was propaganda, is the campaign in one line.
