# Soviet research anchors (#1114)

**Status: research catalogue.** This document exists so that the fiction never confuses *what
happened* with *what was claimed*. It is not lore by itself, and nothing here proves an alien
capability, a psychic power or a secret programme.

Three labels are used throughout:

- **[HISTORY]** — independently documented in the real world.
- **[CLAIM]** — someone asserted it; the assertion itself is documented, the content may be false.
- **[GAME CANON]** — invented for NoöPunk. Fiction, clearly marked, never presented as history.

The rule: **a real institution, programme or reported incident is texture and evidence, never proof
that a fictional capability works.**

Related: [`ALTERNATE_HISTORY_1114.md`](ALTERNATE_HISTORY_1114.md),
[`PROGRAMMA_1114.md`](PROGRAMMA_1114.md).

## 1. SETKA-MO / SETKA-AN (1978–1990/91)

**[HISTORY]** Soviet Ministry of Defence and Academy of Sciences investigation of anomalous aerial
phenomena, coordinated by Yu. V. Platov and B. A. Sokolov. They described approximately **3,000
reports**, of which a minority remained anomalous; **most had prosaic explanations**.

**[CLAIM]** That the programme was a cover for, a precursor to, or a rival of a classified
psychotronic effort.

**[GAME CANON]** ПРОГРАММА knows SETKA existed and had no operational relationship with it — and that
this is exactly what a cover story would say.

**Use:** an investigator can obtain SETKA's published conclusions and discover that a thoroughly
boring report is the most useful document in the case. The interesting fact is the *minority* that
was never explained, not the majority that was.

Sources: Platov & Sokolov's published account; the Sreda archive interview.

## 2. Soviet telepathy and psychophysiology research

**[HISTORY]** L. L. Vasiliev, B. B. Kazhinsky, I. M. Kogan and others produced documented research
proposals, laboratories, publications and experiments in telepathy and psychophysiology. There is
also documented US intelligence interest in that work.

**[CLAIM]** That these programmes produced verified operational telepathy or a working mind-control
machine.

**[GAME CANON]** ПРОГРАММА inherited the literature, the personnel and the institutional habits — and
treats its own partial successes as far larger than they are.

**The discipline that matters here:** the documents establish *that experiments were run and
reported*. They do **not** establish *that the effects were real*. An intelligence assessment of a
Soviet claim is evidence of American belief, not of Soviet capability.

Sources: Psi Encyclopedia entry on Russian psi research; CIA Reading Room holdings.

## 3. Cold War American assessments

**[HISTORY]** Declassified *Controlled Offensive Behavior – USSR* and other US defense assessments
describe claimed Soviet experimentation in hypnosis, telepathy, psychokinesis, cosmonaut
communications and Kirlian imaging.

**[CLAIM]** The claimed successes themselves.

**[GAME CANON]** Both agencies cite the same documents to each other, in bad faith, for decades.

**Use:** these are ideal tier-3 clues. They show that someone took something seriously. They cannot
show that it worked — and a player who treats "the CIA wrote it down" as proof has made the mistake
the campaign is about.

## 4. ZATO — closed cities

**[HISTORY]** The Soviet Union maintained closed administrative-territorial formations (ZATO):
geographically concealed, compartmentalised cities with restricted access, their own infrastructure
and their own internal legitimacy.

**[CLAIM]** That any particular closed city hosted consciousness research rather than nuclear work.

**[GAME CANON]** ПРОГРАММА's facility is a fictional ZATO. **Its site is undisclosed and must not be
pinned to a real closed city.** Inventing a site is acceptable; claiming a real one is not.

**Use:** the ZATO system explains *how* a facility can be missing from the map without requiring
supernatural concealment — which is precisely what makes the later supernatural suspicion credible.

## 5. The Voronezh episode (1989)

**[HISTORY]** TASS publicised dramatic alleged alien sightings at Voronezh. Subsequent reporting
found that a principal correspondent had interviewed children and had not himself witnessed the
event.

**[CLAIM]** That a landing occurred.

**[GAME CANON]** The episode is taught in Europol training as a case study in how an official story
acquires extravagant detail without substantiation.

**Use:** the perfect first-act debunking. A player who expects the campaign to be about aliens learns
that the most famous Soviet UFO story is a journalism failure — right before something that is not.

## 6. The Law of One as an in-universe source text

**[HISTORY]** *The Law of One* is a channelled text. It is **not** independently validated
archaeology, geopolitics or science.

**[GAME CANON]** A poor, ideologically distorted Russian translation of it becomes the doctrinal
seed of ПРОГРАММА's Marxist–Leninist theory of consciousness. The participants do not regard it as a
religious import.

Used as material, with the passage index preserved:

| Passage | What the text claims | How NoöPunk uses it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **8.6** | Confederation technology given to Russians around the 1950s; later judged a mistake | Origin of the contact — and of the break |
| **65.8** | Russian experiments with psychotronic weather manipulation and geopolitical coercion | An archival **allegation**, not a settled capability |
| **10.15**, **21.24** | Alleged Mu/Lemurian survivors across regions including Russia | A mythic archaeological stratum beneath Soviet secret history; no archaeology is forced into canon |
| **17.3** | Tunguska described as involving a destroyed fission reactor | A speculative in-universe dossier, not a scientific finding |

**Never** present a channelled passage as the setting's omniscient narrator. It is a source that
characters can find, misread, translate badly and kill each other over.

## 7. What this document forbids

- Presenting a real programme's existence as proof of a fictional capability.
- Presenting an intelligence assessment as an established fact.
- Presenting a channelled text as verified history.
- Locating ПРОГРАММА's facility at a real closed city.
- Presenting a Mandela-effect memory as the playable baseline, or the baseline as the only memory.
