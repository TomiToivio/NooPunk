# Europol Helsinki Field Office — campaign frame (#1114)

> **Status: canonical campaign frame.** The default NoöPunk player characters work for **Europol**,
> initially from the **Europol Helsinki Field Office**.
>
> This replaces the earlier "rookie UNSA field agent" framing. UNSA still exists and still matters;
> it is not the player's default employer.
>
> Setting history and the authority for this change: [Alternate history — the NoöPunk timeline](ALTERNATE_HISTORY_1114.md).

## 1. Why Europol

NoöPunk's Europe is a fortified, integrationist EU that survived the Singularity and the first
shock of Disclosure. Europol is the institution through which European states pool cross-border
investigative capacity without dissolving into a single sovereign police force.

That is the useful fictional space for the default campaign:

- the characters have **cross-border reach**, but not unlimited power;
- they depend on national police, prosecutors and intelligence services;
- they can work NHI, psychic, cyber and ordinary crime cases in the same office;
- institutional friction is built into the premise rather than added as a complication.

## 2. The office

The **Europol Helsinki Field Office** is a small, overworked EU institution embedded in a Finnish
capital that already has its own security culture.

It is not a fortress. It is a set of rooms, a secure network, a filing system with too many
compartments, and a staff that knows the difference between a lead and a warrant.

The office handles cases that cross borders or exceed a single member state's analytical
capacity: NHI-adjacent crime, hybrid infiltration, psychotronic trafficking, cyber-intrusion,
identity and memory fraud, anomalous deaths, and the ordinary organised crime that turns out to
be connected to all of the above.

## 3. Powers and limits

The following is the working model. Exact legal citations, ranks and budget are **open author
decisions**; do not invent them as settled canon.

Europol can:

- own and coordinate a cross-border investigation where member states have delegated competence;
- convene **joint investigation teams** with Finnish police, Supo and other national authorities;
- task analysis, intelligence fusion and forensic/cyber support;
- issue alerts, requests and liaison requests through EU channels;
- negotiate access to UNSA databases, NATO remnants, corporate archives or NHI-response assets —
  but it must negotiate.

Europol cannot:

- arrest, search or detain on its own authority in Finland;
- override a national prosecutor or a member state's intelligence service;
- compel UNSA, the military or a corporation to hand over material;
- declare someone an NHI, a hybrid or a psychic threat. It can request an assessment; the
  assessment is evidence, not a verdict.

The practical rule for play: **Europol opens doors; national officers walk through them.** A
Europol investigator who forgets this creates a jurisdictional incident, not a shortcut.

## 4. Relations

### Finnish police and Supo

The working relationship is close, unequal and occasionally resentful. Finnish officers have the
arrest powers; Supo has the institutional memory and the files; Europol has the cross-border
mandate and the EU budget line. A case can be legitimate in The Hague and politically impossible
in Helsinki.

### EU institutions

The Commission, the Council, the European Parliament and the EU's security structures are not a
single voice. Europol answers to a mandate written by compromise. When a case touches a member
state's government, the mandate becomes a live political question.

### UNSA

UNSA still exists. It coordinates science, intelligence, diplomacy and NHI response where states
allow it. It is not a world government and not the player's employer.

Europol may:

- second an officer to a UNSA-led task force;
- receive UNSA analysis as a partner;
- find that UNSA has already made a deal with a national government, a corporation or an NHI
  faction;
- discover that a UNSA officer is a rival, a source, a liability or all three.

### National governments and corporations

Sovereignty matters. A Europol case that embarrasses a member state can be starved of resources,
reclassified, leaked or handed to another agency. Corporations treat Europol as a compliance
cost and a possible competitor. Neither is monolithic.

## 5. The player characters

The default unit is a **newly assigned Europol field team** in Helsinki, not a group of global
super-agents. They may be:

- seconded national police or intelligence officers;
- Europol analysts who have moved into field work;
- specialists in cyber, forensics, psychology, noetics or NHI casework;
- a Finnish officer whose national role is the source of the team's legal powers.

The shared training package is the existing academy baseline. In-setting it is the **Europol Academy** (working name); mechanically the
`data/rules/unsa_academy.json` package and its tests remain the compatibility reference, not a
second rules set.

Ranks, appointment procedures and the exact mixture of seconded and direct Europol staff are
**author decisions**. The campaign needs the hierarchy to be legible, not fully specified in this
document.

## 6. Campaign start

A first case should be small enough for a field office and large enough to cross a border.

Recommended shape:

1. An ordinary incident in Helsinki with a plausible mundane explanation.
2. A second jurisdiction, a second record, or a second witness that does not fit.
3. A national authority with a reason to close the case.
4. A UNSA or EU interest that is real but not automatically trustworthy.
5. A decision the characters can make with incomplete authority, and consequences they cannot
   file away.

The NHI, PSI and cyberpunk layers enter as **evidence and jurisdiction problems**, not as a
mandatory escalation ladder. A case can remain human, criminal and political and still be
NoöPunk.

## 7. What this frame does not decide

- Europol's precise legal competence in the alternate timeline.
- Ranks, pay, appointment and disciplinary procedure.
- Whether the Helsinki Field Office is a permanent Europol office, a joint team, or a temporary
  mission with a permanent name.
- How much UNSA influence remains inside EU security structures.
- Whether the office survives the later NHI Crisis or becomes something else.

These are recorded as open questions in
[Alternate history §9](ALTERNATE_HISTORY_1114.md#9-open-author-decisions).
