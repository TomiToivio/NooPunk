# Social-system sources for issue #107

This file records the sources directly grounding NoöPunk's Affect/Faction/Contacts design.

## Discourse theory

- Ernesto Laclau & Chantal Mouffe, Hegemony and Socialist Strategy (2nd ed., 2001): articulation, equivalence/difference, antagonism, hegemony and relational political identity.
- Ernesto Laclau, Emancipation(s) (1996): empty signification and political representation.
- Ernesto Laclau, On Populist Reason (2005): equivalential chains, collective subjects, affective investment, empty signifiers and frontier construction.
- Emilia Palonen, The Birth and Death of Liberal Democracy in Hungary: The Populist Logic of Polarisation as Hegemony (2025): Formula of Populism and the Us^Affects + Frontier^Affects heuristic.
- Tomi Toivio, LaclauGPT: Ideological contestation over AI, in LaclauGPT-Discourse-Analysis/paper/PAPER.md: computational operationalization of articulation, demands, subjects, signifier roles, frontiers, affective investment, evidence and provenance.

NoöPunk generalizes Palonen's formula beyond populism. The RPG uses it as a social/faction representation, not an algorithm deciding which actor is populist.

## RPG and interactive-fiction references

- Eclipse Phase 2E: starting point for factions, motivations and reputation. NoöPunk replaces the setting-specific lists with its own Affect graph/content.
- Cyberpunk RED: a comparison point for friends/enemies, relationship hooks and street-level Contacts (design comparison only; no derivation claim).
- The Expanse Roleplaying Game: crew-centered SF play, Ties/Relationships/Reputation and Belt/station political economy.
- Citizen Sleeper: text-forward hub/task/relationship loop.
- Citizen Sleeper 2: crew structure, travel between hubs and missions away from hubs.
- Richard K. Morgan, Altered Carbon, and its TV adaptation: embodiment, identity transfer, inequality, crime and resleeving-like social consequences.
- James S. A. Corey, The Expanse novels, and the TV adaptation: Belt society, crews, political economy and human conflict around non-human technology.
