# Scenario 01: First Day in Ö-Mappi

> **Status:** first playable scenario draft for issue #141.
>
> **Scope:** scenario-local material. This file may introduce temporary names, clues,
> incidents and interpretations for play. It does **not** redefine the wider NoöPunk
> timeline, psionics rules, hacking rules, combat rules or NHI canon.
>
> **This entire document is fictional alternate-history lore. Real people, governments,
> companies and institutions are used as fictionalized setting elements; the events
> described here are not claims about real-world history or evidence.**
>
> **Chronology in this document is scenario-local.** The historical anchors it uses —
> the 1930s Ghost Flier wave and the 1946 Ghost Rockets — are canon; the Ö-Mappi
> institutional history built on them is this scenario's narrative variant, not a global
> chronology. No exact future year is assigned anywhere in this file: the setting is
> **20XX**, and the anchors are historical canon years used as such.
>
> **Rules boundary:** use the canonical NoöPunk core where a check is needed:
>
> ```text
> STAT + Skill + 1d10 vs Difficulty Value
> ```
>
> The six base STATs are **FIT, REF, INT, SOC, CYB and PSY** (RULEBOOK.md §9.2; issue
> #131 supersedes the earlier twelve-stat draft). Where the current
> rulebook has not yet defined a subsystem, resolve the scene fiction-first and mark
> any mechanical detail as a TODO rather than inventing a new subsystem.
>
> **Names and statistics remain editable while drafting.** This file fixes the playable
> *situation*; the character-generation procedure and the final numbers are the
> character-creation pass's business.

## 1. Scenario pitch

**First Day in Ö-Mappi** introduces NoöPunk through a new Europol graduate arriving
for their first posting in Helsinki.

The character expects ordinary probationary work. Instead they are assigned to the
Suojelupoliisi / Europol psychological-operations unit known informally as
**Ö-Mappi**, a bureaucratic afterlife for cases involving anomalous psychology,
UAPs, strange technology, intelligence embarrassments and incidents nobody wants
to classify in writing.

Before the recruit can finish the first coffee, a mundane evidence-transfer problem
becomes an active incident. A sealed evidence case from an old Suojelupoliisi
archive has appeared inside a modern police logistics chain under an impossible
tracking identity. A frightened courier insists that the case was empty when he
loaded it. A building-security camera shows somebody walking beside him who does not
appear on any other sensor. The evidence case is now emitting an obsolete radio
identifier associated with a 1946 file.

The first session should begin as administrative police work and end with the
player realizing that Ö-Mappi exists because the impossible repeatedly learns how
to fill out forms.

## 2. Player character: Europol graduate

The player begins as a newly graduated Europol officer assigned to Helsinki.

All player characters have completed a common academy foundation:

- standard law-enforcement training;
- counterintelligence training;
- evidence handling and chain-of-custody procedure;
- interviewing and report writing;
- operational security;
- basic familiarity with Europol and national-police cooperation.

Do **not** define a complete character-generation procedure in this scenario. The
current character-generation rules are still under design.

For this scenario, the player chooses one specialist-training track as part of the
character concept.

### 2.1. SWAT / tactical operations

The character has additional training in high-risk entry, protective operations,
threat assessment, scene security and coordinated tactical response.

Use this background to justify competence in physical-security scenes. Do not infer
new combat mechanics.

Early spotlight:

- securing the evidence-transfer area;
- noticing unsafe access routes or surveillance positions;
- controlling a frightened witness without escalating the situation;
- leading the team if the case produces an immediate physical threat.

### 2.2. Psionic asset

The character has completed specialist training as a monitored psionic asset.

The exact powers, ranges, costs and procedures of psionics are **not** defined here.

For this scenario, the character may be permitted to report anomalous impressions,
empathic reactions or other author-approved psychic phenomena as narrative
information. Do not invent a complete PSI move list.

Early spotlight:

- reading the emotional atmosphere around a witness or location;
- distinguishing ordinary fear from something more anomalous;
- recognizing when another sensitive person is reacting to the same stimulus;
- comparing impressions with the team's experienced empath.

### 2.3. Infosec asset

The character has additional training in digital forensics, defensive security,
network investigation, authentication, logging and incident response.

The full NoöPunk hacking subsystem is still deferred. This track therefore grants
fictional access and professional competence, not a new hacking minigame.

Early spotlight:

- verifying the evidence case's tracking identity;
- finding contradictory audit logs;
- separating ordinary spoofing from system-level impossibility;
- tracing who or what inserted an obsolete identifier into a modern logistics
  system.

### 2.4. Intelligence analyst

The character has additional training in source evaluation, link analysis,
counterintelligence, pattern recognition, archival research and intelligence
reporting.

Early spotlight:

- reconstructing the chain of custody;
- identifying historical references in the evidence code;
- separating witness error, deception, bureaucratic failure and anomaly;
- connecting the modern incident to old Ö-Mappi material without jumping to a
  conclusion.

## 3. Opening: first morning in Helsinki

### 3.1. Arrival

Open with the character arriving at the Suojelupoliisi / Europol office in Helsinki
for the first day of work.

The exterior and lobby should feel secure, competent and disappointingly ordinary.
The strange material belongs behind several doors.

Suggested opening narration:

> Your academy credentials work on the first door.
>
> The second door requires a separate Europol token.
>
> The third refuses both and opens only after a receptionist phones somebody who
> says, after a long silence, "Yes. Apparently that one is ours."
>
> Your first posting begins with a handwritten visitor badge attached to a lanyard
> printed with three different organizational logos.

A normal onboarding officer is missing. Instead, the player is collected by one of
the Ö-Mappi team.

If the player asks why they were assigned there, nobody gives the same answer:

- "specialist staffing";
- "joint European mandate";
- "temporary secondment";
- "security vetting";
- "Romppainen requested a graduate";
- "there was a spreadsheet."

All of these may be true.

### 3.2. The walk downstairs

Ö-Mappi should not occupy a glamorous secret command center.

Place it in an awkward institutional space: old archive rooms, a converted secure
office area, obsolete acoustic panels, modern terminals bolted beside old filing
cabinets, too many locks, and one corridor whose signs look newer than the walls.

Visual details:

- a secure cabinet labelled with a modern QR code over an older paper inventory
  number;
- archival boxes beside encrypted storage;
- a wall map of Finland with several eras of pins and transparent overlays;
- a whiteboard covered in ordinary case references, with several entries simply
  marked **Ö**;
- a framed newspaper clipping about an aviation mystery;
- a locked glass cabinet containing objects that look disappointingly mundane;
- one office plant that nobody admits owning;
- three incompatible coffee systems.

The unit feels less like a hidden conspiracy and more like decades of classified
bureaucracy sedimenting in one room.

## 4. The Ö-Mappi team

The following names are **draft scenario names** except for Dr. Harri Romppainen.
They can be changed later without affecting the scenario structure.

### 4.1. Dr. Harri Romppainen

**Role:** team lead, senior investigator, institutional memory.

Romppainen has the air of somebody who has spent years explaining absurd facts in
rooms where everybody is pretending not to be surprised.

He speaks precisely, dislikes melodrama and rarely uses the word "alien" unless
somebody else uses it first.

His office contains far too many physical folders for a modern intelligence unit.
He insists this is because "paper has a better threat model than most people."

Useful traits:

- treats extraordinary claims as evidence problems;
- knows the unit's history but admits that parts of it are missing, contradictory or
  deliberately falsified;
- is respected enough to ignore some organizational etiquette;
- can become fascinated by elegant theories;
- has blind spots around institutional loyalty and old cases;
- refuses to use uncertainty as an excuse for sloppy procedure.

Suggested first line:

> "Congratulations on graduating. I am sorry about your career."

Then, after a beat:

> "That was a joke. Probably."

### 4.2. Draft NPC: Aino Laakso

**Role:** tactical specialist.

Aino is young, physically fit and visibly more comfortable in operational gear than
in meetings. She has the brisk focus of somebody who assumes every room has an
entrance, an exit and at least one person standing in the wrong place.

She is not the team's blunt instrument. She is the person most likely to ask:

- Who has access?
- Who is exposed?
- What can physically hurt us?
- Who has not been searched?
- Why are we discussing ontology before establishing a perimeter?

Personality:

- dry, competitive humor;
- little patience for mystical language;
- protective toward civilians and inexperienced officers;
- respects competence immediately;
- mildly offended by Ö-Mappi's storage practices.

Relationship to a tactical PC:

Aino initially treats the player as an academy graduate who has not yet learned the
difference between training and a bad corridor at 03:00. She can become a mentor,
friendly rival or trusted field partner.

### 4.3. Draft NPC: Mika "Mikke" Rautio

**Role:** infosec specialist.

Mikke has long hair, smells unmistakably of weed, wears a faded Hawkwind T-shirt
under an open shirt and regards the existence of police hierarchy as a software bug.

He hates ordinary police work.

He particularly hates:

- forms;
- parking enforcement;
- procurement;
- mandatory briefings;
- the phrase "cyber hygiene";
- people who call every suspicious login "AI".

He is nevertheless exceptionally useful because he understands how state systems,
criminal infrastructure, legacy databases and badly maintained classified networks
actually intersect.

He should never be only comic relief.

Useful traits:

- suspicious of claims that a system is secure;
- deeply serious about source protection;
- contemptuous of institutional surveillance used lazily;
- knows a great deal about obsolete communications hardware;
- has personal reasons for preferring Ö-Mappi to mainstream cyber policing;
- immediately likes a player who questions an audit trail instead of trusting it.

Suggested introduction:

The player first sees Mikke under a desk, arguing with a secure terminal.

> "If anyone asks, this is authorized maintenance."
>
> "Is it?"
>
> "No. That's why I need you to remember the first sentence."

### 4.4. Draft NPC: Leena Saarinen

**Role:** experienced police investigator and psionic asset.

Leena is older and more experienced than Aino and Mikke. She was an ordinary police
investigator before becoming publicly or institutionally known as a psionic asset.

Her strongest established ability in this scenario is **empathy**.

Do not define a full psychic subsystem around her.

Leena's professional rule is that psychic impressions are **leads**, not proof.

If she says someone feels terrified, she still wants an interview.
If she senses hostility, she still wants physical evidence.
If she experiences something impossible, she writes down the time.

Useful traits:

- calm interviewing style;
- notices emotional contradictions;
- compassionate without being naive;
- uncomfortable with people treating PSI as magic;
- experienced enough to know that frightened witnesses often lie for ordinary
  reasons;
- sees empathy as simultaneously social and psychic.

Suggested line to a psionic PC:

> "First rule: write the impression down before somebody tells you what they think
> happened. Second rule: never confuse certainty with intensity."

## 5. Romppainen's briefing: what is Ö-Mappi?

The first briefing should happen informally, ideally while the character is still
waiting for normal onboarding paperwork.

Romppainen does not deliver a complete setting lecture. He gives the recruit just
enough history to explain why the unit exists.

### 5.1. The name

"Ö-Mappi" is an old nickname for the file where cases went when they did not fit
ordinary classifications.

The exact origin of the nickname can remain institutionally disputed.

Romppainen's preferred explanation:

> "A, B, C and so on describe things the organization understands. Ö is where the
> alphabet ends."

### 5.2. Ghost Flyers, 1930s

Romppainen says the unit's institutional ancestry begins with Finnish security and
aviation authorities investigating strange aerial reports during the **Ghost Flyer
wave of the 1930s**.

The important point is not that every report was extraordinary. Most were not.

The enduring problem was a small residue of cases involving combinations of:

- credible witnesses;
- anomalous flight behavior;
- intelligence concerns;
- ambiguous foreign activity;
- evidence that did not fit available aircraft.

The intelligence service learned an early lesson:

**A phenomenon can be both an intelligence problem and an anomalous phenomenon at
the same time.**

### 5.3. Ghost Rockets, 1946

During the **Ghost Rocket wave of 1946**, investigators were drawn into what later
Ö-Mappi records describe as a crash-retrieval incident.

Romppainen does not show the player the evidence.

He says only:

- material was recovered;
- contemporary investigators concluded that at least some of it represented
  technology not attributable to a known terrestrial state;
- parts of the file were removed into other programs;
- some records contradict each other;
- later copies appear to have been edited;
- Ö-Mappi inherited fragments, references and institutional suspicion rather than a
  complete archive.

If pressed on whether this proves extraterrestrial technology, Romppainen answers:

> "It proves that people with security clearances in 1946 believed they had a
> problem important enough to lie about for eighty years. That is already an
> interesting fact."

### 5.4. Europol and the UN Security Administration

Over time, the unit survived by changing administrative homes.

In the current campaign it sits awkwardly across:

- Suojelupoliisi;
- Europol;
- newer international security arrangements;
- the UN Security Administration — i.e. the **UNSA** structure that RULEBOOK.md §38
  makes the default campaign institution.

The UNSA connection is what turns a Finnish curiosity into a live institutional problem:
UNSA holds formal jurisdiction in NHI and X-Risk matters across the human Solar System,
but **legal jurisdiction is not actual access and not political cooperation** (§38). A
basement unit in Helsinki sits exactly in the gap between those three things, which is why
its chain of command can be simultaneously real and unanswerable.

This creates overlapping authority.

Use the bureaucracy for play:

- one system recognizes the player's Europol credentials;
- another identifies them as a national-police secondment;
- a third has them listed as "external specialist";
- Romppainen's team roster may not match payroll records.

The unit is real enough to issue orders and ambiguous enough that everybody prefers
not to ask who owns it.

## 6. Inciting incident: the impossible evidence transfer

Interrupt Romppainen's briefing before it becomes comfortable.

A secure-logistics alert appears.

A sealed evidence case has arrived at the building loading area.

The problem is that nobody requested it.

The second problem is that its tracking identity is valid in the current logistics
system but maps to an archival reference created decades before the system existed.

The third problem is that the archival reference belongs to an Ö-Mappi file.

Romppainen stops talking.

He reads the identifier twice.

Then:

> "Right. Your orientation is now practical."

### 6.1. What the team initially knows

The courier is a legitimate contracted secure-evidence driver.

The vehicle is legitimate.

The route is legitimate.

The case was not on the original Helsinki delivery manifest.

The manifest changed while the vehicle was in transit.

The digital audit trail claims the modification was authorized by an account that
does not exist.

The physical case is old but has been fitted with a current electronic evidence
seal.

The modern seal is intact.

The case is broadcasting a short-range identifier associated with an archival code:

**Ö-46 / [draft suffix]**

Do not define the suffix as wider canon yet.

### 6.2. The witness

The courier, **Jari Niemi** (draft scenario name), is frightened and embarrassed.

He initially gives a normal explanation:

> "It must be a loading mistake."

But under careful questioning he admits:

- he did not see the case loaded;
- during the last part of the route he became convinced somebody was sitting beside
  him;
- he never clearly saw the person's face;
- when he looked directly, the seat was empty;
- he assumed exhaustion;
- at one traffic stop he remembers the passenger saying: **"Take it back."**

He has not told his employer because he fears losing medical clearance.

This is where Leena's empathy becomes useful. The courier is genuinely afraid of
being considered unstable.

### 6.3. The camera anomaly

Building-security video shows the courier bringing the case inside.

On one camera, for several seconds, a second person appears to walk beside him.

On adjacent cameras, no second person is visible.

The figure is indistinct.

Do **not** establish in this scenario whether this is:

- sensor error;
- manipulated footage;
- a person using advanced concealment;
- a psychic/perceptual phenomenon;
- an NHI manifestation;
- something else.

The first scenario is about disciplined uncertainty.

## 7. Investigation routes

The player should have several simultaneous routes. None is the "correct class
path." The specialist training changes what information becomes easy to notice.

### 7.1. Tactical / physical route

A tactical character may:

- secure the loading area;
- identify who could physically access the vehicle or loading dock;
- inspect the case exterior before anybody opens it;
- notice whether the courier could have moved the case alone;
- review escape routes;
- check for a real second person;
- organize safe movement of the case.

Useful discovery:

There are no convincing physical traces of a second passenger in the vehicle.

However, one seat sensor logged intermittent weight during the final section of the
trip.

This clue should create a problem, not solve one.

Possible checks:

- **Difficult (DV 15):** spot an inconsistency in the loading area's physical
  security narrative.
- **Professional (DV 17):** reconstruct a plausible access route that avoids the
  obvious cameras.

Use an appropriate STAT + relevant trained Skill if the character sheet supports it.

### 7.2. Infosec / cybernetic route

An infosec character may:

- verify signatures on the electronic evidence seal;
- compare logistics logs across systems;
- inspect the vehicle's local records;
- detect whether the anomalous account existed only at transaction time;
- compare camera timestamps;
- determine whether footage was conventionally edited.

Useful discoveries:

1. The evidence seal is cryptographically valid.
2. No currently authorized account signed it.
3. The authorization chain resolves to a retired system namespace preserved only for
   archive migration.
4. The archive namespace contains the string corresponding to **Ö-46**.
5. The camera anomaly does not look like an ordinary post-recording edit.
6. Several sensors disagree about the exact number of people present.

Mikke becomes interested when the player asks **which system is lying** rather than
"was it hacked?"

Possible checks:

- **Difficult (DV 15):** establish that the manifest change was not a normal user
  action.
- **Professional (DV 17):** connect the authorization chain to an obsolete archive
  namespace.

Do not introduce offensive hacking rules.

### 7.3. Intelligence-analysis route

An analyst may:

- reconstruct the chain of custody;
- identify the archival code;
- search prior case references;
- compare historical events with the present incident;
- evaluate competing hypotheses.

Useful discovery:

The Ö-46 reference is not the identifier of the famous 1946 crash-retrieval file
itself.

It appears in marginal references to a **missing transfer record** associated with
material moved after the event.

One note, preserved in a later summary, says:

> "Object not received at destination. Transport personnel report discrepancy.
> Further interviews restricted."

The modern case may be reenacting, repeating or referencing an old failure of chain
of custody.

Possible checks:

- **Everyday (DV 13):** find the historical connection once archive access is
  granted.
- **Difficult (DV 15):** distinguish the missing-transfer reference from the main
  crash-retrieval file.
- **Professional (DV 17):** identify deliberate later redaction patterns.

### 7.4. Social / interview route

Any character, especially one strong in SOC, may:

- interview the courier;
- compare his first statement with later details;
- reassure him about medical and employment consequences;
- question loading personnel;
- identify whether somebody is concealing an ordinary mistake.

Useful discovery:

The courier is minimizing the strange part but not inventing it.

With trust, he recalls one more detail.

The unseen passenger did not sound threatening.

The voice sounded **tired**.

And when it said "Take it back," the courier had the strong impression that it did
not mean the building.

It meant **somewhere else**.

Possible checks:

- **Everyday (DV 13):** get the courier to admit there was an anomalous experience.
- **Difficult (DV 15):** obtain the detail about the phrase "Take it back."

### 7.5. Psychic route

A psionic asset may examine the emotional or psychic context of:

- the courier;
- the vehicle;
- the evidence case;
- the room in which the case is stored.

Because NoöPunk's psionic mechanics are not yet finalized, do **not** assign powers,
ranges or costs here.

Fictional guidance:

- the courier carries ordinary fear, shame and confusion;
- the strongest anomalous impression should be associated with the **case**, not the
  courier;
- Leena independently reacts to the same location, confirming that the player's
  impression is not automatically solitary;
- the impression may feel like **displacement**, **return**, **unfinished transfer**
  or **wrong destination**.

Do not reveal an objective metaphysical truth through empathy.

Leena's response:

> "Good. Write exactly what you felt."
>
> "What does it mean?"
>
> "No idea. That's why we write it down."

## 8. Opening the case

The team must eventually decide whether to open the case.

The decision should matter.

### Option A: open it immediately

Aino argues for physical control and a proper containment procedure.
Mikke wants every signal captured first.
Leena asks for baseline impressions before the seal breaks.
Romppainen wants the chain of custody documented.

If the player insists on opening it quickly, allow it. The scenario should not block
action merely to preserve atmosphere.

### Option B: investigate before opening

The team gains more context and may identify the 1946 transfer reference first.

This makes the contents more meaningful.

### Option C: transfer it elsewhere

This produces immediate bureaucratic conflict.

Another authority may refuse custody because the case lacks a valid originating
agency.

The player learns that sometimes anomalous containment is mostly about convincing
somebody to sign the form.

## 9. What is inside

The case contains three things.

### 9.1. An old evidence envelope

The envelope is physically old and bears archival markings consistent with the
post-war period.

Its seal has been broken and resealed at least once.

Inside is a small quantity of dark metallic-looking fragments.

Do not define their composition as canonical alien material in this scenario.

### 9.2. A modern evidence tag

A modern tag has been attached to the old envelope.

It was printed recently.

The issuing field is blank.

The destination field contains only:

**RETURN TO SOURCE**

### 9.3. A photograph

A black-and-white photograph shows several investigators beside covered wreckage in
a wooded area.

One figure in the background is marked in pen with a circle.

The figure is indistinct.

Romppainen recognizes the photograph as a variant of an image from the 1946 file,
but this copy contains a person who is not visible in the version he knows.

That same silhouette resembles the second person seen on the loading-dock camera.

Do not make the match perfect.

Let players argue about it.

## 10. First-session escalation

Once the case is opened, the situation becomes active.

Choose one or combine two depending on pacing.

### 10.1. Sensor cascade

Nearby secure systems begin logging mutually inconsistent occupancy data.

- one badge reader reports an extra person;
- a room sensor reports nobody present;
- a camera briefly shows the wrong number of people;
- a legacy radio receiver activates without a known transmitter.

This supports infosec and tactical play without requiring a firefight.

### 10.2. Emotional intrusion

Leena and any psionic PC experience the same sudden emotional impression:

**urgency without hostility.**

Then:

**wrong place.**

The effect ends.

Treat this as an event, not a psionic rules precedent.

### 10.3. Institutional interference

A message arrives from outside Ö-Mappi ordering the case transferred immediately to
another authority.

The order is authentic enough to be dangerous and ambiguous enough to be
suspicious.

This creates a social/counterintelligence problem:

- obey;
- delay;
- verify;
- refuse;
- duplicate records first;
- ask who initiated the order.

This route is especially useful if the table prefers conspiracy and bureaucracy over
phenomenology.

## 11. First-session climax: the custody decision

The climax is not "fight the alien."

It is a decision about **custody, evidence and trust**.

The team has an anomalous object, conflicting orders, incomplete history and no
reliable explanation.

Give the player meaningful agency.

### Choice 1: keep the case in Ö-Mappi

The player supports Romppainen's argument that the unit has the best historical
context.

Consequence:

Ö-Mappi gains time, but another authority may consider the team obstructive.

### Choice 2: transfer the case

The player prioritizes formal chain of command.

Consequence:

The case leaves, but the player may preserve copies, samples of data or testimony
depending on prior choices.

The receiving authority becomes a future hook.

### Choice 3: split the evidence

The player proposes preserving independent records before transfer.

Consequence:

This may be procedurally questionable but intelligence-smart.

Romppainen approves of the reasoning even if he dislikes the paperwork.

### Choice 4: follow "RETURN TO SOURCE"

If the player interprets the tag as an operational lead, the team begins trying to
identify what "source" means.

This should not be resolved in session one.

Possible interpretations:

- the original 1946 recovery location;
- a storage site;
- the person or organization that moved the object;
- a nonhuman source;
- something in Noöspace;
- deliberate deception.

The phrase becomes the bridge to later scenarios.

## 12. Spotlight matrix

The first session should allow every specialist track to matter.

| Specialist track | Early spotlight | Mid-session spotlight | Climax contribution |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| SWAT / tactical | secure arrival scene | reconstruct physical access / protect team | control custody transfer or anomalous scene |
| Psionic asset | sense witness/case atmosphere | compare impressions with Leena | identify change in psychic/emotional state |
| Infosec asset | audit logistics identity | inspect sensors, seal and camera data | preserve or challenge digital custody |
| Intelligence analyst | trace Ö-46 | reconstruct 1946 transfer history | evaluate competing custody/source hypotheses |

No route should be mandatory.

A player may solve an infosec problem socially, a tactical problem analytically, or
a psychic problem by refusing to trust an impression.

That flexibility is intentional.

## 13. Scene order for the GM

A simple first-session structure:

1. **Arrival at Helsinki office**
2. **Meet one Ö-Mappi member**
3. **Enter the unit**
4. **Romppainen begins orientation**
5. **Brief history: Ghost Flyers**
6. **Brief history: Ghost Rockets**
7. **Logistics alert interrupts briefing**
8. **Meet the rest of the team in action**
9. **Interview courier**
10. **Investigate physical / digital / archival / psychic evidence**
11. **Decide when to open the case**
12. **Discover envelope, tag and photograph**
13. **Trigger escalation**
14. **Receive or discover custody pressure**
15. **Player makes custody decision**
16. **End on "RETURN TO SOURCE"**

This order can be rearranged freely.

## 14. GM information layers

Keep three layers separate during play.

### 14.1. Public / player-facing facts

These can be established in session one:

- Ö-Mappi is a real Suojelupoliisi / Europol team with a strange remit;
- its institutional roots reach back to anomalous aviation investigations;
- it inherited material connected to Ghost Flyer and Ghost Rocket cases;
- a 1946 crash-retrieval investigation exists in its history;
- the current evidence case entered a modern logistics system anomalously;
- multiple sensors disagree about a possible second person;
- the case contains old material, a modern "RETURN TO SOURCE" tag and an anomalous
  photograph.

### 14.2. Classified but discoverable facts

Reveal according to investigation quality:

- the Ö-46 code points to a missing transfer associated with the 1946 incident;
- some old records were intentionally altered;
- modern systems used an obsolete archive namespace during the incident;
- an external authority is already aware of the case;
- this may not be the first time an object linked to Ö-Mappi has re-entered a later
  chain of custody.

### 14.3. Deeper truth: deliberately unresolved

Do **not** canonize the following in this draft:

- who or what the second figure is;
- whether the fragments are extraterrestrial;
- whether the case physically moved through time;
- whether the incident is psychic, cybernetic, technological, NHI-related or a
  hybrid;
- what "source" means;
- which organization altered the historical file;
- whether the 1946 transfer failed accidentally or intentionally.

Future issues can define these.

The first scenario works better if the mystery remains wider than the answer.

## 15. Tone guidance

Keep the strange material embedded in professional behavior.

Good tone:

- somebody experiences something impossible and then has to complete an incident
  form;
- advanced surveillance produces contradictory rather than omniscient information;
- Romppainen discusses a possible nonhuman artifact while looking for the correct
  evidence label;
- the hacker is irreverent about police bureaucracy but serious about evidence;
- the empath refuses to call feelings proof;
- the tactical officer treats uncertainty as a reason for better procedure, not
  panic.

Avoid:

- immediate cosmic monologues;
- NPCs who already know the complete metaphysics;
- every anomaly being an alien;
- every system failure being "AI";
- psychic impressions functioning as perfect truth;
- turning Helsinki into generic neon cyberpunk scenery;
- introducing major factions or technologies the author has not specified.

The opening should feel specifically like a Finnish / European institutional space
that has been forced to accommodate a category of problem it was never designed to
name.

## 16. Hooks for Scenario 02 and beyond

Use one or more according to later author direction.

### Hook A: Return to Source

The team identifies a physical location connected to the missing 1946 transfer.

This becomes a field investigation.

### Hook B: The receiving authority

If the case was transferred, the receiving organization later reports that it never
arrived.

The paperwork says otherwise.

### Hook C: The photograph

Image analysis reveals that the circled figure appears in other unrelated historical

## 18. What this scenario tests

This scenario is also a design test for NoöPunk.

It should reveal whether the game can support:

- ordinary investigative play;
- persistent NPC relationships;
- the six-STAT character model without excessive complexity;
- specialist identity without rigid character classes;
- multiple routes through one problem;
- social, physical, cybernetic and psychic approaches inside the same scene;
- narrative consequences without requiring bespoke "moves" for every action;
- anomalous material without replacing investigation with exposition;
- cyberpunk and Noösphere elements in the same playable space.

Any missing rule exposed by play should become a **separate design issue** rather
than being silently invented inside this scenario.
