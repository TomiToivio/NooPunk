## Pitch

Three patients in separate units report a red door and an unfamiliar forest road during unrelated clinical interviews. One also writes a grid of numbers. A clinician notices their exact descriptions appear in sealed study data from another Nordic country. The hospital fears a privacy breach; an external research sponsor demands the study continue. Romppainen's cell is invited to investigate possible cross-border research misconduct, **not to diagnose supernatural ability**.

### Opening, player roles and clear authority

Choose 2–5 investigators: national seconded officer (search authority through national channels), hospital liaison, digital forensics specialist, interviewing specialist, folklorist or trained SWN psychic; if fewer, make allied roles NPCs. Romppainen directs and Lotta Kallio protects witnesses and handles field security. A patient advocate challenges procedural shortcuts. For a trainee introduction, borrow the academy-arrival structure from [First Day in Ö-Mappi](01_FIRST_DAY_IN_OMAPPI.md).

At minute one give players **two leads without a roll**:
- interview a willing witness with a patient advocate present;
- inspect the sponsor's ethically releasable audit extract and request authority for additional records.

Consent and lawful disclosure determine access; nonconsenting witnesses can be protected without becoming obstacles to the campaign.

## Truth layer for the GM (choose one before play)

**A. Tech crime with unexplained residue:** the sponsor's dream-therapy system leaked stimuli across sites through a training-data pipeline. An archived real-world image accounts for the road; an independently timed acoustic anomaly remains unattributed.

**B. Noetic coupling:** a machine-assisted protocol accidentally couples participants' experiences to a place-bound noetic intelligence. Corporate mistakes still caused the unsafe study. Entity is not necessarily hostile.

**C. Adversarial intervention:** a third party (Orion contractor, a rogue AI cutout, or an unaffiliated actor) inserted experiences to map susceptible minds and divert the team's attention from an actual material theft.

Keep clinical conditions and care valid in all three. Players should be able to identify misconduct and stop harm even if they never prove a paranormal cause. Do not choose the GM truth retroactively to thwart well-supported player conclusions.

## Scene map and guaranteed clue network

| Scene | Read aloud / stakes | Core discovery (automatic with access) | Follow-up |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Ward conference | argument between clinical duty and sponsor's secrecy; one staffer is missing from roster, not necessarily missing in reality | three independently recorded descriptions of red door; consent list is inconsistent | interview nurse; audit log |
| 2. Patient advocate | family worried about publicity, one participant wants to speak, another declines | witness independently drew distinctive river bend before new interviews | old photo archive; source contamination audit |
| 3. Research office | PI fears trial suspension, corporate officer invokes contracts | remote sessions reused a shared stimulus library after a supposedly isolated study started | sponsor logs; study registry |
| 4. Digital forensics | access to local copies available under warrant or consent | file timestamps conflict with published treatment timeline, and remote gateway contacted a facility tagged only 'K-17' | request international assistance; physical site |
| 5. Folklore collection | a 20th-century local description of a 'mill' near forest lake circulates in multiple versions | motifs resembling Sampo/Pohjola appear but differ; recent tourism blog copied details | interview local historian; compare original archival variants |
| 6. Forest station K-17 | derelict but secured research annex; comms intermittent | obsolete projection chamber, power draw after shutdown; physical seal broken before recent study | device evidence; signs of third-party access |
| 7. Containment meeting | municipal inspector, patient advocate, hospital lawyer, state investigator and sponsor disagree | costs of disclosure, shutdown and entering the chamber are made explicit | player choice |

**Three clue paths to K-17:** the clinical study's server endpoint; forest-road image metadata; procurement/permit logs available from municipal records. Thus failed rolls cannot stall the investigation. Each path can reveal different motives. An optional PSI impression can help but is never required.

### Scene 6 complications by GM truth

- A: abandoned generator powers a projection rig. Acoustic anomaly recurs after it is disconnected, leaving a genuine mystery.
- B: patterns spoken in the room alter a measurable field and witnesses share a verifiable new detail only if conditions and consent are controlled. The entity sends an invitation, not instructions.
- C: planted psychotronic recordings and missing equipment reveal a paid extraction team; comms spoofing mislabels a harmless witness as dangerous.

## Player decisions and endings

1. **Protect and pause:** suspend unsafe experiments, ensure care and independent ethics review. Strong public trust; weakened sponsor relationship.
2. **Expose and investigate:** document unlawful conduct and publish only defensible, consent-safe findings. Wider scrutiny helps but the anomaly may attract opportunists.
3. **Quarantine and negotiate:** secure K-17 with national authorities/UNSA liaison, protect witnesses, attempt cautious two-way contact. Preserves rare evidence but raises autonomy questions.
4. **Accept the sponsor's deal:** controlled research continues under conditions. Gains resources but risks capture or future scandal.

No one outcome requires assuming a patient was secretly NHI. The choice changes faction trust, legal exposure, local willingness to cooperate and who possesses the evidence.

## Practical tabletop instructions

- **One session (~3 hours):** ward → research office → K-17 → hearing. Give the other essential discoveries in summarized records.
- **Two to three sessions:** include independent interviews, folklore research and Nordic cross-border assistance.
- **No mandatory rolls for core clues.** Use standard CWN 2d6 checks where consequences matter (e.g. WIS+Notice for a hidden alteration, INT+Program for speed of log reconstruction, CHA+Talk for voluntary cooperation, INT+Administer for urgent paperwork). Difficulty per published CWN; no invented stats. A successful check may reduce delay or reveal extra corroboration, failure complicates rather than erases the case.
- **SWN PSI:** only through legal source character packages, with techniques/Effort applied as written; unusual impressions alone are not proof.
- **No combat requirement.** If it arises, CWN combat and injury rules apply; local law and de-escalation take precedence.
- **NPC agenda clocks, narrative only:** advocate = protect consent; researcher = preserve study; sponsor = prevent liability; officer = maintain chain of custody; K-17 custodian = keep site sealed. Move each goal when players make a decision, without adding numerical rules.

## Handouts to create at table

1. Three anonymized excerpts with slight differences in wording; no real medical data.
2. Red-door sketch and a forest-road photo with conflicting dates.
3. Study consent matrix, sponsor change request and K-17 procurement record.
4. Two versions of a folkloric text labeled by date/source and a copied modern blog excerpt.
5. Blank four-column evidence board: observed / ordinary hypothesis / extraordinary hypothesis / test.

## Debrief questions

Did all investigators contribute? Could the group resolve the actual harm without using PSI? Were hospital staff and patients portrayed as full agents? Did evidence distinguish source contamination from anomalous correlation? Were institutional relationships clear? Was there a consequential ending with no combat? Was the humor directed at bureaucratic contradictions rather than illness?
