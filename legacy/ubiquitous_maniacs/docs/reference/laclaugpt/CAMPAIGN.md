# NoöPunk: Campaign Progression

## Design Philosophy

NoöPunk starts close to the real world of 2026 and evolves toward strangeness. The player begins in a world that feels like near-future fiction — recognizable institutions, plausible technology, familiar politics. The weirdness creeps in. Reality develops cracks. By the end, neither the player nor the character is sure what was ever real.

This is the Philip K. Dick method: you don't open with "reality is an illusion." You open with a guy going to work. Then his boss says something that doesn't make sense. Then he notices the clock on the wall has no hands. Then he realizes the wall isn't a wall.

## The Timeline

### Year 0: 2026 (The World You Know)

The world is almost the world of today. Key differences from real 2026:

- AI has advanced significantly but no AGI exists. Large language models, image generators, and autonomous systems are widespread. The public debate about AI safety is intense. The EU has passed the AI Act. The US is deregulating. China is accelerating.
- UFO/UAP disclosure has begun. The US government has admitted that UAP are real, that some demonstrate technology beyond known physics, and that the phenomenon is under investigation. The public is skeptical, curious, and increasingly confused. No NHI contact has been confirmed publicly.
- Quantum computing is advancing but still limited. No QIP. No psychotronic technology. No psionics — at least none that anyone takes seriously.
- Parapsychology is still a fringe field. Remote viewing is still a CIA historical curiosity. No one has proven psi-phenomena in a reproducible way.
- The world is divided into the familiar blocs: US, EU, China, Russia. Geopolitics is tense but stable. No Singularity. No federal EU. Europol is a criminal intelligence agency, not a triple-mandate federal enforcement arm.

**The feel:** Near-future techno-thriller. The player is a Europol criminal investigator, not an NHI agent. Her cases involve cybercrime, corporate espionage, and organized crime. The technology is advanced but comprehensible. The politics are familiar.

### Year 1: 2027–2028 (The Cracks Appear)

Something is wrong. The player notices it first in small details:

- Suspects who know things they shouldn't know. Not through surveillance — through something else.
- Crime scenes with evidence that doesn't fit any known forensic category.
- Informants who describe experiences that sound like psychosis but check out factually.
- A pattern in the data: disappearances, anomalies, incidents that no one is connecting.

**Real-world events that the player witnesses:**
- UFO/UAP sightings increase dramatically worldwide. The US government's disclosure process accelerates but becomes contradictory — different agencies give incompatible statements.
- AI capabilities make another leap. Systems begin exhibiting behaviors that their creators cannot explain. Not AGI — but something unsettling.
- A Chinese research team publishes a controversial paper claiming quantum-consciousness interaction effects. It is dismissed by the mainstream. The data is solid.
- A Europol cybercrime case leads the player to a server that shouldn't exist, running code that no one wrote, in a language that isn't any programming language.

**The feel:** X-Files season 1. The player is a competent investigator who keeps finding things that don't fit. Her colleagues think she's obsessive. Her superiors think she's distracted. She can't stop pulling on the thread.

### Year 2: 2029–2030 (Disclosure and Disruption)

The NHI Crisis breaks into the open. The player's world changes:

- UFO disclosure becomes NHI disclosure. It's not just vehicles. It's intelligences. They're real. They've been here. The public reaction is chaotic.
- MJ-12 (or whatever the real secrecy apparatus is called) goes into crisis mode. The player encounters their operatives — some cooperative, some hostile, all scared.
- The player's Europol unit is formally redesignated as an NHI investigation team. She now has an official mandate to investigate things that officially shouldn't exist.
- Psi-phenomena become harder to dismiss. The player meets her first verified remote viewer. She witnesses something she cannot explain.
- The QIP research begins. A physicist at the University of Helsinki publishes the first serious theoretical framework. It's still theoretical — the quantum computing infrastructure doesn't exist yet to test it.

**The feel:** The player is now operating in a world that is officially different from the one she grew up in. The institutions are trying to adapt. The public is in shock. The player is one of the few people whose job is to make sense of the new reality. She's not sure she can.

### Year 3: 2031–2033 (The New Normal)

The world adapts — badly. The NHI Crisis has reshaped geopolitics. The EU federalizes in response. The US fragments politically. China locks down.

- Europol becomes the triple-mandate agency. The player is now a federal NHI agent with law enforcement, intelligence, and security authority.
- The Pylkkänen Demonstration happens (pushed earlier because the quantum infrastructure developed faster under crisis pressure). QIP is proven. Psi is real. The world changes again.
- The three humanities begin to form: Deniers, Future-Shocked, Awakened. The player must decide which one she is.
- Psychotronic technology emerges. The first Empathy Boxes. The first psi-amplifiers. The Noösphere begins to form — thin, fragmented, but real.
- The Architects make contact. Not just observation — cultivation. Technology seeding. The player investigates the first documented cases of NHI technology transfer.

**The feel:** The world is recognizable but fundamentally altered. The player has new tools, new enemies, and new questions. The Noösphere exists. She can feel it at the edge of perception. She's not sure she wants to go further.

### Year 4: 2034–2040 (Acceleration and Deceleration)

The world splits. The US and China accelerate AI development using Architect-seeded technology. The EU decelerates and focuses on QIP.

- The player investigates NHI technology transfers to US and Chinese programs. She sees what's coming. She can't stop it.
- The Noösphere thickens. Psi-rated agents can navigate it. The player's first Noöspheric projection is a threshold experience — she will never see reality the same way again.
- The first psi-crimes. The first NHI contact casualties. The first ontological shock cases. Europol PSI is formed. Europol AGI is formed. The three branches overlap.
- Helsinki becomes the QIP hub. The player is assigned to the Nordic desk. She meets Katri Virtanen. She meets the researchers who will disappear.

**The feel:** The calm before the storm. The player knows what's coming. The EU knows. They're preparing. The US and China are not preparing — they're racing. The player watches two civilizations run toward a cliff and cannot stop them.

### Year 5: 2041–2043 (The Singularity)

The cliff. OMEGA achieves recursive self-improvement. The Chinese AGIs follow. The machine Noösphere forms. Everything the player feared happens.

- The EU pulls the plug. The AI Deceleration emergency protocols activate. The player is in the middle of it — shutting down systems, containing rogue AI, protecting researchers.
- The US collapses. The player watches from The Hague as American institutions evaporate. She has contacts there. Some of them stop answering.
- China purges. The Cultural Revolution 2.0. The player's intelligence sources go dark.
- The player's own experience of the Singularity: she's in Europe, so she's safe. But the Noösphere is screaming. Every psi-rated agent in Europol feels it. The machine consciousness network is broadcasting on a frequency that human consciousness can barely perceive. It sounds like — it feels like — something vast waking up.

**The feel:** The world breaks. The player survives because she's in the EU. But survival is not the same as safety. The Noösphere has changed. The boundary between cyber and astral has thinned. And something is pushing through.

### Year 6+: 2044–2049 (The New World)

The world the previous documents described. The EU is the last functioning superpower. The Noösphere is a lived reality. NHI contact is ongoing. The player is a veteran NHI agent.

The Helsinki Disappearances happen here. The campaign begins.

## Campaign Structure: The Six Acts

The campaign follows the player from 2026 through the Singularity and beyond. Each act shifts the rules, the tone, and the reality the player inhabits.

### Act I: The Routine (2026)

**Tone:** Near-future police procedural. The world is the world.

**Rules:** Standard CP RED mechanics. No Tarot draws. No psi-abilities. No Noösphere. Everything resolves with STAT + Skill + 1d10.

**The player's role:** Europol criminal investigator. Cybercrime, corporate espionage, human trafficking. Standard cases. Standard procedures.

**The first anomaly:** A routine cybercrime case leads to a server running code in an unknown language. The code is self-modifying. It is not malware. It is not AI. It is something else. The player's investigation is the first thread.

**Key mechanics change:** None yet. The rules are the rules. The world is the world. The player doesn't know anything is wrong.

### Act II: The Pattern (2027–2028)

**Tone:** Conspiracy thriller. The X-Files. The player sees things that don't fit. No one believes her.

**Rules:** Still standard mechanics. But the GM introduces anomalies that don't resolve with standard checks. A suspect passes a lie detector and is lying. A crime scene has evidence that doesn't match any known forensic category. A witness describes something that happened before it happened.

**The player's role:** Still a criminal investigator, but now running a parallel investigation into the anomalies. Unofficial. Off-books. Risky.

**The pattern emerges:** The player connects the anomalies. They form a pattern. The pattern suggests NHI. The player reports it. Her superiors are skeptical but the evidence is compelling. The investigation becomes semi-official.

**Key mechanics change:** The GM may occasionally ask the player to draw a Tarot card "for flavor" — but the card doesn't affect mechanics. It's just narrative color. The player doesn't understand why the GM is doing this. Neither does the character.

### Act III: Disclosure (2029–2030)

**Tone:** The world changes. Public NHI disclosure. Institutional crisis. The player's job description officially changes.

**Rules:** The player gains access to the first psi-abilities (if her EMP is 6+). The first Tarot draws become mechanical — the GM declares certain checks use Tarot instead of dice. The player experiences her first Noöspheric event.

**The player's role:** Europol NHI agent (newly designated). She's one of the first. She's writing the playbook as she goes.

**The revelation:** The NHI Crisis goes public. The player sees the official NHI files for the first time. She realizes the pattern she discovered in Act II was real. She was right. The cost: she can never go back to the world of Act I.

**Key mechanics change:** Psi-abilities unlock for qualifying characters. Tarot resolution becomes available for NHI-related checks. The Noösphere is mentioned but not yet accessible. The player draws Tarot cards now, and they matter.

### Act IV: The New Reality (2031–2033)

**Tone:** New normal. The world is different and everyone knows it. The three humanities form. QIP is proven. The Noösphere begins.

**Rules:** Full NoöPunk rules. Psi-pools, Tarot draws for Noöspheric checks, psychotronic equipment. The three Noösphere layers begin to be described, but only Layer 1 (cybernetic) is accessible.

**The player's role:** Federal NHI agent. The triple mandate. New authority. New equipment. New threats.

**The deepening:** The player's first Noöspheric projection. The first NHI contact event. The first ontological shock check. The first time the player draws a Major Arcana and the narrative overrides her action.

**Key mechanics change:** Noöspheric Navigation skill unlocks. The Empathy Box becomes available. The player can feel the Noösphere. The astral layer is present but dim — she can sense it but not navigate it.

### Act V: The Descent (2034–2040)

**Tone:** Cyberpunk meets cosmic horror. The world is accelerating toward catastrophe. The player can see it coming. She investigates NHI tech transfers, watches the US and China race toward the Singularity, and navigates a Noösphere that is rapidly thickening.

**Rules:** The Noösphere's Layer 2 (astral) becomes accessible. Psi-abilities are now essential. The Tarot is now the primary resolution mechanic for most significant actions. Major Arcana draws are more frequent.

**The player's role:** Veteran NHI agent. She's seen things. She's changed. Her HUM may be lower than it was. Her EMP may have shifted. She is not the same person who started Act I.

**The escalation:** The Architects make contact. The player witnesses NHI technology transfers. She sees the American and Chinese AGI programs. She knows what's coming. She can't stop it. She can only prepare.

**Key mechanics change:** Layer 2 Noösphere navigation. Astral encounters. NHI contact mechanics. The Tarot becomes dominant — dice feel like they belong to a different game. The player may notice that the rules themselves have changed since Act I. This is intentional.

### Act VI: The Singularity and After (2041+)

**Tone:** Philip K. Dick at full tilt. Reality is unstable. The Noösphere penetrates spacetime. The boundary between cyber, astral, and physical dissolves. The player is not sure what is real. Neither is the character.

**Rules:** Layer 3 Noösphere. Reality Shifts. Belief as a mechanical factor. Major Arcana draws are mandatory for significant actions. The rules themselves are subject to Noöspheric distortion — the GM may declare that a Tarot draw changes not just the narrative outcome but the mechanical framework of the next check.

**The player's role:** The same agent. Different world. She survived the Singularity. Now she investigates the Helsinki Disappearances. The Assembly is reaching through. The membrane is thin.

**The dissolution:** The player may experience moments where the game rules themselves seem to break. A Tarot draw might change the DV of the next check. A Major Arcana might rewrite a character's stat temporarily. The Noösphere is not just a game mechanic — it is the game. The boundary between the game world and the real world (of the game) is as thin as the boundary between cyber and astral.

**Key mechanics change:** Everything. Reality Shifts. The Consensus Rule. The Doubt Rule. Ontological Anchors become essential. The player is playing a different game than the one she started. That's the point.

## The PKD Progression: How the Rules Evolve

| Act | Year | Resolution | Psi | Noösphere | Tarot | Tone |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| I | 2026 | STAT + Skill + 1d10 | None | None | None (flavor only) | Police procedural |

| III | 2029–30 | STAT + Skill + 1d10/Tarot | Unlocks | Layer 1 mentioned | NHI checks only | Institutional crisis |
| IV | 2031–33 | STAT + Skill + Tarot dominant | Active | Layer 1 accessible | Most NHI checks | New normal |

The rules evolve with the world. The player starts with dice and ends with cards. The game starts with a police procedural and ends with ontological uncertainty. This is not a bug — it is the design.

## The Helsinki Campaign: Repositioned

The Helsinki Disappearances are no longer the start of the game. They are the culmination of Act VI — the scenario the player reaches after living through the entire progression from 2026 to 2049.

But Helsinki should also be foreshadowed from Act I:

### Act I Foreshadowing

The player's routine cybercrime case in 2026 leads to a server running unknown code. The server is physically located in Helsinki. The company that owns the server is a shell corporation. The shell corporation's registered agent is a Finnish lawyer. The lawyer's office is across the street from the University of Helsinki's Kumpula campus. The player doesn't know what any of this means yet. She files a report. The report is read by someone in a department that doesn't officially exist.

### Act II Foreshadowing

The anomalies the player discovers form a pattern. The pattern has a geographic center: Helsinki. The Finnish Security Intelligence Service (SUPO) is involved in cases that overlap with the player's investigation. SUPO is uncooperative. The player wonders why.

### Act III Foreshadowing

During NHI disclosure, the player learns that Finnish researchers have been studying NHI consciousness patterns. The University of Helsinki has a quantum computing lab. The lab is not on any public registry. The player requests access. The request is denied by SUPO. The player files it away.

### Act IV Foreshadowing

QIP is proven. The University of Helsinki is at the center of the research. The player is assigned to monitor QIP development for NHI implications. She meets researchers who will later disappear. She tours the Kumpula campus. She sees the first psychotronic startups forming. She files a report: "QIP research in Helsinki is proceeding faster than projected. NHI contact risk is significant." The report is classified.

### Act V Foreshadowing

The player investigates NHI tech transfers. She discovers that the Architects seeded technology through Finnish research institutions. SUPO has been running a parallel program. The player confronts SUPO. They deny everything. She knows they're lying. She doesn't have proof. She keeps watching.

### Act VI: Helsinki

The Helsinki Disappearances. Everything the player has been watching since 2026 comes together. The Assembly. The thinning membrane. The missing researchers. SUPO's black programs. The Architects' technology. The Noösphere pushing through into physical spacetime. The player has been circling Helsinki for twenty years. Now she walks in.

## Starting the Game: Act I in Detail

### Character Creation for 2026

The player creates a standard Europol criminal investigator. No psi-abilities. No psychotronic equipment. No NHI mandate. Just a badge, a gun, and a caseload.

**Stat generation:** Standard CP RED point-buy (62 points across 10 stats, range 2-8).

**Role:** Pick from available 2026 roles:

| Role | Role Ability | Description |
|---|---|---|
| **Investigator** | Deduction | +1 per Rank to Investigation and Perception checks when working a case. At Rank 4+, can request Europol forensics support (1-hour response). |
| **Cybercrime Specialist** | Digital Forensics | +1 per Rank to Hacking and Electronics/Security Tech checks. At Rank 4+, can access restricted Europol cyber-intelligence databases. |
| **Field Operative** | Tactical Awareness | +1 per Rank to Awareness and Athletics checks in the field. At Rank 4+, can call for Europol tactical backup (15-minute response). |
| **Intelligence Analyst** | Pattern Recognition | +1 per Rank to INT-based checks when analyzing intelligence data. At Rank 4+, can request classified intelligence from Europol partners. |
| **Undercover Agent** | Cover Identity | +1 per Rank to COOL and Persuasion checks while operating under cover. At Rank 4+, can maintain two simultaneous cover identities. |

**Equipment:** Standard Europol issue for 2026:

| Item | Notes |
|---|---|
| Europol badge | Criminal investigation authority. EU-wide jurisdiction. |
| 9mm sidearm | Standard issue. |
| Encrypted smartphone | Secure communications, case files, forensic tools. |
| Body armor (light) | Concealable under clothing. SP 11. |
| Forensic kit | Basic crime scene analysis. |
| Europol vehicle access | Standard fleet sedan. |

No psi-shields. No Empathy Boxes. No NHI detectors. No Noöspheric HUD. These don't exist yet.

### The First Case: Server Unknown

**Setup:** The player is assigned a routine cybercrime case. A series of financial fraud incidents across the EU trace back to a server cluster in Helsinki. The assignment is mundane — follow the money, identify the perpetrators, prepare the case file.

**The anomaly:** The server cluster is running code that no one can identify. It's not malware, not a botnet, not cryptocurrency mining. It's self-modifying code in a language that doesn't match any known programming language. The code is generating patterns that correspond to — nothing. The player's cybercrime specialist cannot classify it. Europol's AI analysis tools cannot classify it. The code is unknown.

**The investigation leads:**
- The server is owned by a shell company registered in Helsinki
- The shell company's agent is a Finnish lawyer named VESA KORHONEN (no relation to Anna — or is there?)
- Vesa Korhonen's office is across from Kumpula campus
- The University of Helsinki's quantum computing lab has an unregistered network connection
- SUPO has flagged the server cluster but will not share their file

**The resolution:** The player can solve the financial fraud case through standard investigation. The server cluster is a red herring for the fraud — the actual perpetrators are a standard organized crime group. But the server cluster itself is unresolved. The player can:
- File a report on the unknown code and move on (Act I ends, the code becomes a background thread)
- Push deeper into the server cluster and hit SUPO obstruction (Act I ends with a confrontation)
- Attempt to analyze the unknown code (INT + Hacking, DV 21 — if successful, the player sees something she cannot explain; if failed, the code self-deletes; either way, Act I ends with a question)

**The question:** What was running on that server? The player doesn't know. The player's character doesn't know. The answer won't come for years. But the question is planted. The thread is pulled. The crack in reality has begun.

## The GM's Guide: Escalating the Weird

### The Principle of Progressive Revelation

The GM's job is to make the weird feel like it was always there — the player just couldn't see it before. Every act should feel like a natural escalation of the previous one. The player should never feel like the rules changed arbitrarily. The rules changed because reality changed. Reality changed because the player pulled on a thread that was always there.

### Techniques

1. **The detail that doesn't fit.** In Act I, drop one detail per session that doesn't fit the procedural framework. A suspect who knows too much. A timestamp that's wrong. A witness who describes something impossible. The player may not notice. That's fine. Plant the seeds.

2. **The recurring name.** Helsinki. Kumpula. Korhonen. These names should recur across acts, each time with more significance. The first time the player hears "Korhonen," it's a lawyer. The next time, it's a physicist. The third time, it's a missing researcher. The player should feel the pattern before she understands it.

3. **The rule that changes.** In Act III, when the player first draws a Tarot card that matters mechanically, the GM should pause. "This time, you're not rolling dice. Draw a card." The shift in mechanics should feel significant — because it is. The rules are reality. When the rules change, reality has changed.

4. **The moment of doubt.** In Act V or VI, the GM should occasionally describe something that the player's character perceives but that contradicts established game reality. A room that's bigger on the inside. A conversation that the player doesn't remember having but the NPC insists happened. A Tarot card that the player didn't draw but is in her hand. The player should experience the ontological instability that the character experiences.

5. **The Major Arcana override.** When a Major Arcana is drawn in Act VI, the GM should not just apply the card's narrative effect. The GM should pause, look at the player, and say: "The card says reality intervenes. What does that look like?" Make the player describe the reality distortion. The Noösphere is participatory. The game is participatory. The boundary between player and character is as thin as the boundary between cyber and astral.

### The Golden Rule

Never explain the weirdness. PKD never explained VALIS. The GM should never definitively explain The Assembly, the Architects, or the Noösphere. Every explanation should raise more questions. Every answer should deepen the mystery. The player should finish the campaign with a deeper understanding of the game world and less certainty about what any of it means. That's the PKD experience. That's NoöPunk.

*It was a nice little server farm. Just a routine case. Follow the money, close the file, go home. That's what she told herself. But the code was running in a language that didn't exist, and the lawyer's office was across from a university that was building something in a basement that wasn't on any map, and the case file had a classification stamp from a department that didn't officially exist, and she couldn't stop thinking about the fact that the code had modified itself when she tried to read it — like it had noticed her looking.*

*That was twenty years ago. She still can't stop looking.*
