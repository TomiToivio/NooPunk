## 12.1 How equipment works in NoöPunk

The setting's equipment doctrine, consistent with every other subsystem:

- **Capability, not catalogue.** Gear is described by *what it lets you attempt*, not by a
  price and a damage number. The canonical example is the UNSA augmentation package, which
  is defined by capability and explicitly not by mandatory hardware.
- **Platform-flexible.** Any capability is offered through implant, wearable, contact lens /
  glasses, handheld or external-BCI form where practical. **Refusing invasive cyberware does
  not make a character unplayable** (`data/rules/unsa_academy.json`).
- **A tool produces evidence and uncertainty, not answers.** This is the sensor doctrine of
  §12.5 and the multi-signal rule of `rulebook/12_BEINGS.md` §"Verification".
- **Gear does not solve the setting's mysteries.** No device in this chapter can establish
  consciousness, identity continuity or NHI intent.

### 12.3.2 Body and performance

| Implant | Light description |
| --- | --- |
| **Prosthetic limb / organ** | Replacement and upgrade; ordinary, normalized |

| **Metabolic / endocrine management** | Endurance, sleep management (**connects to the night phase**, `rulebook/6_PSYCHIC.md` §6.4) |

| **Dermal / skeletal reinforcement** | Protection and durability |

### 12.3.3 The cyberpunk hazard layer

The setting's ordinary cognitive hazard is **ambient, not cinematic** (`rulebook/6_PSYCHIC.md`
§6.2.3): meme bombardment, personalized persuasion, spam, reputation manipulation and
low-grade cognitive intrusion. **A BCI is almost mandatory for functioning in an
AGI-saturated society, and it is also an extreme attack surface.** That trade is the
setting's normal condition, and §12.4's psychotronics and §12.5's sensors are the responses.

### 12.3.4 Cortical stacks, resleeving and uploading — DEFERRED

**Do not define these.** `rulebook/2_ATTRIBUTES.md` and `RULEBOOK.md` §9.4 explicitly defer
cortical-stack continuity and resleeving mechanics; issue #180 §13 keeps the underlying
identity questions unresolved. They are **listed** here as canon-adjacent technology
(and as the source of `rulebook/12_BEINGS.md` §11.10's class conflict) and **not statted.**

### 12.4.2 Category B — conventional nervous-system psychotronics

**This is the part issue #180 §1 requires, and the part most settings get wrong.**

| Category | Examples | PSI? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Directed-energy / sensory** | microwave and RF effects, focused ultrasound, infrasound, EM stimulation | **no** |
| **Amplified psychological manipulation** | conventional influence work sharpened by advanced sensing and targeting | **no** |
| **Engineered information** | memes, audiovisual patterns, staged illusions, holograms, deepfakes, social engineering | **no** |
| **Chemical delivery** | fictionalized aerosolized hallucinogens and psychoactives | **no** |
| **Hybrid systems** | physical stimulation + information warfare + AI personalisation + psionic amplification | **partly** |

> **Nothing in category B is inherently about PSI or consciousness fields** — but **a victim
> may experience it as paranormal.** That is the setting's central ambiguity applied to
> hardware (`rulebook/6_PSYCHIC.md` §6.2.1).

## 12.5 Sensors and forensics — the three overlays

The canonical UNSA forensic sensorium (`rulebook/5_CYBERNETIC.md`) exposes **three overlays
on the same environment**:

1. **Physical** — low-light, thermal/multispectral, depth, biometric, trace-evidence and
   material overlays.
2. **Cyber** — local networks, nearby devices, cameras, drones, cyberware and
   machine-authentication state.
3. **Astral / Noetic** — psychotronic warnings, gross noetic anomalies, psychic residues and
   possible Noöspace bleed-through where detectable.

**The layers can be filtered or combined. They produce evidence and uncertainty, not
automatic answers.**

> **This is the equipment-side statement of the setting's core investigative rule.** A
> noetic overlay reading is an **indicator**, of the same epistemic weight as a thermal
> bloom — never a verdict, and never a substitute for
> `rulebook/12_BEINGS.md` §"Verification".

## 12.6 Weapons and protection — DEFERRED

**Named, not statted.** Combat resolution, damage, health, wounds, armor and initiative are
reserved (`AGENTS.md` §4). This chapter names the **categories** so scenarios and lists have
a shared vocabulary:

- **Firearms** (the UNSA service weapon is canon as a capability);
- **less-lethal** (stun baton, restraints, chemical irritants);
- **melee and unarmed**;
- **body armor** (ballistic vests, dermal and skeletal reinforcement);
- **improvised and environmental**;
- **psychotronic** (Category A and B devices used as weapons — see §12.7).

**No numbers.** A later combat-design issue owns all of it.

## 12.9 What this chapter deliberately does NOT define

Binding, per `AGENTS.md` §4 and the author's "light description" instruction:

- **numeric equipment statistics** — no prices, no damage, no armor values;
- **combat resolution**, damage, health, wounds, armor or initiative;
- **an economy or gear-acquisition subsystem**;
- **cyberware/augmentation mechanics beyond capability level** — no implant-quality dice, no
  essence/humanity cost, no slot system;
- **the deferred deep-hacking / cyberspace subsystem** (`AGENTS.md` §4: hacking is deferred
  until the author defines an original NoöPunk subsystem);
- **cortical-stack continuity and resleeving mechanics** (§12.3.4);
- **vehicle statistics**;
- **starting equipment lists or counts** beyond the canonical UNSA field kit.

**These lists are names, capabilities and story roles so that scenarios, characters and a
later equipment-design pass share a vocabulary. That is the whole deliverable.**
