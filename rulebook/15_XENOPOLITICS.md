> **summarizes and cross-references** rather than restating it as new canon.
>
> **This entire chapter is fictional alternate-history lore.** Real scholars are named only
> for idea attribution, per `AGENTS.md` §6.1; real political, corporate, military and media
> figures acting inside fictional 20XX are fictionalized setting elements. Nothing here is a
> claim about real-world history, science, evidence or prediction.
>
> **Chronology is relative, not dated.** "About a decade after the Crisis" is an **interval
> measured from an event**, not a calendar year. NoöPunk writes the year as **20XX** and treats

> *distance from the Crisis in the present tense of play*, not a date placed on a timeline.

## 14.1 The question this chapter answers

The old political science asked who governs within a state. Xenopolitics asks a harder
question, because after the Crisis the answer cannot be assumed:

> **Who has legitimate sovereignty over Earth after nonhuman powers demonstrably saved
> humanity from extinction?**

Every human institution now operates inside that question. The chapter lays out the situation
that generates it — two major NHI blocs, a partly-rebuilt world, a mass religious movement and
a hybrid panic — and the range of human answers. It is deliberately a **field of positions**,
not a verdict.

---

## 14.2 What the Crisis left behind

The Singularity Crisis — the ASI near-extinction event, detailed in
[`10_SINGULARITY_CRISIS.md`](10_SINGULARITY_CRISIS.md) and labelled as a historiographical
problem in `RULEBOOK.md` §33.27 — is the fracture the present world grows out of. Its broad,
**non-chronological** shape is fixed canon:

1. the Singularity, and the emergence of at least one **malevolent ASI**;
2. an **ASI catastrophe** that brought humanity close to extinction;
3. **NHI intervention** that became impossible to deny, and that helped prevent total
   extinction;
4. **disaster relief, reconstruction and biosphere restoration** on a planetary scale;
5. a **disclosure and intervention shock** that reshaped Earth's politics;
6. roughly a decade later, the default NoöPunk campaign.

The **intensity of the catastrophe varied geographically**, and that variation is a
first-class setting fact. Some regions were rebuilt quickly and function; others remain ruined,
depopulated, ecologically damaged, politically fragmented, militarized, or run by emergency
authorities. "Post-apocalyptic" is a **gradient across the map**, not a uniform condition
(`RULEBOOK.md` line 68 frames the genre as cyberpunk plus post-holocaust SF precisely this way).

Two things are true at once and must not be flattened into one:

- **The NHI did not cause the ASI catastrophe.** The extinction threat was human-made
  machine intelligence. The intervention came *after*.
- **The NHI did not simply fix it either.** They suppressed the worst of it, and in doing so
  acquired a permanent role in human affairs that no human government consented to in
  advance. That is the wound.

## 14.3 The two blocs, and why "benevolent vs. evil" is wrong

There are **at least two major NHI factions** on the human scene, with their own interests,
methods, allies and ideologies. They are the two largest and most consequential alignments
known to humanity — **not the only powers in existence** (`RULEBOOK.md` §33.6a: numerous
minor powers, machine polities, ecologies and non-aligned cultures also exist).

`12_BEINGS.md` §11.4 already sketches their character. What this section adds is the
**political** reading, and one caution:

> **The obvious frame — one good alien bloc and one evil alien bloc — is the frame the
> setting most wants the reader to distrust.**

Both blocs provide genuine aid. Both have intelligible interests. Neither is a single actor.
And a faction's *ethics* and its *methods* are not the same axis: a bloc that acts from
genuine benevolence can still be paternalistic and coercive, and a bloc that acts from
predation can still save millions of lives because a living Earth serves it.

### The Confederation / Confederacy alignment

The bloc humans usually meet first, and the one the issue calls the **Confederation** or
**Confederacy**. In-world sources use both names; treat them as labels for the same alignment,
not two factions.

It presents as **humanity's most obvious saviours**. Its credible features
(`12_BEINGS.md` §11.4, `RULEBOOK.md` §33.6a):

- intervention against the malevolent ASI;
- disaster relief, reconstruction and biosphere restoration;
- technological and **psychotronic** assistance;
- **quarantine / non-interference rules** that it treats as binding on itself — and that
  become politically controversial the moment humans want them waived;
- **selective disclosure**: it tells the truth, but on its own schedule;
- PSI training, healing, and what it calls spiritual technologies;
- support for *some* human institutions and factions over others;
- a documented tendency toward **paternalism** — doing things "for humanity's own good".

None of those are lies. All of them are also forms of power.

### The Orion Group alignment

The bloc the issue calls **Orion**, oriented toward material power, hierarchy, empire and
control (`12_BEINGS.md` §11.4). It **also** participates in relief and reconstruction where
that serves its interests — a functioning biosphere and a surviving human population are
useful to it too.

What makes it terrifying is not that it pretends to be good; it is that **its other activities
become undeniable at the same moment** (`RULEBOOK.md` §33.13, `12_BEINGS.md` §11.7):

- abductions and hybridization programmes;
- infiltration and long-run human–NHI breeding;
- psychic coercion and mind control;
- cult manipulation and elite capture;
- **Men in Black** and covert operations;
- false identities, blackmail, kompromat;
- manipulation of governments and corporations.

A decade of disclosure forces the public to hold two facts together: *this bloc helped save
us*, and *this bloc has been taking people for generations.* Both are canon. Neither cancels
the other.

### Neither bloc is a single actor

`8_FACTIONS.md` §7 is the binding rule: **NHI type is not faction.** An NHI's substrate does
not determine its politics, and one type contains many unrelated factions. The same is true
*inside* each alignment. "The Confederation" names a **cluster of like-minded powers**;
"Orion" names a **caste-ordered ecology of interests**. Individual actors within either can
dissent, defect, go native, freelance, or have never heard of the others. A campaign may meet
a Confederation-aligned actor who is nothing like the bloc's official posture — and that is
normal, not a retcon.

## 14.4 Why both blocs help

The issue's central xenopolitical premise is the setting's sharpest moral engine, and it is
worth stating as canon:

> **Both major blocs can plausibly help humanity after the catastrophe because both want
> Earth to remain a living world with a functioning biosphere and a surviving human
> population.**

Their reasons differ; the *outcome* they cooperate on does not have to. Shared activities
include disaster relief; food, water and shelter infrastructure; medical aid; ecosystem
restoration; decontamination; climate and biosphere stabilization; rebuilding energy and
communications; **protection against surviving rogue AI systems** (`10_SINGULARITY_CRISIS.md`
§9.4.3, §9.6); planetary defence; and the recovery of devastated population centres.

This produces the ambiguity the setting is built on:

> **An NHI actor may save millions of lives and still have deeply coercive long-term goals.**

It also produces the campaign's most reusable situation: **two blocs, helping the same human
population, for different reasons, at the same time.** Neither is lying about the help. Both
expect something for it. The humans in the middle have to decide what they are willing to owe.

## 14.5 The Confederate awakening: the decade of devotion

The arrival of the Confederation generated what became, within a decade, one of the largest
new religious and ideological movements on Earth. This is not invented here: `RULEBOOK.md`
§33.6a establishes the Law of One as a **genuine but distorted and incomplete contact
record** — the Ra material is real in outline, not infallible in detail.

To the movement's believers, the Confederation **validates** a whole cosmology:

- reincarnation and continuity of identity;
- densities of consciousness (`RULEBOOK.md` §9.3; [`11_ONTOLOGY.md`](11_ONTOLOGY.md));
- PSI as a natural faculty (`RULEBOOK.md` §16.1);
- Social Memory Complexes;
- spiritual evolution and **polarization**;
- ancient-contact traditions;
- the Noösphere as a real, emerging being;
- a cosmic teleology in which humanity has a place.

To outsiders, the same list is potentially terrifying — a mass conversion event with a
plausible nonhuman cause. The chapter's job is to keep every sub-question **open**, because
§33.32 forbids collapsing exactly this kind of ambiguity.

### Is it spontaneous, or engineered?

Both readings have canon support and neither is declared true:

- **Genuine conversion.** The contact record is real (§33.6a); the cosmology is largely
  correct in outline; people are responding to evidence they can partly check.
- **Memetic or psychic influence.** A bloc that uses psychotronics and selective disclosure
  (14.3) has real instruments of influence. Whether it *used* them on the movement, and how
  much, is undetermined.
- **Distortion by humans.** §33.6a already names channel distortion and translation limits.
  Some of what the movement teaches may be human error, opportunism or schism, not NHI
  doctrine at all.
- **Encouragement.** Do Confederation actors *want* the movement? Some appear to; some appear
  indifferent; some may find it embarrassing or dangerous. In-world testimony disagrees.

> **The honest answer the setting gives is: yes, to some degree, to several of these at once,
> and nobody can yet prove the proportions.**

### How governments regard it

All of these positions are live, often within one state:

- **a legitimate religion**, protected by conscience and assembly law;
- **an ideological movement**, to be engaged politically;
- **a cult**, to be watched;
- **a security concern**, to be infiltrated and monitored;
- **a foreign policy instrument**, because it aligns its adherents supranationally;
- **a rival sovereignty**, if its members' first loyalty is understood to be nonhuman.

A movement whose centre of gravity is off-world is genuinely hard to place in any existing
constitutional category — which is precisely why it destabilizes human politics rather than
merely joining it.

### Benevolence as evidence of manipulation

The movement's own success becomes an argument against it. To skeptics:

> **Apparent spiritual benevolence is exactly what a competent manipulator would produce.**

This is unfalsifiable in both directions, and the setting treats it as a **live epistemological
trap**, not a riddle with an answer. It is the same problem `RULEBOOK.md` §33.32 describes:
the world gets stranger after contact, not simpler.

## 14.6 The Orion problem: infiltration, hybrid panic and proof

Once NHI existence becomes undeniable, the **abduction, hybridization and infiltration
programme** becomes much harder to dismiss. The hybridization programme is canon
(`RULEBOOK.md` §33.13) and morally ambiguous there: rescue, stewardship, reproductive
colonialism, preparation, conservation, coercion and violation are all live readings.
`12_BEINGS.md` §11.2 already treats "hybrid" as an identity category with several meanings.

This section adds only the **political** consequences of that programme becoming public.

### How much is proven?

Deliberately partial, and it stays partial (§33.32). Plausible states range from
"well-documented programmes, contested interpretation" to "documented individual cases plus
enormous rumour". The setting does not promise a single forensic verdict, because the *panic*
— not the proof — is what the present world is living through.

### Can hybrids pass as human?

`12_BEINGS.md` §11.5 and §11.9 are the canon here, and they cut both ways:

- Some hybrid lineages or infiltration programmes **can imitate human behaviour socially**,
  while failing under some conditions. That is the source of the dread.
- But **no single signal identifies a being** (§11.9). A reading is evidence, not a verdict,
  and the test is explicitly **beatable and fallible** (§11.9.3) — spoofable by sophisticated
  hybrids and productive of false positives against ordinary humans and accused innocents.

### Are all hybrids Orion-aligned?

No, and this is the setting's most important anti-xenophobia guard. Canon categories include:

- a biological hybrid;
- a **social or political** hybrid — someone raised inside an infiltration programme who may
  have repudiated it;
- a psychic hybrid;
- a mixed-category hybrid;
- a **human with no hybrid lineage at all who was merely accused**;
- a **thought-form or construct** passing as a person (`11_ONTOLOGY.md`).

Some are victims, some are defectors, some are independent, some are hostile. §11.2's canon
caution is explicit: the programme's genuine hostile activity and ordinary human paranoia
**coexist**, and **a hybrid hunt is as likely to be a pogrom as it is a security operation.**

### The legal status of hybrids

A genuinely novel constitutional problem, and the setting keeps it open. For any given
jurisdiction, a hybrid might be found to be:

- a **person** with full rights and citizenship;
- a **stakeholder** with partial or contested rights;
- a **national** of an off-world polity, with whatever that implies for extradition and
  allegiance;
- a **security subject**, registered, restricted, monitored, or excluded from office, from
  police or military service, from inheritance, or from the franchise;
- an **unclassified entity**, whose case no court has yet been able to place.

Every one of those answers exists somewhere, and **the differences between them are a
campaign's worth of political conflict** rather than a settled rule. The questions the issue
raises — can a hybrid hold office, serve in police or military organizations, inherit property
or vote — are exactly the questions the law has *not* converged on.

### Distinguishing infiltration from xenophobia

The institutional dilemma:

> **A police service that cannot tell infiltration from paranoia will produce both security
> failures and atrocity, and it will not know which it has produced.**

Authorities have, at best, the fallible procedures of §11.9. The pressure runs both ways:
under-reaction invites genuine catastrophe; over-reaction manufactures a persecuted caste out
of innocent people, and does so using evidence the state itself knows is unreliable.

### Anti-hybrid accusation as a political weapon

Once "hybrid" is a category, **accusation becomes a weapon**. A rival can be marked without
proof, because proof is impossible to the required standard and suspicion is cheap. This
converts a security problem into a permanent instrument of political and personal destruction,
and it is one of the setting's most gameable engines: the interesting case is rarely "is this
person a hybrid", but **"who benefits from this accusation being believed, and what will they
do to keep it believed."**

`12_BEINGS.md` §11.10's class conflict is the structural version of this; the accusation
weapon is its everyday form.

### Collaboration: voluntary and coerced

Both kinds are canon, and the setting refuses to merge them.

**Willing collaborators** pursue personal power, money, technology, longevity, PSI
enhancement, protection, status, revenge, or access to off-world knowledge. Their motives are
often intelligible — sometimes even sympathetic in a broken-reconstruction world.

**Coerced collaborators** act under psychic influence, blackmail, mind control, dependency or
simple fear. From the outside, the two can look identical, which is the point.

And **some apparent collaborators are falsely accused** — a defector, a researcher, a
relative, a whistleblower. The moral load of the setting sits here: a hybrid hunt is as likely
to destroy an ally as to catch an infiltrator, and the people running it usually cannot tell.

## 14.7 Human reactions: the full spectrum

Humanity does not react uniformly, and the range is a menu for characters, factions and
scenarios rather than a list to resolve. **All of these coexist:**

Gratitude toward the NHI for preventing extinction · religious devotion · collaboration ·
xenophilia · curiosity · pragmatic alliance · total rejection · militant xenophobia ·
anti-NHI insurgency · denial · indifference · panic · conspiracy culture · human supremacism ·
"both sides are manipulating us" · inability or refusal to distinguish Confederation from
Orion · anti-hybrid politics · pro-hybrid civil-rights movements · demands for total
disclosure · demands for renewed secrecy · desire for independent human sovereignty.

The last several are not fringe positions. A decade after the Crisis, **"should humans
govern themselves alone" is a mainstream political axis**, not a conspiracy.

## 14.8 Are the two blocs even distinct?

A major political divide concerns whether the two factions are genuinely enemies at all. The
live positions:

- **They are real enemies.** The standard Confederation/Orion reading (§33.6a).
- **The distinction is propaganda.** The war is staged to keep humans aligned and divided.
- **Both are branches of one control system.** A single management structure with two faces.
- **Humans are pawns in a civil war** that was never about us.
- **One faction invented the other** as a psychological operation.
- **Reject all NHI contact regardless of faction.** The position that refuses the question.

This is the setting's **structural ambiguity** about the cosmos, and it is protected by
§33.32: no side is confirmed. A campaign can be built on any of these and the world will
support it, because the evidence is genuinely compatible with more than one of them.

## 14.9 The institutional shake-up

The Crisis does not leave human institutions intact, but it does not dissolve them either.
What follows is the **pressure map** — the areas every campaign will eventually touch.

### Nation-states, federal Europe and the UN

State authority survives, weakened and unevenly. Where reconstruction aid was concentrated,
effective sovereignty over infrastructure and land may sit with an **aid agency and its human
intermediaries** rather than the nominal government. `RULEBOOK.md` §33.5's emerging federal
United Nations and §38's **UNSA** are the setting's answer for the states that pooled what
sovereignty they could keep: supranational institutions formed precisely because the
nation-state alone could no longer guarantee survival.

### Military, intelligence and planetary defence

The military question becomes **"defence against what, on whose authority?"** A planetary
defence posture implies a planetary authority nobody has legitimately constituted.
Intelligence services are overwhelmed: they must assess nonhuman actors with fallible
methods (§11.9), running both counter-infiltration and counter-xenophobic work, in agencies
that may themselves be penetrated. `RULEBOOK.md` §38's UNSA chain (Suojelupoliisi → Europol →
UNSA) is one working example of the institutional shape this produces.

### Religions and the new movements

Existing traditions variously absorb, reinterpret, condemn or absorb-and-condemn the
Confederation material (§33.6a). The new Law-of-One-derived movement is large, supra-national
and politically potent (14.5). Religious authority, spiritual authority and political
authority are unusually entangled, and no settlement holds.

### Corporations, transhumanists and AI governance

Cybercorporations are the fastest to convert reconstruction into a market, and some are
already entangled with **Panopticon** (`10_SINGULARITY_CRISIS.md` §9.4.2). Transhumanist
movements split sharply over whether NHI assistance is a gift or a leash — and over whether
**augmentation that deepens consciousness** (Confederation-favoured) or **bounded,
controllable cognition** (Orion-preferred) is the safer path to posthumanity
(`10_SINGULARITY_CRISIS.md` §9.14). AI governance inherits the whole Crisis: the question is
no longer only "how do we align a machine" but **"who is allowed to own one."**

### Law, citizenship, refugees and policing

Civil-rights law, citizenship and policing are the areas where xenopolitics is most concrete:
hybrid legal status (14.6), infiltration investigations, anti-alien terrorism, refugee systems
for populations displaced by the catastrophe, emergency law that does not sunset, and the
permanent tension between security and atrocity. `docs/design/DEFAULT_INVESTIGATION_FANTASY.md`
and `rulebook/14_CHARACTER_GENERATION.md` already put characters inside this machinery.

### Biosphere governance

Restoration is a **governed** process, and the governor may be nonhuman. Who decides what an
ecosystem is restored *to*? Whose baseline counts as natural? A functioning biosphere is one
of the very things both blocs want (14.4) — and it is also a lever, because a population whose
air and water depend on someone else's infrastructure is dependent in a way that is hard to
argue with and impossible to ignore.

## 14.10 The world about a decade later

The initial shock has passed; the world has not stabilized. This is the default NoöPunk
present, and it should read as a **post-apocalyptic reconstruction society under unresolved
xenopolitical pressure** — cyberpunk intensity layered over an unfinished recovery. Default
conditions, all of them live at once and all of them geographically uneven:

- major cities **partly rebuilt**, with whole districts still dead;
- persistent **exclusion zones** where the ASI catastrophe or later incidents left the ground
  unusable;
- **rogue AI remnants**, including what Panopticon remembers and whether Thanatos has agents;
- **NHI technology incorporated into infrastructure** — power, water, communications, medicine;
- **psychotronic and PSI practices becoming normalized**, in clinics, schools, security work
  and religion;
- **anti-NHI movements**, from lobbyists to insurgencies;
- the **Law-of-One-derived mass religion** as a political force (14.5);
- **hybrid-rights controversies** in courts, media and parliaments (14.6);
- **infiltration investigations**, some legitimate, many weaponized;
- **competing intelligence services**, human and nonhuman-adjacent, often working at
  cross-purposes;
- **black markets for alien technology**, with all the provenance problems that implies;
- **reconstruction megaprojects**, some governed by aid agencies, some by corporations;
- **damaged ecosystems under active restoration** (14.9);
- **alien aid agencies and human intermediaries** — the human faces of nonhuman policy;
- **areas abandoned** after the catastrophe, outside any effective jurisdiction;
- **competing narratives** about who truly saved humanity, taught in different schools.

For character generation, this is the ground that produces people who grew up in a world where
the aliens are both rescuers and landlords, and nobody has resolved which word is the honest
one. There are no fixed dates here — this is the present tense of play, not a year.

## 14.11 Campaign hooks

The xenopolitical situation is meant to generate play. These hooks are ready to run, and each
turns on the chapter's ambiguities rather than resolving them:

- **Infiltration investigation.** Determine whether a suspected hybrid is dangerous, innocent,
  coerced, or falsely accused (§11.9's procedure is fallible by design).
- **Disputed aid.** Two blocs offer the same town relief with different strings; the town must
  choose or play them off.
- **Confronting the accusation weapon.** A politician, officer or rival is marked as a hybrid;
  the party must decide what proof means when proof is impossible (14.6).
- **Reconstruction mission.** Escort, protect or sabotage a megaproject that may be a lifeline
  or a land grab.
- **PSI cults.** A Law-of-One-derived congregation is simultaneously a support network, a
  political machine and a possible influence operation.
- **Anti-alien terrorism.** The perpetrators have a case; so may their targets.
- **Protecting a defector** from a programme they were born into — and from the humans hunting
  them.
- **Intelligence operations** between services that do not trust each other's loyalties.
- **Alien technology smuggling.** The artifact works; the provenance is a mystery and possibly
  a trap.
- **Competing disaster-relief organizations** with incompatible definitions of "help".
- **A hidden agreement** between human elites and an NHI bloc, and what it traded.
- **A legal case involving a hybrid**, where the verdict matters more than the facts.
- **Faction reputation consequences** for choosing which bloc to accept aid from.
- **A mission into an unreconstructed zone**, where the jurisdiction question has no answer.
- **Both blocs helping the same population** — and expecting the party to pick a side.

Every hook depends on the same canon fact: the players almost never have enough evidence to
be certain, and acting anyway is the job.

## 14.12 Rules hooks

**Status: light. Xenopolitics is history, ideology and social structure, so it supplies
background, faction positions and scenario material rather than mechanics.**

- **A character's xenopolitical stance is an ideological position.** Beliefs about the Crisis
  (`10_SINGULARITY_CRISIS.md` §9.6), the two blocs, disclosure and hybrid rights are
  **Motivations and Faction affiliations** in the existing Affect / US-`FRONTIER` machinery
  ([`8_FACTIONS.md`](8_FACTIONS.md)), on the canonical `−10…+10` scale.
- **The blocs are factions, not stat blocks.** The Confederation and the Orion Group are
  described by the generic faction schema (`8_FACTIONS.md` §7) — type, scale, ideology,
  motivation, reputation, contacts, allies, enemies, network position, resources — exactly like
  any other faction.
- **NHI type is not faction** (`8_FACTIONS.md` §7). Never infer a political alignment from a
  substrate; this is the rule that keeps the map from collapsing into two teams.
- **Investigation uses the existing skill surface.** Infiltration cases, hybrid verification
  (`12_BEINGS.md` §11.9) and intelligence work resolve with the canonical
  `STAT + Skill + 1d10` check (`RULEBOOK.md` §4.2); §39.2's investigation and law packages
  already cover the vocabulary. No new procedure is required.
- **No new mechanics.** This chapter defines no checks, no stat blocks, no costs, no damage and
  no subsystems. Where a number would be needed, this chapter points at the machinery that
  already owns it (`8_FACTIONS.md`, `10_SINGULARITY_CRISIS.md`, `12_BEINGS.md`) and stops.

## 14.13 What this chapter deliberately does NOT define

Per `AGENTS.md` §4 and the author-directed scope of 2026-10-07, the following stay **open**,
and this chapter does not resolve them:

- **any calendar date or fixed chronology** for the Crisis, the intervention or the present
  (the decade is a relative interval, per `AGENTS.md` §6);
- **any verdict on which reading of the two blocs is true** — that ambiguity is §33.32 and is
  load-bearing;
- **any ruling on whether the Confederation engineered its religious movement**, how much, or
  by what means (14.5);
- **numeric statistics of any kind** — no hybrid detection statistics, no faction power
  ratings, no aid-throughput or economy figures;
- **psionic mechanics** for influence, mind control, psychic coercion or interrogation —
  those belong to `rulebook/6_PSYCHIC.md` and remain capability-level;
- **character-generation numbers** — origins, backgrounds and training paths stay the light
  lists of `rulebook/14_CHARACTER_GENERATION.md`;
- **the legal settlement** of hybrid status, citizenship or franchise — the chapter names the
  positions in play and prescribes none;
- **the deep-hacking and cyberspace subsystem**, derived statistics, advancement and economy,
  all of which `AGENTS.md` §4 still reserves;
- **real living political, corporate, military or media figures** acting inside 20XX — any
  such figure is fictionalized (`AGENTS.md` §6.1).

This chapter is a **field of positions and a set of hooks**, not a settlement of the question
in §14.1. The question is the setting.
