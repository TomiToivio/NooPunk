# Tommy Pilgrim

Tommy Pilgrim is a cult science fiction author. His best-known book is NoöPunk (1974) which was originally a cult classic but has grown into a prophetic masterpiece since it seemed to predict the future with a prophetic accuracy. Tommy Pilgrim claims he received NoöPunk in 1974 as a telepathic transmission from a Council of Nine aliens in the orbit of Saturn. Tommy Pilgrim thinks this was the same group of intelligences Philip K. Dick communicated with. It is well-known that he received the transmission when he was tripping on LSD in a Hawkwind concert. NoöPunk really reads like an accurate vision of 2045 seen through Seventies acid kaleidoscope, NoöPunk is like watching Zardoz with a Hawkwind soundtrack.

Tommy Pilgrim is now known as one of the all-time great science fiction authors. He is compared with Philip K. Dick and William Gibson, which is rather ironic as Tommy Pilgrim had a legendary flame war with Philip K. Dick during 1974-1982. Tommy Pilgrim suggested PKD got VALIS all wrong and he should just abandon gnostic christianity, take a heroic dose of acid and perform a magickal ritual to Hermes. After an incident in a science fiction convention William Gibson and Tommy Pilgrim have engaged in a long, pointless and destructive flame war.  

Tommy Pilgrim is now seen as the pioneer of the NoöPunk science fiction genre, subculture and lifestyle. He has published 14 science fiction books seen as the foundation of the NoöPunk genre, three non-fiction books about philosophy, futurology and sociology, NoöPunk tabletop and computer role-playing games, as well as a NoöPunk television series and movie adaptation. He became quite rich and famous. He is also seen as a psychedelic philosopher and futurist like Terence McKenna or Timothy Leary. He has constantly claimed he is still in communication with the Council of Nine and all of his books are based on this communication. 

Tommy Pilgrim has a legion of fans and is now very rich. He worked as a bicycle roadie, mycologist and mime artist for Hawkwind and similar psychedelic rock groups throughout the Seventies, Eighties and Nineties, because he liked to follow Hawkwind as a bicycle roadie, additionally his books were cult classics but didn't sell too well. In late nineties science fiction fans and academics noticed how well NoöPunk predicted reality and he eventually became a rich and famous bestseller instead of a starving cult author. 

When UFOs and PSI became mainstream topics, Tommy Pilgrim started to be seen as a prophet rather than a science fiction author, and he started to attract attention of new age cultists, ufologists, parapsychologists and intelligence agencies. He disappeared from publicity and moved to an unknown location, only communicating online and in cryptic blog posts. UNHA would like to talk to him, but so would many others.

Tommy Pilgrim is based on PKD and his books VALIS and The Man in the High Castle, as well as Andrija Puharich and Law of One.
