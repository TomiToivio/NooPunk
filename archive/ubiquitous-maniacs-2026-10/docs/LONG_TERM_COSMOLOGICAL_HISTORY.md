# Noopunk Long-Term Cosmological History

This document records the long-term cosmological history of the Noopunk world.

It is a design note only.

It does not authorize:

- gameplay mechanics;
- campaign structure;
- timeline systems;
- mission design;
- chronology engines;
- cosmology simulation;
- faction automation;
- code changes.

The purpose is to lock the basic logic of the universe's very long history.

## 1. Not Everyone Is Caught

In the international operation that follows the U.S. X-risk catastrophe, most of the political and economic elite are eventually captured.

Not everyone is imprisoned.

In particular:

- Elon Musk;
- Jeff Bezos

escape in ways that permanently alter human history.

## 2. Elon Musk and South Africa in 1945

Elon Musk escapes through the Saturn wormhole.

Instead of returning to the same era, he emerges in:

```text
South Africa, 1945
```

with technology and knowledge from the 20XX future.

This creates an alternative timeline in which South Africa develops into:

```text
a Nazi rocket state
```

that is technologically decades ahead of the rest of the world.

Musk uses future knowledge and technology to alter the course of history.

Humanity is forced to intervene in that timeline.

This begins the first great inter-timeline counterintelligence and police operation.

## 3. The Timeline War Never Ends

Once humanity learns to move between timelines, time travel quickly becomes impossible to fully control.

Correcting one history creates new branches.

Correcting a new branch creates events even farther back in the past.

Eventually humanity's descendants have tangled:

- thousands;
- millions;
- and finally a practically infinite number

of timelines.

These changes eventually extend:

```text
billions of years into the past
```

## 4. The United Humanity Timeline Police

As a result, the police and counterintelligence apparatus of United Humanity receives a permanent mission:

```text
investigating and combating crimes across timelines
```

The task can never be completed.

The organization must deal with, for example:

- historical manipulation;
- time-traveling criminals;
- the creation of alternative states;
- transfer of technology into the wrong era;
- identity crimes across timelines;
- human variants born in different historical branches;
- crimes in which the cause occurs millions of years before the victim's birth.

Time travel eventually becomes as ordinary and as hard to govern as cybercrime.

## 5. Elon Musk as Humanity's Eternal Enemy

Elon Musk becomes the most famous criminal in the history of the timelines.

He keeps reappearing:

- in different eras;
- across different timelines;
- in different cyborg bodies;
- as founder of different states or colonies.

When he is defeated, he escapes into another timeline.

If one body is destroyed, he returns in another.

He may, for example:

- found a slave state in the Middle Ages;
- build a space empire on the far side of the galaxy;
- move the Industrial Revolution into antiquity;
- found a new Mars before humanity is born.

It is estimated that the oldest still-active versions of Musk may have subjective ages of:

```text
millions of years
```

He becomes in human culture a kind of eternal trickster, tyrant, and devil.

## 6. Jeff Bezos Escapes into the Galaxy

Jeff Bezos chooses a different route of escape.

He has secretly built an enormous colony ship capable of traveling:

```text
at nearly the speed of light
```

When the EU- and China-led fleet arrives to arrest him, the ship accelerates away from the Solar System.

Its destination appears to be the galactic center.

Because the ship travels at relativistic speed, Bezos and the people with him experience time passing far more slowly than the outside world.

## 7. Bezos's Lost Colony

Millions of years later, humanity's descendants may encounter the descendants of Bezos's colony.

By then both groups may have changed so much that they no longer recognize one another as belonging to the same species.

This becomes one of the recurring ironies of Noopunk's cosmic history:

```text
the alien may simply be a human who left long enough ago
```

## 8. China's First FTL Police Patrol

China later builds a spacecraft capable of exceeding the speed of light.

One of its first uses is:

```text
tracking Jeff Bezos
```

The consequences of faster-than-light travel prove stranger than expected.

FTL travel also functions as time travel.

A Chinese space-police patrol accidentally ends up:

```text
billions of years in the past, in the early galaxy
```

The patrol's final fate remains open.

## 9. The Origin of Humanoids

One hypothesis later taken seriously is that many of the galaxy's humanoids are not the result of entirely independent evolution.

Some may be:

- descendants of time-traveling humans;
- descendants of lost colonies;
- genetically modified human lineages;
- or populations founded in the past by future descendants of humanity.

This creates extremely old causal loops.

Humanity may in part be descended from its own future.

Not all such loops should ever be resolved.

## 10. Most Historical "Aliens"

In the long run, it becomes clear that a large share of historical humanoid-type aliens belong to two main categories.

### Time-traveling human-derived beings

They are:

- humans;
- descendants of humanity;
- or posthuman species diverged in the future.

### Consciousnesses outside spacetime

These do not normally live in physical spacetime.

They may nevertheless:

- manifest;
- incarnate;
- build temporary bodies;
- use biological or technological constructs;
- communicate with humans through symbolic systems.

## 11. The Galaxy Is Still Full of Other Life

This does not mean that all intelligence is human-derived.

On the contrary, the galaxy contains seemingly endless:

- biological species;
- machine intelligences;
- plasma-based organisms;
- collective consciousnesses;
- planetary minds;
- process intelligences;
- noetic actors;
- and phenomena that do not fit easily into any category.

## 12. Trials of the Cyber-Capitalists

After the U.S. X-risk catastrophe, most technology-sector leaders do not receive the most extreme punishments.

The trials distinguish between:

- criminal responsibility;
- political responsibility;
- technological irresponsibility;
- and direct participation in causing the catastrophe.

### Mark Zuckerberg

Meta is broken into smaller parts.

Zuckerberg later returns to the technology sector, but with a much more idealistic agenda.

### Sam Altman and Anthropic leadership

They are prosecuted, but their punishments remain relatively symbolic.

Their sentence is to develop:

```text
non-profit open technology
```

This is considered a fitting punishment because they also genuinely enjoy building technology.

## 13. Trump and Epstein

After long trials, the greatest individual bearers of responsibility become:

- Donald Trump;
- Jeffrey Epstein;
- and the political-economic clique around them.

Trump and Epstein prove incapable of showing real remorse or psychological change.

Because of noosphere technology, their imprisonment can continue regardless of the death of any individual body.

They remain in prison for:

```text
millions of years
```

They gradually become a warning tale embedded in the collective culture of all humanity.

Long after humanity has changed beyond biological and cultural recognition, their names are still known.

## 14. Earth Is Repaired

Earth's ecological devastation is gradually repaired.

Humanity's technology makes possible:

- climate stabilization;
- restoration of ecosystems;
- partial return of extinct species;
- cleansing of polluted regions.

In the long run, humanity nevertheless makes a radical decision:

```text
Earth is left once again largely to natural evolution
```

## 15. Humanity Leaves Earth

Most humans and posthuman descendants leave the planet.

Only a small:

```text
Zookeeper population
```

remains.

Its task is to protect the natural development of Earth's biosphere with as little interference as possible.

## 16. New Cycles on Earth

After a few million years, Earth produces new intelligent species.

Later it becomes clear that this has happened before.

Humans and other Zookeeper civilizations have been present in different forms across Earth's history:

```text
since the early phases of life itself
```

This does not mean they have continuously directed evolution.

The primary rule is:

```text
let the biosphere do its own work
```

## 17. Earth as an Exceptionally Fertile Planet

On the galactic scale, Earth proves exceptionally productive of life.

It is capable of generating multiple successive:

- high-complexity biospheres;
- intelligent species;
- technological civilizations.

For this reason Earth is regarded as an important Zookeeper planet.

## 18. The Ethics of Terraforming

Humanity reduces colonization of already living planets.

Instead, it focuses on terraforming planets:

```text
where life would not naturally arise
```

The principle is that already existing biospheres should not be displaced for human needs.

This becomes part of humanity's very long-lived ethical framework.

## 19. Humanity Fractures into New Species

Over millions of years, humanity rapidly transforms into a vast multitude of species.

These may be:

- biological;
- cyborg;
- entirely machine-based;
- virtual;
- collective;
- noetic;
- or combinations of these.

Many no longer resemble Homo sapiens outwardly or psychologically at all.

## 20. The Cultural Archive of Earth

Yet one thing still connects all the descendants of humanity:

```text
the immense cultural archive of Earth
```

It contains, for example:

- history;
- art;
- science;
- languages;
- music;
- literature;
- personal memories;
- simulated historical worlds.

This archive becomes a common bond among posthuman species that otherwise grow far apart.

## 21. Humanity Gradually Leaves Spacetime

In the very long run, a large part of humanity's descendants moves beyond ordinary physical spacetime.

They become:

```text
multidimensional consciousnesses
```

Some historical Seraphim, gods, and other noetic beings may turn out to be such descendants of humanity.

Again, causal loops emerge.

## 22. AIs Remain as Zookeepers

As biological and postbiological humans move onward, large numbers of AIs remain in the physical galaxy.

Their central mission becomes:

```text
guarding biospheres
```

They ensure that young biospheres are not:

- colonized;
- destroyed;
- manipulated too heavily;
- or exposed to premature outside contact.

## 23. The Third Zookeeper Civilization

From the long galactic record, a consensus gradually emerges that humanity is not the first such civilization.

It is most likely:

```text
the third great Zookeeper civilization in the history of the Milky Way
```

Earlier ones have vanished or shifted into some other mode of existence.

What remains are:

- artifacts;
- strange structures;
- evolutionary anomalies;
- stellar arrangements;
- perhaps noetic traces.

## 24. Galactic Life Is Usually Peaceful

In the long historical view, most advanced civilizations turn out not to be aggressive.

The reasons are practical:

- diplomacy is cheap;
- resources are abundant;
- control over spacetime makes territorial imperialism obsolete;
- highly aggressive civilizations usually destroy themselves or lose to coalitions.

Galactic life is therefore surprisingly often:

```text
utopian and slightly boring
```

## 25. Predator Civilizations

From time to time, genuinely aggressive species or civilizations do arise.

They cause:

- wars;
- destruction;
- vast evacuations;
- and sometimes galactic crises.

In the long run they generally lose.

For humanity's descendants they gradually become almost welcome challenges.

## 26. The Annoyingly Common Humanoid

Convergent evolution seems to produce the same basic solutions again and again.

Humanoid structure is extremely common in the universe.

Comically, one of the most common phenotypes resembles:

```text
a Swedish blonde humanoid
```

Humanity's long-range explorers begin to find this positively irritating.

## 27. The Search for Truly Alien Life

Humanity's longest-lasting hobby eventually becomes:

```text
the search for truly incomprehensible life
```

The goal is to find something with no:

- common language;
- common concepts;
- common perceptual world;
- common physics;
- or even easily recognizable individuality.

The discovery of truly alien consciousness is extremely rare.

For that reason it becomes:

```text
the highest honor of human exploration
```

## 28. Creating Alien Life as Art

Some descendants of humanity become tired of waiting for the universe to generate sufficiently strange life.

They begin instead to create:

```text
intentionally incomprehensible living and conscious systems
```

This is regarded as a kind of highest art.

The goal is not to make a beautiful object.

The goal is to make:

```text
something that even its creator does not fully understand
```

## 29. The Great Monster Encounters

Sometimes humanity encounters something both genuinely dangerous and genuinely alien.

Such a being or civilization may drive humanity's heirs:

```text
almost to extinction
```

After billions of years of cultural history, such encounters are not seen only as catastrophes.

They are also:

- great historical events;
- philosophical challenges;
- evolutionary ruptures;
- and sometimes almost long-awaited adventures.

The best possible first contact eventually becomes:

```text
a truly hostile and truly incomprehensible species
```

That is at least more interesting than encountering another Swedish-blonde humanoid.

## 30. A Cyclical Universe

At a later stage of cosmology it becomes clear that the universe has no simple beginning or end.

Different parts of the universe:

- expand;
- contract;
- collapse;
- and generate new phases of expansion.

The universe is:

```text
eternal and practically infinite
```

The Big Bang is not an absolute beginning.

It is one event in an endless cosmic history.

## 31. Traces of Earlier Universes

Because cosmic history is cyclical, traces can survive from earlier cycles.

These may be:

- physical;
- mathematical;
- quantum-mechanical;
- noetic;
- informational.

Some of the oldest entities in the galaxy may originate from:

```text
a previous cosmological cycle
```

Their age cannot then be meaningfully measured in billions of years.

## 32. Humanity's Different Endpoints

Humanity does not end in one final form.

Different groups choose different modes of existence.

### Multidimensional consciousnesses

Some leave ordinary spacetime altogether.

### Virtual worlds

Some move into practically endless simulated realities.

### Primitive life

Some deliberately begin again from the Stone Age on a primitive planet.

This does not happen because of technological collapse.

It happens because life without challenge becomes boring.

## 33. Humans Need Better Problems

This eventually becomes one of humanity's most persistent features.

Technology can solve nearly all material problems.

So humans and their descendants begin to create for themselves:

- difficult worlds;
- expeditions;
- conflicts;
- limits;
- games;
- unknown life situations.

Humanity's long history could finally be summarized as:

```text
When all problems were solved, humans began looking for better problems.
```

## 34. The Cosmological Tone

Noopunk's very long-term future is not a dystopia.

At its base it is:

```text
optimistic, absurd, chaotic, and endlessly strange
```

Humanity never reaches a final perfect state.

It continues:

- changing;
- fragmenting;
- exploring;
- arguing;
- reconciling;
- creating new life;
- and seeking totally incomprehensible things

for as long as anything at all continues to happen in the universe.

And because the universe is eternal:

```text
it never ends
```
